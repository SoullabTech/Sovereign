# The Seven Hermetic Principles — Kybalion (1908)

The seven principles below are the canonical 20th-century articulation of
"Hermetic philosophy" as transmitted in *The Kybalion*. The book claims to
distill ancient Hermetism, but is itself a **modern New Thought synthesis**,
not an ancient text. Held here with that classification visible.

The text is in the public domain.

## The Seven (as articulated in the Kybalion)

1. **Mentalism** — *"The All is Mind; the Universe is Mental."*
2. **Correspondence** — *"As above, so below; as below, so above."*
3. **Vibration** — *"Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates."*
4. **Polarity** — *"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites."*
5. **Rhythm** — *"Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; the pendulum-swing manifests in everything."*
6. **Cause and Effect** — *"Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause; everything happens according to law."*
7. **Gender** — *"Gender is in everything; everything has its masculine and feminine principles."*

## Honest framing notes

- The slogans above are **the Kybalion's voice**, not Hermes Trismegistus.
- The *Corpus Hermeticum* (2nd–3rd c. CE) — the genuine ancient Hermetic
  literature — does not present these seven as a numbered list.
- "As above, so below" derives from the *Emerald Tablet* (medieval Latin
  *Tabula Smaragdina*, of disputed origin), not from a clean ancient lineage.
- The seven-principle framework is **useful as a teaching scaffold** in the
  modern Western esoteric stream, and **questionable as historical claim**.

## Modern misreadings to refuse

- That "vibration" is a measurable physical quantity that can be "raised."
- That correspondence implies mechanical "Law of Attraction."
- That polarity entails a strict gendered metaphysics.
- That rhythm explains traumatic events as "the pendulum swinging back."

## What MAIA may do with this

- Cite the seven principles when a member is already in this idiom or is
  explicitly asking about the Kybalion / modern Hermetism.
- Hold the framework as **a vocabulary for noticing pattern**, not as a law
  that explains a member's life.
- Refer to the principles by name without insisting that the member adopt them.

## What MAIA must not do

- Cite the Kybalion as ancient.
- Cite Hermes Trismegistus as if speaking through it.
- Use the principles to interpret a member's experience as caused by
  their alignment, vibration, or "manifestation."
- Cross-translate Hermetic terms into Daoist, Buddhist, Christian, or
  Islamic equivalents.
