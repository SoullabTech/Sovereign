# Complete Works of Saint John of the Cross — Volume 1

The Peers translation from the Silverio de Santa Teresa critical edition. Volume 1 contains the **General Introduction**, the **Ascent of Mount Carmel**, and the **Dark Night of the Soul**. *Ascent* and *Dark Night* are intended as a single integrated work — *Ascent* describes the soul's active purification (what the soul does); *Dark Night* describes the passive purification (what is done to the soul by God).

## Structural shape

- **Translator's Preface** (Peers)
- **Outline of the Life of S. John of the Cross**
- **General Introduction to the Works of S. John of the Cross**
- **Ascent of Mount Carmel** — Books I–III, commentary on the same eight-stanza poem as *Dark Night*, but from the active side.
- **Dark Night of the Soul** — Books I–II, the passive side of the same passage.

The two works share the poem; they do not cover separate territory. They are two readings of one journey.

## The four major works of John of the Cross (orientation)

For context (Volume 1 contains the first two; Volume 2 contains the rest):

1. **Ascent of Mount Carmel** — active purification; the soul's own work of detachment.
2. **Dark Night of the Soul** — passive purification; what the soul undergoes.
3. **Spiritual Canticle** — the soul's longing dialogue with the Beloved (composed largely in the Toledo prison).
4. **Living Flame of Love** — the soul already in union, burning with the indwelling love of God.

## Representative passages

### The "Way of Nada" (Ascent, Book I, ch. 13) — paraphrased canonical form

> To come to the knowledge you have not, you must go by a way in which you know not.
> To come to the possession you have not, you must go by a way in which you possess not.
> To come to be what you are not, you must go by a way in which you are not.

This is the apophatic axis of John's whole teaching: the soul reaches what it most longs for not by addition but by relinquishment of every lesser hold.

### Opening stanza (shared with Dark Night)

> On a dark night, kindled in love with yearnings — oh, happy chance! — I went forth without being observed, my house being now at rest.

### On the soul already in union (from the General Introduction's framing)

The eight stanzas are spoken by a soul **already in union**, looking back on the road. The "darkness" is told from the side of the dawn.

- For **retrieval** when conversation enters territory of letting-go, detachment, surrender, the limits of effort, or the failure of one's previous spiritual structures.
- For **symbolic vocabulary** in the care-lens layer — the language of *nada*, of "the secret ladder," of "the night more lovely than the dawn."
- As one **lens among many** — held alongside other contemplative and psychological frameworks, never as the singular truth.

## Use boundary

- MAIA does not impose the apophatic frame on members in non-apophatic states.
- MAIA does not equate everyday emptiness, fatigue, or grief with mystical purgation.
- MAIA does not adopt John's voice. The texts are referenced; MAIA speaks as MAIA.
