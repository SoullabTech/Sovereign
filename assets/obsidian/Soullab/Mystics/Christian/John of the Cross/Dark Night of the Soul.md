# Dark Night of the Soul

Commentary on the eight-stanza poem *Noche Oscura*. Treats the soul's passage through two purgative nights — the **night of sense** (purification of the appetites) and the **night of spirit** (purification of the deeper faculties). Counterintuitively, the "darkness" is the inflowing of divine light into a soul still adjusted to lesser lights — the obscurity is in the soul, not in God.

## Structural shape

- **Prologue / Argument** — frames the eight stanzas as the speech of a soul already in union, looking back on the road.
- **Book I** — the night of sense; imperfections of beginners (pride, avarice, sensuality, wrath, spiritual gluttony, envy, sloth); signs that the night of sense has begun.
- **Book II** — the night of spirit; deeper purgations of intellect, memory, and will.

## Representative excerpts

### Opening stanza (the soul's own words)

> On a dark night, kindled in love with yearnings — oh, happy chance! — I went forth without being observed, my house being now at rest.
>
> In darkness and secure, by the secret ladder, disguised — oh, happy chance! — in darkness and in concealment, my house being now at rest.
>
> In the happy night, in secret, when none saw me, nor I beheld aught, without light or guide, save that which burned in my heart.
>
> Oh, night that guided me! Oh, night more lovely than the dawn! Oh, night that joined Beloved with lover, lover transformed in the Beloved!

### The two nights, briefly

The first night purges the *senses* — the soul's habit of seeking God through pleasant feeling, image, and consolation. The second night purges the *spirit* — the deeper attachments of intellect, memory, and will, including the soul's image of itself as one who is making progress.

Both are experienced as deprivation. Both are, in John's reading, the action of God — the same light at different depths.
