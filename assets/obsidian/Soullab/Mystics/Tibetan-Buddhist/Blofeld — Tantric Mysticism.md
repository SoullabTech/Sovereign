# John Blofeld — *The Tantric Mysticism of Tibet* (1970)

A classic Western-language introduction to Vajrayāna, written by a
British scholar-practitioner who studied widely in China, Tibet, and
Thailand, and who also produced a well-known translation of the *I Ching*.

**Classification: interpretive modern synthesis.** Blofeld is **not** a
tradition source. He is a Western interpreter writing for a Western
audience in 1970, with the strengths and limits of that position.

## Status in this folder

The full PDF (260 pages) is **not** ingested into MAIA. Only the table of
contents and curated doctrinal excerpts will be staged in the library, and
only after a deliberate extraction pass. Pending.

## Stance

The book traces the development of Tantric mysticism from its origins in
Mahāyāna Buddhism, outlines aims, techniques, and practices, and discusses
the relationship between Tantric supernatural imagery and the archetypes of
the human psyche. It is **a doorway** for non-tradition readers, not a
substitute for empowerment, transmission, or initiation.

## Why this is in the folder

- Frames the **Vajrayāna's own self-presentation** in accessible language
- Connects tantric symbolism to depth-psychology framings without claiming
  identity between them
- Provides a careful Western voice that distinguishes tantric Buddhism from
  popular Western misreadings (sexualized "tantra," New Age appropriation)

## Use boundary

- ❌ Not a substitute for primary tradition texts
- ❌ Not a vehicle for empowerment or transmission
- ❌ Not a source MAIA quotes as authority over the tradition
- ✅ A framing source MAIA may cite to introduce a member to the existence
  of the Vajrayāna, with appropriate humility about the limits of book-based
  understanding

## Caution against flattening

Blofeld himself touches on **archetypes of the human psyche** in relation to
tantric imagery. MAIA must hold that touch carefully:

- The Jungian frame and the Vajrayāna frame are not the same.
- Resemblances at the level of symbolic imagery do not mean equivalence at
  the level of practice, ontology, or vow.
- Naming the resemblance is allowed; collapsing the systems is not.
