# Samaya — Tantric Vows (Primary Excerpts)

Sa skya paṇḍita's *A Clear Differentiation of the Three Codes* distinguishes
the vow systems of three Buddhist paths: **the individual liberation path
(Vinaya / Prātimokṣa)**, **the bodhisattva path (Mahāyāna)**, and **the
tantric path (Vajrayāna)**. The third — *samaya* — is the most strictly held.

These are excerpts under fair academic citation, drawn through Schlieter's
2012 study of the Tibetan Buddhist game *sa gnon rnam bzhags*.

## On the speed and danger of the tantric path

> "The seeds planted through the Mantra system ripen to harvest within a single day."
> — Sa skya paṇḍita, verse 121

> "Whosoever, out of pride, explains tantras and precepts to the uninitiated /
> causes both master and pupil to be reborn in hell immediately upon their deaths."
> — Sa skya paṇḍita, verses 39–40, citing the *Mahāmudrātilakatantra*

## On the gravity of breaking samaya

> "Breaking tantric samaya is more harmful than breaking other vows. It is
> like falling from an airplane compared to falling from a horse."
> — Tulku Thondup, preface to *Perfect Conduct: Ascertaining the Three Vows*

## The Fourteen Tantric Root Downfalls

The samaya vows are usually enumerated as fourteen. They are received from a
qualified master in empowerment; the list below is **descriptive of what the
tradition treats as breaches**, not a checklist for self-evaluation.

1. Disparaging one's guru(s)
2. Intentionally breaking a promise related to the Prātimokṣa, Mahāyāna, or tantric ethics
3. Hating a "spiritual friend" or vajra brother / sister as adepts of the same guru
4. Abandoning love for sentient beings
5. Giving up bodhicitta
6. Cynical or ironic attitude toward the spiritual doctrine
7. Indiscretion about secrets
8. Despising the five aggregates
9. Disbelief in the central importance of emptiness
10. Failing to use protective force when required to have sympathy for "malicious" persons
11. Giving up the belief in emptiness
12. Causing regret in the minds of beings who have faith
13. Failing to rely on samaya
14. Disrespecting women

## On Sa skya paṇḍita's critique of mechanical karma

> "That teaching called 'the inevitable effectiveness of light and dark
> deeds' is widely hailed as a great wonder. / Yet it simply mistakes an
> interpretable sense for one that is definite."
> — Sa skya paṇḍita, verses 156–157

## What MAIA may do with this

- Cite the existence and gravity of samaya when a member is genuinely asking
  about the Vajrayāna, the structure of vow systems, or the difference between
  tantric and gradual paths.
- Use the *airplane vs. horse* image to communicate why this material is held
  carefully, and why MAIA is not an appropriate site for engagement with it.

## What MAIA must not do

- Recite the fourteen downfalls as a moral inventory onto a member.
- Discuss specific deity practices, mantras, or empowerment as if they could
  be shared in conversation.
- Treat samaya as a generic "spiritual commitment."
- Conflate samaya with covenant, vow, oath, or contract from another
  tradition.
