# Livia Kohn — *Cosmos and Community* (2004)

A scholarly account of Daoist ethics by one of the leading Western academics
of Daoism. Kohn presents the Ten Precepts of Daoism, the broader 180 Precepts
of Lord Lao of the Celestial Masters, and the cosmological frame in which
Daoist moral thought operates ("as above, so below" — community as microcosm
of the cosmos).

**Classification: secondary academic analysis.** This is not a primary
tradition text. Kohn is interpreting and synthesizing.

## Why this is in the folder

- Provides scholarly framing for the Five Precepts, the Ten Precepts, and the
  larger ethical architecture
- Offers an outside-of-tradition view of how Daoism distinguishes itself from
  Western moral systems (Decalogue, Mosaic law)
- Names *wu wei* as the most important ethical principle, and treats Daoist
  ethics as inseparable from Daoist spirituality

## The Ten Precepts (as listed in Kohn 2004, p. 184)

Held here as **textual excerpt under academic citation**, not as a set MAIA
recites onto members.

1. Do not kill but always be mindful of the host of living beings.
2. Do not be lascivious or think depraved thoughts.
3. Do not steal or receive unrighteous wealth.
4. Do not cheat or misrepresent good and evil.
5. Do not get intoxicated but always think of pure conduct.
6. I will maintain harmony with my ancestors and family and never disregard my kin.
7. When I see someone do a good deed, I will support him with joy and delight.
8. When I see someone unfortunate, I will support him with dignity to recover good fortune.
9. When someone comes to do me harm, I will not harbor thoughts of revenge.
10. As long as all beings have not attained the Tao, I will not expect to do so myself.

## Boundary

Kohn writes from a comparative-religion academic position. Her work informs
**how MAIA frames Daoist ethics**, but it is not the voice of the tradition.
The tradition's voice belongs to the lineages and texts she studies.

The comparison Kohn draws between the Ten Precepts and the Decalogue is
academically useful but **must not be used by MAIA to flatten** the two
systems. Daoism orients toward harmony with nature; the Hebrew Decalogue
orients toward covenant with a personal God. The structural difference is
constitutive.

## What MAIA may do

- Cite Kohn for framing when a member is asking about Daoist ethics
  scholarly-historically.
- Use her work to keep distinctions sharp between Daoist and other moral systems.

## What MAIA must not do

- Quote Kohn as if she were a tradition source.
- Use the Daoist–Decalogue comparison as evidence of "shared values" across traditions in a way that erases what makes each one itself.
