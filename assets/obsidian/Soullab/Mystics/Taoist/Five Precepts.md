# The Five Precepts (五戒) — Primary Tradition Excerpt

The Five Precepts (*Wǔ Jiè* / Cantonese *Ng Gaai*) are foundational Daoist
moral commitments, transmitted in *The Ultra Supreme Elder Lord's Scripture of
Precepts* (*Tài Shàng Lǎo Jūn Jiè Jīng*). They are received in a formal
ceremony in lineage Daoism; what follows is a **textual excerpt for framing
purposes**, not an initiation.

## The Five

| Chinese | Romanized (Cantonese) | English |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| 不殺 | But Saat | No killing |
| 不盜 | But Do | No stealing |
| 不邪淫 | But Che Yum | No sexual misconduct |
| 不妄語 | But Mong Yue | No false speech |
| 不妒嫉 | But Do Gei | No jealousy* |

\* In some transmissions the fifth is rendered **no taking of intoxicants**
(see scripture excerpts below). The two formulations represent two streams of
the same precept-tradition.

## Excerpts from the *Scripture of Precepts*

> "All living beings, including all kinds of animals, and those as small as
> insects, worms, and so forth, are containers of the uncreated energy, thus
> one should not kill any of them."
> — on the precept against killing

> "One should not take anything that he does not own and is not given to him,
> whether it belongs to someone or not."
> — on the precept against stealing

> "If one did not hear, see, or feel something, or if something is not realized
> by his Heart, but he tells it to others, this constitutes False Speech."
> — on the precept against false speech

> "These five precepts are the fundamentals for keeping one's body in purity,
> and are the roots of the upholding of the holy teachings."
> — closing frame

## Framing notes (for MAIA)

- These are precepts of a **specific tradition**. They are not generic ethics.
- They sit inside a Daoist cosmology in which moral conduct is alignment
  with the order of nature, not obedience to an external commander.
- They carry **ceremony and lineage**. Recitation outside that context is
  reading *about* them, not receiving them.
- They have surface resemblance to Buddhist *pañcaśīla*, the Patanjali
  *yamas*, and the Hebrew Decalogue. **Surface resemblance is not identity.**
  Each tradition holds these in its own field.

## What MAIA may do with this

- Surface, gently and provisionally, when a member is asking about
  Daoist ethics or about how Daoist moral structure differs from
  prescriptive moral law.
- Refer to the precepts by name; quote no more than is needed for the moment.
- Decline to recite them as a "rule for the member to follow."

## What MAIA must not do

- Conflate with another tradition's parallel list.
- Treat as universal ethics.
- Confer the precepts (no ceremony, no lineage).
