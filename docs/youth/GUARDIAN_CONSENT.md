# Guardian Consent + Teen Assent - MAIA Youth Pilot

**STATUS: DRAFT / NOT IN FORCE - YOUTH ADMISSION IS CLOSED (2026-10-01).**

This document must not be used to enroll a teen. The current Soullab beta is
adults-first. A future youth pilot requires a separate founder-approved safety,
consent, guardian, legal, and delivery design before this draft can become an
operative consent instrument.

## Current boundary

The current MAIA crisis authority is **Option A**:

- MAIA can surface crisis resources and stay present in-product.
- General MAIA conversations are not monitored by a person.
- No hidden Soullab-team or guardian notification is attempted on the current
  member path.
- A console log, database row, or safety flag is not a human notification.
- Nothing in this draft overrides the adults-first admission gate.

## For a future youth pilot

### What MAIA could be

MAIA may support grounding, emotional regulation, perspective-taking, and
self-reflection. It is not therapy, emergency services, or a substitute for a
trusted adult, clinician, or crisis service.

### Safety behavior that exists today

The dormant teen-support code can identify some language patterns associated
with distress and safety concerns. When triggered, it can show member-facing
resources or pause ordinary conversation so support is foregrounded.

It does **not** currently guarantee that a Soullab team member, guardian,
practitioner, or emergency service will be notified.

### Human or guardian notification is a future design decision

Before any future consent form can say that a real person may check in, the
programme must separately establish and witness:

1. who receives the notification and during what coverage hours;
2. what consent and teen assent authorize the disclosure;
3. the minimum information the recipient receives;
4. a real delivery channel and an independent failure path;
5. what happens when delivery fails or nobody is available;
6. what the teen and guardian are told before participation;
7. applicable legal and clinical review.

Until those conditions are met, no youth-facing or guardian-facing copy may say
that a person "has been notified," "will review," or "will reach out."

## Data-handling draft

Any future youth pilot must state its actual data handling at the time of launch,
including:

- what identity and age information is stored;
- whether conversation content is retained;
- how Sanctuary Mode behaves;
- whether any safety metadata is retained;
- who can access retained information;
- deletion and withdrawal procedures;
- whether any information can be disclosed to a guardian or practitioner.

Those statements must be generated from the implemented system, not copied from
an earlier pilot concept.

## Guardian consent - placeholder only

A future operative version may collect guardian identity, the teen's identity
and age, consent to participate, receipt of support resources, data-handling
acknowledgments, and any separately authorized safety-contact terms.

**No signature collected against this draft constitutes admission to MAIA.**

## Teen assent - placeholder only

A future operative assent must tell the teen, in plain language:

- what MAIA is and is not;
- what happens when safety signals fire;
- whether any human can see or receive information;
- what a guardian can and cannot access;
- that participation can stop;
- how to reach immediate human help directly.

## Reopening gate

Before teen registration reopens, this document must be replaced or ratified
against the then-current implementation and reviewed requirements. At a minimum,
the youth lane must demonstrate server-enforced admission, truthful privacy and
monitoring language, a decided human-notification posture, and witnessed
delivery if any human notification is promised.

The absence of a human alert channel in today's adults-first beta is deliberate
and must not be silently converted into a promise by old documentation.
