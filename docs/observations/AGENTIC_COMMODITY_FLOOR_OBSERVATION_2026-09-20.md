## Why an external marketing artifact is recorded at all

It carries no instruction worth following. It is recorded as **evidence about the market**, which is a different evidence class from evidence about the system. Its instructional content is strictly weaker than what the JARVIS manual already enforces — outcome → steps → fallback → test, minus custody, stop conditions, and any definition of what counts as evidence.

Three extractions follow. Each states what it supports and what it does not.

---

## E1 — The commodity floor is visible, and it is low

The artifact prices agentic work openly: a *"Business Insights Report for $500+ that took you 15 minutes"*, site-health maintenance as a monthly subscription, lead-sorting scripts for local trades. Its stated premise: **the barrier to entry is gone.**

**Supports:** the ability to wire an agent to a business outcome is approaching zero marginal cost and is being sold as such.

**Does not support:** any claim about MAIA's competitive position, market size, or defensibility. One piece of marketing collateral is not a market study.

---

## E2 — When execution commoditizes, the differentiator moves upstream

What cannot be reproduced in a weekend: constitutional constraint, consent architecture, traceable authority, refusal that holds under pressure, evidence discipline that survives its own inconvenient results.

Applying the **Failure Test** to Soullab itself: strip the governance layer and what remains is an agent wired to a wellbeing outcome — the commodity floor described in E1. The governance layer is therefore **the product distinction, not overhead carried by it.**

⚠️ **Read with suspicion.** An external artifact that flatters the current strategy is the class of evidence to distrust most. Recorded as orientation, ⛔ never as validation, and it moves no claim from Designed toward Live.

---

## E3 — The inverse risk, which is the one that actually applies here

The artifact's closing advice — *build systems, not cool prompts* — names a problem this project solved and then continued past.

**Present state:** the lane apparatus (charters, censuses, falsifier corpora, witness records, freeze protocols, founder rulings) is mature and load-bearing. Rigor is not the scarce resource. **Velocity is.**

**Named risk:** governance weight applied to work that does not carry constitutional load — a copy change, a dependency bump, a log line, a typo. Ceremony spent where nothing is at stake is not free; it is drawn from the same finite attention the constitutional work needs.

⛔ **This observation proposes relaxing nothing.** The Deep-Intelligence Gate, the Co-Lab release gate, the sovereignty invariant check, the growth-obligation check and every existing freeze stand unchanged. The narrower question it raises:

> **Is there a documented ordinary-work path, or does every change enter through the heavy lane by default — and if the latter, what is the test that sorts one from the other?**

⛔ Not answered here. The sorting test is a constitutional question (a mis-drawn boundary would let real constitutional work escape through the light path), and answering it is a founder act.
