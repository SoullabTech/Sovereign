# Client Prep: Flourishing Platform
**Date**: 2026-06-23  
**Client**: [Positive psychology speaker — flourishing/happiness framework]  
**Purpose**: Pitch how AIN + MAIA can serve their platform vision

---

## What I Hear in This Work

The client has spent years articulating a precise problem: success and flourishing are not the same thing, and most platforms built for "successful people" recreate the very forces that undermine flourishing (hedonic treadmill, comparison, attention capture, consumption). His framework is grounded, well-cited, and has a clear thesis:

> *Flourishing doesn't happen by accident. It requires intention. And it is a practice, not a destination.*

This is not a productivity app client. This is someone who would walk away from the wrong kind of platform.

## Platform Concept Sketch

**Not**: A positive psychology content library. Not a goal tracker. Not another app competing for screen time.

**Instead**: A sovereign flourishing companion — a private space that helps the client's community *practice* what he teaches.

**Three layers**:

1. **Orientation** — Where am I in my flourishing journey right now? (Spiralogic-mapped, not score-based — element/phase, not a dashboard of deficits)
2. **Practice** — What am I actually doing? Relationship quality check-ins, presence practices, meaning prompts, contribution reflection. Memory that tracks the *practice*, not just the intention.
3. **Companion** — MAIA as the voice that remembers across sessions. Not a coach. Not a guru. A witness that holds context and asks the questions that move things forward.

**What it explicitly is NOT**:
- Not a comparison engine ("how am I doing vs. others")
- Not an achievement tracker ("complete your daily flourishing score")
- Not an engagement optimizer (no streaks, badges, or retention mechanics)
- Not a substitute for relationships (it points outward, not inward toward itself)

## Meeting Strategy: Three Gates

Don't lead with a demo. Lead with the architectural conversation — that's what determines whether he's the right client. A demo answers "can it do this?" The meeting should first answer "should this platform exist, and if so, what kind is it?"

**Gate 1 — Does he recognize the gap?**  
Can he see that his keynote necessarily ends where stewardship begins? If he says "yes — that's exactly what people ask me after every talk," the first gate is open.

**Gate 2 — Does he recognize the architecture?**  
When you show the "Typical AI vs. AIN" exchange, does he say "that's exactly how I'd want it to behave"? Now he isn't buying software — he's recognizing his own philosophy expressed operationally.

**Gate 3 — Only then: "Can I see it?"**  
That's the moment for demonstration. Not a product tour — just the stewardship exchange run live. Show the architecture in a single turn.

**Opening line (use early, before anything else):**  
> "We're not here because we think AI needs another wellness application. We're here because, reading your work, we thought: this already describes the architecture of a platform. We wanted to see if you see the same thing."

This isn't "here's what we want to build." It's "here's what we believe your own work implies." That invites collaboration without presuming agreement.

**If he's already in feature-evaluation mode when you walk in:**  
Honor it briefly, then redirect: "Before we get there — can we share what we believe your own work implies? We think it changes what those features should even be."

## Questions to Ask the Client

1. **Who is this for?** — Is this for people who attend his talks? His existing coaching clients? A broader consumer market? The answer shapes whether this is a B2B (speaker → audience) or B2C product.

2. **What does the community look like today?** — Does he have a current platform, newsletter, community? We're building *into* a relationship he already has, not from scratch.

3. **What's the one thing he wants people to walk away doing differently?** — This anchors the first practice loop.

4. **How does he feel about AI in this context?** — His slides warn about the attention economy and tech manipulation. He needs to trust that MAIA doesn't recreate those dynamics. This is worth addressing directly, not hoping he doesn't ask.

5. **What's the business model?** — Subscription? Cohort-based? Bundled with speaking/coaching? This shapes what "platform" means architecturally.

6. **Is there a community/relationship layer he wants?** — His slides call relationships the #1 predictor. Does the platform need peer-to-peer features, or is it purely the companion?

---

## Governance Flags (For Our Side)

- **No engagement optimization**: whatever we build cannot include retention mechanics (streaks, notifications, "come back" nudges) — his own framework indicts them.
- **Memory with consent**: Sanctuary Mode is non-negotiable if people are going to bring their flourishing struggles here. This should be positioned as a feature, not a footnote.
- **No scoring/comparison**: if we build any "how am I doing" layer, it must be self-referential (am I more present than last month?), never comparative.
- **Sovereignty first**: the platform's success metric is NOT daily active users. It's something more like: do people report growing capacity to flourish independent of the platform?
