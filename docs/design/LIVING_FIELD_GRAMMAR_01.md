# LIVING-FIELD-GRAMMAR-01
## Transferable Living Field Grammar

**Status:** cross-surface design grammar
**Date:** 29 September 2026

Living Field Grammar is the transferable language through which Living Field Architecture becomes implementable across Soullab surfaces.

It contains:

- semantic tokens;
- experience dials;
- theme inheritance;
- elemental behaviors where explicitly governed;
- motion roles;
- continuity rules;
- semantic zoom;
- presence laws;
- return grammar;
- relational/provenance markings;
- member / MAIA standing;
- retrofit / canon rules;
- accessibility and reduced-motion requirements.

## Core grammar

### Identity
Identity persists through scale unless the member or source truth establishes otherwise.

### Relation
Relation requires standing. Proximity is not relation.

### Time
Time unfolds within stable identity where possible.

### Zoom
Zoom increases semantic resolution, not merely graphic size.

### Motion
Motion reveals change of view, continuity, or member action; it does not manufacture urgency or importance.

### Atmosphere
Atmosphere holds experience; it does not interpret the member.

### Provenance
Authorship and standing remain perceptible at the point of encounter.

### Return
Every deep exploration preserves a truthful path back to the wider field.

### Agency
Member-authored changes outrank model-suggested interpretations.

### Accessibility
Meaning remains available without color, motion, fine pointer control, or hidden hover-only states.


---

## Technology-layer inheritance

Living Field Grammar may be implemented through DOM, SVG, Canvas, WebGPU, audio, haptics, or future media.

The grammar remains authoritative across rendering technologies.

No rendering tier may redefine identity, provenance, authorship, relation, standing, or importance.

> **The medium may reveal the grammar. It may not rewrite it.**

## Return continuity grammar — 2026-09-29

Living Field return is governed by `LIVING-FIELD-RETURN-CONTINUITY-LAW-01`.

> **Return restores orientation; it does not rewind reality.**

A truthful return preserves, where applicable:

- stable source identity;
- prior field topology / expansion state;
- spatial neighborhood;
- viewport position;
- keyboard focus / attentional locus;
- member-authored state that has legitimate standing.

A return must not preserve as truth merely because it was visible during exploration:

- rejected MAIA frames;
- unconfirmed model hypotheses;
- temporary comparison state;
- hidden salience;
- transient visual prominence.

If the underlying source legitimately changes during an exploration, return should restore orientation to that source's current standing rather than fabricate an obsolete state for the sake of visual continuity.

> **Continuity serves truth; continuity does not overrule truth.**
