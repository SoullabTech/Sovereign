# LIVING-FIELD-EPISTEMIC-PERCEPTION-LAW-01

**Status:** constitutional design law candidate
**Scope:** Living Field and any member-facing projection of member life material
**Authority:** design / presentation constraint only
**Runtime authority granted:** none

## Governing law

> **Visual prominence, proximity, persistence, color, motion, and spatial position must not imply importance, relationship, certainty, or authorship beyond what the underlying governed evidence establishes.**

The Living Field may make life perceptible.

It may not manufacture meaning through presentation.

## Why this law exists

Human perception assigns meaning before a person reads explanatory copy.

A larger item feels more important.

Nearby items feel related.

Persistent items feel more established.

Bright or animated items feel more urgent.

Central items feel more significant.

Repeatedly surfaced material can begin to feel true merely because the system keeps showing it.

Therefore visual treatment is not epistemically neutral.

## The six protected visual channels

### 1. Prominence

Size, contrast, isolation, whitespace, ordering, and typographic weight may express importance only when that importance has a governed source.

Permitted sources include:

- explicit member selection;
- the item currently opened by the member;
- current room / current task context;
- an explicit member-authored priority;
- a factual source lifecycle state that is being represented honestly.

Array order, database order, model confidence, retrieval score, or implementation convenience do not constitute importance.

### 2. Proximity

Spatial nearness must not imply semantic or psychological relationship unless such relationship has standing.

Two presences may appear near one another for layout reasons, but the interface must not create graph-like grouping that a reasonable person would read as meaningful association without evidence.

No embedding similarity, keyword overlap, model inference, repeated co-occurrence, or visual convenience may silently become a relationship.

### 3. Persistence

Repeated visibility must not be allowed to harden a suggestion into autobiographical truth.

A MAIA candidate remains a candidate no matter how often it has been displayed.

A member may confirm, revise, dismiss, or leave a candidate open.

The interface must preserve that standing across repeated appearances.

### 4. Color

Color may support atmosphere, readability, source distinction, or explicit standing.

Color must not independently encode hidden psychological interpretation, elemental assignment, importance, risk, maturity, or developmental stage unless that state has an explicit governed source and a textual equivalent.

Meaning must remain legible without color.

### 5. Motion

Motion may respond to a member gesture, reveal a change of view, or preserve continuity during navigation.

Motion must not imply urgency, aliveness, importance, relational pull, or model confidence without evidence.

Ambient animation must never make one life area appear more psychologically active than another merely because it moves.

### 6. Spatial position

Center, top, first, nearest, and foreground positions are perceived as privileged.

Those positions may reflect current member context or explicit interaction state.

They must not be used as covert rankings of a person's life.

## Visibility is not importance

The quiet Living Field may show only part of the available field.

That partial view must never imply:

> these are the most important parts of your life.

If the system lacks a governed basis for priority, the presentation must frame the visible material as a partial view, sample, current context, or member-selected neighborhood.

A selection mechanism such as `nodes.slice(0, 3)` is a presentation convenience, not an epistemic warrant.

If used, it must be paired with a truthful selection rule or a member-facing frame that makes its partial and non-ranked nature unmistakable.

Preferred sources for a 'currently near' view are factual, member-relevant signals such as:

- explicitly opened in the current session;
- explicitly returned to;
- deliberately revised or authored recently;
- explicitly kept / carried by the member;
- part of the current member-chosen context.

Recency itself is not importance. It may only support a claim such as **recently touched** or **recently returned to**.

## Authorship is perceptual content

The interface must visibly distinguish at minimum:

- member-authored;
- member-confirmed;
- practitioner-authored where constitutionally relevant;
- MAIA candidate.

These differences may not be hidden in metadata available only after inspection.

Member-facing language should be ordinary and humane, for example:

- **your words**;
- **you kept this** / **you confirmed this**;
- **shared with your practitioner**;
- **MAIA noticed this — not yet yours**.

The underlying source enums remain authoritative.

Presentation may translate them; it may not flatten them.

## Atmosphere may support; atmosphere may not assert

Background texture, gradients, light, weather-like depth, and other environmental cues may make the Living Field feel inhabited and humane.

They must not supply psychological meaning that the data does not contain.

A warm region does not mean healing.

A dark region does not mean shadow.

A bright region does not mean importance.

A moving region does not mean activation.

A nearby region does not mean relationship.

Atmosphere is allowed to hold the experience.

It is not allowed to interpret the member.

## AI-specific rule

MAIA may notice, retrieve, compare, and propose.

MAIA does not gain authority because its proposal is visually compelling.

A model-derived candidate must remain perceptually and linguistically subordinate to member-authored or member-confirmed material until the member grants it standing.

No confidence score, salience score, embedding distance, retrieval rank, model attention, or repeated surfacing may be translated into autobiographical importance without an explicit human act.

> **MAIA may notice; the human confers meaning.**

## Review test

Before shipping any Living Field presentation, ask:

1. If this item is larger, nearer, brighter, earlier, more persistent, or moving, what evidence authorizes that difference?
2. Could a reasonable person infer a relationship from this layout that the system has not established?
3. Could a MAIA candidate look equally true as the member's own words?
4. Could the visible subset be mistaken for a ranking of the member's life?
5. If color and animation disappeared, would standing and authorship still be understandable?

If any answer exposes an unsupported inference, the presentation must be revised.

## Relationship to Living Field human-presence law

This law works beside:

> **Living Field presents the member's life as lived presence and continuity, not as categories, ranked nodes, or an abstract psychological model.**

Human presence governs what the experience should feel like.

Epistemic perception governs what the presentation is allowed to imply.
