# CAPACITY-ORCHESTRATION-LAW-01
## Coordination without executive capture

**Date:** 30 September 2026
**Status:** governing architecture law
**Runtime authority:** none

> **Soullab may help make capacities available and coherent. It must not become the permanent executive function that chooses how the member thinks.**

Capacity orchestration is not:

- ranking the member's abilities;
- selecting a best cognitive style;
- forcing a prescribed sequence;
- maximizing tool use;
- deciding that more scaffolding is better.

It is the design of a field in which the member can choose, combine, pause, or decline cognitive supports in relation to the present inquiry.

## Minimum sufficient support

> ## MINIMUM-SUFFICIENT-SUPPORT-LAW-01
>
> **Offer no more structure than the present task requires.**

If the source is already clear:

> no extra scaffold may be needed.

If one capacity is enough:

> do not open five.

If the member is already doing the capacity independently:

> do not interrupt merely to name it.

## Non-ranking

Capacities are not tiers of wisdom.

A present situation may call for:

- Inspect continuity
- Compare versions
- Look for counterevidence
- Change scale
- Leave unresolved
- No tool

No option is inherently more mature.

## Orchestration versus checklist

> ## CAPACITY-NONCHECKLIST-LAW-01
>
> **Capacities may be sequenced when the inquiry genuinely develops, but the system must not convert them into a mandatory checklist.**

Example:

1. member chooses Compare versions;
2. difference reveals unresolved contradiction;
3. member then chooses Look for counterevidence.

The second movement emerges from the inquiry.

It is not:

> Step 2 of the Soullab process.

## MAIA's role

MAIA may, after explicit invitation:

- name a small number of potentially useful capacities;
- explain what each would foreground;
- include no-tool / stay-with-it;
- accept rejection;
- disappear after the member chooses.

MAIA may not:

- rank capacities by hidden fit score;
- select one as the correct capacity;
- keep suggesting after rejection;
- infer a developmental deficit from tool choice.

## Member orchestration

The strongest form is:

> **member notices what kind of support would help and invokes it directly.**

This is consistent with planned obsolescence.

## No-intervention competence

> ## NO-INTERVENTION-COMPETENCE-LAW-01
>
> **Recognizing that no scaffold is needed is itself successful orchestration.**

The system should not treat silence or non-use as missed engagement.

## Failure conditions

03H fails if:

- all capacities are shown by default regardless of need;
- MAIA picks the "best" one without explicit member authority;
- capacity use becomes a progress ladder;
- member cannot choose none;
- sequencing becomes ritualized;
- one capacity remains active after it is no longer relevant;
- the system reasserts a rejected capacity later in the same inquiry.

## Governing sentence

> **Good orchestration helps the person choose their support. Great orchestration increasingly lets them know when they need none.**
