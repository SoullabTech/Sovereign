# SOUL-SERVICE-ACTION-BOUNDARY-LAW-01

**Date:** 30 September 2026
**Status:** governing local design law
**Production authority:** none

> **Soullab must not manufacture action merely to generate feedback.**

Action is one lawful response among several.

Equally lawful responses include:

- ask;
- observe;
- wait;
- rest;
- gather information;
- do nothing;
- decline the experiment.

## 1. Experiment admission tests

A proposed real-world experiment should be considered only when it is:

### Member-authored
The member chooses the act.

MAIA may help clarify wording or constraints.

### Proportionate
The scale of the experiment should match the uncertainty.

Do not use large consequences to answer small questions.

### Reversible where possible
Prefer acts that can be changed, stopped, or recovered from.

### Low-blast-radius where possible
Prefer limited consequences over broad irreversible exposure.

### Consent-respecting
Do not turn another person into an unwitting subject of a manipulative or deceptive test.

### Observable enough
The action should have some prospect of producing information relevant to the question.

### Bounded in time / scope
The system should know what event or window will count as the experiment.

### Non-coercive
The member must be able to choose not to act without being framed as avoidant, resistant, or less developed.

## 2. Action-risk boundary

Soul-Service experimentation is not a blanket permission for action.

High-stakes actions require independent safety / professional standards.

A local Soul-Service prototype should not encourage experiments involving:

- serious physical risk;
- illegal acts;
- medical treatment changes;
- dangerous financial exposure;
- coercion or deception;
- surveillance of others;
- irreversible relationship actions merely to “see what happens.”

## 3. Action bias

> ## SOUL-SERVICE-NO-ACTION-BIAS-LAW-01
>
> **The system must not privilege action over waiting, observation, rest, or non-action simply because action produces more measurable feedback.**

## 4. Exit condition

Every bounded experiment should make visible:

- what the member will try;
- what they expect;
- what would challenge the current frame;
- what would cause them to stop;
- what remains outside the experiment.

## 5. Action authorship

The system must preserve:

> **member-authored action**

not:

> **MAIA told me to do this**

If MAIA supplied candidate wording, that standing remains inspectable.

## Acceptance sentence

> **I can use action to learn without becoming an object of the system's appetite for data.**
