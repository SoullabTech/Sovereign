# DREAM-02R1 — Dream Living Field Visual Authority

**Programme:** SOULLAB-LIVING-ORIENTATION / DREAM-02R1  
**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Status:** founder visual adjudication; reference-state authority only. No runtime build authorized by this document.

## 1. Core ruling

All canonical Dream states are to be recreated as **living fields**, not static pages.

The seven reference states:

1. Arrival
2. Re-member
3. Dream Page
4. Encounter
5. Amplify
6. Dream Series
7. Integrate

belong to one continuous Dream environment.

The visual contract is not seven separate screens. It is one room whose field changes as the member's relationship with the dream deepens.

## 2. Non-determination law

The Dream room must not contain identifiable visual imagery that could pre-shape the member's symbolic experience.

Do not use canonical background imagery such as:

- dogs;
- houses;
- forests;
- doors;
- paths;
- mountains;
- animals;
- human figures;
- faces;
- moons used as symbolic content;
- ritual implements;
- mandalas;
- deities;
- Tibetan iconography;
- shamanic figures;
- archetypal characters;
- alchemical emblems;
- tarot-like imagery;
- dream illustrations.

These may appear only when they originate from the member's own dream material or are explicitly introduced later as amplification.

The governing rule is:

> **The field may evoke, but it must not tell the member what to dream.**

## 3. Abstract symbolic field

The canonical environmental field should work through qualities rather than depictions.

Allowed visual language includes:

- darkness and luminous emergence;
- depth gradients;
- translucency;
- veils and membranes;
- soft axial geometries;
- near-circles and incomplete arcs;
- barely-visible lattices;
- non-figurative thresholds;
- restrained points of light;
- slow parallax;
- subtle atmospheric grain;
- concentric spatial breathing;
- asymmetrical balance;
- empty central space;
- faint radial order that never resolves into a named symbol;
- horizonless depth;
- soft warm/cool transitions;
- slow changes in opacity and scale.

The field should invite projection without supplying content.

## 4. Ceremonial influence without iconography

Tibetan Buddhist, shamanic, archetypal, Jungian, alchemical, and ceremonial traditions may inform the room at the level of **structure**, not illustration.

### Tibetan Buddhist influence may inform

- spaciousness;
- emptiness;
- center/periphery relationship;
- contemplative pacing;
- layered veils;
- luminous dark;
- the sense that form emerges from and returns to openness.

It must not use recognizable Buddhist sacred imagery by default.

### Shamanic influence may inform

- threshold;
- descent / return rhythm;
- liminality;
- attunement to darkness, silence, and sensory depth;
- the felt transition between ordinary and imaginal attention.

It must not use feathers, drums, masks, medicine wheels, animal guides, or culturally specific ceremonial objects as environmental decoration.

### Archetypal / Jungian influence may inform

- depth;
- polarity;
- center;
- shadow/light relation;
- containing vessel;
- descent and return;
- the sense of a larger psyche surrounding conscious attention.

It must not assign archetypal images before the dreamer does.

### Ceremonial influence may inform

- threshold crossing;
- intentional pacing;
- before / during / after distinction;
- a sense of entering;
- a sense of containment;
- a sense of closure without completion.

It must not become theatrical ritual UI.

## 5. Living-field behavior

The Dream field should subtly change with state.

### Arrival

Wide, receptive, mostly empty.

Field qualities:
- deep surrounding dark;
- distant low-luminance structure;
- one calm zone of invitation;
- very little motion.

The field says: **there is room here.**

### Re-member

The visual field becomes slightly more intimate.

Field qualities:
- warmer center;
- softened perimeter;
- almost imperceptible inward gathering;
- reduced external contrast.

The field says: **stay close to what is arriving.**

### Dream Page

The member's dream becomes the brightest and most materially stable object.

Field qualities:
- dark, quiet surround;
- paper / vellum / luminous page;
- almost no ambient movement near the text.

The field says: **the dream itself has authority.**

### Encounter

The relation between Dream and MAIA becomes spatially alive.

Field qualities:
- gentle two-field polarity;
- slight relational tension between member text and MAIA presence;
- faint connective geometry that remains abstract.

The field says: **something is being met.**

### Amplify

The field opens outward.

Field qualities:
- additional faint spatial layers;
- wider depth;
- subtle branching possibilities;
- no fixed symbolic icons.

The field says: **there is more than one way to see this.**

### Dream Series

The field becomes longitudinal.

Field qualities:
- traces;
- temporal spacing;
- distant recurrence;
- softly connected points or passages;
- no pictorial scenes.

The field says: **something has been moving over time.**

### Integrate

The field quiets again.

Field qualities:
- fewer layers;
- warm return;
- softened motion;
- increased negative space.

The field says: **you may carry something, or nothing.**

## 6. Reference-board interpretation rule

The existing founder-approved Dream boards remain valid for:

- composition;
- hierarchy;
- material quality;
- spatial proportions;
- typography;
- MAIA placement;
- page relationships;
- card scale;
- membrane treatment;
- tonal warmth;
- overall emotional register.

Literal illustrative content in those boards is **not** canonical.

The following are examples of non-canonical illustrative content that must be abstracted in implementation:

- dog images;
- houses;
- garden doors;
- forest scenes;
- literal landscape thumbnails;
- narrative image cards.

These are treated as concept placeholders for dream-specific content, not default environmental assets.

## 7. Member-origin image law

Identifiable imagery becomes appropriate only when it originates from the member.

Examples:

- a dream image generated from the member's own description;
- an uploaded drawing;
- a member-selected visual;
- a recalled symbol the member explicitly names;
- imagery created during explicit amplification or active imagination.

Even then, the source must remain clear:

- original dream;
- member association;
- imaginal continuation;
- generated visualization;
- amplification.

No generated image may silently become part of the original dream record.

## 8. Visual induction law

The room may support a dream-compatible state of attention through:

- quiet;
- darkness;
- softness;
- slow rhythm;
- depth;
- ambiguity;
- non-resolved form;
- visual breathing;
- containment;
- emptiness;
- subtle luminosity.

It should not attempt to “induce dreams” by supplying suggestive content.

The desired effect is:

> **receptivity without implantation**

## 9. MAIA visual presence

MAIA's visual presence in Dream should remain abstract.

Prefer:

- luminous orb;
- field condensation;
- restrained halo;
- subtle relational glow;
- text / voice presence.

Avoid:

- face;
- avatar;
- figure;
- animal;
- guru;
- guide character;
- sacred icon.

MAIA should feel present, not personified.

## 10. Visual continuity across all seven states

The following remain constant:

- left House membrane;
- Dream room spatial frame;
- nocturnal material world;
- editorial serif hierarchy;
- warm dark / vellum relationship;
- abstract MAIA presence;
- right-side relational membrane where appropriate;
- restrained gold / lunar-silver accents;
- atmospheric but non-figurative field;
- Soullab architectural proportions.

What changes is:

- density;
- openness;
- depth;
- relational polarity;
- field motion;
- amount of visible structure;
- degree of progressive disclosure.

## 11. Motion law

Motion must be nearly subconscious.

Allowed:

- 12–30 second ambient cycles;
- 1–3% scale drift;
- soft luminance breathing;
- extremely slow parallax;
- faint geometric emergence / dissolution;
- state-change transitions under 800ms for functional UI and longer ambient settling afterward.

Never:

- spinning sacred geometry;
- pulsing “energy” effects;
- particle storms;
- cosmic tunnels;
- obvious psychedelic animation;
- attention-grabbing loops;
- motion synchronized to inferred emotional state.

Reduced-motion mode must preserve the field through static depth and light.

## 12. Acceptance test

The Dream visual system passes when:

- a member can project their own dream imagery into the room;
- nothing in the default environment suggests what symbols they should encounter;
- the room feels unmistakably different from a productivity dashboard;
- the room feels sacred without using religious decoration;
- the room feels psychologically deep without clinical visual language;
- the room feels ceremonial without staging a ritual;
- the room feels imaginal without becoming fantasy art;
- MAIA feels present without becoming a character;
- all seven states feel like the same living place;
- the dream remains more visually authoritative than the interface.

## 13. Exact implementation law

The reference boards are to be recreated by **design grammar**, not screenshot tracing.

Implementation should preserve:

- structure;
- scale;
- hierarchy;
- atmosphere;
- material contrast;
- field relationship.

It should replace literal illustrative environment imagery with the abstract symbolic field defined here.

Member-origin dream imagery may later inhabit the room locally and contextually.
