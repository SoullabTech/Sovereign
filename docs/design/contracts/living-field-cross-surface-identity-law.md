# LIVING-FIELD-CROSS-SURFACE-IDENTITY-LAW-01
## One thing, many surfaces

**Date:** 29 September 2026
**Status:** governing architecture law
**Runtime authority:** none

> **A living object has one governed identity across Soullab. Each surface receives a projection of that identity, not a new copy of the thing.**

A work, relationship, person, question, source, or other governed object may appear in multiple Soullab rooms.

Different rooms may reveal different dimensions and affordances.

They may not silently create different identities for the same governed object.

## Identity spine

Cross-surface identity is carried by an **identity spine** with four distinct layers.

### 1. Living-object identity

The stable object being traversed.

Examples:

- Elemental Alchemy as a continuing work;
- one relationship;
- one source-bearing question;
- one project;
- one person, where governed identity exists.

The living-object identity is not a page route, component ID, file path, or visual node.

### 2. Source lineage

The source-bearing history belonging to that identity.

This may include:

- versions;
- revisions;
- source artifacts;
- provenance;
- timestamps;
- supersession;
- member-authored meaning;
- source-authorized relations.

A source version may change while the living-object identity remains stable.

### 3. Surface projection

The way the same living object is expressed in a particular room.

Examples:

- Living Field — lived context;
- Grokker — relational / spatial context;
- Writer's Studio — creative-development context;
- MAIA — dialogical context;
- Relationships — relational-practice context;
- House — organism orientation context.

Surface projection does not create new ontological identity.

### 4. Encounter state

Temporary state used to support the current visit.

Examples:

- current semantic zoom;
- selected passage;
- scroll position;
- temporary MAIA aperture;
- local comparison;
- currently open relation inspector.

Encounter state is not identity.

It ordinarily does not survive beyond the traversal unless separately authorized.

## Governing sentence

> **Same thing. Different affordance.**

Writer's Studio may edit the work.

Grokker may spatialize its relations.

Living Field may situate it within lived context.

MAIA may converse about it.

None of those surfaces becomes the owner of the object's identity.

## Source-of-truth law

> ## CROSS-SURFACE-SOURCE-TRUTH-LAW-01
>
> **Every surface must reconcile to the governed current source rather than maintaining an independent semantic copy of the object.**

A surface may cache presentation state for performance.

It may not maintain a divergent autobiographical truth.

## Surface-local authority

Each surface has local permissions.

Examples:

- Writer may mutate manuscript text where authorized;
- Grokker may author / revise member relations where authorized;
- MAIA may propose a perspective but not rewrite source;
- Living Field may reveal continuity but not infer narrative.

A surface-local action cannot acquire authority merely because another surface displays it.

## Mutation propagation

When an authorized source mutation occurs:

1. stable living-object identity remains;
2. source version advances;
3. other surfaces reconcile to current truth;
4. the returning surface may expose an inspectable delta;
5. no surface invents the meaning of the change.

## Identity merge / split

Identity itself is high-authority.

The system must not automatically merge two objects merely because they look similar.

If MAIA suspects two objects are the same:

> **possible same identity**

remains a candidate relation until governed confirmation.

Likewise, one continuing work does not split into several identities merely because:

- time passes;
- format changes;
- it appears in another room;
- a new version is created.

If a work genuinely branches or splits, that needs an explicit source/member event and preserved lineage.

## Cross-surface relation law

A relation authored in one surface may be shown in another only when its standing permits that projection.

Example:

> member-authored Elemental Alchemy ↔ teaching relation

may travel.

Example:

> temporary MAIA possible connection

does not silently become a Grokker edge.

## Inspectability

Any surface should be able to answer, at an appropriate level:

> **Why is this the same thing I saw elsewhere?**

The answer should be based on identity / provenance, not visual resemblance.

## Failure conditions

Cross-surface identity fails when:

- the same source becomes unrelated copies across rooms;
- one surface owns a stale semantic copy;
- a temporary AI interpretation becomes part of identity;
- version changes create duplicate living objects;
- object identity changes because the route changes;
- local relation standing is lost in transit;
- a surface cannot reconcile back to current source truth;
- the user cannot tell whether two representations are the same governed object.

## Acceptance sentence

> **I can move to another room and still know I am with the same thing.**
