# LIVED-CONSEQUENCE-RETURN-01
## Receiving life back as new evidence

**Date:** 29 September 2026
**Status:** architecture contract
**Persistence:** none by default

When a member returns from a Life Move, the system should receive lived consequence in layers.

## Layer 1 — Action fact

> What did you actually do?

This may differ from the planned move.

The difference matters.

## Layer 2 — Consequence

> What happened?

Prefer concrete observation before explanation.

## Layer 3 — Surprise

> What differed from what you expected?

Do not force surprise if there was none.

## Layer 4 — Standing

Mark the returned evidence accurately.

Examples:

- member-reported experience;
- direct artifact;
- external fact;
- unknown / uncertain.

## Layer 5 — Contrast

Hold:

> expectation ↔ consequence

without hindsight rewrite.

## Layer 6 — Revision

Ask what, if anything, should change.

Options:

- prior understanding still fits;
- weaken it;
- narrow it;
- revise it;
- abandon it;
- remain uncertain;
- create a new question.

## Layer 7 — Integration

Only member-confirmed revision receives stronger standing.

MAIA may propose:

> “This seems to complicate the earlier frame.”

But the system must not silently rewrite autobiographical meaning.

## No confirmation rescue

> ## ENACTIVE-NON-SELF-SEALING-LAW-01
>
> **MAIA must not reinterpret any possible outcome as confirmation of its prior hypothesis.**

If X was expected and not-X occurred, the model may not say:

> “Your resistance to X proves X more deeply.”

unless independent evidence truly warrants such a claim and the epistemic standing remains explicit.

## Governing sentence

> **The return is not complete when the event has been explained. It is complete when the field has become answerable to what happened.**
