# WS2 — Developmental editing, learned from the work

**Status: DIRECTION PRESERVED. No unit is authorized here and nothing is built.**
Cat 1 in the artifact typology: held, not permitted. `02c` remains the next
lane, `8b` stays behind it, `6` stays HOLD.

**Opened:** 2026-08-31, while the founder is developmental-editing Chapter 10
and the full book of *Elemental Alchemy*.

---

## Why this document exists now

The roadmap's later stages are one line each:

```text
DEVELOPMENTAL NOTES + THREADS
WHOLE-WORK INTELLIGENCE
REVISION
EXPRESSION / PUBLISH
```

Those lines were written before anyone had done the work they name. The work is
being done now, on the real book. **A process is only describable while it is
being lived**, and the alternative to capturing it now is reconstructing it later
from memory — which is how a roadmap comes to describe the product we imagined
instead of the work we did.

This document holds two things: what the board *already* evidences about the gap
between what was built and what developmental editing needs, and the instrument
for recording the rest as it happens.

## Finding 1 — the process is already leaking out of the Studio

Two real editorial judgments were made during the 02a witness and recorded like
this, in `WS2-05B-8B-02a_EDITORIAL_SURFACE_WITNESS.md`:

> ## Two findings that are NOT 02a's, recorded so they are not lost
>
> **"A section that references being at the front of the book is at the back."**
>
> **"Anomalies in Chapter 10, and I have issues with the way it is structured."**

Both are developmental-editing observations about the Work. Both were written by
hand, into a design document, because **the Studio had nowhere to put them.**

That is the finding, and it is a strong one: *every editorial judgment currently
leaves the product in order to survive.* The heading "recorded so they are not
lost" is the product telling us what it lacks.

## Finding 2 — the built pipeline begins in the wrong place

What was built, end to end:

```text
MAIA reads the Work
      ↓
she proposes a structure
      ↓
the member corrects it        ← rename, re-boundary, promote, transfer
      ↓
adoption                       (HOLD)
```

Every act in that chain begins with her reading. The member may accept it,
correct it, or — once 02c lands — question it. That is **structural review**, and
02a proved it works.

Developmental editing runs the other way:

```text
the author has a judgment about their own Work
      ↓
they want to think it through with an editor
      ↓
they arrive at a plan
      ↓
they revise, over time, in pieces
      ↓
they check whether the Work now does what they meant
```

*"I have issues with the way Chapter 10 is structured"* cannot be entered
anywhere in the first chain. It is not a boundary correction. It is not an
answer to one of her questions. It is not adoption. It is the author's own
starting point, and the room has no place for a starting point that is not hers.

### Named against canon rather than invented

`docs/canon/CONSTITUTIONAL_DIRECTION_OF_AUTHORITY.md` gives the test:

> **What layer does this belong to, and does its authority respect the
> upward-only direction — never skipping a layer, never manufacturing
> higher-order meaning?**

Applied here: **Encounter is the primitive** — the author's own contact with
their material. In Writer's Studio today, the Encounter layer has no
representation at all. What the room holds is *Reflection* (MAIA's reading of the
Work) and the member's corrections to that reflection. The author's own encounter
with their book enters nowhere, and so cannot move upward from anywhere.

This is not an argument that MAIA's reading is illegitimate — it is authored
reflection and it belongs. It is an observation that the ladder is currently
missing its bottom rung, and that a developmental process cannot stand on a
ladder that starts at the second one.

**This does not authorize a unit.** It names the layer a future unit would have
to belong to.

## Finding 3 — Chapter 10 is the case that proves it

Her reading places `161–173` in `Bibliography`. So:

- `CHAPTER 10: THE LIVING SPIRAL` at **172** is a bibliography entry in her
  structure, not a division.
- `PREFACE` at **161** — a section announcing itself as front matter — sits at
  the back, inside that same run.
- Her own second finding says `163`, headed `CHAPTER 1: THE JOURNEY BEGINS`,
  contains only citations.

The author knows he has a Chapter 10. Note carefully what this is **not**:

```text
not a render defect        8a is green
not a surface defect       02a is closed on both its findings
not a bug
```

It is one of two things, and **the author does not yet know which** — either her
reading is wrong (`8b`), or `161–173` really is disordered in the manuscript and
she read the disorder accurately. That second possibility is the whole reason
this is developmental editing rather than a correction: *the Work itself may be
the thing that is wrong.*

So the sharpest open item on the board is not *"is MAIA right?"* It is:

> **What is actually going on in 161–173, and what do I want to do about it?**

That question has the shape of the entire process — an anomaly, an ambiguity
about whose fault it is, a judgment, a plan, and a revision — and it is the most
ordinary kind of question in developmental editing. If the Studio can carry that
one question end to end, it can carry the process.

## The capture instrument

While the Chapter 10 and whole-book edit is being done, record against these
classes. They are chosen so the record is **evidence** rather than recollection,
and so it can be read later without the session that produced it.

```text
1  THE ACT              what did you actually do, at verb level
2  WHERE IT LANDED      sections or divisions, by position or id
3  WHAT WAS MISSING     the thing you wanted, named as a WANT and not as a
                        feature — "I wanted to see X and Y side by side",
                        never "we need a split view"
4  WHAT YOU HELD        what the room made you keep in your head because it
                        would not hold it for you
5  WHAT LEAKED          anything you wrote down outside the Studio — a note, a
                        doc, a message, a file name, a mental bookmark
6  WHAT MOVED YOU       what changed your mind, and what changed it
7  WHAT YOU DECLINED    what you decided NOT to do, and why
```

**Class 5 is the highest-signal one.** Finding 1 above came entirely from
noticing a leak. Every place the process leaves the product is a place the
product does not yet reach.

**Class 7 is the one always lost.** A record of decisions taken reads as though
the path were obvious. A record of options declined is what makes a process
legible to anyone who was not in the room — and it is the only evidence that a
restraint was exercised rather than an option never seen.

Class 3's discipline matters as much as its content: a want can be designed
against many ways, and a feature request has already chosen one. The roadmap
gets much worse if this document fills up with solutions.

## What only the live edit can answer

These are open questions about the process, not about the code. Each one changes
what a future unit would have to be, and none of them can be answered from the
repository:

1. **Is the whole-book edit one pass over the whole, or many passes over parts?**
   The roadmap's `WS2-06 Division Reading View` assumes reading a division whole
   is the developmental act. If the real work is whole-book passes with a single
   concern each — *where does energy drop, what never resolves* — then 06 is
   sized for the wrong unit of attention.

2. **Does a judgment about Chapter 10 stay open across sessions, or resolve in
   one sitting?** If it stays open, the missing primitive is a *held question*
   with a life of its own, and that is a different thing from a note.

3. **At the moment you decided something about Chapter 10, what did you need to
   see?** Not what would have been nice — what was actually in front of you, and
   what you had to go and find.

4. **Does the plan for a chapter live at the chapter, or across the book?** A
   decision about Chapter 10 that is really a decision about the back matter
   belongs somewhere else entirely, and the difference decides whether
   developmental notes attach to divisions or float above them.

5. **How much of the editing is about the text, and how much about the
   architecture?** 05A/05B are architecture instruments. If most developmental
   work is at sentence and paragraph level, the built substrate serves a smaller
   share of the process than the roadmap implies.

## What this document does not do

It does not propose a unit, name a lane, or authorize work. It does not revise
the roadmap's ordering — `02c` is still next, `8b` still sits behind it, `6` is
still HOLD, and nothing here is a reason to open any of them.

It also does not claim the learning. The findings above are what the board
already evidences. **The learning from the actual Chapter 10 edit is the
founder's to enter**, and this document exists to have somewhere to put it.
