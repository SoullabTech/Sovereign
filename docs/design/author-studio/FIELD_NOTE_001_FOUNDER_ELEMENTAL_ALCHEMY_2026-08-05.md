# Field Note 001 — The Founder Meets His Own Book in the Room

> **Status**: the first field note of the Creator Encounter phase, in the
> ruled format. Context caveat, stated honestly: this arose during a guided
> URL walkthrough (evidence-gathering), **but the reaction itself was
> unprompted** — the creator opened his real manuscript and the response
> ("this is an odd UIUX") was spontaneous, not elicited. The formal
> unprompted encounter with a non-founder creator remains unburned.

```
Creator:   Kelly (founder — walking as an author, not as a designer)
Context:   His real 209-page published book (Elemental Alchemy, KDP print
           PDF import, 216 print pages / 174 sections) opened in the
           Writer Canvas at /writers-studio/canvas, mid-book scroll.
First thing they did:       Scrolled the manuscript.
First thing they expected:  "I'm working on Elemental Alchemy" — the
                            writing experience as the center of gravity.
Exact words:                "this is an odd UIUX" · "It feels like a
                            dashboard and an editor have been overlaid" ·
                            "I'm standing in the library while
                            simultaneously sitting at the writing desk" ·
                            "The document is visually dominating the
                            environment" · "I'm writing a book. Not:
                            reading page 7 of a PDF." · "persistent
                            reminders that I'm inside software" ·
                            "One room. One purpose."
Gesture they attempted:     Being IN the book as its author — reading/
                            writing continuously as a working draft.
Where the room supported:   Typography and palette ("attractive");
                            honest head facts; the drawers exist as the
                            right adjacencies conceptually.
Where the room failed:      (1) no center of gravity — head-of-room
                            metadata + document overlaid with no
                            transition; (2) the rail reads as persistent
                            software nav while writing, not adjacent
                            rooms; (3) reader/editor ambiguity — "PDF
                            reader" look with an editing gesture floating
                            in it; (4) hierarchy inverted — the imported
                            document dominates the environment; (5)
                            imported print pagination ("-- 6 of 216 --")
                            inside the working text breaks the writing
                            mental model; (6) "Keep a version" floats
                            with no relationship to the act of writing.
```

## The creator's own direction (founder, same encounter)

- **The environment hasn't committed to one identity.** After the
  five-room ontology, the Canvas deserves the same clarity: *one room,
  one purpose.*
- **Work becomes radically simple**: the manuscript is the center; title
  and status quieter; everything else recedes.
- **Rooms, not panels**: clicking Structure / Materials / History changes
  the room completely — moving through a house, not operating an
  application. Consistent with the rest of AIN.
- **Writing mode shows a continuous working draft**; print pagination
  belongs to an intentional print/proofing view, never the default
  writing surface.

## Gate assessment (per the ruled authorization gate)

The qualifying sentence exists: **the creator tried to be in his book as
its author, and the room could not support that mental model.** Not
"would be nice" — an attempted human gesture the environment failed to
honor. This opens ONE design conversation: **the Canvas room-identity
refinement** (center of gravity · rooms-not-panels · writing-vs-proofing
view · versioning as a consequence of writing). The stop-rule still bars
everything else (collaboration, MAIA expansion, publishing, etc.).

## One substrate finding, separated from the UX finding

The `-- N of 216 --` markers are **inside the draft text itself** — the
PDF ingest carried print-page separators into the section bodies, and the
draft was founded verbatim from that source. Hiding them is therefore not
a rendering toggle on the current data: it is (a) an ingest-quality
question (strip/segment print-page artifacts at the confirm-the-cuts
threshold, under the member's eye) and (b) a view question (writing view
vs print/proof view) for text already imported. Both belong to the opened
design conversation; neither is a CSS fix.
