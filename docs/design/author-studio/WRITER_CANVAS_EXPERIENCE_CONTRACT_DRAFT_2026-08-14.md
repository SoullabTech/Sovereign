## What this room is for

A creator continuing a work that is already theirs. The room's whole claim is made on
arrival: **"Your work is here. Continue."** It is not a place to manage writing, choose
between capabilities, or decide what to do — it is the place the work is mid-motion on the
table where it was left. *(Room Map, "The experiential question first")*

## What this room is NOT for

*(Recovered from the ruled refusals — recorded so they need not be refused again.)*

- ⛔ Not a capability inventory. No tab row, no card grid on arrival. *(Room Map, "Arrival state")*
- ⛔ Not a file manager, repository, or sync target. *(Materials design, "What Materials is NOT")*
- ⛔ Not an outline tool, and no structure templates, auto-outline, or inferred hierarchy. *(Structure design, "Refused now")*
- ⛔ Not a progress surface. No bars, percentages, streaks, counts styled as progress, or "3 of 12 chapters complete." *(Room Map, "Orientation, not measurement"; Structure design)*
- ⛔ Not a place MAIA generates the work. *(Room Map, five questions, #5)*
- ⛔ Not a publishing or typesetting machine — advanced production structure belongs to Press Editor. *(Division ruling)*

## Arrival

> **Your work is here. Continue.**

The member sees exactly three things, and nothing else *(Room Map, "Arrival state")*:

1. **The work, named by its becoming** — the member's own title, the member's declared
   expression. An unnamed work stays unnamed.
2. **The creative field, open at the last gesture** — never a blank pane unless the work is
   genuinely new, and even then the thing carried through the doorway is already on the table.
3. **Two quiet edges** — a folded context spine left, a folded presence right. Closed.
   Openable. Silent.

Arrival **is** continuation. "Continue" is the default state, not a button — and per the copy
voice, *the strongest rewrite is deletion*: if the page already opens where they were, no
sentence should say so.

## Gestures

| Gesture | Language used | Why this wording |
|---|---|---|
| Continue the work | *(no label — the field is simply open, focused, mid-motion)* | The design says it; the copy must not |
| Tend the work's identity | **Give it a name when you're ready** | The member's words; naming is never required |
| Declare a form | **What is this becoming?** | A declaration, never a classification |
| Bring a material in | **Bring this to [work]** | A belonging, not an upload |
| Set a material beside the work | **Bring to the table** | Physical, not "attach" |
| Keep a version | **Keep a version** | Preservation, not version control |
| Invite reflection | **the Window, folded until opened** | Invited; never announced |

## MAIA's role and limits

**May**: remain folded at the right edge as a standing offer · open only when invited ·
reflect a passage · surface material the member already brought · offer — *"this may be a
section boundary"* · retrieve Field Objects — *"you wrote about something similar last year.
Would you like to see it?"*

**May never**: appear in the writing surface uninvited · generate the work · assert what the
work is becoming · cluster, theme, or auto-organize materials · say *"this belongs in this
chapter"* or *"these three form a theme"* · create, alter, or dissolve a belonging.

The grammar, carried whole: **the system offers · the system preserves · the creator adopts.**
Only the third makes meaning.

## House vs Room

**Shared with the House**: the token layer and field hierarchy · human-verb gesture language ·
provenance voice · MAIA folded until invited, exactly as in Journal.

**Distinct to this room**: Journal opens on a *question*; the Canvas opens on the member's own
*sentence*, already in motion. Journal's activity is inscription; the Canvas's activity is
continuation. The Canvas holds one instrument at a time and lets both wings fold to nothing —
the work is the only thing that cannot disappear.

## What persists, and what does not

**Persists because it is relationship** *(calm-computing check, Q5)*: the work's name and its
becoming · the folded Window as a standing offer.

**Persists as authored fact**: declared expressions · belongings and their sentences · kept
revisions · the last gesture and open instrument.

**Does not persist**: which drawer was open · anything inferred about the member. Doorway
intentions and starting conditions configure only *which instrument opens* and *which drawers
arrive seeded* — they **never brand the room, fork the product, or fix the work's identity**.
*(Room Map, "Mode is posture, not identity")*

## Transitions in and out

**In** — from the House (`/writers-studio`, label *Writer's Studio*); from Studio Home into
the Canvas; by entry condition, which seeds drawers without changing the room.

**Out** — back to Studio Home; to the Source/Working Draft surface; and eventually into Press
Editor. That last transition is governed: **it is not a transfer.** *The writer never leaves
the manuscript; they open one expression of it for publication.* The Working Draft remains
intact; the Edition derives from a declared, immutable revision; later writing is **offered**
to the edition, never synced over it. *(Division ruling; substrate binding §2.3)*

## Minimum inhabitable experience

The room is inhabitable when a creator can, in one sitting, without leaving:

1. arrive and find their work mid-motion, named by its becoming;
2. write, and have the words held;
3. bring something of theirs to the work, in their own sentence;
4. keep a version deliberately;
5. leave, return, and recognize the relationship — not merely reopen a file.

Below that it is a surface, not a room. **The return test is the acceptance criterion:**
*when a creator returns, do they recognize their own relationship with the Work?*

## The two brand tests

**Same house?** Yes — token layer, gesture verbs, provenance voice, and the folded-MAIA
pattern are Journal's, unchanged.

**Distinct room?** Yes, and a member could tell it from Journal with the labels removed:
Journal opens on a prompt and an empty measure; the Canvas opens on the member's own unfinished
sentence with two folded edges. One asks you to begin. The other assumes you already have.
