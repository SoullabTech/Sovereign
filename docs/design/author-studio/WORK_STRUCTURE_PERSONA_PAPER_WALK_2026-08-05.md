# Structure — Five-Persona Paper Walk

> **Status**: The walk the Structure design gates on
> (`WORK_STRUCTURE_DESIGN_2026-08-05.md`), run on paper 2026-08-05 against
> the founder's stress tests — the exact places conventional tools fail.
> One question at every structural moment: **whose act made this shape
> visible?**
>
> **Verdict up front**: the design survives. Five findings (S1–S5). The
> most consequential: the deferral of "work shape" substrate is confirmed
> safe — threads (Materials grammar) and the expression list already carry
> every shape the walk surfaced, and the gesture that turns emergent shape
> into declared shape is simply **naming**. No new ontology required for
> any persona.

---

## Persona 1 — Novelist: "I have 300 pages."

- *Can I see the shape already present?* Yes — the carried cuts, by name,
  in the rail (A2). The creator wrote those cuts; the system only renders
  them. ✅
- *Can I move through sections naturally?* A part is a door; jumping lands
  the field. ✅
- *Can I distinguish the manuscript's structure from the Work's larger
  arc?* — the founder's named failure: *a chapter outline becomes the
  whole Work.*

> **S1 — The rail must say what it is a map OF.** The rail's header names
> the expression ("The Orchard Winter — manuscript"), never the Work. The
> book is one expression of the Work, not the Work's identity; a rail
> that presents chapter structure as *the* structure quietly collapses
> the two. One label prevents it.

## Persona 2 — Scholar: "I have research, arguments, citations, chapters."

- *Does Structure support inquiry without forcing a book model?* The
  unit-word principle holds naming (argument · evidence · questions ·
  findings — never imposed "chapters"). But the deeper strain:

> **S2 — A Work may hold more than one simultaneous shape.** The
> dissertation has a chapter sequence AND an argument arc AND a question
> map — conventional tools force one hierarchy and destroy the rest.
> Walked against the current design: the chapter sequence is expression
> structure (already real); the argument arc and question map are
> **threads of materials and fragments** — the Materials arranging
> grammar (M2/M3) already carries them. Deferral of a work-shape
> substrate is confirmed safe; multiplicity must be remembered as a
> requirement whenever work shape is ruled: ⛔ never a single mandatory
> hierarchy.

- A dissertation may become a book; the Work cannot assume it. Expression
  fluidity (A3) already holds this. ✅

## Persona 3 — Blogger: "I have ideas for a series."

The founder's best test against outline thinking: themes, fragments,
recurring questions, possible posts — structure may emerge only after
several expressions exist.

> **S3 — For a multi-expression work, the shape IS the expression list.**
> The series' structure is its posts, in order — which the Shape register
> already renders once expressions are declared. No internal outline is
> imposed on a work whose parts are expressions. Structure and Shape meet
> here without a new mechanism: the drawer for such a work shows its
> expressions as its parts.

Before any posts exist: fragments and recurring questions are just
materials and threads. The room shows no structure because none exists —
correct absence, again. ✅

## Persona 4 — Elder: "I have 40 years of teachings." (the hardest)

A traditional system says *"organize these into categories."* The Canvas
asks *"what shape is already appearing?"*

- The elder arranges materials into threads (their act). The system may
  state the fact: *"you have gathered these into three threads."* ✅
- Where does *appearing* shape become *declared* shape?

> **S4 — Naming is the gesture that converts emergent shape into declared
> shape.** The moment the elder names a thread — "the craft of
> attention" — that named thread is the first unit of the work's shape,
> authored in one word by the member. No categorization step, no
> reorganization ceremony, no system taxonomy: the name IS the
> declaration. This is the lightest possible answer to the founder's
> question, and it composes entirely from existing grammar (threads +
> the declaration gesture family).

Possible forms (teachings · stories · principles · practices · memories)
are unit-words the elder chooses — never offered as a picker, simply
whatever they name. ✅

## Persona 5 — Collaborator: "We are creating together." (held)

Not walked; recorded for the future lane:

> **S5 — "How did this shape emerge?" is answered by declarations being
> attributable acts.** Every shape change is a declaration (carry ·
> declare · name), and declarations land in History with a hand attached.
> When collaboration arrives, shape offerings ride the offered/adopted
> grammar like everything else — the outline is never silently co-edited.
