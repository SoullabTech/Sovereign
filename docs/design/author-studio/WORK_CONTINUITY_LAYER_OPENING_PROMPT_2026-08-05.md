## Where this lane sits: the room and its organs (founder identity statement)

What exists after v0.1 **is the integrated Writer Canvas architecture — but
not yet the full integrated canvas.** It is the first production slice: the
creative room. The center is correct — the Work is the organizing object,
the writing surface is an instrument, MAIA is beside the work rather than
the author, materials and history surround the creative process. The
achievement of v0.1 is that the center's question is no longer *"where do I
write?"* but *"how does my work come into being?"*

The full integrated canvas emerges by adding **the organs of this same
room** — Work Continuity · Materials (a full material ecology: journals,
notes, images, quotes, recordings, research, old manuscripts, references,
conversations, fragments — that *become materials feeding a Work*, not
uploads) · Structure · Expressions (moving between idea → essay → series →
chapter → course → book without losing the underlying Work) · Collaboration
(contribution, suggestion, adoption, attribution — the creator remains the
owner of meaning) · Reflection (MAIA understanding the Work's context
without becoming the author).

**The mistake to refuse: building those as separate tools.** They are
capabilities inside this one Canvas. The governing principle over all of
them: *the system can hold continuity without owning meaning.*

## The ruling: one lane, not four

The friction map's four convergences — the work↔manuscript seam, materials,
structure, collaboration — are **not four build lanes**. They are four faces
of one underlying question:

> **How does a Work gather, organize, and transform the things that belong
> to it?**

The lane is the **Work Continuity Layer**:

> How does a living Work hold its expressions, materials, structure, and
> relationships without collapsing into a document?

## The first design question

**"What does a Work need to know about itself?"** — experientially, not
technically.

A Work knows:

- **what it is** — its becoming, in the member's words;
- **what feeds it** — materials as *things that belong to the thinking*,
  never "uploads";
- **what forms it has taken** — expressions, and their evolution
  (idea → essay → book → course);
- **what shape it has** — internal structure, near the worktable, because
  it affects creation;
- **who has participated** — held for later, but the Work's self-knowledge
  reserves the place.

That is the next room.

### The four seams, each as an experiential question (founder, on closing v0.1)

The likely discoveries come from the seams — and each seam's question is
phrased from the creator's side, never the database's:

| Seam | The question is… | …never |
|---|---|---|
| Work ↔ Expression | *What forms has this Work taken?* (draft · article · chapter · dissertation · teaching · presentation · book) | "what rows reference it?" |
| Work ↔ Materials | *What does the creator say belongs here?* | "what files are uploaded?" |
| Work ↔ Structure | *What shape does this work have?* | "how many chapters?" |
| Work ↔ People | *Whose contribution becomes part of the work?* | "who has access?" |

### The guard on this lane (founder, verbatim intent)

**Do not let "Continuity Layer" become a metadata project.** The question is
not *"what attributes should a Work have?"* It is:

> **"What does a creator naturally expect the Work to remember?"**

That keeps the lane experiential. A schema answers the first question; only
a walked room answers the second — and the second governs.

### The authorship law this lane inherits

The same law is now appearing independently across import, collaboration,
and reflection, and the Continuity Layer must keep it whole:

- **the system offers** ("this may be a section boundary");
- **the system preserves** (nothing pasted, imported, or original is ever
  discarded or degraded);
- **the creator adopts** (only their gesture makes something part of the
  work).

Sibling rule from the v0.1 close, kept as a systems boundary: *a
representation used for publishing/rendering is not the representation used
for human creation — preserve the meaning of each layer over code reuse.*

## Boundaries the ruling sets

1. **Work ↔ manuscript seam is the highest priority.** The expression
   relationship (`living_works → living_work_expressions → manuscript`) is
   already the conceptual spine; the room exposed the gap between that spine
   and lived experience. The novelist's friction was never "a better editor"
   — it was *"this writing belongs to my Work, and the room cannot yet
   understand that relationship."*
2. **Materials: design the belonging, not the upload.** The question is
   *"what makes something belong to a Work?"* — not "how do we add more file
   types?" The scholar wants *these sources are part of the thinking behind
   this work*; the elder wants *my life's material can gather around what is
   emerging*.
3. **Structure is the first conditional instrument to have earned
   attention** — chapter names, jumping, orientation. Not an outline
   feature: the Work saying *"I have internal shape."* It belongs close to
   the Worktable.
4. **Collaboration is HELD.** The walk confirmed the restraint. What
   precedes co-writing is *"look at this"* — sharing, witnessing, offering,
   adoption. The offered/adopted grammar (persona walk A4) may eventually be
   the foundation, but the lane does not open yet.

## Standing guards carried forward

- Members' authored facts only; no inferred state anywhere in the layer.
- No silent promotion: belonging to a Work is a member declaration, never a
  system inference or a display implication (the v0.1-close head fix is the
  floor, not the ceiling, of this discipline).
- Reuse `living_works` / `living_work_expressions` as the spine; no new
  ontology until the exploration proves a gap.
- The temptation to refuse: *"great, now make the editor amazing."* Every
  reach in the five walks was environmental — how material enters, how a
  work evolves, how a person returns, how a body of work becomes visible
  over time.

## The five things a Work can answer (founder, opening the lane)

Start from the creator's experience — never from fields, tables, or
features. A Work should be able to answer:

1. **Identity** — *"What is this?"* — title · purpose · creator intention ·
   current expression(s).
2. **Origins** — *"Where did this come from?"* — ideas · journals · notes ·
   conversations · research · previous manuscripts.
3. **Shape** — *"What form is it taking?"* — essay · book · dissertation ·
   course · blog series · teaching · other expressions.
4. **History** — *"How has it evolved?"* — drafts · versions · renewals ·
   transformations.
5. **Relationships** — *"Who has participated?"* — creator · collaborator ·
   advisor · editor · witness.

## Design order within the lane (ruled)

1. **The Work drawer first.** It is the anchor of the Study Wall. A creator
   opens it and understands *"this is what I am bringing into form"* —
   never *"here are my database properties."*
2. **Then Materials.** The biggest opportunity. A material is not a file in
   a cabinet; it has a *relationship* to a Work — the journal entry that
   sparked it, the quote that shaped the argument, the recording of a
   teaching. The question: *what does it mean for something to feed a Work?*
3. **Then Structure.** Not chapter counts or outlines: *how does this Work
   organize itself?* — chapters, sections, themes, arguments, movements,
   collections.
4. **Then return to the integrated Canvas** — only after those
   relationships are clear.

## What is NOT next (ruled)

❌ collaboration · ❌ AI writing assistance · ❌ research tools ·
❌ publishing workflows · ❌ more editor features.

Those emerge naturally after the Work can hold itself.

> The Writer Canvas is the house. The Work Continuity Layer is the
> foundation that lets the house remember what happens inside it.

## The unifying test across every layer (founder, on accepting the Work drawer)

Every layer of this lane is forced to answer the same question:

> **Who makes the meaning?**

If the answer remains *"the creator"*, the architecture stays coherent.

And the question the Materials design must carry (the parallel of the
Work drawer's question): **"What does a creator need to do for something to
become part of a Work?"** A repository answers *"what files exist?"* — a
creative environment answers *"what belongs here?"*

Begin with the experiential question, not the schema.
