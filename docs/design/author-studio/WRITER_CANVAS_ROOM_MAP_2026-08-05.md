## The experiential question first

*What does it feel like to enter a professional creative studio?*

You do not enter a studio and face a form. You enter and your work is
**mid-motion on the table where you left it** — the canvas on the easel,
the manuscript open to the marked page, the notes pinned where you pinned
them. The room says one thing on arrival:

> **"Your work is here. Continue."**

Everything else — materials, structure, history, another mind to think
with — is *within reach but not in your face*.

That is the room this map draws.

## Arrival state (what the member encounters first)

On entering the Canvas the member sees exactly three things:

1. **The work, named by its becoming** — "*Field Notes* — becoming a book
   of essays" (the member's own title, the member's declared expression).
2. **The creative field, open at the last gesture** — the paragraph they
   left, the fragment they captured, the chapter they were shaping. Never
   a blank pane unless the work is genuinely new — and even then, the
   thing they brought through the doorway (the idea they typed, the file
   they imported) is already on the table.
3. **Two quiet edges** — a folded context spine on the left, a folded
   presence on the right. Closed. Openable. Silent.

Nothing else. No tab row, no card grid, no capability inventory.

## The room

```
 ┌────────────┬──────────────────────────────┬───────────┐
 │  STUDY     │         WORKTABLE            │  WINDOW   │
 │  WALL      │      (creative field)        │  (MAIA)   │
 │  (folded   │                              │  (folded  │
 │   spine)   │   the work, mid-motion,      │  presence)│
 │            │   under one instrument       │           │
 │  ▸ Work    │   at a time:                 │  invited, │
 │  ▸ Materials   · writing surface          │  never    │
 │  ▸ Structure   · development surface      │  ambient  │
 │  ▸ History     · renewal surface          │           │
 └────────────┴──────────────────────────────┴───────────┘
```

Three zones. No fourth. (The brief's bottom strip is amended below.)

### Zone 1 — The Worktable (center: the creative field)

- **Purpose**: where the work develops. The hearth of the room.
- **Emotional arrival state**: *"my work is here, mid-motion."* Continuity,
  not initiation.
- **Primary gesture**: **continue** — write, arrange, connect, revise.
- **The instrument principle**: the worktable holds the work under **one
  instrument at a time**. The instruments are not modes of the room; they
  are tools picked up and put down:
  - **Writing surface** — the Working Draft engine. Prose develops here.
  - **Development surface** — fragments, ideas, questions, connections.
    Pre-prose work. Arranging, not yet writing.
  - **Renewal surface** — an original work held beside its new expression.
    The original is preserved and read-only; the new expression develops.
    (Renewal is encounter → preserve → continue, never open → edit → save.)
- **Allowed objects**: the work's current expression; the instrument in
  hand; the member's own words.
- **Forbidden objects**: metrics of any kind; other works; system
  taxonomy; MAIA-generated prose; anything that scores, counts, or
  congratulates.

### Zone 2 — The Study Wall (left: context)

- **Purpose**: the member's relationship to *this* work — what it is, what
  feeds it, how it is shaped, where it has been.
- **Emotional arrival state**: *"everything that feeds this work is within
  reach, not in my face."*
- **Primary gesture**: **open a drawer; bring a material to the table.**
- **Four drawers** (folded to a thin spine by default; one open at a time):
  - **Work** — identity and becoming: the member's title, what they
    declared it is becoming, their own description. Editable only by them.
  - **Materials** — sources, imports, captures, references. What feeds the
    work. A material can be *brought to the table* (placed beside the
    field while working).
  - **Structure** — chapters, sections, series, movements. **This drawer
    exists only after the work has structure** — declared by the member or
    carried in by an imported manuscript. A new idea has no Structure
    drawer, and its absence is correct, not a gap.
  - **History** — versions, preserved originals, past working sessions.
    Where the work has been. Nothing here is ever silently overwritten.
- **Allowed objects**: objects of this work only, member-authored or
  member-imported.
- **Forbidden objects**: global navigation; other works; counts styled as
  progress; anything MAIA inferred rather than the member authored.

### Zone 3 — The Window (right: MAIA / reflection)

- **Purpose**: another mind to think with — reflection **without
  authorship**.
- **Emotional arrival state**: *"I am not alone with this, and I am not
  being watched."*
- **Primary gesture**: **invite.** The Window is closed by default and
  opens only on the member's gesture. It never opens itself.
- **What MAIA may do here**: reflect on material the member shows it;
  surface connections **between the member's own materials**; hold
  questions; witness a renewal encounter.
- **Forbidden objects**: unsolicited suggestions; generated draft text
  presented as the work; praise, scoring, or encouragement mechanics;
  ambient commentary on the member's process; any inference about the
  member's psychological state. (Sovereignty rider: anything adaptive here
  keys on authored facts only.)

### Amendment to the brief's hypothesis: drawers, not a shelf

The starting hypothesis included a persistent bottom strip —
`| Sources | Drafts | Versions | Collaborators |`. **The strip is deleted
from the room.** A permanently visible band of object categories is
inventory grammar — the warehouse failure mode in a thin horizontal form.
Its contents are rehoused:

- **Sources** → Materials drawer.
- **Drafts / Versions** → History drawer (and the worktable itself, which
  always holds the living draft).
- **Collaborators** → not a drawer at all: a **presence**. When the work is
  shared, the people in it appear as quiet presence at the room's edge —
  attributed contributions, not a management panel. When the work is
  solitary, no trace of collaboration UI exists. *(To be tested hard by
  Persona 4 in the walk.)*

## Orientation, not measurement

The room offers **one quiet phrase of place** — "gathering," "shaping,"
"drafting," "renewing," "sharing" — shown with the work's name, nowhere
else. Its source is strictly **member-authored acts**: they declared an
expression, they created a renewal, they invited a collaborator, they
marked a draft ready. The member may rename their stage in their own
words at any time, and their word wins. No bars, no percentages, no
streaks, no "you're 80% done." The phrase orients like a studio assistant
saying "you were shaping chapter three" — it never grades.

## Mode is posture, not identity

The doorway intentions (Write · Book · Idea · Journal · Research · Share)
and starting conditions (new · existing manuscript · collaboration ·
research · in progress) configure only two things:

1. **which instrument the worktable opens under**, and
2. **which drawers arrive seeded** (e.g. Research seeds Materials;
   an imported manuscript seeds Structure and History).

They never brand the room, never fork the product, and never fix the
work's identity. An idea may become a book may become a course — the room
does not have to be re-entered through a different door for that to
happen; the member declares a new becoming and the same room re-forms
around it.

## Arrival by entry condition

| Entry condition | The worktable opens under… | Seeded drawers |
|---|---|---|
| A new idea | Development surface, the captured idea already on the table | Work |
| An existing manuscript | Renewal surface: original preserved in History, working expression beside it | Work · Structure · History |
| A collaboration | Writing/development surface + presence of others, attributed | Work · (presence at edge) |
| A research project | Development surface | Work · Materials |
| Something in progress | Exactly where they left it | Whatever they had open |

## The five questions the room must answer

1. **What am I working on?** — The work named by its becoming, always at
   the head of the room, in the member's words.
2. **What materials feed this work?** — The Materials drawer; a material
   can be brought to the table.
3. **Where does the work develop?** — The worktable. Always the center,
   always the largest thing, always holding the work mid-motion.
4. **How do I continue?** — Arrival *is* continuation: the room opens at
   the last gesture. "Continue" is the default state, not a button.
5. **How does intelligence support without replacing authorship?** — The
   Window: invited, reflective, connection-surfacing, forbidden from
   generating the work. The member writes; MAIA thinks alongside.

## The calm-computing check (design-law five questions, answered for this surface)

1. *Current state?* — authored facts only: the work, its declared
   becoming, the last gesture, the open instrument.
2. *Next meaningful gesture?* — continue the work (write/arrange/connect).
3. *What can disappear?* — everything except the work: both wings fold;
   structure absent until it exists; collaboration invisible until real.
4. *What emerges only when invited?* — MAIA; drawers; instruments not in
   hand.
5. *What persists because it is relationship?* — the work's name and
   becoming (the member's relationship to the work) and the folded Window
   (the standing offer of reflection, never the imposition of it).
