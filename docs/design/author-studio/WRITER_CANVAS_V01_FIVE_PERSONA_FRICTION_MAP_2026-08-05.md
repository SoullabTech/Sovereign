> **Status**: The walk the founder ordered after accepting v0.1 ("do the five
> persona walk before opening another build lane… create a friction map, not
> a feature backlog"). Captures only: natural gestures · confusions · missing
> capabilities · moments the environment felt alive. **This document
> authorizes nothing.** The next architecture comes from what creators
> naturally attempted here, ruled by the founder — not from this list
> becoming tickets.
>
> **Provenance (instrument ↔ referent)**: walked 2026-08-05 in the rendered
> product at branch `feature/writer-canvas-v01`, commit `ab8e326d8`, dev
> server (worktree), authenticated dev member `687d2ed5…` ("Letters from the
> Field"), shared dev database. Novelist/scholar conditions were created

## Persona 1 — Novelist ("I have a manuscript")

**Walk**: pasted a four-chapter manuscript through Import; confirmed cuts;
entered the Canvas.

**Natural gestures that landed**
- Paste the whole book and have the room take it. The confirm-the-cuts stage
  (edit headings, merge) read as respect: *my* chapter names, checked with me.
- Arriving in the Canvas with the entire draft already on the table,
  verbatim, "drafting" in the head — continuation, not setup.
- Structure appearing in the spine only after the import carried sections —
  the conditional worked exactly as designed and felt like the room noticing.

**Confusions**
1. **Chapters died silently at the threshold on the first try.** Prose-style
   headings ("Chapter One — The Late Frost") were not detected: one
   untitled section. The confirm stage can edit or merge cuts but cannot
   **add** a cut the detector missed — the member's only recovery is going
   back and reformatting to `#` headings, which no novelist knows to do.
2. **The `#` marks leak into the writing surface.** The markdown scaffolding
   used to carry structure in is sitting inside the novelist's prose on the
   worktable.
3. **The head of the room names the wrong thing.** The book sits under
   "Letters from the Field" — a work declared months apart from this
   manuscript. One-work-per-member assumption (arrivalWork) + no
   work↔manuscript link = a novel headlined by an unrelated work.

**Missing capability actually reached for**
- Chapter names and chapter jumping. The Structure drawer says "4 sections,
  carried in with your import" and points at the Source — the novelist
  wanted the *names*, *here*, and to land the worktable at chapter three.
  This is the walk's A2 (pinnable structure rail) arriving on schedule, and
  it belongs at the table, not in a sentence.
- **Import should end in the room.** Saving the manuscript lands in the
  old seven-tab workbench (Layer 3), not the Canvas. The room exists; the
  main entry path predates it.

**Alive**: the draft founding itself from the Source on first arrival — no
"begin" ceremony inside the room; the book was simply there, mid-motion.

## Persona 2 — Scholar ("I have a dissertation")

**Walk**: same import mechanics (proven above); the scholar-specific walk is
about where research lives.

**Natural gestures with no target**
- "Bring a source in." Materials holds exactly one object: **Your Source —
  what you brought in, unchanged** — which is the *origin of the draft*,
  not the *materials that feed the work*. The scholar's 120 references have
  no drawer to land in. The room currently uses "Materials" for provenance,
  not for feeding — two meanings, one drawer, and the scholar needs the
  second one.
- "Cite this." No gesture at the writing surface reaches toward any
  material. (Not built, correctly — but the reach happens within the first
  paragraph of academic prose.)

**Confusion**: an advisor cannot be shown the work at all — nothing in the
room acknowledges another human exists. For the scholar this surfaces
earlier than co-authorship: *feedback* precedes *collaboration*.

**Alive**: revision cycles as ordinary History — "keep a version" before
sending a chapter out is exactly the scholar's rhythm, and it already works.

## Persona 3 — Blogger / Creator ("I have an idea for a series")

**Walk**: the blank-page room (walked as "Your writing" before the imports).

**What held**: the room at its smallest is nearly bare — spine of three,
empty drawers that say true things, the whole surface given to a few
sentences. Small creative acts are not dwarfed; the blogger is not made to
feel they are using a book tool badly. **This persona passes v0.1.**

**Confusions**
1. **The table cannot change its manuscript from inside the room.** The
   Canvas shows the most recent manuscript; the blogger's earlier post is
   unreachable — Studio Home's Current Book card also shows only the most
   recent ("2 manuscripts imported. The most recent is open."). A second
   piece strands the first. This is A3 (expression switching) arriving as
   lived friction, at the *manuscript* level before the *expression* level.
2. A series has no way to *be* a series: two posts are two unrelated
   manuscripts under one unrelated work headline.

**Alive**: page-estimate and saved-time as the only numbers in the room. The
blogger is not scored, streaked, or measured.

## Persona 4 — Collaborator ("We are creating this together")

**Boundary observations (not faked)**
- The room contains no second human anywhere: no invite, no presence, no
  way to show the work. **Absence is currently honest** — nothing pretends.
- The real boundary the walk exposes: v0.1's one-member room means
  *showing* work to anyone (advisor, co-author, editor, friend) requires
  leaving the platform entirely (export, copy-paste). The first collaboration
  gesture creators will attempt is not co-editing — it is **"look at this."**
- When collaboration is built, the walk's A4 grammar (offered/adopted; only
  the author's hand makes something part of the work) is already ruled
  design; History already records member-attributed versions, which is the
  substrate attribution will stand on.

## Persona 5 — Elder / Teacher ("I have decades of notes and teachings")

**Walk**: attempted as gathering; the doors themselves shape what happens.

**Missing capability, structurally**
- **Gathering has no mouth.** The only way material enters is Import
  Manuscript — one document, one "book," one confirm ceremony. Forty years
  of journals, talks, and handouts would become dozens of sibling
  *manuscripts*, each posing as a book the elder must pretend they are
  writing. The elder's actual first act — *pour it all in somewhere safe,
  look at it later* — has no door. This is A6 arriving exactly as the
  design walk predicted, and it is the deepest gap v0.1 has.
- No threads: nothing lets the elder group or relate what they brought.
  Materials (as feeding-layer) and gathering are the same missing organ
  seen from two personas.

**Alive**: "What you bring in is kept as your Source and is never altered"
— the preservation vow, already true in the running system, is *the*
sentence for this persona. The trust precondition for handing over a life's
work exists before the room that would receive it.

## Cross-walk reading (what the friction agrees on)

Read together, the five walks converge on four seams — stated as what
creators reached for, not as a backlog:

1. **The work↔manuscript seam** (novelist confusion 3, blogger confusion 2,
   elder's sibling-books): almost every friction touches the unwired
   `living_work_expressions` link. The head of the room cannot be honest
   about *whose work is on the table* until belonging is declarable.
2. **Materials as feeding, not provenance** (scholar, elder): one drawer
   currently carries two meanings; the second meaning has no substrate.
3. **Structure at the table** (novelist): names and jumping, where the work
   is — A2 as built need, not comfort.
4. **"Look at this"** precedes collaboration (scholar's advisor,
   collaborator walk): the first social gesture is showing, not co-editing.

**Editor-pull check (founder's standing caution)**: nothing in the five
walks asked for a better editor. Every reach was environmental — how
material enters, how the work is named, how structure stays near at hand, how
another person sees it. The Worktable itself generated zero friction. The
room's center holds; the walls are where the product wants to grow.
