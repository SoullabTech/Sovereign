## What the drawer is

The anchor of the Study Wall. A creator opens it and understands:

> **"This is what I am bringing into form."**

Not: "here are my database properties."

The drawer is the creator's **relationship to the work made visible** — the
work answering, in the creator's own words, the five things a Work can
answer. It reads like the flyleaf of a working notebook, not a record card.

- **Emotional arrival state**: recognition. *"Yes — that's what this is."*
- **Primary gesture**: **tend the identity** — name it, say what it is
  becoming, correct it when the work has changed underneath the words.
- **Forbidden**: counts styled as progress, computed properties, inferred
  themes, timestamps doing the talking, any field the member didn't author.

## The five registers

The drawer renders the Work's self-knowledge as five quiet registers. Each
is prose-shaped, member-authored, and **honestly absent when empty** — an
empty register states its emptiness as a true fact about a young work,
never as a nagging blank to fill.

### 1. Identity — "What is this?"

- The member's title (or its honest absence: an unnamed work stays
  visibly unnamed — nothing is invented).
- The becoming, in their words: *"an idea, becoming an essay"* · *"a body
  of teaching, gathering."*
- The intention, if they have said one — a sentence they wrote, shown as
  they wrote it.
- **Gestures here**: name / rename / restate the becoming. These move INTO
  the drawer from Studio Home over time — identity is tended where the
  work lives. (v0.1 points home; the drawer becomes the home of identity.)

### 2. Origins — "Where did this come from?"

- What the member has *declared* fed this work into being: the journal
  entry that sparked it, the conversation, the old manuscript it renewed.
- **Substrate honesty**: nothing records this yet. The register's design
  slot exists now; its contents arrive with the Materials design (Step 3),
  because an origin is a material with a particular relationship
  ("sparked", "renewed from") — one grammar, not two systems.
- **Empty state**: *"Where this came from hasn't been written down."* An
  invitation of fact, not a task.

### 3. Shape — "What form is it taking?"

- The expressions the member has declared: essay, book, series, course —
  and which one is on the table now.
- This register is where the **work ↔ manuscript seam** becomes lived: a
  declared expression is the member saying *"this manuscript is a form of
  this work."* The declaration is a member gesture in this drawer — never
  an import side-effect, never a display implication (no silent promotion).
- **Empty state**: the work has taken no declared form yet — and the room
  keeps working exactly as v0.1 does today (near, not claimed).

### 4. History — "How has it evolved?"

- The Work drawer does **not** duplicate the History drawer. It carries one
  line of self-knowledge — *"began as an idea in March; renewed from a 2014
  manuscript; became a series in June"* — composed ONLY of member acts
  (declarations, renewals, kept versions), each of which already lives in
  full in History. The drawer answers "how has it evolved"; History holds
  the evidence.

### 5. Relationships — "Who has participated?"

- Held. Collaboration is explicitly not next. The register reserves the
  place in the design so the Work's self-knowledge has somewhere for
  people when the offered/adopted grammar arrives — and reserves it as
  **participation** ("whose contribution became part of the work"), never
  access control.
- Until then the register does not render at all. A solitary work carries
  zero trace of collaboration UI — the v0.1 rule, kept.

## The governing test

Open the drawer as each persona and ask one question: **does the drawer say
what the creator would say about their own work?** If any line could only
have been written by the system — a count, an inference, a property — it
fails. The drawer may only ever say the work back to its creator in the
creator's own words, arranged.

This is the anti-metadata guard in operable form: the design question is
never "what attributes should a Work have?" but **"what does a creator
naturally expect the Work to remember?"** — and the drawer shows exactly
that remembering, nothing else.

## What this design waits for

- **Materials design (Step 3)** before the Origins register has substrate.
- **A ruled implementation slice** before anything here is built. The
  likely first slice, for the ruling to consider when the time comes:
  Identity gestures in the drawer + declared expression (Shape) as the
  first member-authored write to `living_work_expressions` — the seam the
  whole friction map converged on, entered through the member's own
  declaration gesture, exactly as the ontology intended.
