# Writer Canvas ⊥ Press Editor — the division of fields

> **Status**: Founder ruling, 2026-08-05 (late). It answers the open
> question the capability record left standing — *what is a manuscript made
> of?* — by dividing the question between two fields instead of forcing one
> canvas to be both sanctuary and typesetting machine.
>
> ⛔ Design record. No build authorized; the Creator Walk still comes first.

---

## The two fields

**Writer Canvas — the living manuscript.** Its job: capture and continue
the work · hold the writer's voice · organize chapters and materials ·
support reflection and discovery · preserve Source and Working Draft ·
make writing feel immediate and alive. **It does not need to become a
publishing application.** The manuscript stays primarily text-centered
here, because the human task is *write, shape, and deepen the work.*

**Press Editor — the edition being made from it.** Its job: typography and
page design · images and captions · footnotes and citations · tables and
figures · headers, folios, front matter, appendices · trim size and print
layout · ebook formatting · production checks and export. It answers a
different question: *how does this work become a publishable edition?*

> **Writer Canvas helps the work become itself.
> Press Editor helps the work become an edition.**

This mirrors the existing ontology exactly: the **living manuscript** and
the **edition derived from it**.

## What Writer Canvas therefore needs (and no more)

Natural writing conventions only: paragraphs · headings · emphasis · block
quotations · lists · scene or section breaks · perhaps basic images and
notes where they are genuinely part of composition. **Advanced production
structure belongs to Press Editor.**

This is the answer to *what is a manuscript made of*: **text with light
compositional structure** — not a production document model. The heavy
question moves to the field that actually needs it.

## Not a transfer — the Work stays the center (founder refinement)

⛔ **Do not call it a transfer.** That implies leaving one place for
another. The writer never leaves the manuscript; they open one expression
of it for publication:

```text
Work
    │
    ├── Working Draft  ← Writer Canvas
    │
    ├── Edition 1  →  Press Editor
    │
    └── Edition 2  →  Press Editor
```

**The Work is the enduring reality; editions are forms the Work takes.**
The Canvas stays attached to the Work and tends the living manuscript;
Press Editor opens a *particular form of its expression*. Multiple
editions coexist without competing, exactly as multiple expressions
already do.

## Why the separation protects the writer

Most writing tools introduce production concerns too early: you are
writing a sentence and suddenly you are managing font sizes, image
alignment, citation formats, page numbers, export settings — and attention
moves from **meaning to mechanics**. Two rooms keep the two conversations
apart:

> The Canvas asks: **"What is this work becoming?"**
> Press Editor asks: **"How should this edition be made?"**

### The desks differ because the objects differ

**Writer Canvas — creative objects.** *Write*: paragraph · heading · scene
break · dialogue · block quotation. *Bring Something In*: photograph ·
interview · voice memo · document · letter · PDF · website. *Reference*:
book · article · research note · citation · timeline · character · place.
*Organize*: chapter · part · card · collection · gathering. *Think*: ask
MAIA · reflect · compare passages · find related material.

**Press Editor — production objects.** Typography · layout · margins ·
running heads · footnotes · cross-references · figure numbering · index ·
front matter · back matter · print preview · EPUB preview.

Almost nothing on the first list is a publishing object; almost nothing on
the second belongs in a sanctuary for composition.

## THE KEY RULE — transfer must not mean replacement

The danger is two unrelated copies. The transition is explicit and
traceable:

```text
Working Draft
     ↓
Create Edition
     ↓
Press Editor
```

- **The Working Draft remains intact.** Press Editor creates an **Edition
  derived from a declared revision** — the writer names which revision the
  edition is made from.
- **Later writing changes never silently overwrite the edition.** The
  system may offer: *"A newer draft exists." · "Review changes." · "Bring
  selected revisions into this edition."*
- ⛔ **No automatic synchronization** that breaks layout or changes
  published text without consent.

## Why this is the same law three times over

The platform keeps arriving at one grammar from independent directions,
which is the strongest evidence it is real:

| Where | The form it took |
|---|---|
| Renewal (persona walk A1) | the original is preserved; renewal creates a new expression beside it |
| Materials (walk M1–M7) | a belonging is a relationship, never a copy; the thing keeps its home |
| Collaboration (A4) | contributions are *offered*; only the author *adopts* |
| **Edition (this ruling)** | the draft is preserved; the edition is derived from a declared revision; newer drafts are *offered*, never synced |

The edition case adds one turn of the screw: the offer/adopt grammar now
governs the writer's relationship **with their own past self** — a later
draft has no more right to overwrite an edition than a collaborator has to
overwrite a paragraph.

## Substrate note (observation, not design)

An **Edition** is, in the platform's own vocabulary, *a form the Work has
taken* — which is what `living_work_expressions` already records. So the
Create Edition gesture is likely the same declaration act the Canvas
already performs, with a revision id attached, rather than a new ontology.
⛔ Not designed here; recorded so the future lane starts from the existing
spine instead of inventing one.

## What this resolves for the capability record

`WRITING_CRAFT_CAPABILITY_RECORD_2026-08-05.md` lists the grammar of books
and the Writer's Desk. This ruling divides that list:

- **Writer Canvas drawers**: the compositional acts — chapter, scene
  break, quote, poem, letter, dialogue, journal entry, reflection, basic
  image, note.
- **Press Editor**: footnotes, endnotes, citations, bibliography, tables,
  figures, plates, folios, front matter, appendices, index markers, trim,
  export.

Neither field carries the other's burden, and each stays coherent.
