## The identity ruling

**One room. One purpose.** The Canvas commits to being a writing room the
way the five-room ontology commits everywhere else. When the creator is in
Work, everything disappears except what helps them write.

## The four moves (founder direction, verbatim in substance)

1. **Work becomes radically simple.** The manuscript is the center of
   gravity; title and status become quieter; everything else recedes. The
   eye lands on "I'm working on Elemental Alchemy," never on the imported
   document dominating the environment.
2. **Rooms, not panels.** Clicking Structure / Materials / History changes
   the room completely — moving through a house, not operating an
   application. The drawers were the right adjacencies with the wrong
   grammar: they are adjacent *rooms* the creator intentionally visits,
   not persistent reminders of software.
3. **Writing view ≠ proofing view.** The default surface is a continuous
   working draft. Print pagination (page markers, print layout artifacts)
   belongs to an intentional print/proofing view only. Substrate half of
   this finding: the `-- N of 216 --` markers are IN the imported text
   (PDF ingest carried print-page separators into section bodies) — so the
   full answer is (a) strip/segment print artifacts at the
   confirm-the-cuts threshold under the member's eye, and (b) the view
   split for already-imported text.
4. **Versioning is a consequence of writing.** "Keep a version" stops
   floating as a detached utility and becomes part of the writing gesture's
   own neighborhood.

## Writing Surface (founder direction, second message — part of this same conversation)

**Principle: the environment serves the writer, not the brand.** Identity
and working environment separate: *the house doesn't repaint itself, but
the writer chooses their paper.*

- **Room theme stays Soullab** — navigation, title, surrounding
  environment keep the warm charcoal identity of the platform.
- **The manuscript surface changes independently** — the desk adapts:

  ```
  Writing Surface
  ○ Warm Studio    (default — charcoal, bronze, cream; matches the house)
  ○ Ivory Paper    (soft ivory, black text, minimal accents)
  ○ White Paper    (white, black text, high contrast — long editing)
  ○ Midnight       (near black, soft gray text)
  ○ Focus          (very low chrome, almost no UI, maximum manuscript)
  ```

- **Member-facing name: "Writing Surface"** — never "Theme." A writer
  choosing the conditions under which they work, not changing an
  application's appearance.
- **Remembered per member (global default) with a per-manuscript
  override** — a poetry collection may live on a dark canvas while a
  technical book always opens on white paper. Both are member-authored
  facts; adaptation keys on them and nothing else (sovereignty rider
  satisfied by construction).
- Note: Focus is a *chrome* choice as much as a color choice — it may
  compose with any paper rather than being a peer color (slice design
  decides; flagged, not assumed).
