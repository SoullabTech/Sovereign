## Governing question

> **Can future generations extend the system without accidentally changing what it is?**

⭐ Tracks 1–6 create and operate a truthful system. **Track 7 asks whether the system can outlive its founders without losing its integrity.** Every institution reaches the point where the founders stop being present for every decision, and the architecture must begin carrying the judgments that once lived in people.

---

## 7.1 Constitutional stewardship

The constitution becomes an **active instrument**. Every proposed change asks:

- Which principle governs this?
- Is this a clarification or a revision?
- Does this introduce a new authority?
- Does this weaken an existing invariant?
- Does this require a founder ruling?

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Most changes should never reach founder level. The constitution should absorb ordinary evolution.**

## 7.2 Canon formation

Track 6 *matures* observations. Track 7 **ratifies** them.

```
Observation → Repeated evidence → Cross-domain convergence
            → Candidate → Founder review → Canon → Institutional invariant
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Canon is ⛔ not accumulated. It is earned.**

## 7.3 Constitutional memory

Every ruling needs durable provenance — ⛔ not merely *"this is the rule"*, but: Why? · What problem produced it? · What alternatives were refused? · What evidence supported it? · What changed afterward?

> ⭐ **Future stewards inherit reasoning, ⛔ not merely conclusions.**

## 7.4 Steward qualification

> **Who may change the architecture?**

⛔ Not everyone who can write code. ⛔ Not everyone who understands the domain. ⭐ Stewardship itself must be **earned**: authority comes from **demonstrated fidelity to the system's principles**, ⛔ not from organizational position or technical ability.

## 7.5 Institutional self-review

Which principles have never been challenged? · Which repeatedly save us? · Which assumptions no longer fit reality? · Where are we accumulating exceptions? · What has become **folklore instead of explicit knowledge**?

⭐ This keeps the constitution **alive rather than frozen**.

## 7.6 Successor safety

Test: a founder leaves for six months. Would new engineers understand the principles · know where authority comes from · distinguish policy from implementation · know when to stop modeling and **ask for a ruling**?

⛔ If not, the institution still depends on personalities. ⭐ Track 7 reduces that dependency.

## 7.7 Constitutional audit

Every few years: **would we build this the same way today?** ⛔ Not to chase novelty — to discover whether the principles still explain reality, the architecture still expresses the principles, and the implementation still reflects the architecture. ⭐ If all three remain aligned, stewardship has succeeded.

## 7.8 Legacy

> **What has this institution learned that should survive the institution itself?**

Some discoveries belong only to this platform. Others become **generally applicable methods**. The publishing work has already produced examples of the latter:

- **Measure before model.**
- **Refuse on the true ground.**
- **Evidence scope before absence claims.**
- **Acts create facts.**
- **Authority is authored or held.**

⭐ These cease to be *"publishing principles"* and become **ways of reasoning about trustworthy systems**.

## Success criterion

⛔ Not: *"the platform still exists."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **People who never met the founders can extend the system, resolve new problems, and preserve its integrity — because the institution carries its own reasoning.**

### Founder observation (2026-08-06)

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The tracks do not end with software; they end with an institution.** The progression moves from **discovering reality (1)** → **making judgments about it (2)** → **expressing those judgments (3–4)** → **living inside them (5–6)** → **handing that capacity to others (7)**. At that point the ultimate product is ⛔ no longer the platform alone, but **a disciplined way of thinking that can continue generating truthful decisions after the original designers are no longer making them.**
