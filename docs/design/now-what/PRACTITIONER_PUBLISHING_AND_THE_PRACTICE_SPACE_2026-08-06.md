## 0. The governing principle

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The system does not organize information. It preserves intentional human acts.**

Every ruling below is a consequence of that sentence. When a future decision is unclear, ask which intentional act is being preserved — not where the artifact should be stored.

## 1. The reframe

> Larry does not need a **Documents feature**. He needs a **Publishing system**.

The model is not *coach stores files*. It is *coach cultivates a living body of work that can be intentionally placed into a client's journey.*

This is the correct reading of the four-circle rule. A manual, a program, a worksheet, a video is a **Practitioner Field** object by default — it lives in Larry's practice and belongs to him. It enters the **Relationship Workspace** only by a deliberate practitioner act. It never becomes a **Client Field** object: the client's own reflections on it remain theirs.

### ⛔⛔⛔ Folders are forbidden as an organizing principle

**This is a standing invariant, not a style preference.** It is the single sentence that prevents almost every future UI regression in this area.

Any reach for `Resources / Documents / Manuals / Week 1 / Week 2` is an attempt to rebuild Dropbox inside a coaching relationship — the warehouse failure mode named in Inhabitable Architecture. The organizing principle is **the practitioner's act**, never storage location. The client never sees a directory tree; they see *Larry shared something with you.*

⚠️ A hierarchy that is merely *renamed* (Programs → Modules → Weeks, rendered as nested lists) is the same violation wearing better vocabulary. The test is not what the levels are called; it is whether the client is asked to **navigate a structure** or to **receive an act**.

## 2. Three gestures — the whole crossing

The practitioner has exactly three ways to place something. They differ by **what claim they make**, not by what they attach.

| Gesture | Claim | Client experience | Creates obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Share** | *"I want you to have this."* | It is simply yours now, held in the relationship | No |
| **Recommend** | *"This might help you right now."* | Offered, not owned — the client may take it or not | No |
| **Assign** | *"Complete this before we next meet."* | Active practice, not a resource | Yes — **from Larry, never from the system** |

⭐ **The three gestures are distinguished by the claim they make, not by mechanics.** That is the correct abstraction, and it is why the set is complete at this level: any new capability must reduce to one of these three, or it is a new *claim* and must be earned as such (§10).

### The Assign constraint (load-bearing)

Assignment is legitimate: it is a human making a request of another human inside a commitment they both entered. What is **not** legitimate is the system acquiring an interest in compliance.

⭐⭐ The distinction to hold, because these are **different authorities**:

| ✅ Permitted | ⛔ Refused |
|---|---|
| *Larry assigned this.* | *The system is tracking whether Kelly complied.* |

- ✅ The system may **remember that something was assigned** — that is provenance.
- ✅ Whether the work was done is a subject **Larry may raise**, in conversation, if he chooses.
- ⛔ No completion scoring, streaks, adherence rates, or ranking.
- ⛔ No nagging, no escalation, no "you are behind."
- ⛔ Assignment state may not feed pattern detection or any developmental inference.
- ✅ The client may decline, defer, or ignore an assignment with no system consequence.

If the platform ever cares whether the work was done, the coach has been replaced by a compliance engine.

## 3. Objects exist. Gestures move them.

⭐⭐⭐ **This separation is architectural, and it simplifies the entire domain model:**

```
Objects exist.        ← authored in the Practitioner Field, independently of any client
Gestures move them.   ← Share / Recommend / Assign change an object's relationship
                        to one Relationship Workspace
```

⛔ **A gesture never creates an object.** Share, Recommend, and Assign do not author, copy, duplicate, or own anything. They establish a relation between an object that already exists and a commitment that already exists. One object may be placed into many relationships; each placement is its own act with its own provenance.

Consequences that fall out for free: Larry can revise a manual once, not per-client · a placement can be described without duplicating content · authorship never fragments across clients · "who authored it" and "who placed it" can never be confused.

⭐ **Lifecycle state is the fourth element of the model and is kept separate from all three — see §13.** Objects have states, placements have states, and neither set may be expressed as a gesture.

### The objects

Program · Course · Module · Practice · Exercise · Reading · Video · Audio · Reflection · Assessment · Checklist · Worksheet · Template · Collection · Announcement · Message · Session · Commitment.

Each carries: title · description · **who authored it** · when · structure appropriate to its kind. ⛔ Placement facts (who placed it, into which relationship, when, under which gesture) belong to the **placement**, not to the object.

A **Program** is a first-class object, not a folder. The client's experience is *"I am in Executive Leadership,"* not `Programs/Leadership/Week1/`.

A **Manual** is a living reference with sections, concepts, practices, and models — so a citation can be *"Larry develops this in Chapter 4"* rather than a download link.

## 4. Collection — an authored composition

⭐ **Promoted to first-class on founder review.** A Collection is neither a Program, nor a Manual, nor a Resource, nor — emphatically — a folder.

> *Difficult Conversations Starter Kit* — a chapter · a worksheet · two videos · one practice · one assessment
>
> *Before Our Next Session* — a journal prompt · a breathing exercise · a reading · reflection questions

**The distinguishing test, and it is precise:**

| | Folder | Collection |
|---|---|---|
| Removing an item | changes what is inside | **changes what it means** |
| Order | incidental | authored |
| Authorship | none — a container | **Larry's, as a composition** |

A folder holds things. A Collection **says something** by holding exactly these things in this arrangement. That makes it a publishable work in its own right — Larry can place *Difficult Conversations Starter Kit* as one act, repeatedly, across many relationships, without inventing a new Program each time.

⚠️ Design consequence: a Collection may not be silently partially delivered. If an item within it is withdrawn or unavailable, the composition has changed and that change is visible — it is not a missing file, it is an altered statement.

## 5. Three note types — the consent boundary

| Type | Owner | Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Larry's private notes** | Practitioner Field | Larry only. Never shared, never surfaced, never MAIA-readable |
| **Shared coaching notes** | Relationship Workspace | Deliberately shared; client can revisit, discuss with MAIA, reference later |
| **Client reflections** | Client Field | **Private by default**; the client may share selected reflections back |

This is the consent-carried model already established: the crossing is always an act by the owner of the material, in the direction they choose. ⛔ Neither party may pull from the other's field.

## 6. Session follow-up is a relational event

Larry closes a session and sends one thing — summary, insights, practices, files, videos, assignments, reflection questions — that **arrives together**. Not five notifications. Not a folder that gained items.

⭐⭐ **What is being defined here is a relational event, not a batch operation.** The unit is not *upload file · send worksheet · assign practice*. The unit is:

> **"This was today's session."**

Everything associated with it arrives as one moment, because that is how the client actually experienced it. A session is a thing that happened between two people; its follow-up should have the same shape in the system that it has in memory.

⛔ Implementation may not decompose a follow-up into independent deliveries that happen to be near each other in time. The coherence is the point.

## 7. The four questions — organizing grammar for the platform

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder elevation:** these are not merely the client home's sections. They are the **organizing grammar for the entire practitioner platform** — client home, practitioner dashboard, MAIA, publishing, and any future notification surface all reduce to them. They are experiential questions rather than software categories, which is exactly why they generalize.

> **What are we practicing? · What has been shared? · What has been discovered? · Where do we continue?**

In the client's home they read:

1. **What am I practicing?** — active program or current focus
2. **What has Larry shared with me?** — new resources, notes, practices, follow-up
3. **What have I been discovering?** — the client's own reflections, commitments, continuity
4. **Where can I continue?** — MAIA, today's practice, assigned work, a recommended resource

⚠️ **Sovereignty rider applies without exception.** Ordering keys on *authored facts only* — Larry placed a thing, the client kept a thing, a session occurred. ⛔ Never on inferred readiness, engagement, or psychological state.

Mapping to the five-room ontology: (1) → My Work · (2) → My Coaching · (3) → My Story · (4) → The Room. **This is not a sixth room.** Practitioner-placed objects appear inside existing rooms according to which question they answer.

## 8. Provenance — four constitutional relations, not metadata

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder ruling on review: this is the foundational implementation problem.** These four are distinct relationships, and collapsing any two makes all later behavior ambiguous:

| Relation | Means | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| **practitioner authored** | Larry made this | the object |
| **practitioner shared** | Larry placed it into *this* commitment | the placement |
| **practitioner attributed** | the system associated it with Larry | ⛔ **never sufficient to act on** |
| **member adopted** | the client took it up as their own | the Client Field |

⛔ **They are not metadata. They are constitutional relationships**, and the schema must make them non-collapsible from the first migration.

This is the exact defect the 2026-08-06 containment ruling hit from the other direction: `studio_field_signals.source = 'practitioner'` was refused precisely because it recorded a *category* where a *provenance* was needed — nothing established whether a row was authored **by** the practitioner or merely attributed **to** them. A publishing system that stores "practitioner-related" without storing which of these four holds inherits that ambiguity at far greater scale.

⚠️ **member adopted** is the one most easily lost. When a client takes up Larry's practice as their own, that adoption is a **Client Field** fact — it does not travel back to Larry, and it does not convert the object into shared material.

## 9. MAIA stays on the provenance side of the line

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder ruling: hold this position until an explicit constitutional ruling says otherwise.** MAIA may know **that Larry shared something**. Whether MAIA may conclude *"this is relevant to what you are experiencing"* is **not settled**. These are different acts:

| | Act | Status |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | *"Larry shared this with you during your work together."* | **provenance** — permitted, factual, attributable |
| ⛔ | *"This is what you need right now."* | **interpretation** — unruled, refused pending a founder ruling |

The first states a fact about an act that occurred. The second makes a claim about the member's present state — which is the same authority the containment ruling closed when it barred system-inferred developmental claims from reaching the practitioner. The direction is reversed; the constitutional question is identical.

⛔ Until ruled, no contextual surfacing, no relevance ranking, no "recommended for you," no timing judgment about practitioner material.

## 10. Held candidates — gestures that must be earned by evidence

⛔ **Not authorized. Not scheduled. Recorded so they are recognized if they arrive, and refused if they are merely proposed.**

| Candidate | The claim it would make | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|
| **Offer** | *"If you want this, it's available."* — Leadership Library · Marriage Exercises · Grief Practices | Makes **no timing claim and no relevance claim**. The client discovers it voluntarily. This sidesteps the §9 containment concern entirely — nothing decides relevance, so nothing interprets |
| **Invitation** | *"When you're ready, I'd love you to explore this."* | Creates neither ownership nor obligation, and unlike Recommend does **not assert present relevance** |

⭐⭐ **The standing rule for both:** a fourth gesture may be added **only when it emerges from actual practitioner experience** — never because the model looks incomplete. Symmetry is not evidence. A gesture earns its place by a practitioner needing to make a claim that Share, Recommend, and Assign cannot make.

## 11. Sequencing

⛔ Nothing here is authorized. If and when it is, order is:

```
objects → provenance → Share → Recommend → session follow-up → Programs → Assign → cohorts
```

Assign comes late deliberately — it is the only gesture that creates obligation, and it should not exist until the non-obligating gestures have proven the crossing model works.

⭐ **Note what is absent from that sequence, and keep it absent:** no notifications · no analytics · no dashboards · no completion tracking · no engagement metrics. If any of these ever appear, they must be derived from the ontology and justified against §0 — never imported because platforms usually have them.

## 12. Remaining open questions

1. **Withdrawal.** Larry can update a manual — can he retract something already placed? What happens to a client reflection anchored to a retracted object, or to a Collection that loses a member (§4)? Neither party may unilaterally remove relationship-owned things. (Partially addressed by §13's `withdrawn` states; the *consequence* for anchored client material is still unruled.)
2. **Cohort / group objects.** Announcements and cohort discussions cross into a many-member field with no ontology yet. Deferred to last in §11 for that reason.

## 13. Lifecycle state — separate from gestures

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder addition, 2026-08-06.** The model had Objects, Gestures, and Provenance. **State is the fourth element**, and keeping it separate is what prevents gestures from becoming overloaded.

> A gesture is an **act** performed once. A state is a **condition** that persists and changes.

### ⚠️ Applying §3 to the proposed list

The founder-sketched object lifecycle read: `Draft · Published · Shared · Recommended · Assigned · Withdrawn · Archived · Superseded`. Applying the document's own Objects/Gestures separation, **three of those cannot be object states**:

⛔ `Shared` · `Recommended` · `Assigned` are not conditions of the Manual. A single Manual can be **Shared into one relationship and Assigned in another at the same moment** — so they cannot be a single value on the object. They belong to the **placement**, and even there they are the placement's **type** (which gesture created it), not its state.

This is not a correction of the direction; it is the direction applied one level further.

### Object lifecycle — a property of the work itself

```
draft → published → superseded
                 ↘ withdrawn
                 ↘ archived
```

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `draft` | authored, not yet placeable |
| `published` | available to be placed; ⛔ **not** the same as placed anywhere |
| `withdrawn` | ⛔ no longer placeable; existing placements are **not** silently revoked (§12.1) |
| `archived` | retired from Larry's active practice; history preserved |
| `superseded` | a newer version exists; **the link is part of provenance** — prior placements must still resolve to what the client actually received |

⭐ `published → withdrawn` must never rewrite the past. A client who received something did receive it.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Placement **type** ⊥ placement **state** — two axes, never one enum

Founder ruling: these are independent dimensions and must not be collapsed into a single field.

| Placement type (which gesture created it) | Placement state (where it stands now) |
|---|---|
| Share | delivered |
| Recommend | delivered · *(see `viewed` below)* |
| Assign | accepted · declined · completed |

⛔ **Never `enum { shared, recommended, assigned, delivered, completed }`.** That single-enum shape is exactly what makes "the same Manual, Shared to A and Assigned to C" unrepresentable — the collapse §13 exists to prevent. Type is immutable and set by the act; state moves.

⚠️ **`viewed` is recorded as unruled, not adopted.** It appears in the founder sketch under Recommend, and it does not clearly pass the §13 test: viewing is an act, but not an *authored* one — it is incidental, and the member did not perform it to say anything. Two separable questions, neither settled:

1. May the system record it at all?
2. May the **practitioner see it**? — a read receipt on coaching material is a surveillance surface, and §2's compliance guard applies with full force. *"Kelly hasn't opened it yet"* is the accountability engine arriving through a different door.

⛔ Until ruled, no `viewed` state and no practitioner-facing open/read indicator.

📌 **Disposition (founder, confirming the hold):** accuracy alone does not make a fact relationally neutral. A practitioner-facing *"not opened"* signal becomes implicit accountability · compliance monitoring · pressure to perform engagement · a basis for inference about avoidance or readiness — the last being a §9 violation outright.

⭐ **At most**, `viewed` may someday exist as (a) **private member-side continuity** or (b) **technical delivery telemetry** — and ⛔ **each requires its own stated purpose and retention boundary before it may exist at all.** Neither crosses to the practitioner.

### Placement lifecycle — a property of one crossing

```
created → delivered → accepted | declined
                              ↘ completed
                              ↘ withdrawn
```

| State | Guard |
|---|---|
| `created` | the act occurred |
| `delivered` | it reached the client's field |
| `accepted` / `declined` | ⚠️ **only meaningful for Assign.** A Share needs no acceptance — it is simply theirs. Forcing acknowledgment on a Share converts a gift into a task |
| `completed` | ⛔⛔ **client-authored only.** Never inferred from opening, viewing, dwell time, or elapsed days. Never scored, ranked, or aggregated (§2) |
| `withdrawn` | the practitioner retracted the placement; ⛔ the client's own reflections on it remain theirs (§8, *member adopted*) |

### ⛔ `expired` is refused

The sketch included `expired`. **A time-based automatic state is the system acquiring an interest in compliance through the back door** — it manufactures deadline pressure that no human authored. If Larry wants something done before Thursday, Larry says so; that is a fact of his request, not a state machine reclaiming the object.

⭐⭐ **The governing rule, stated generally (founder refinement):**

> **Time may be recorded. Deadlines may exist. State transitions happen because of authored human acts** — unless an exception is intentionally and explicitly decided.

So *"Larry asked me to look at this before Thursday"* is a legitimate recorded fact carried by the placement. What is refused is the system converting it, unasked, into *"the system now believes this obligation has lapsed."* Those are different claims, made by different authorities. ⛔ Any future automatic transition must be an explicit, argued exception to this rule — never a default.

⛔ `declined` and an untouched `delivered` must both be **terminal without consequence**. No escalation, no reminder cascade, no state that exists only to be chased.

## 14. Watch item — the four publishers

⛔ **Not a broadening of this document.** Recorded so it is recognized if it proves out during implementation.

The practitioner is the publisher here. The ecosystem eventually contains at least four:

**practitioner** · **organization** · **author** · **member**

If the grammar in §§2–4 and §13 holds for all four without modification, then what is described here is not *coach publishing* — it is a general **AIN Publishing Grammar** that Larry merely instantiates first, and it belongs one layer below this document.

⭐ **The test to apply during implementation, not before:** does any rule here depend on the publisher being a practitioner? If none does, the layer exists and should be extracted. If several do, the generalization is false and this stays where it is. ⛔ Do not extract it speculatively — an abstraction built before its second instance is a guess.

## 15. Next design work — the domain model, not more capabilities

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder sequencing ruling:** the next artifact adds **no new capabilities**. It turns this into an explicit domain model with six parts:

| | Part | Where it stands today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Objects** — what exists | §3, §4 |
| 2 | **Relationships** — how things connect | §8 (the four relations) |
| 3 | **Gestures** — how humans create relationships | §2 |
| 4 | **States** — how objects and relationships evolve | §13 |
| 5 | **Permissions** — who may perform which gesture | ⛔ **undefined** |
| 6 | **Events** — what enters the relationship timeline | partial — §6 defines the session event only |

Rows 5 and 6 are the genuine gaps. Permissions is where role-held-inside-the-commitment (`THREE_FIELDS`, §2) must become mechanical. Events is where §6's relational event generalizes beyond session follow-up.

⛔ Until those six are specified together, no implementation should begin — including the §11 sequence.

### 15a. Permissions — jurisdiction inside the commitment

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder ruling: permissions are defined relative to the commitment, never as global platform roles.** This is what prevents *organization* · *assistant* · *coach* · *member* from becoming detached capabilities floating above the relationship in which the authority actually exists. It is the mechanical form of `THREE_FIELDS` §2 — *"roles are held inside the commitment, not globally."*

⭐⭐ **The six questions to ask of every act:**

| | Question | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who **authored** the underlying object? | §8 `authored` |
| 2 | Who holds the relevant **role in this commitment**? | jurisdiction, not identity |
| 3 | Who may **perform** the gesture? | the act |
| 4 | Who may **observe that it occurred**? | ⭐ visibility, separable from the act itself |
| 5 | Who may **reverse or supersede** it? | §13 `withdrawn` / `superseded` |
| 6 | Which **historical fact remains irreversible**? | what no authority may erase |

⭐ Q4 and Q6 are the ones a conventional permission model omits, and they are why this cannot be an ACL. *Performing* an act, *seeing* that it happened, and *undoing* it are three different authorities — and Q6 asserts that some facts outlive all of them.

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Q6 is not a permission — it is a constitutional invariant

Founder observation, and it reclassifies the question: *"which fact is irreversible?"* is not authorization in the ordinary software sense. It asserts that **some historical facts remain true regardless of who holds what authority afterward.**

> A Placement occurred. · An Uptake occurred. · A recommendation was made.

These remain true even when permissions change · organizations change · practitioners leave · Works are withdrawn.

⛔ **No role, at any level, may make an act un-have-happened.** Withdrawal changes *availability going forward*; it does not edit the past (§13). An organization may revoke a practitioner's access; it may not revoke the fact that they placed something.

⭐ **Permissions and irreversibility answer different questions:**

| Permissions ask | Irreversibility asks |
|---|---|
| *Who may act **now**?* | *What remains historically true **after every later act**?* |

A person may lose access, a Work may be withdrawn, and a commitment may end — **and the fact that a Placement occurred remains true.**

⛔ **Consequence, stated plainly: destructive deletion of relational events is refused.** Whatever erasure obligations exist (and they do exist) must be met by a mechanism that does not require the history to have never happened. That mechanism is unspecified and is part of the §15 work — ⛔ it is not solved by `DELETE`.

⭐ This belongs to the **declared ≠ derived** family, not to authorization — which is why it must be specified alongside permissions rather than inside them. A permission model that can express *"admin may delete"* without qualification has already violated it.

**Seed cases** (each is a permission question, not a UI question):

1. Can an **assistant coach** Share?
2. Can an **organization withdraw** something a practitioner placed?
3. Can two practitioners **co-author** a Work?
4. Can a **client re-share** something they received?
5. Can a **supervisor inspect** a commitment?
6. ⛔ Can **MAIA place anything**? *(The document's posture says no — MAIA stewards continuity without owning anything, §9 — but it must be ruled, not assumed.)*

⚠️ Cases 2 and 4 are the sharp ones: both are crossings **the placing party did not perform**, which is precisely the authority §8 exists to keep unambiguous.

### 15b. Events — likely the nervous system

Today there is one event (§6, session follow-up). The eventual grammar is richer — ⛔ **relationship events, not notifications**:

`session concluded` · `object placed` · `recommendation made` · `assignment requested` · `reflection shared` · `reflection kept private` · `manual revised` · `program begun` · `program completed (client-declared)` · `object withdrawn` · `invitation accepted`

⭐⭐⭐ **The invariant that makes this list safe, and the admission test for every future event:**

> **Every event is an authored occurrence. Never a system inference.**

Read the list back: each one names an act a *person performed*. ⛔ Nothing derived, scored, or noticed by the system is admissible — which is the same line §13 draws against `expired` and `viewed`, now stated at the event layer. `program completed` carries `(client-declared)` in its own name for exactly this reason.

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Recording ⊥ visibility — the design question for the next session

> **Which events belong to the commitment's durable history, and which remain private to one field?**

⭐⭐ **Not all of these should be visible to both parties, even if all are durably recorded somewhere** (founder). Recording and visibility are independent decisions — this generalizes the `viewed` hold in §13 into a rule that applies to every event, and it is Q4 of §15a asked at the event layer.

⭐⭐⭐ **Three independent axes, and they need not coincide:**

```
recorded   ⊥   visible   ⊥   actionable
```

| Event | Recorded | Visible to practitioner |
|---|---|---|
| member viewed a resource | *maybe* — §13 bounds it | ⛔ **not ruled; presently no** |
| member took up a practice (Uptake) | ✅ yes | only if **explicitly shared** |
| member private reflection | ✅ yes | ⛔ **no** |
| practitioner placement | ✅ yes | ✅ yes |

⛔ **If these axes collapse, the event stream becomes surveillance rather than history.** All four combinations are legitimate and must be expressible:

- recorded but **private**
- recorded and visible but **inert**
- recorded, visible, and **actionable**
- recorded and actionable **for one party**, invisible to another

⭐⭐ **MAIA is the clearest test case.** Seeing a Placement grants it **no authority** to recommend · interpret · re-place · withdraw · or otherwise act on it (§9). Visibility is not permission; it never was.

The hard cases, to be settled together rather than one at a time:

| Event | Question it forces |
|---|---|
| placement created | presumably shared history — but does *drafting* it count? |
| placement withdrawn | does the client see that something was retracted, or only that it is gone? |
| member declined | ⚠️ does Larry see a decline, or only an absence? §2's compliance guard bears directly |
| member completed | client-declared, so presumably visible — but must not aggregate |
| member shared a reflection | the act crosses; does the *decision to share* also become history? |
| practitioner revised a Work | client-visible only where it changes what they received (§13 `superseded`) |
| organization changed practitioner access | ⚠️ whose history is this? Neither party performed it |
| session follow-up issued | the §6 relational event — the one already settled |

⛔ None of these are settled here. They are recorded as one question because deciding them individually is how a coherent history degrades into an activity feed.

## 16. Watch item — the Relationship as a durable object

⚠️ **Founder watch item, and the evidence may already be in.** The concern: this document increasingly treats the relationship as a durable object in its own right, accumulating placements · conversations · commitments · follow-ups · acknowledgements · shared notes · shared history. The instruction was to watch whether implementation keeps asking questions answered by *"this belongs to the relationship, not to either person."*

📌 **That recurrence has arguably already occurred — in prior canon.** [`THREE_FIELDS_AND_THE_RELATIONSHIP_2026-08-06.md`](./THREE_FIELDS_AND_THE_RELATIONSHIP_2026-08-06.md) §2 already rules:

> ⭐⭐⭐ The Relationship is a first-class object, **conceptually above** clients · practitioners · sessions · keeps · programs — not another table alongside them.

…refined the same day to *"the primitive is a **shared developmental commitment**, not a relationship,"* with the consequence stated explicitly: *"Programs, Shared Questions, Offerings, Sessions, Practices belong to the **commitment**, not to either individual — which is why **neither may unilaterally remove them** and why neither owns the crossing."*

⭐ **So the watch item is not open in the way it appears.** The question already recurs inside this document and is already answered by that ruling:

| This document asks | Commitment ruling answers |
|---|---|
| §12.1 — can Larry retract something already placed? | ⛔ No — placements belong to the commitment; neither party removes unilaterally |
| §15a.2 — can an organization withdraw a practitioner's placement? | same principle, one layer out |
| §13 — why doesn't `withdrawn` rewrite the past? | because the placement was never solely Larry's to erase |

⛔ **What remains genuinely open is narrower:** whether the commitment needs its own **state** and **event stream** (§13, §15b) rather than only owning contents. That is a real question, and it is the one to watch — not whether the object exists.

### 16a. Resolved — the commitment is the event container

📌 **Founder ruling on review, closing the ontological half of the watch item:**

> **The commitment already exists in prior canon; publishing simply participates in it.**

⛔ **No fifth publishing object is created.** Publishing does not introduce a container — it contributes acts to a container that was already ratified. The structure that follows:

| Element | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Work** | authored content |
| **Arrangement** | authored composition (§4) |
| **Placement** | a practitioner act into a commitment |
| **Uptake** | a member declaration |
| **Commitment** | ⭐ the shared event history in which those acts occur |

⭐⭐ **`Uptake` is not a rename — it is new.** §8 carried *member adopted* as a **provenance relation**; naming it an **act** makes it the structural mirror of Placement:

```
Placement:  practitioner → commitment     (an offer crosses)
Uptake:     member       → commitment     (a taking-up crosses)
```

Both are authored crossings performed by the party whose material it is. That symmetry **is** the consent-carried model expressed in the domain model rather than in policy — neither party pulls from the other's field; each may only place into the shared one.

⚠️ **Vocabulary status: emerging, not adopted.** `Work` and `Arrangement` are candidate names for what §3 calls Objects and §4 calls Collection. They read better and carry authorship in the noun. ⛔ Renaming is deferred to the §15 domain-model artifact, where it can be done once and completely rather than leaving two vocabularies in the tree.

⭐⭐⭐ **The reframe this settles — recorded because it changes what the next session is about:**

> **The major unresolved work is no longer ontology. It is jurisdiction inside the commitment: who may do what, whose act it is, and where its consequences become visible.**

Withdrawal, organizational authority, and historical preservation looked like questions about *ownership of individual placements*. They are not. They are questions about **what the shared commitment permits, preserves, and records over time.**

## 17. Candidate constitutional principle — **Acts create facts**

🟡 **Candidate, not canon.** ⛔ This document cannot ratify a constitutional principle; it can only nominate one and show the evidence. Ratification requires a founder ruling and placement in `docs/canon/`.

⭐⭐⭐ **The nominated primitive, suspected to underlie all six parts of the §15 model:**

> **Acts create facts.** Every durable fact in the relationship is traceable to an authored act.
>
> **The test:** *What authored act created this fact?* ⛔ If there is no answer, the fact does not belong in the relationship's durable history.

| The act | The fact it creates |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | a Work entered this commitment |
| **Uptake** | the member took something into their own work |
| **Withdrawal** | availability changed *(going forward — §15a Q6)* |
| **Completion**, if ever modeled | the member **declared** completion |

⛔ **The system never manufactures durable facts through observation alone.**

### Why this is worth ratifying rather than leaving implicit

It is already load-bearing in four separate places, each having derived it independently:

| Where | The same principle, locally stated |
|---|---|
| **Containment** (2026-08-06) | `pattern_ledger` refused because inference is not declaration |
| **Provenance** (§8) | *authored* ≠ *attributed* — a system association creates no authority |
| **Publishing** (§13) | `expired` and `viewed` refused — no human authored the transition |
| **Relationship** (`THREE_FIELDS`) | contents belong to the commitment because acts, not roles, put them there |

⭐⭐ **Its relation to `declared ≠ derived` is precise, and worth stating in the ruling:** that rule is a **prohibition** — it says what may *not* be treated as declared. *Acts create facts* is the same principle stated **positively** — it says what makes a durable fact legitimate in the first place. One refuses; one generates. A prohibition tells you what to block at a read path; a generative rule tells you what to build, which is why it reaches further into design than containment ever could.

### ⚠️ Required scope boundary — or it will be misapplied

The principle governs **facts admitted to the relationship's durable history**. ⛔ It does **not** govern all data the system may lawfully hold.

| ✅ Legitimately not act-derived | Why unaffected |
|---|---|
| security audit logs | must exist precisely because no one authored the act |
| delivery/technical telemetry (§13) | operational, purpose- and retention-bounded, ⛔ never relational history |
| error and system-health records | describe the system, not the relationship |

⭐ The boundary is the discriminator: *does this fact enter the shared history two people will read as their story together?* If yes, an authored act must stand behind it. If it is infrastructure describing the machine, the principle is silent — ⛔ but it may never be promoted into relational history later, because promotion would launder a derived fact into a declared one.

⭐⭐⭐ **The precise statement, and the move it forecloses (founder):**

> **Only authored human acts may create facts admitted to the relationship's durable history.** Security logs, delivery telemetry, failures, and system diagnostics may record what the machine observed. They do not thereby become part of the shared human story.

⛔ **The dangerous later move this blocks:** *"The system observed it, therefore the relationship knows it."* **It does not.** Machine observation and relational knowledge are different orders of fact, and no volume of the first ever amounts to the second.

### If ratified

It gives future design one question in place of many, and it is the same question at every layer — object, event, permission, surface: **what authored act created this fact?**

### ⭐⭐⭐ 17a. The pairing — §0 and §17 are one principle in two registers

Founder observation, and it changes how ratification should be proposed:

| | Statement | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **§0** | *The system does not organize information. It preserves intentional human acts.* | **teleological** — what the platform is **for** |
| **§17** | *Acts create facts.* | **generative** — the mechanism by which it **fulfills** that purpose |

⛔ **These are not two principles. They are the aim and the discipline of the same architecture** — and they should be ratified **as one pairing**, sitting alongside each other, never independently.

⚠️ **Why the pairing matters practically:** §0 alone is a value statement, and values are easy to affirm and hard to enforce — nothing about it tells an engineer what to refuse at a schema. §17 alone is a mechanism without a reason, and mechanisms detached from purpose get optimized away by the next person who finds them inconvenient. **Each protects the other from its characteristic failure.**

### ⭐⭐ The architecture became act-centered

Recorded because it was not the starting position and no single amendment made it happen:

```
Objects       matter because people act upon them
Relationships matter because acts occur within them
Permissions   matter because they authorize acts
Events        matter because they preserve acts
Facts         matter because acts create them
Records       matter because they preserve those facts
```

The document opened object-centered — *what is a Manual, a Program, a Collection?* Those questions are now largely settled and comparatively inert. **The live questions are all about acts and their consequences**, which is what §16a's jurisdiction reframe was already reporting from a different angle.

## 18. Act · Fact · Record — three layers, for the Events work

⭐⭐⭐ **Founder refinement, carried into the §15 domain-model artifact.** When Events are designed, these three must not be conflated:

| Layer | Example |
|---|---|
| **1. Act** | Larry places a Manual |
| **2. Fact** | that Manual entered this commitment through Larry's **Share** gesture, at this time |
| **3. Record** | the persisted event that keeps the fact historically recoverable |

⛔ **The event row is not the act, and it is not the fact.** Conflating them is how a durable history quietly becomes a mutable log: edit the row and you appear to edit what happened. Separating them makes §15a Q6 mechanical — the *fact* is irreversible; the *record* is merely how it stays recoverable.

### ⭐⭐ Attestation — the case this resolves

⚠️ **Opened here for the first time; not previously specified in this document.** Founder-supplied, and it is the sharpest test of the three layers:

| Layer | For a practitioner attestation |
|---|---|
| **Act** | the practitioner records having heard the member say something |
| **Fact** | ⭐ *that the **practitioner recorded** hearing it* |
| ⛔ **Not the fact** | ⛔ *that the **member declared** it* |

⭐⭐⭐ **This is the whole ruling.** An attestation is the practitioner's authored act, and the fact it creates is about **the practitioner's recording**, never about the member's declaration. The member's declaration would require the member's own authored act — an **Uptake**, or a reflection they chose to share (§5, §16a).

⛔ Without this separation, attestation becomes a laundering path: the practitioner writes *"Kelly said she's ready to lead the team,"* and the system treats it as Kelly's declaration. That is a member-authority violation of exactly the kind the 2026-08-06 containment ruling closed — arriving this time through practitioner authorship rather than system inference, which is why it would not be caught by the same guard.

#### ⭐⭐⭐ The laundering chain — a failure class, not a bug

```
Larry writes  →  database stores  →  MAIA later reads  →  system renders "You said…"
```

⭐⭐ **No single step is obviously wrong. The error emerges from the cumulative promotion of one fact into a different fact.** This is why the defect cannot be caught by reviewing any individual step, and why the separation must be structural rather than a rule someone remembers to apply.

⭐ **What the separation protects is epistemic ownership**, not database normalization. The model must never allow *"someone recorded hearing it"* to become *"the person declared it."*

⭐ **And it needs no fifth domain object.** Attestation is a practitioner-authored act creating a practitioner-scoped fact — already expressible in Act · Fact · Record with the §8 provenance relations. The temptation to model "what the member said" as its own object is precisely the temptation to refuse.

### 18a. It reaches beyond publishing — second canon candidate

🟡 **Candidate, not canon.** The structure appears to explain rulings already made in unrelated domains:

| Domain | Act | Fact created | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Member declaration | member declares | member declared | persisted declaration |
| Placement | practitioner places | placement occurred | placement record |
| Uptake | member adopts | uptake occurred | uptake record |
| Attestation | practitioner attests | ⭐ *practitioner* attested | attestation record |
| **Ratification** | founder ratifies | canon changed | constitutional record |

⭐ Governance fits its own structure — **including this document.** Each amendment above is an act, creating the fact that a ruling was made, preserved by a record. The amendment trail is not bookkeeping; it is the principle applied reflexively.

#### ⚠️ The falsifiability check — required before nomination is credible

⛔ **A structure that fits every domain risks explaining nothing.** Universality is weak evidence; it is equally consistent with the structure being vacuous. The real test is **what it forbids.**

It forbids something specific and non-obvious:

| ⛔ Forbidden | Because |
|---|---|
| the laundering chain (§18) | a fact may not silently become a different fact |
| `expired`, `viewed` (§13) | no act, therefore no fact |
| practitioner attestation read as member declaration | wrong author on the fact |
| promoting telemetry into relational history (§17) | machine observation is a different order of fact |
| `DELETE` as erasure semantics (§15a) | records preserve facts; facts do not un-happen |

⭐ **Five refusals with teeth is what makes it a principle rather than a description.** Had the structure forbidden nothing, its cross-domain fit would have been an argument *against* ratifying it.
