# SOULLAB WRITER'S STUDIO
# Intent-First Entry + First Ten Minutes v1

Status: FLAGSHIP EXPERIENCE AUTHORITY  
Purpose: define how a member enters Writer's Studio without needing professional writing vocabulary, how MAIA translates human intent into governed intelligence, and how the first ten minutes create meaningful discovery through the member's own Work.

This canon is additive. It does not replace:
- the Flagship Experience Canon;
- constitutional observation / provenance / coverage law;
- the governed Develop observation substrate;
- proposal / Apply / Undo authority;
- founder visual acceptance.

---

# 1. North Star

> **The writer should not have to diagnose their own writing problem before Soullab can help.**

A member may arrive knowing only:

> "Something feels off."

That is enough to begin.

The product's job is not to force the member to choose machinery.

The product's job is to help the member turn a human concern into a lawful, evidence-grounded exploration of their own Work.

---

# 2. Core Experience

```text
human concern
        ↓
plain-language invitation
        ↓
MAIA reflects what she can look at
        ↓
member agrees / narrows / changes direction
        ↓
governed reading or existing evidence
        ↓
one meaningful discovery
        ↓
follow it into the Work
        ↓
conversation at the exact passage
        ↓
safe experimentation
        ↓
return to the Work
```

No hidden commission.
No inferred professional intent.
No requirement that the writer know the right editorial term.

---

# 3. The Front Door

The primary entry question is:

> **What are you trying to understand?**

Recommended starting invitations:

- **Something feels off**
- **Help me see the shape**
- **I'm losing the thread**
- **This doesn't sound like me**
- **I'm not sure what this chapter is doing**
- **Show me what keeps appearing**
- **I want to go deeper here**
- **I'm worried I'm repeating myself**
- **I don't know — help me look**

These are human concerns, not editorial categories.

---

# 4. Hidden Intelligence, Visible Intent

The writer does not need to choose:

- Structure
- Continuity
- Voice
- Themes
- Pacing
- Reader Perspective
- Arc
- Developmental pass
- Lens
- Range
- Analysis type

Those may exist underneath.

They are not the required front door.

---

# 5. Intent → Intelligence Map

The routing below is guidance for governed orchestration.
It is not a new authority grant.

| Writer intent | Possible underlying intelligence |
|---|---|
| Something feels off | broad orientation from existing evidence; if insufficient, propose a bounded reading |
| Help me see the shape | structure + continuity + member-declared map |
| I'm losing the thread | continuity + exact return addresses |
| This doesn't sound like me | voice observations |
| I'm not sure what this chapter is doing | chapter role, declared structure, continuity, local movement |
| Show me what keeps appearing | continuity map + recurring textual entities / declared themes |
| I want to go deeper here | local passage observation + related passages |
| I'm worried I'm repeating myself | recurrence / phrase / image / idea presence |
| Where might a reader lose me? | Reader Perspective; explicitly hypothetical |
| I don't know — help me look | one low-claim observation, then an invitation to continue |

Routing is internal.

The member-facing experience remains human.

---

# 6. Commission Boundary

Intent is **not automatically a commission**.

MAIA must distinguish:

1. what she already knows from an admitted reading;
2. what she can answer from the currently visible passage;
3. what would require reading more of the Work.

Before a new reading, MAIA names the proposed scope.

Example:

> **"I can look with you. I'd start with this chapter and the one before it, because you're asking where the thread changes. Want me to read those two?"**

The member may:
- agree;
- narrow scope;
- broaden scope where allowed;
- cancel;
- choose another question.

Navigation never silently commissions a read.

---

# 7. First-Response Grammar

The first response should make the member feel accompanied, not diagnosed.

## Good pattern

> **Reflect**
>
> "You're noticing that this chapter isn't landing the way you want."

> **Name a possible way to look**
>
> "I could start by seeing where its direction changes and whether the thread from the previous chapter carries through."

> **State scope**
>
> "That would mean reading this chapter and Chapter 5."

> **Invite**
>
> "Would you like me to look there?"

---

## Avoid

> "I recommend a structural analysis."

> "This is probably a pacing issue."

> "Choose a lens."

> "Run continuity."

> "Your manuscript needs developmental editing."

The member's uncertainty must not be converted into machine certainty.

---

# 8. "I Don't Know — Help Me Look"

This is a first-class state.

MAIA should not respond with a menu of expert categories.

Preferred behavior:

1. begin with what is already available;
2. surface one low-claim, evidence-grounded observation;
3. show the exact place;
4. invite the member to follow it.

Example:

> **"One thing I notice: the river image appears here and twice earlier in this chapter. Want to see those places together?"**

If current evidence is insufficient:

> **"I don't have enough of this chapter in view to say anything useful yet. I can read this chapter with you if you'd like."**

"I don't know" must not become an excuse for MAIA to take broad authority.

---

# 9. The First Meaningful Discovery

> **The first meaningful discovery is the onboarding.**

A new member should learn what Writer's Studio is by learning something real about their own Work.

A good first discovery is:

- concrete;
- evidence-linked;
- addressable;
- non-evaluative;
- surprising or clarifying;
- easy to follow.

Examples:

- "This image appears in Chapters 2, 5, and 9."
- "Clara disappears from the Work for three chapters."
- "You move from scene into explanation here."
- "This phrase returns at three transition points."
- "Belonging doesn't appear until Chapter 6."
- "Direct address becomes more frequent after Chapter 8."

---

# 10. What Does NOT Count as a First Discovery

Not sufficient:

- "Your writing is strong."
- "This chapter has good pacing."
- "Your themes are coherent."
- "This is emotionally powerful."
- "You have an authentic voice."
- "This section needs work."
- "This chapter is important."

These are grades, verdicts, or vague conclusions.

The first discovery should give the writer something they can **see**.

---

# 11. First Ten Minutes

## Minute 0–2 — Recognition

The member should immediately know:

- which Work they are in;
- where they are in it;
- whether they are in Write / Develop / Review;
- how to return to the manuscript.

Recognition may use:

1. member-declared purpose;
2. exact line from the manuscript + factual reason it is surfaced;
3. Work facts.

Never fabricate:
- what the Work is "really about";
- author intent;
- a thematic thesis MAIA inferred from free prose.

---

## Minute 2–4 — First Meaningful Act

The member should naturally do one of:

- write;
- read;
- hold a passage;
- ask MAIA;
- follow a visible thread;
- choose a plain-language intent.

Do not interrupt this moment with a tutorial carousel.

---

## Minute 4–6 — First Discovery

The Studio should surface one evidence-grounded thing the writer could not easily see from inside the draft.

The member should be able to ask:

- Where?
- Show me.
- What else connects to this?
- Is this mine or MAIA's observation?
- What did MAIA read?

---

## Minute 6–8 — Follow the Thread

The writer can:

- open the mapped location;
- arrive at exact passage;
- preserve the observation that brought them;
- see related passages;
- talk with MAIA;
- return to the larger pattern.

The observation travels.
The member does not get dropped into an unrelated tool.

---

## Minute 8–10 — Safe Experiment

The member can:

- try a possibility;
- understand it is optional;
- Read in context;
- Keep original or Apply;
- Undo;
- return.

The writer should understand:

> **I can explore without losing my Work.**

---

# 12. Plain-Language Entry Cards

Recommended default cards:

## Something feels off
> Start with what feels different, confusing, flat, rushed, or hard to name.

## Help me see the shape
> See how this chapter or Work is put together and where its movements connect.

## I'm losing the thread
> Follow an idea, character, image, or question through the Work.

## This doesn't sound like me
> Look at shifts in voice, cadence, distance, and language.

## Show me what keeps appearing
> See recurring images, phrases, characters, places, or member-declared themes.

## I want to go deeper here
> Stay with this passage and explore what is happening on the page.

## I don't know — help me look
> Begin with one concrete thing MAIA can support with evidence.

---

# 13. Empty / Honest States

Empty states must carry meaning.

## MAIA has not read enough

> **MAIA hasn't read enough of your Work to trace this yet.**

Show:
- what MAIA has read;
- what is missing.

Actions:
- `See what MAIA read`
- `Read this chapter`
- `Choose a smaller scope`

---

## No recurring pattern found

> **This appears only here so far.**

That is information.

Do not force a pattern because the member asked for one.

---

## No declared structure

> **You haven't named the larger shape of this Work yet.**

Actions:
- `Start with what you already know`
- `Choose a template`
- `Not now`

If a Soullab template is selected:
> `You chose the Spiral template.`

Not:
> `You named these.`

---

## MAIA read it and found nothing admissible

> **MAIA read this chapter for continuity and didn't find a break she could support with evidence.**

This is distinct from:
> `MAIA hasn't read this for continuity yet.`

---

## Work changed since reading

> **This chapter has changed since MAIA last read it.**

Actions:
- `Read this chapter again`
- `Not now`

No silent refresh.

---

# 14. Language Canon — Invitations

Prefer:

- Help me understand this
- Show me where this appears
- Take me to the passage
- What might I be missing?
- Show me another possibility
- Keep my original
- Read in context
- What else connects to this?
- Where does this thread go?
- Help me see the shape
- Stay with this passage
- Talk this through with me
- What did MAIA read?
- Add your own observation

---

# 15. Language Canon — Uncertainty

Use:

- I notice...
- I wonder whether...
- One possible reading is...
- A reader might...
- This could...
- I don't have enough evidence yet...
- MAIA hasn't read that part yet...
- This appears only here so far...
- I can look at this chapter with you...

Avoid false certainty.

---

# 16. Language Canon — Trust

Use:

- This is your observation.
- MAIA noticed this.
- This appears in your text.
- You named this.
- You chose the Spiral template.
- MAIA read Chapters 1–4.
- This chapter has changed since MAIA last read it.
- MAIA will not read the new version without your request.
- Your original is preserved.
- Undo this change.
- Back to Review.
- Back to your manuscript.

---

# 17. Language Canon — Avoid

Avoid member-facing machinery such as:

- Run analysis
- Execute pass
- Select lens
- Scope range
- Generate developmental output
- Optimization
- Recommended revision
- Best option
- Highest priority
- Most relevant

Avoid manuscript grading:

- strong
- weak
- good
- bad
- authentic
- engaging
- beautiful
- luminous
- ready
- key
- important
- polished
- professional

Unless clearly first-person MAIA stance where permitted.

---

# 18. Guided / Learning / Direct

The same human intent enters the same governed intelligence.

## Guided

MAIA:
- translates gently;
- offers plain-language choices;
- explains what she proposes to look at.

Example:

> "I can start by looking at where this chapter changes direction and whether the previous thread carries through."

---

## Learning

Same evidence and scope.

Adds craft teaching:

> "A transition often becomes visible when scene, time, or explanatory mode changes. Here, you move from scene into reflection in one sentence."

Teaching never changes the finding itself.

---

## Direct

Same evidence, same authority, same proposal scope.

More compact:

> "The thread drops here. It appears in Ch. 5, disappears for 18 sections, then returns in Ch. 7."

Direct is faster.
It is not less governed.

---

# 19. MAIA Must Not Diagnose the Writer

The Studio may identify properties of the text supported by evidence.

It must not infer:

- writer skill level;
- intelligence;
- professionalism;
- confidence;
- creative ability;
- seriousness;
- readiness;
- competence.

The facet is chosen by the member.
It is not assigned by MAIA.

---

# 20. Intent Routing Must Be Inspectable

The member does not need to see routing machinery by default.

But where trust requires it, they may ask:

> **Why are you looking at this?**

MAIA can answer plainly:

> "You said the chapter feels disconnected, so I started with continuity — where characters, ideas, and images carry through. I haven't run a separate voice reading."

This is transparency, not configuration.

---

# 21. No Silent Lens Expansion

If the member asks:

> "Where does this thread go?"

MAIA may not silently add:
- voice critique;
- pacing critique;
- reader-effect analysis;
- structure judgment.

A new direction is invited explicitly.

Example:

> "I can also look at whether the voice changes around those moments. Want me to?"

---

# 22. Next-Move Law

> **Every meaningful observation should invite a next move.**

Examples:

- Go to passage
- See other places
- Talk it through
- Ask why
- Add your observation
- Try a possibility
- Read in context
- Back to Review
- See the larger map

No dead-end analytical cards.

---

# 23. First-Ten-Minutes Acceptance

A candidate passes only if a first-time member can, without coaching:

1. identify the Work;
2. identify where they are;
3. make one meaningful action;
4. encounter one evidence-grounded discovery;
5. follow it to an exact place;
6. understand why they arrived;
7. talk with MAIA there;
8. safely try something;
9. preserve or restore their original;
10. return.

---

# 24. Intent-First Acceptance Gates

## I1 — HUMAN LANGUAGE FIRST
Primary entry uses writer concerns, not editorial machinery.

## I2 — NO DIAGNOSIS REQUIRED
The member does not need to know the correct problem category.

## I3 — ROUTING DOES NOT GRANT AUTHORITY
Intent does not silently commission a reading.

## I4 — SCOPE IS NAMED
MAIA states what she proposes to read before new reading.

## I5 — FIRST DISCOVERY IS EVIDENCE-GROUNDED
No grade/verdict qualifies as onboarding discovery.

## I6 — "I DON'T KNOW" WORKS
The member can begin without choosing a category.

## I7 — EMPTY STATES ARE HONEST
Not read / read-nothing / appears-only-here / Work-changed remain distinct.

## I8 — FACET INVARIANT
Guided / Learning / Direct never change truth, authority, or proposal breadth.

## I9 — NO SILENT LENS EXPANSION
One concern does not trigger unrelated readings.

## I10 — NEXT MOVE
Observations lead somewhere in the Work.

## I11 — FIRST TEN MINUTES
A new user completes the flagship loop without a tutorial.

## I12 — NON-PRO-WRITER PASS
A person who does not know editorial vocabulary can still begin.

# 25. Mechanical Falsifiers

Examples of candidates that should die:

### D-I1
Opening screen requires choosing:
`Structure · Voice · Continuity · Themes`

### D-I2
"Something feels off" immediately launches whole-Work analysis.

### D-I3
"I don't know" responds with a technical menu.

### D-I4
MAIA answers a cross-Work question despite insufficient coverage.

### D-I5
First discovery says:
`Your pacing is strong.`

### D-I6
Guided produces more MAIA-authored prose than Direct.

### D-I7
Clicking `Reader Perspective` silently triggers a new read.

### D-I8
No-pattern result is replaced with a fabricated "emerging theme."

### D-I9
A user cannot get from discovery back to exact manuscript passage.

### D-I10
Member must complete an onboarding tutorial before touching the Work.

---

# 26. Human Founder Questions

Only a human answers:

1. Could I begin without knowing writing terminology?
2. Did the Studio understand what I was trying to do without pretending to know more than I said?
3. Did I know what MAIA proposed to read?
4. Was the first discovery genuinely useful?
5. Could I immediately see where it came from?
6. Did I want to follow it?
7. Did I ever feel graded?
8. Did I ever feel pushed into machinery?
9. Could I always get back to my Work?
10. Did I understand that experimenting was safe?
11. Did MAIA feel beside me rather than ahead of me?
12. Did the Studio teach itself through my own manuscript?

---

# 27. Product Principle

> **The flagship should teach the writer how to use it by helping them understand their own Work.**

No long tutorial is required.

No expert vocabulary is required.

No diagnosis is required.

Curiosity is enough.
