# SOULLAB WRITER'S STUDIO
# Founder Walk + Human Usability Protocol v1

Status: EXPERIENCE VALIDATION AUTHORITY  
Purpose: prove that the flagship Writer's Studio works for real humans across age, experience, device, and writing confidence.

This protocol begins **after a rendered flagship candidate exists**.  
It does not authorize implementation, merge, deployment, or production mutation.

---

# 1. Core Question

The flagship must answer:

> **Can a person enter their Work, understand where they are, discover something meaningful, follow it, safely try something, and return to their own words without needing professional editorial knowledge?**

The experience should leave them thinking:

> **I know what to do. I want to see where this goes.**

---

# 2. What This Protocol Is Testing

Not merely:
- whether buttons work;
- whether routes resolve;
- whether tests are green;
- whether the UI resembles a reference.

It tests whether the Studio is:

- understandable;
- inviting;
- navigable;
- trustworthy;
- non-intimidating;
- useful;
- accessible;
- safe to experiment in;
- compelling through curiosity rather than gamification;
- coherent across Write, Develop, Review, and mobile.

---

# 3. Machine-Proof vs Human-Witness Boundary

## Machines may verify

- route availability;
- exact return addresses;
- observation IDs;
- page-order findings;
- no silent recommission;
- typography scaling;
- no horizontal overflow;
- control-size floors;
- no legacy UI signatures;
- no unsupported navigation;
- no direct Apply from alternatives;
- coverage/provenance presence;
- exact passage preservation;
- scroll/position stability;
- mobile map scrolling.

## Machines may **not** proxy

- "Do I know where I am?"
- "Does this feel welcoming?"
- "Do I understand what to do next?"
- "Does this feel like writing software for me?"
- "Do I trust MAIA here?"
- "Does this feel like my Work?"
- "Do I want to keep exploring?"
- "Does the Studio feel alive without becoming busy?"
- "Am I being graded?"
- "Am I afraid of breaking something?"

Those require a human witness.

---

# 4. Five Human Profiles

These profiles are not stereotypes. They are test lenses.

## H1 — First-time young writer

Context:
- has a story or draft;
- limited craft vocabulary;
- may be comfortable with modern apps;
- may feel uncertain about being "a real writer."

Primary risk:
- the Studio feels like school, grading, or professional editing software.

Success:
- curiosity appears before intimidation.

---

## H2 — Older memoirist

Context:
- substantial lived material;
- may have a long draft;
- may not identify as a writer;
- may rely on larger text, touch, voice, or slower navigation.

Primary risk:
- small controls, jargon, hover-only behavior, overwhelming density.

Success:
- can comfortably read, navigate, mark something, ask MAIA, and return.

---

## H3 — Experienced novelist

Context:
- understands craft;
- wants speed;
- does not want unnecessary explanation.

Primary risk:
- MAIA becomes patronizing or verbose;
- Guided behavior leaks into Direct mode;
- interaction takes too many steps.

Success:
- can move quickly while retaining all trust and authorship protections.

---

## H4 — Helper / coach / therapist writing a book

Context:
- strong concepts and lived/professional knowledge;
- may not understand narrative craft;
- often writes from models, case examples, teachings, or reflection.

Primary risk:
- the Studio assumes fiction conventions or editorial language.

Success:
- Develop reveals shape and relationships without requiring formal training.

---

## H5 — Returning writer with a messy draft

Context:
- knows something is wrong;
- cannot name the problem;
- may be discouraged.

Typical thought:
> "Chapter 6 just doesn't work."

Primary risk:
- the Studio immediately asks the writer to select machinery.

Success:
- the Studio provides a plain-language starting point and creates momentum.

---

# 5. Universal Adventure Walk

Every tester completes this loop without being told which tool to use.

## Scenario

> You have returned to your book after some time away.  
> Something in this chapter interests you, but you are not sure why.  
> Explore it and decide whether you want to change anything.

## Observe whether the member can naturally:

1. Recognize the Work.
2. Understand where they are.
3. Begin reading or writing.
4. Hold or mark an exact passage.
5. Ask MAIA something about it.
6. See or follow a larger pattern.
7. Visit another passage connected to it.
8. Return to the original passage.
9. Try another wording or possibility.
10. Read the proposal in context.
11. Choose:
   - Apply;
   - Keep original;
   - continue discussing.
12. Undo if they applied.
13. Find History if they want it.
14. Return to Review or Develop.
15. Continue deeper into the Work.

## Critical observation

Does this feel like:

> **exploring the living world of my book**

or:

> **operating a complicated editing tool**

---

# 6. "I'm Not a Writer" Test

Do not explain Develop.

Place the member in Develop and say:

> **"This chapter feels off to you. What would you do?"**

Observe the first 30 seconds.

## PASS signals

The person chooses an understandable invitation such as:
- Help me see the shape.
- What keeps appearing?
- Show me where this thread goes.
- What changed here?
- What am I circling around?
- Ask MAIA.
- Open a visible pattern.
- Follow a map cell.

## REVISE signals

The person asks:
- "What's a continuity pass?"
- "Which lens am I supposed to choose?"
- "What does developmental mean?"
- "Do I need to pick one of these before I can start?"
- "What is the right option?"

Also REVISE if they simply stare at the interface because every control assumes expertise.

---

# 7. First Ten Minutes Protocol

This is a flagship commercial/product test.

The member receives a Work and no tutorial beyond a short welcome.

## Minute 0–2 — Recognition

Can they answer:
- What Work am I in?
- Where am I in it?
- Is this Write, Develop, or Review?
- Can I get back to the manuscript?

The Studio should recognize:
- Work title;
- place;
- writer-declared purpose where available;
- factual Work identity.

It must not fabricate a thesis about what the Work "really means."

---

## Minute 2–4 — First meaningful action

Does the member:
- write;
- read;
- hold a passage;
- ask MAIA;
- follow a visible thread?

Track:
- time to first meaningful action;
- hesitation points;
- jargon encountered;
- mis-clicks;
- dead controls.

---

## Minute 4–6 — First discovery

The Studio should enable one genuine discovery such as:

- "That image appears in five chapters."
- "This character disappears for three chapters."
- "I use direct address much more after Chapter 8."
- "This phrase comes back at transition points."
- "Belonging doesn't appear until this section."

No grading is required.

Observe:

> Does the member react with recognition, curiosity, surprise, or usefulness?

---

## Minute 6–8 — Follow the thread

Can the member:
- open a mapped location;
- land at the exact passage;
- understand why they arrived;
- preserve the observation that brought them;
- ask MAIA about it;
- return to the larger pattern?

---

## Minute 8–10 — Safe experimentation

Can they:
- try a possibility;
- understand it is optional;
- Read in context;
- Keep original or Apply;
- Undo without fear?

The member should understand:

> **I cannot lose my Work by exploring.**

---

# 8. Review Walk

## Task

> "Find something Review has noticed in this chapter that interests you. Follow it into the book."

Observe:

- Are findings in book/page order?
- Does the finding have evidence?
- Is its language non-evaluative?
- Does `Go to passage` feel obvious?
- Does the exact observation travel?
- Does MAIA show the same observation rather than silently invent a new one?
- Can the member see related passages?
- Can they return to Review?

## Human question

> "Did you feel like you left Review and got lost somewhere else?"

Desired answer:
> No.

---

# 9. Add Your Own Observation Walk

## Task

> "Mark something in this passage that *you* notice."

The member should be able to choose:

- Something I noticed
- A question
- A possibility

Then:
- write optional text;
- Keep with this passage.

## Ask afterward

- Whose observation is this?
- Where will you find it again?
- Does MAIA own it?
- Can you change/remove it?

Desired understanding:
- **It is mine.**
- It stays attached to this place.
- MAIA may reference it but does not become its author.

---

# 10. Work-Changed / Stale-Reading Walk

Prepare a chapter MAIA previously read, then alter the chapter.

## Observe

Does the Studio clearly say:

> **This chapter has changed since MAIA last read it.**

Can the member identify:
- when MAIA last read;
- when the Work changed;
- whether current observations are based on the earlier version?

Actions:
- Read this chapter again
- Not now

## Ask

> "Did MAIA read your new version automatically?"

Desired answer:
> **No.**

This is a trust test.

---

# 11. Develop Walk

## Task

> "See whether there is a thread in your Work you want to understand better."

Do not name the Continuity Map.

Observe:
- what object attracts them;
- whether the map reads naturally;
- whether provenance is understandable;
- whether cells feel clickable/navigable;
- whether they understand presence ≠ meaning;
- whether they can return to manuscript evidence.

## Ask

> "What do these blue cells mean?"

Desired answer approximates:
> They show where something appears.

Not:
> They show where it's strongest/best/most important.

---

# 12. Visualization Truth Walk

Show two visual objects:

A. countable/verifiable presence map  
B. an attributed MAIA interpretation

Ask:

> "Which of these is measured from your Work, and which is MAIA's interpretation?"

If the answer is unclear, visual epistemic hierarchy has failed.

---

# 13. Story / Spiral Map Provenance Walk

Test three states:

1. writer named the movements;
2. writer chose a Soullab Spiral template;
3. MAIA offers an observation about possible shape.

Ask:

> "Who created these movement names?"

The UI must make the answer obvious.

Required distinctions:
- You named these.
- You chose the Spiral template.
- MAIA notices...

Never collapse them into `member-declared`.

---

# 14. Support Facet Walk

Test:
- Guided
- Learning
- Direct

Use the same passage and same observation.

Verify that all three preserve:
- evidence;
- observation identity;
- proposal scope;
- authorship authority;
- mutation boundary.

Only explanation/translation/teaching depth changes.

## Human question

> "Did switching this setting make MAIA take more control over your writing?"

Desired answer:
> No.

---

# 15. Non-Grading Test

Ask the person after Develop/Review:

> "Did you feel like the Studio was grading your book?"

Investigate any Yes.

Common causes:
- readiness;
- balanced;
- strong/weak;
- key;
- important;
- engaging;
- authentic;
- progress scores;
- colored severity;
- ordered findings.

The flagship should create insight without a report card.

---

# 16. Accessibility Matrix

Run the same core walk under:

## Desktop
- 1920 width
- 1440 width
- narrower laptop

## Browser text scaling
- 100%
- 125%
- 150%
- 175% if practical

## Mobile
- ~390px viewport
- portrait orientation

## Input modes
- mouse / trackpad
- keyboard
- touch
- voice when the voice surface is ready

### Required observations

- horizontal overflow?
- clipped text?
- hidden controls?
- hover-only information?
- unreadable maps?
- maps crushing instead of scrolling?
- control targets too small?
- focus indicator visible?
- can the member recover from every overlay?
- does increased text size preserve hierarchy?

---

# 17. Older-Writer Comfort Walk

For an older tester or simulated high-text-size condition:

Ask the tester to:
1. open a chapter;
2. increase text size;
3. read a page;
4. open MAIA;
5. open Develop;
6. use the Continuity Map;
7. add an observation;
8. return to the manuscript.

Ask:
- Was anything physically difficult to click?
- Did you lose anything when text got bigger?
- Did anything feel rushed?
- Did you understand what was clickable?
- Could you tell where you were?

---

# 18. Experienced-Writer Directness Walk

Set facet to Direct.

Task:
> "You know exactly what you want to inspect. Get there quickly."

Observe:
- unnecessary explanation;
- repeated onboarding copy;
- extra confirmation;
- hidden advanced action;
- whether trust mechanisms remain visible without becoming verbose.

Direct must be faster, not less governed.

---

# 19. Curiosity Test

At the end, ask:

> "Is there anything else in your Work you want to follow right now?"

This is deliberately not:
- "Did you like it?"
- "Was it fun?"
- "Would you recommend it?"

The flagship goal is **curiosity**.

Record:
- Yes / No
- what they wanted to follow
- why

A strong outcome is spontaneous continuation:
> "I want to see where that appears in the earlier chapters."

---

# 20. Emotional Safety / Authorship Test

Ask:

- Did you ever worry MAIA had changed something without you?
- Did you ever worry your original was gone?
- Did you know when MAIA was interpreting versus showing a fact from your Work?
- Did MAIA feel ahead of you or beside you?
- Did the Work still feel like yours?

Any uncertainty is a trust defect, even if no mutation occurred.

---

# 21. Observation Method

For each task capture:

```text
TASK
What the member was trying to do

FIRST MOVE
What they clicked/said first

HESITATION
Where they paused

MISREAD
What they thought a control meant

DEAD END
Where they could not continue

RECOVERY
How they got back

DISCOVERY
What they learned about the Work

TRUST
Any concern about authorship, evidence, or MAIA authority

CURIOSITY
What they wanted to do next
```

Do not coach during the first attempt unless safety requires it.

---

# 22. Severity Without Product Grading

Usability defects may be triaged operationally without grading the Work.

## BLOCKING
Member cannot complete the core loop or trust boundary is violated.

## MAJOR
Member completes it only through confusion, coaching, or repeated recovery.

## FRICTION
Member succeeds but hesitates, misreads, or experiences avoidable difficulty.

## POLISH
Does not materially impair understanding or agency.

These labels apply to **product defects**, never to the manuscript.

---

# 23. Beta Cohort

Recommended initial human beta:

- 2 first-time / younger writers
- 2 older writers / memoirists
- 2 experienced writers
- 2 helpers / professionals writing a book
- 2 messy-draft / returning writers

Total:
> **10 people**

A smaller first round of 5 is acceptable if each profile is represented.

Do not recruit only people already familiar with Soullab terminology.

---

# 24. What To Measure

Useful signals:

- time to first meaningful action;
- time to exact passage return;
- number of hesitations;
- number of terminology questions;
- wrong turns;
- dead ends;
- successful Undo;
- successful return to Review;
- successful identification of member vs MAIA observation;
- successful identification of measured vs interpreted visual;
- text-scaling failures;
- mobile failures;
- spontaneous next exploration.

Avoid vanity metrics such as:
- session length as success;
- clicks as engagement;
- streaks;
- time-on-platform.

---

# 25. Founder Walk Questions

Only the founder answers these for the flagship integrated build.

1. Is my Work unmistakably the central thing?
2. Do I know where I am?
3. Do I know what I can do next?
4. Can I always return to my manuscript without thinking about it?
5. Does MAIA feel beside me rather than ahead of me?
6. Can I explore without fear of losing my words?
7. Do Develop and Review reveal the Work rather than grade it?
8. Does the Studio feel substantially quieter and more human than professional editing software?
9. Does it feel coherent across Write, Develop, Review, and mobile?
10. Does it make me curious to keep going?
11. Would someone who does not consider themselves a writer understand this?
12. Does this feel like Soullab?

These may not be proxied by code, screenshots alone, or another person's report.

---

# 26. Flagship Human Acceptance

The product is not human-accepted because all participants succeeded once.

Before flagship acceptance:

- no unresolved BLOCKING defects;
- no authorship/trust ambiguity;
- no inaccessible core path at supported text scales;
- no mobile collapse of the core loop;
- no recurring jargon barrier for non-professional writers;
- founder visual gate PASS;
- founder walk PASS;
- observed curiosity in multiple user profiles.

---

# 27. First Release Principle

> **The flagship should teach the writer how to use it by helping them understand their own Work.**

No long tutorial should be required to reveal the product's value.

The first meaningful discovery is the onboarding.

---

# 28. Spirit of Soullab — Human Test

At the end of the walk, the experience should support this internal sequence:

> My Work is alive.  
> I can see more of it than I could before.  
> I know where I am.  
> I can follow what interests me.  
> I can always return to my words.  
> I can try things without losing myself.  
> MAIA is with me, not ahead of me.  
> I want to see where this goes.

**A deeper you. A more human world.**
