# 3. REVIEW — Remaining Report Smell

The largest remaining issue is not the token system.

It is the **composition and language at the top of Review**.

The page currently opens with:

> **What MAIA found**

That recenters MAIA as the owner of Review.

The long-term product law is:

> **Return to what you've noticed.**

Review will contain:
- textual facts;
- member observations;
- MAIA observations;
- followed threads.

Therefore `What MAIA found` is likely transitional copy, not the final flagship identity.

Suggested direction for later adjudication:

- `Review`
- `What you've noticed`
- `What you're following`

Do not change merely for wording preference if member-observation capability is not yet implemented. But do not let `What MAIA found` silently become permanent product law.

# 4. STALE STATE — Primary V10 Revision Candidate

The stale-reading state is truthful and well designed semantically.

Visually, however, it remains the dominant object in Review.

At desktop:
- it is the largest warm region;
- it competes with the manuscript;
- the eye reaches stale status before the Work relationship.

At mobile:
- it consumes essentially the whole first meaningful viewport after the header;
- the member must travel a long distance before reaching findings.

The previous ruling remains right:

```text
BEFORE ACK
prominent and unmistakable

AFTER "NOT NOW"
compact persistent trust strip
```

The current pre-ack state is not wrong.
It is simply heavier than necessary.

### Recommended V10 refinement

Do **not** remove or hide information.

Reduce vertical and chromatic dominance:

- retain status border/icon;
- retain dates;
- retain exact change facts;
- retain `Read this chapter again`;
- retain `Not now`;
- retain historical reference;
- retain sovereignty statement.

But consider:
- tighter fact layout;
- less large warm fill;
- collapsible/secondary `What changed` detail on mobile;
- quieter background;
- stronger visual relationship to the manuscript than to the status panel.

The trust state should interrupt false confidence without becoming the subject of Review.

- button size is strong;
- warm/cool roles survive;
- text is not crushed;
- status content remains legible.

But there are two visible pressures.

## A. Stale state dominates first run

The entire initial content area is trust/status before discovery.

Prominence is required.
This amount of vertical takeover may not be.

## B. Navigation density

The top carries:
- Work title;
- Guided;
- Review;
- Ask MAIA;
- horizontal lens tabs.

The lens row visibly runs offscreen.

Horizontal scrolling may be an acceptable mechanism, but the human witness should answer:

> Does this feel like intentional exploration, or like desktop tabs overflowing a phone?

Do not infer PASS from the mechanical mobile witness.
