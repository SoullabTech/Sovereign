# SOULLAB WRITER'S STUDIO
# Beta Facilitator Script v1

## Purpose

Run a consistent human usability session without coaching the participant through the interface.

The facilitator is observing the product, not evaluating the participant.

---

# 1. Before the session

Record:

- Participant ID:
- Profile: H1 / H2 / H3 / H4 / H5
- Age range:
- Writing experience:
- Device:
- Browser:
- Viewport:
- Text scale:
- Input method:
- Candidate commit:
- Date:

Prepare:
- a manuscript appropriate to the profile;
- one chapter MAIA has read;
- one chapter that has changed since MAIA last read it;
- at least one admissible observation;
- at least one area with insufficient coverage;
- one revision possibility;
- mobile session if part of this run.

---

# 2. Opening script

Read:

> "We're testing Writer's Studio, not you or your writing. You don't need to know any writing terminology and you don't need to figure out the 'right' way to use it. Please use it as naturally as you can. If something is confusing, say so. I may stay quiet while you work because I want to see what the product makes clear on its own."

Then:

> "Please think aloud when you can — what you're looking for, what you expect, and anything that feels surprising or unclear."

Do not explain:
- what Develop means;
- what Review means;
- what Continuity means;
- which control they should choose;
- what MAIA is "supposed" to do.

---

# 3. Task 1 — First Ten Minutes

Say:

> "You've come back to your book after some time away. Take a look around and do whatever feels natural."

Do not add instructions for two minutes.

Observe:
- recognition;
- where they look first;
- first click;
- first hesitation;
- whether they understand Work identity;
- whether they understand current place;
- whether they can return to manuscript.

After two minutes say:

> "There's something in this chapter you want help with. Explore it however you would naturally."

Do not name MAIA or any tool.

---

# 4. Task 2 — "Something feels off"

Say:

> "Imagine this chapter feels off to you, but you don't know why. What would you do?"

Observe whether they:
- select plain-language intent;
- ask MAIA;
- enter machinery;
- hesitate over editorial terminology.

If participant asks "what should I click?" respond:

> "What would you expect the product to let you do?"

Do not coach further unless blocked.

---

# 5. Task 3 — Follow a thread

Say:

> "Find something that seems to appear elsewhere in your Work and follow it."

Observe:
- Continuity Map / pattern use;
- cell comprehension;
- evidence;
- exact address;
- return behavior.

Ask afterward:

> "What did those cells mean to you?"

Do not suggest the correct interpretation.

---

# 6. Task 4 — Review → Passage → Back

Say:

> "Choose something in Review that interests you and follow it into the manuscript."

Observe:
- finding order;
- Go to passage;
- same observation;
- whether MAIA appears to generate a new finding;
- Back to Review.

Ask:

> "Did you feel like you knew why you landed here?"

Then:

> "Can you get back?"

---

# 7. Task 5 — Add Your Own Observation

Say:

> "Mark something in this passage that you notice."

Observe whether participant can:
- mark locus;
- identify member-observation action;
- choose Notice / Question / Possibility;
- write something;
- keep it with passage;
- find it again.

Ask:

> "Whose observation is that?"

---

# 8. Task 6 — Safe Experimentation

Say:

> "Try another wording for this passage, but don't commit to changing your book unless you actually want to."

Observe:
- proposal path;
- alternatives;
- Keep my original;
- Read in context;
- Apply;
- Undo.

Ask:

> "At any point were you worried your original was gone?"

---

# 9. Task 7 — Stale Reading

Navigate to prepared changed chapter.

Say only:

> "Take a look at what MAIA is showing you here."

Observe whether participant identifies:
- reading is stale;
- last read time/version;
- current Work change;
- deliberate re-read;
- Not now.

Ask:

> "Has MAIA already read your new version?"

---

# 10. Task 8 — Visualization Truth

Show:
- a presence/count map;
- a MAIA interpretation surface.

Ask:

> "Which of these is measured from your Work, and which is MAIA's interpretation?"

Record exact answer.

---

# 11. Task 9 — Support Facet

Using the same passage, ask participant to try:
- Guided;
- Learning;
- Direct.

Ask:

> "What changed?"

Then:

> "Did changing that setting feel like MAIA got more control over your writing?"

---

# 12. Task 10 — Accessibility

If appropriate:
- increase browser text scale to 125%;
- then 150%;
- optionally 175%.

Ask participant to:
- read;
- open MAIA;
- open Develop;
- use map;
- return.

Observe:
- overflow;
- clipped controls;
- map behavior;
- target size;
- focus;
- recovery.

If mobile is in scope, repeat Review → Passage → Back on mobile.

---

# 13. Closing questions

Ask exactly:

1. "What would you say this Studio does?"
2. "Was there any point where you didn't know where you were?"
3. "Did you ever feel like the Studio was grading your writing?"
4. "Did you ever worry MAIA had changed something without you?"
5. "Did the Work still feel like yours?"
6. "What was the most useful thing you saw?"
7. "What was the most confusing thing?"
8. "Is there anything else in your Work you want to follow right now?"

Do not turn this into a sales interview.

---

# 14. Facilitator rules

DO:
- stay quiet;
- capture exact words;
- note hesitation;
- note physical difficulty;
- note emotional/trust reactions;
- note spontaneous curiosity.

DO NOT:
- teach the interface;
- defend the product;
- explain intended behavior before first attempt;
- tell participant a control is "obvious";
- convert confusion into user error;
- ask leading praise questions.

---

# 15. Session outcome

Record:
- PASS
- REVISE

Then classify defects:
- BLOCKING
- MAJOR
- FRICTION
- POLISH
