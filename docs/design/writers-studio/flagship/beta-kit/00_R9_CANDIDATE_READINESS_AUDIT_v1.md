## 2. ⭐⭐ TASK 9 IS THE ONE THAT WOULD MANUFACTURE A FALSE FINDING

The script asks the participant to try Guided, Learning and Direct, then:

> "What changed?"

**Today the honest answer is: a label.** `FACET_COPY` carries three labels and
three lines; ⛔ no observation, no explanation depth, no disclosure default and
no copy anywhere in `lib/writersStudio/studio/**` varies by facet. The facet is
plumbed through every room and changes nothing downstream of the chip.

⚠️ So the participant would answer *"nothing"* — and that answer is **correct
about the candidate and uninformative about the product.** Worse, the follow-up
— *"Did changing that setting feel like MAIA got more control over your
writing?"* — would return a clean NO for the wrong reason: nothing changed at
all, so nothing could feel like a transfer of authorship.

⭐ **This is the §14 facet-invariance test I have already declined to fake**,
arriving from the other direction. A conformance test would pass by proving
nothing; a beta task would fail by measuring nothing. ⛔ Both are the same gap.

**Recommendation (⛔ a recommendation, not a ruling):** hold Task 9 until the
facets differ in translation depth. Running it early costs a real participant's
attention and buys a finding that cannot be acted on.

## 3. Two tasks that ask the candidate to remember

Tasks 5 and 6, and the sheet's own `Did they successfully Undo?` /
`Could they distinguish member observation from MAIA observation?`, all
presume the member's marks **persist**.

⛔ They do not. This is the stop condition already named in the R3–R12 audit:
persisting a member observation is a schema act, and `OBSERVATION-ADDRESS-01`
— not opened — gates member actions against `observation_id`.

⭐ The distinction that keeps the task honest: *mark it, choose its kind, write
it, see it held with the passage* is **buildable now**. *Close the Studio and
find it tomorrow* is ⛔ not, and no amount of composition work changes that.
If Task 5 runs before that lane closes, the facilitator must know which half
is being tested — otherwise *find it again* records as a usability failure
when it is an unbuilt capability.

## 4. The session environment is itself a deliverable

§1 of the facilitator script asks for a prepared manuscript with: a chapter
MAIA has read · a chapter changed since · an admissible observation · an area
of insufficient coverage · a revision possibility.

⭐ Every one of those states exists **as a witness fixture**. ⛔ None exists as
a seedable environment a facilitator could open on the day. Producing one is
its own bounded act — and it is a **data** act, so it belongs to the same
persistence question as §3, not to composition.

⚠️ Sequencing consequence worth stating plainly: R9 cannot be scheduled off a
green R8. It needs a seeded environment that does not yet exist.

## 5. Where the consent script meets this project's own law

⭐ The privacy principles align with the house rules and one point deserves
naming rather than assuming: *"do not use beta manuscripts for model training
unless an explicit, separate consent framework authorizes it."*

⚠️ The unanswered operational question is narrower and more immediate: **does a
beta session persist MAIA's readings of a participant's manuscript at all?**
Sanctuary Mode's law is that there is no stealth memory. A participant told
*"we may take notes"* has consented to the **facilitator** remembering; ⛔ they
have not thereby consented to **the system** retaining a reading of their book.

⛔ Not mine to rule. ⭐ Raised because it is cheaper to answer before a
manuscript is in the environment than after.
