# SOULLAB WRITER'S STUDIO
# First Arrival + Onboarding Canon v1

Status: FLAGSHIP EXPERIENCE AUTHORITY  
Purpose: define how Writer's Studio welcomes a member, recognizes their Work, establishes trust, and creates first value without a tutorial or professional-writing prerequisite.

This canon is additive to:
- Flagship Experience Canon
- Intent-First Entry + First Ten Minutes
- Founder Walk + Human Usability Protocol
- visual and constitutional law

---

# 1. North Star

> **Do not teach Writer's Studio before the member has something worth doing in Writer's Studio.**

The product should reveal itself through the member's Work.

The first experience is not:

```text
Welcome
→ tutorial
→ feature tour
→ choose settings
→ choose a lens
→ import / configure
→ finally reach manuscript
```

It is:

```text
Here is your Work.
        ↓
Here is where you are.
        ↓
What would you like to understand or do?
        ↓
One real discovery.
        ↓
Follow it.
        ↓
"Oh — I understand what this place is."
```

---

# 2. First Promise

The Studio's first promise is not:

> Write better.

Nor:

> Become a better writer.

Nor:

> AI-powered editing.

The first promise is:

> **See more of the Work you're already making.**

Supporting line:

> **Write, explore, and develop your Work without losing your voice — or your place in it.**

---

# 3. Recognition Before Instruction

The first screen should recognize the Work before it explains the software.

Recognition may use, in preference order:

1. member-declared purpose;
2. a line from the Work at an exact address, with a factual reason for surfacing it;
3. Work facts.

Examples:

> **Elemental Alchemy**  
> Chapter 10 · The Living Spiral

> **The River Between**  
> Chapter 6 · The Current Changes

> **You last worked here three days ago.**

> **This chapter is 2,134 words.**

> **You have 12 chapters in this draft.**

### Never fabricate recognition

Do not open with:

> "This is a book about belonging and transformation."

unless the member explicitly declared that.

Do not infer author intent as welcome copy.

Recognition should feel like:
> "Yes, this is my Work."

Not:
> "MAIA has already decided what my book means."

---

# 4. First Arrival States

Writer's Studio needs distinct first-arrival experiences for:

1. Existing Work
2. New Work
3. Returning Work
4. Imported / incomplete Work
5. Work changed since MAIA last read
6. No MAIA reading yet

---

# 5. Existing Work — First Arrival

Primary composition:

```text
Work identity
current place
manuscript
quiet invitation
```

Suggested copy:

> **Welcome back to The River Between.**

> **You're in Chapter 6 — The Current Changes.**

Then:

> **What would you like to do?**

Primary actions:
- Keep writing
- Help me understand something
- See the shape of this chapter
- Show me what keeps appearing
- I don't know — help me look

No tour.

No "Start here" wizard.

No forced Develop/Review choice.

---

# 6. New Work — First Arrival

The first question should be about the Work, not software configuration.

Suggested opening:

> **What are you beginning?**

Allow:
- title
- optional short description in the member's own words

Then:

> **Where would you like to start?**

Options:
- Start writing
- Bring in something I've already written
- Make a simple map of what I know
- I don't know yet

### Avoid

- genre taxonomy as prerequisite
- experience-level selection
- "Are you a beginner?"
- mandatory project templates
- mandatory outline
- AI-generated premise before the member writes

A new Work should feel open.

---

# 7. Returning Work — Recognition

For a member returning after time away:

> **Welcome back.**

Then show:

- Work
- chapter/place
- last member action
- truthful Work change information
- any stale MAIA reading state

Example:

> **You last worked in Chapter 6.**

> **You changed this chapter after MAIA last read it.**

Actions:
- Continue here
- See what changed
- Read this chapter again
- Not now

The product helps the member re-enter their own context.

---

# 8. Imported / Incomplete Work

If the Work is newly imported or parsing is incomplete:

Be literal.

Example:

> **Your manuscript is here.**

> **Writer's Studio has identified 9 chapters. Two sections still need names.**

Actions:
- Start reading
- Review the structure
- Fix section names
- Not now

Do not pretend:
- the manuscript is fully understood;
- MAIA has read it;
- structure is declared if it was merely detected.

Detected structure and member-declared structure remain distinct.

---

# 9. No MAIA Reading Yet

This is not an error.

Suggested state:

> **MAIA hasn't read this Work yet.**

Supporting copy:

> **You can write without her. When you want another set of eyes, invite her to read a chapter or a passage.**

Actions:
- Keep writing
- Ask MAIA about this passage
- Read this chapter with MAIA
- What would MAIA read?

The Studio should remain fully inhabitable without an active reading.

---

# 10. First Trust Moment

Within the first session, the member should encounter one quiet statement of agency.

Examples:

> **Your original is always preserved.**

> **MAIA will not read more of your Work without your request.**

> **You can undo any revision you apply.**

Do not stack all trust copy into a legal-looking box.

Trust should appear at the moment it matters.

---

# 11. First Question

After recognition, ask:

> **What are you trying to understand?**

Recommended responses:

- Something feels off
- Help me see the shape
- I'm losing the thread
- This doesn't sound like me
- Show me what keeps appearing
- I want to go deeper here
- I don't know — help me look

Or:

> **Keep writing**

Writing is always a valid answer.

---

# 12. The "Keep Writing" Principle

Writer's Studio must not make exploration feel more important than writing.

At every first-arrival state:

> **Keep writing**

is a legitimate primary path.

The Studio is not successful if it constantly pulls the writer out of the manuscript into analysis.

Develop and Review serve the Work.

They do not compete with it.

---

# 13. First Meaningful Discovery

The onboarding moment happens when the member sees something real in their own Work.

Examples:

> **The river image appears three times in this chapter.**

> **Clara disappears from the manuscript for Chapters 3–5.**

> **This phrase returns at two transitions.**

> **You move from scene into explanation here.**

> **You named belonging as part of this Work, but it doesn't appear until Chapter 6.**

Each first discovery includes:
- evidence
- exact place
- provenance
- coverage where needed
- a next move

---

# 14. First Discovery Card

A first discovery should be visually light.

Example:

> **Something I notice**

> *"The current" appears here and twice earlier in this chapter.*

Actions:
- Show me
- Why are you showing me this?
- Not now

No:
- score
- recommendation badge
- severity
- ranking
- "important insight"

---

# 15. Why Are You Showing Me This?

This question should always be answerable.

Example:

> **You said this chapter felt disconnected. I noticed the river image appears at the beginning, disappears through the middle, and returns here. I thought it might be useful to look at those places together.**

This gives:
- member intent
- evidence
- routing transparency
- no hidden authority

---

# 16. First MAIA Encounter

MAIA's first appearance should feel relational, not theatrical.

Good:

> **I can look with you.**

Then:
> **I notice the chapter changes direction here. Want to stay with this passage or see where that shift begins?**

Avoid:

> "Hello! I'm MAIA, your AI writing assistant."

Avoid capability catalogues.

MAIA is understood through relationship and action.

---

# 17. First MAIA Scope Request

When more reading is required:

> **I can look at this chapter and the one before it to see whether the thread carries through. Want me to read those two?**

Actions:
- Yes, read those
- Just this chapter
- Not now

No silent expansion.

---

# 18. First Develop Experience

Do not introduce Develop as:

> Developmental editing tools.

Use:

> **See more of your Work**

Supporting invitation:

> **Follow patterns, structure, voice, and continuity through the manuscript.**

First visible object:
- Continuity Map or another governed, addressable object

The member learns Develop by using a real object.

---

# 19. First Review Experience

Do not introduce Review as:

> Run a review.

Use:

> **Return to what you've noticed**

Supporting line:

> **See observations, follow them back into the manuscript, and decide what you want to explore next.**

Review is memory + navigation.

Not a report card.

---

# 20. First Facet Choice

Do not force a facet choice during onboarding.

Default may remain the member's existing preference.

If surfaced, explain plainly:

### Guided
> Help me shape this with you.

### Learning
> Help me write — and teach me as we go.

### Direct
> Give me the detail without extra explanation.

Then:

> **This changes how MAIA explains things, not how much control she has over your Work.**

That sentence is important.

---

# 21. First Revision Experience

The first revision flow should demonstrate safety.

Sequence:

```text
held passage
→ MAIA proposes possibilities
→ Keep my original remains visible
→ Read in context
→ Apply
→ clear receipt
→ Undo
```

After Apply:

> **Revision applied to this passage.**

Actions:
- Undo this change
- View history
- Keep writing

No celebration animation.
No "Great choice!"
No reward language.

---

# 22. First Undo Experience

Undo is trust education.

After Undo:

> **Your previous wording is back.**

Supporting:
> **The revision is still in History if you want to revisit it.**

This teaches:
- nothing disappeared;
- exploration is reversible;
- history is truthful.

---

# 23. First Empty State

Empty is not failure.

Examples:

### Pattern appears once
> **This appears only here so far.**

### MAIA found nothing admissible
> **MAIA read this chapter for continuity and didn't find a break she could support with evidence.**

### MAIA hasn't read
> **MAIA hasn't read this chapter for continuity yet.**

These states must remain visibly distinct.

---

# 24. First Error State

Errors should preserve agency and place.

Example:

> **MAIA couldn't finish that reading. Your Work was not changed.**

Actions:
- Try again
- Choose a smaller scope
- Keep writing

Never:
- lose selected passage;
- lose scroll;
- attach late response elsewhere;
- imply partial result is complete.

---

# 25. First Mobile Arrival

Mobile first arrival should not become an abbreviated second-class product.

Show:
- Work
- current place
- manuscript
- simple navigation
- contextual MAIA pill/sheet

Primary actions remain:
- Write
- Develop
- Review

Intent-first entry remains available.

No desktop-column compression.

---

# 26. First-Time Accessibility

The first session must survive:

- browser text enlargement
- keyboard-only use
- mobile touch
- reduced motion
- screen reader semantics where supported

Do not introduce an onboarding overlay that itself breaks accessibility.

Avoid:
- tiny coach marks
- hover tooltips as instruction
- timed disappearance
- auto-advancing tours
- modal stacks

---

# 27. No Tutorial Carousel

A flagship anti-pattern:

```text
1 of 7
Welcome to Writer's Studio
Next →
```

This should not be the default onboarding.

A short optional "Show me around" may exist later, but it cannot be required for comprehension or value.

The Work itself is the orientation surface.

---

# 28. Optional "Show Me Around"

If offered, keep it contextual and dismissible.

Example:

> **Want a quick orientation?**

Options:
- Show me
- I'll explore on my own

Orientation points only to:
- Work
- current place
- Write / Develop / Review
- Ask MAIA
- how to get back

Do not tour every feature.

---

# 29. First Arrival — Existing Writer

Experienced writers may want no onboarding.

For Direct or returning experienced members:

> **Welcome back to Chapter 6.**

Then immediately:
- manuscript
- command/action access
- no repeated explanatory cards

The system must remember that onboarding has already happened.

---

# 30. First Arrival — Nervous Writer

For someone uncertain about writing:

Do not say:
> "Don't worry — anyone can write."

Instead make the interface prove safety.

Use:
- plain language
- Work recognition
- no grades
- Keep my original
- Undo
- easy return
- MAIA asking before reading more

Confidence emerges from agency.

---

# 31. First Arrival — Older Writer

Prioritize:
- readable type
- strong hierarchy
- calm density
- obvious click targets
- no hidden hover behavior
- clear return path
- text size resilience

Do not create a separate "senior mode."

Accessible design is the flagship design.

---

# 32. First Arrival — Young Writer

Do not gamify.

Curiosity is enough.

Invite:
- Follow this thread
- See where it appears
- Try another possibility
- See the shape
- Add your observation

The adventure is the Work itself.

---

# 33. First Arrival — Helper / Expert in Another Field

Recognize conceptual writing.

Do not force fiction assumptions.

Intent cards work across:
- memoir
- psychology
- philosophy
- coaching
- spirituality
- research-informed nonfiction
- teaching
- clinical/professional material where appropriate and permitted

Use:
> **What are you trying to understand?**

not:
> **What's your plot problem?**

---

# 34. Onboarding Does Not Equal Account Setup

Account/auth flows remain separate.

Do not conflate:
- sign-in
- billing
- role
- Work import
- Writer's Studio onboarding

The product experience begins when the member meets the Work.

---

# 35. First Arrival Acceptance Gates

## O1 — WORK FIRST
The Work appears before feature explanation.

## O2 — PLACE CLEAR
Member knows where they are.

## O3 — WRITE ALWAYS AVAILABLE
Member can simply continue writing.

## O4 — HUMAN INTENT
Entry uses human-language concerns.

## O5 — NO EXPERTISE TEST
No skill/experience diagnosis required.

## O6 — NO TUTORIAL REQUIRED
Value appears without completing a tour.

## O7 — FIRST DISCOVERY IS REAL
Onboarding value comes from evidence in the member's Work.

## O8 — SCOPE CONSENT
New MAIA reading names scope first.

## O9 — FIRST EXPERIMENT IS REVERSIBLE
Original + context + Undo are obvious.

## O10 — TRUST IN CONTEXT
Trust language appears where relevant, not as legal clutter.

## O11 — EMPTY IS HONEST
No fabricated insight fills silence.

## O12 — RETURN IS OBVIOUS
Member can get back to their Work immediately.

## O13 — ACCESSIBLE FIRST RUN
Onboarding survives scaling, keyboard, touch, mobile.

## O14 — RETURNING USER QUIET
Onboarding does not repeatedly interrupt experienced/returning members.

## O15 — CURIOSITY
The first session invites another meaningful move.

---

# 36. Defeat Candidates

The following should fail:

### D-O1
A seven-step feature-tour carousel blocks manuscript access.

### D-O2
New user must choose a professional writing level.

### D-O3
New user must choose Structure / Voice / Continuity before help begins.

### D-O4
MAIA auto-reads whole manuscript during onboarding without consent.

### D-O5
Welcome screen states what the book is "really about."

### D-O6
First insight is a grade:
`Your voice is strong.`

### D-O7
The product celebrates Apply as the "correct" choice.

### D-O8
No Keep original / Undo in first revision experience.

### D-O9
Returning user sees full onboarding every session.

### D-O10
Mobile onboarding is a squeezed version of desktop coach marks.

---

# 37. Human First-Arrival Questions

After the first ten minutes ask:

1. What did you think this Studio was for?
2. Did you know where you were?
3. Did you know what to do first?
4. Did you need to know writing terminology?
5. Did you discover anything real about your Work?
6. Did you know where that discovery came from?
7. Did you know what MAIA had read?
8. Did you feel safe trying something?
9. Did your original still feel protected?
10. Did you want to keep exploring?

---

# 38. First-Arrival Definition of Success

A successful first session sounds like:

> **It knew where I was without telling me what my book meant.**
>
> **I didn't need to know what kind of editing I needed.**
>
> **I found something in my Work I hadn't noticed.**
>
> **I could follow it.**
>
> **MAIA asked before going farther.**
>
> **I tried something and knew I could undo it.**
>
> **I knew how to get back.**
>
> **I wanted to keep going.**

---

# 39. Product Principle

> **Writer's Studio does not onboard the member into software. It welcomes the member back into relationship with their Work.**

---

# 40. Spirit of Soullab

The first arrival should communicate, mostly through behavior:

> This is your Work.
>
> You do not have to know everything about it yet.
>
> You do not have to know the right writing terminology.
>
> You can begin where you are.
>
> MAIA can look with you.
>
> She will tell you what she has seen and what she has not.
>
> She will ask before going farther.
>
> You can try things without losing your words.
>
> You can always return.
>
> There is more here to discover.

**A deeper you. A more human world.**
