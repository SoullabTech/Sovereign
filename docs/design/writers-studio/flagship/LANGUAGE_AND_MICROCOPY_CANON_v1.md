# SOULLAB WRITER'S STUDIO
# Language + Microcopy Canon v1

Status: FLAGSHIP CONTENT / PRODUCT-LANGUAGE AUTHORITY  
Purpose: define the member-facing language system for Writer's Studio so every surface speaks with one coherent product voice and preserves agency, provenance, capability honesty, and non-grading.

This canon is additive to:
- Flagship Experience Canon
- Intent-First Entry + First Ten Minutes
- First Arrival + Onboarding
- MAIA Relational Conversation + Facet Canon
- Durable Place + Observation Address
- Failure, Recovery + Concurrency
- Visual System Canon
- Work Navigation + Discovery Canon

It does not authorize implementation, merge, or deployment.

---

# 1. North Star

> **The language should make the writer feel oriented, respected, and free to decide.**

Writer's Studio should sound:

- clear;
- calm;
- human;
- literate;
- precise;
- relational;
- modest about what MAIA knows;
- confident about what the system actually did.

It should not sound:

- clinical;
- managerial;
- gamified;
- motivational;
- salesy;
- therapeutic by default;
- grading;
- AI-theatrical;
- technically opaque.

---

# 2. Core Language Law

> **Say exactly what happened, who did it, what it means, and what the member can do next — without overstating certainty.**

---

# 3. The Four Language Voices

Writer's Studio has four distinct language sources.

## A. The member
Member-authored language:
- manuscript;
- observation;
- question;
- possibility;
- declared structure;
- named themes;
- notes.

## B. The Work
Factual descriptive state:
- chapter names;
- counts;
- occurrence;
- version;
- location;
- presence.

## C. MAIA
Attributed observation / interpretation / teaching / proposal.

## D. The system
Navigation, status, persistence, errors, trust, capability.

These voices must not blur.

---

# 4. Member Language Is Primary

If the member names something:

> **Belonging**

the product may preserve that label.

Do not silently rename it:
> Identity and Attachment

unless the member asks.

Member wording should travel.

---

# 5. System Language Is Literal

The system says what it knows.

Good:
> **Saved**

when durable write is confirmed.

> **Not saved yet**

when not confirmed.

> **MAIA hasn't read this chapter yet.**

when true.

Avoid vague system anthropomorphism:
> **We're thinking...**

when the action is actually:
> MAIA is reading Chapter 6.

---

# 6. MAIA Language Is Attributed

MAIA may say:

> **I notice...**

> **I wonder...**

> **One possibility...**

> **I read this as...**

She should not silently convert interpretation into system fact.

---

# 7. Observation Grammar

Preferred sequence:

```text
WHAT
where / evidence
interpretation if any
limits
next move
```

Example:

> **I notice the river image returns here after being absent through the middle of the chapter.**
>
> **You use it in the opening and again in this paragraph.**
>
> **It may be carrying more than the literal setting by this point.**
>
> **That doesn't tell me whether you intended it.**
>
> **Want to put the two passages beside each other?**

---

# 8. Avoid Verdict Grammar

Avoid:

- strong
- weak
- effective
- ineffective
- successful
- unsuccessful
- compelling
- engaging
- beautiful
- powerful
- polished
- awkward
- clunky
- confusing

as unqualified product verdicts.

If used:
- attribute them to MAIA as stance;
- ground them;
- or make them member language.

Good:
> **I find the restraint here powerful because the sentence suddenly shortens.**

Not:
> **This is a powerful passage.**

---

# 9. Frequency Language

Preferred:

- Often
- Sometimes
- Once or twice
- Not present

Use counts where factual.

Avoid:

- Strong presence
- Weak presence
- High
- Low

unless they literally refer to a defined quantity.

---

# 10. Provenance Language

Use stable member-facing provenance.

## Member text
> **IN YOUR TEXT**

## Member-declared
> **YOU NAMED THIS**

## Member-selected template
> **YOU CHOSE THE SPIRAL TEMPLATE**

## MAIA observation
> **MAIA NOTICED THIS**

## Historical MAIA observation
> **MAIA NOTICED THIS IN AN EARLIER VERSION**

Do not collapse these.

---

# 11. Capability Language

Say only what the product can actually do.

Good:
> **Read this chapter with MAIA**

if chapter reading exists.

Avoid:
> **Explore your whole manuscript**

if whole-Work reading does not exist.

Good:
> **Open this passage**

Avoid:
> **Jump to source**

if source semantics are not implemented.

---

# 12. Intent-First Language

Primary question:

> **What are you trying to understand?**

Allowed intents:

- Something feels off
- Help me see the shape
- I'm losing the thread
- This doesn't sound like me
- I'm not sure what this chapter is doing
- Show me what keeps appearing
- I want to go deeper here
- I'm worried I'm repeating myself
- I don't know — help me look

Avoid:
- Choose analysis type
- Select developmental lens
- Pick an editorial diagnostic
- Start evaluation

---

# 13. "I Don't Know"

When the member chooses:

> **I don't know — help me look**

MAIA should not pretend to know the problem.

Good:
> **I can start with what is already visible in this chapter.**

If insufficient evidence:
> **I don't have enough of this chapter in view to say anything useful yet. I can read it with you if you'd like.**

---

# 14. Write Language

Prefer:

- Keep writing
- Continue here
- Open Contents
- Ask MAIA
- Hold this passage
- Read in context
- View History

Avoid:
- Execute
- Run
- Process
- Optimize
- Enhance
- Improve
- Generate

unless describing an actual technical action in developer/admin UI.

---

# 15. Develop Language

Prefer:
> **See more of your Work**

Supporting:
> **Follow patterns, structure, voice, and continuity through the manuscript.**

Avoid:
> **Developmental analysis dashboard**

or:
> **Run developmental diagnostics**

---

# 16. Review Language

Prefer:
> **Return to what you've noticed**

Supporting:
> **See observations, follow them back into the manuscript, and decide what you want to explore next.**

Avoid:
- Review score
- Writing health
- Readiness score
- Quality report
- Priority issues

---

# 17. MAIA Invitation Language

Prefer:
- Want to look at those places together?
- Stay here or see the earlier passage?
- Want the craft language for this?
- Want to try another possibility?
- Keep your original?
- Want me to read those two chapters?

Avoid:
- You should
- You need to
- I recommend
- Best practice
- Fix this
- Correct this

unless the member explicitly requests prescriptive guidance and the claim remains appropriately bounded.

---

# 18. Scope Language

MAIA names scope in plain words.

Good:
> **I can read this chapter and the one before it to see whether the thread carries through.**

Then:
- Yes, read those
- Just this chapter
- Not now

Avoid:
> **Execute cross-chapter continuity analysis**

---

# 19. Facet Language

## Guided
> **Help me shape this with you.**

## Learning
> **Help me write — and teach me as we go.**

## Direct
> **Give me the detail without extra explanation.**

Supporting line:
> **This changes how MAIA explains things, not how much control she has over your Work.**

---

# 20. Revision Language

Proposal labels describe direction.

Allowed:
- More embodied
- Quieter ending
- More direct
- More lyrical
- Less explanatory
- Closer to your cadence
- Keep my original

Avoid ordinal ranking:
- Option 1 / Best
- Recommended
- Strongest
- Improved version

Numbering is acceptable only as neutral navigation if it does not imply rank, but descriptive labels are preferred.

---

# 21. Apply Language

Before Apply:
> **Read in context**

Then:
> **Apply this revision**

After:
> **Revision applied to this passage.**

Actions:
- Undo this change
- View History
- Keep writing

Avoid:
> **Success!**

> **Great choice!**

> **Upgrade complete**

---

# 22. Undo Language

After Undo:

> **Your previous wording is back.**

Optional:
> **The revision is still in History if you want to revisit it.**

Avoid:
> Reverted successfully

unless developer-facing.

---

# 23. History Language

History should read like authored change, not source control.

Prefer:
- Before
- After
- Applied
- Undone
- Restored

Avoid:
- commit
- diff
- rollback
- patch
- mutation

in member-facing UI.

---

# 24. Stale Reading Language

Primary:

> **This chapter has changed since MAIA last read it.**

Support:
> **MAIA read this version on Sep 14. You changed the chapter on Sep 18.**

Actions:
- Read this chapter again
- See what changed
- Not now

After acknowledgment:

> **MAIA's findings are based on an earlier version.**

Actions:
- View changes
- Read again

Avoid:
- stale cache
- outdated analysis
- invalid result

---

# 25. Reading Freshness Language

## Current
> **Based on the current version**

## Partially stale
> **Some passages changed after this reading**

## Stale
> **Based on an earlier version**

## Unknown
> **Writer's Studio can't confirm whether this reading matches the current version**

No stronger claim.

---

# 26. Coverage Language

Prefer:

> **MAIA read Chapters 1–6.**

> **This observation uses passages in Chapters 2 and 6.**

> **MAIA hasn't read Chapter 8 for this question.**

Avoid:
- coverage 62%
unless the percentage is genuinely meaningful and useful.

---

# 27. Empty State Language

### Nothing found
> **MAIA read this chapter for continuity and didn't find a break she could support with evidence.**

### Not read
> **MAIA hasn't read this chapter for continuity yet.**

### Appears once
> **This appears only here so far.**

### Not enough coverage
> **MAIA hasn't read enough of the Work to answer that yet.**

Do not collapse to:
> No results

---

# 28. Error Language

Error copy answers:

1. what failed;
2. whether the Work changed;
3. what the member can do.

Examples:

> **MAIA couldn't finish that reading. Your Work was not changed.**

> **Writer's Studio couldn't open that passage. You're still in Chapter 6.**

> **This passage changed since this possibility was created. Read it in context again before applying.**

Avoid:
- Oops!
- Something went wrong
- Error 502
- Please try again later

unless no better information exists.

---

# 29. Offline / Save Language

Use exact state:

- Saving…
- Saved
- Not saved yet
- Offline
- Reconnecting…

If offline persistence is not guaranteed:

> **You're offline. Your latest change is not confirmed saved yet.**

Never show:
> Saved

without durable confirmation.

---

# 30. Persistence Language

For session-only state:
> **Available in this session**

if the distinction matters.

For durable:
> **Saved with this passage**

if true.

Do not imply durability from visual persistence alone.

---

# 31. Returning Language

Use only persisted facts.

Allowed:
> **Welcome back to The River Between.**

> **You last worked in Chapter 6.**

> **You left this question with this passage.**

Not allowed without evidence:
> **You were struggling with this transition.**

> **You were trying to solve the ending.**

---

# 32. Navigation Language

Prefer explicit returns:
- Back to Review
- Back to manuscript
- Back to Continuity Map
- Return to Chapter 6

Avoid generic:
- Back

when relationship matters.

---

# 33. Related Passage Language

When following relation:

> **You followed this here from Chapter 6.**

Possible explanation:
> **MAIA connected these passages because the phrase "the current" appears in both.**

Avoid:
> Related because relevant

---

# 34. Visualization Language

Measured:

> **Appears in 4 chapters**

Interpretive:

> **MAIA sees a possible shift here**

Never label an inference:
> **Trend**

unless it is actually measured.

---

# 35. Reader Perspective Language

Always hypothetical.

- A reader might...
- This could...
- One possible reader effect...
- Someone reading quickly may...

Avoid:
- Readers will...
- This is confusing...
- This lands well...

unless the member uses that language and MAIA is reflecting it.

---

# 36. Praise Language

Ground it.

Better:
> **I love the compression here — the paragraph drops from long sentences to six words.**

Avoid:
- Amazing
- Brilliant
- Perfect
- Excellent
- Gorgeous

as generic reinforcement.

Writer's Studio is not a praise machine.

---

# 37. Disagreement Language

When member says:
> I disagree.

Prefer:

> **Then let's keep your reading primary. I was responding to [evidence], but that doesn't establish what you meant.**

or:

> **Tell me what feels wrong in my reading.**

Avoid:
> Let me explain why I'm right.

---

# 38. Uncertainty Language

Use explicit epistemic markers:

- I notice...
- I read this as...
- It may...
- One possibility...
- I can't establish...
- I don't have enough evidence...
- This doesn't tell me whether...

Avoid hedging everything.

Facts can be stated plainly when factual.

---

# 39. Refusal / Refrain Language

Useful:

> **I don't have enough evidence to say that yet.**

> **I can stay with the passage rather than invent an answer.**

> **I haven't read enough of the Work to compare those chapters.**

Avoid:
> I cannot assist with that request

unless actually required by policy/system constraints.

---

# 40. Privacy / Trust Language

Plain, factual.

Good:
> **MAIA will not read more of your Work until you ask.**

only if technically true.

Good:
> **This observation is saved with your Work.**

only if true.

No soft promises unsupported by implementation.

---

# 41. Technical Terms

Technical terms may appear where useful, especially in Direct or Learning.

When first introduced in Guided:
- translate them.

Example:

> **The chapter shifts from scene into reflection — sometimes called a change in narrative mode.**

Not:
> There is a modal transition.

---

# 42. Avoid Internal Vocabulary Leakage

Do not expose internal terms unless intentionally productized:

- locus
- seam
- admission
- mutation
- canonical
- provenance object
- falsifier
- substrate
- machine phase
- epistemic state
- provider routing

Use member language.

---

# 43. Avoid AI Theater

Avoid:

- powered by advanced AI
- intelligent assistant
- analyzing deeply
- thinking
- reasoning engine
- magic
- co-author

MAIA is understood through what she actually does.

---

# 44. Avoid Therapy Drift

Unless a separate experience explicitly calls for therapeutic language, avoid:

- healing your writing
- processing trauma
- safe space
- inner child
- therapeutic journey

Writer's Studio may hold intimate material without claiming therapy.

---

# 45. Avoid Gamification Language

Never:
- streak
- XP
- level up
- badge
- achievement
- quest
- unlock
- score
- points
- progress reward

Curiosity is enough.

---

# 46. Tone by Context

## Work / manuscript
quiet, spacious.

## Navigation
short, literal.

## MAIA
relational, evidence-aware.

## Error
calm, specific.

## Trust
plain, factual.

## Teaching
clear, alive, not academic unless desired.

## Revision
precise, nonjudgmental.

---

# 47. Button Verb Law

Buttons should begin with meaningful verbs where natural.

Good:
- Keep writing
- Read again
- Go to passage
- Show me
- Add observation
- Keep with this passage
- Read in context
- Apply this revision
- Undo this change
- View History

Avoid vague:
- Continue
- Submit
- Confirm
- Proceed

when a more specific action exists.

---

# 48. Confirmation Dialog Law

Use confirmation only for consequential or irreversible actions.

Example:

> **Remove this observation?**
>
> It will no longer appear with this Work.

Actions:
- Remove observation
- Keep it

Do not ask:
> Are you sure?

without explaining consequence.

---

# 49. Labels Should Not Carry Hidden Judgment

A label such as:
> More embodied

describes a proposal direction.

It must not imply:
> current version is insufficiently embodied.

A label such as:
> Continuity

describes a way of looking.

It must not imply:
> continuity problem exists.

---

# 50. Empty Space Language

Do not fill silence with inspirational copy.

If nothing needs to be said:
> say nothing.

Whitespace is preferable to:
> Keep going — you're doing great!

---

# 51. Mobile Copy

Mobile may shorten without changing meaning.

Desktop:
> **This chapter has changed since MAIA last read it.**

Mobile:
> **Changed since MAIA read it**

Acceptable only if expanded state retains full meaning.

Do not shorten away:
- provenance;
- stale status;
- uncertainty;
- authority.

---

# 52. Accessibility Copy

Controls need understandable accessible names.

Examples:
- `Close MAIA`
- `Open Contents`
- `Return to Review finding 3 of 4`
- `Open Chapter 6 passage`

Do not rely only on icon title.

---

# 53. Cross-Mode Vocabulary

Stable across Write / Develop / Review:

- Work
- Chapter
- Passage
- Observation
- Question
- Possibility
- MAIA
- Review
- Develop
- History
- Related
- What MAIA Read
- Read in context
- Keep my original

Do not invent synonyms per room.

---

# 54. Acceptance Gates

## L1 — AGENCY
Language leaves decisions with the member.

## L2 — PROVENANCE
Speaker/source is clear.

## L3 — NON-GRADING
No hidden quality verdicts.

## L4 — CAPABILITY HONESTY
Copy does not promise unbuilt behavior.

## L5 — SCOPE CLARITY
Read scope is understandable.

## L6 — UNCERTAINTY
Interpretation is not phrased as fact.

## L7 — FAILURE TRUTH
Errors state what happened and whether Work changed.

## L8 — PERSISTENCE TRUTH
Saved/session-only language matches reality.

## L9 — HUMAN INTENT
Entry language does not require professional terminology.

## L10 — RETURN LANGUAGE
Back/return controls name the relationship.

## L11 — CROSS-MODE CONSISTENCY
Core terms do not drift.

## L12 — NO INTERNAL LEAKAGE
Implementation vocabulary stays out of member UI.

## L13 — NO AI THEATER
MAIA is described through actual behavior.

## L14 — NO GAMIFICATION
No engagement mechanics disguised as language.

## L15 — ACCESSIBLE LABELING
Control names remain understandable without visual context.

`Your voice is strong`

`Recommended revision`

### D-L4
`Readers will be confused`

### D-L5
`Saved` shown before durable confirmation

### D-L6
`Home` active when no destination exists

### D-L7
`MAIA analyzed your entire manuscript` when she read two chapters

### D-L8
`Your strongest theme`

### D-L9
`Continue` where consequence is applying a revision

### D-L10
`You were struggling with this last time` without durable evidence

### D-L11
`We found no results` for not-read state

### D-L12
`Something went wrong` when exact failure is known

### D-L13
`Expert mode` for Direct

### D-L14
`Unlock deeper insights`

### D-L15
internal term `locus` appears in member UI

# 56. Human Language Test

Ask participants:

1. What do you think this button will do?
2. Does this sound like a judgment of your writing?
3. Whose observation is this?
4. Do you know what MAIA has actually read?
5. Does this wording make you feel pushed toward a choice?
6. Is anything too technical?
7. Is anything vague?
8. Does MAIA sound certain where she shouldn't?
9. Does the Studio sound like one product across Write / Develop / Review?
10. Is there any sentence you would rewrite in your own words?

Capture exact participant phrasing.

Their natural language is valuable design evidence.

---

# 57. Product Principle

> **Good product language reduces the amount of interpretation the member has to do about the software, leaving more attention for interpretation of the Work.**

---

# 58. Spirit of Soullab

The language should leave the member feeling:

> I know what this means.
>
> I know who is speaking.
>
> I know what MAIA actually saw.
>
> I know when something is only a possibility.
>
> I know what will happen if I click.
>
> I don't feel graded.
>
> I don't feel managed.
>
> I can stay with my own words.

**A deeper you. A more human world.**
