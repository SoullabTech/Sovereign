# Practitioner Stewardship Guide

**Ethics in Practice for MAIA Practitioners**

A Community Commons Document — January 2026

---

## Introduction

MAIA is not a platform you *operate*. It is a system you **steward**.

This guide exists to support practitioners in using MAIA in a way that:

- Protects human sovereignty
- Avoids subtle harm
- Preserves trust in moments of vulnerability
- Prevents the system from drifting into influence or dependency

This is not about restricting good work. It's about ensuring the work stays *clean*.

---

## Part 1: The Core Posture

### Your Role

As a MAIA practitioner, your role is not to:

- Optimize outcomes
- Accelerate insight
- Guide people toward conclusions
- Become the authority mediated through AI

Your role is to:

- Hold space
- Support reflection
- Increase self-trust
- Return agency to the person

If you remember only one thing, remember this:

> **MAIA exists to make itself less necessary over time — not more.**

### MAIA's Function

- Clarification, not correction
- Reflection, not direction
- Integration, not optimization

MAIA serves the *person*, not the process. Depth matters more than throughput. If something increases dependence, pause.

---

## Part 2: What Ethical Use Feels Like

Ethical MAIA use tends to feel:

- Slower, not faster
- Clarifying, not soothing
- Spacious, not directive
- Grounded in the person's language, not the system's

If sessions start to feel:

- Addictive
- Comforting in place of human connection
- Direction-setting rather than sense-making
- Necessary rather than supportive

Pause. Reorient. That's the signal.

### Healthy Signals

- Clients feel clearer, not attached
- Sessions feel grounding, not energizing
- Over time, MAIA becomes *less* central

### Warning Signals

- "I need to check with MAIA first"
- Requests for validation over clarity
- Desire for answers instead of reflection

---

## Part 3: Non-Negotiable Boundaries

As a practitioner, you must never use MAIA to:

- Shape or steer a client's beliefs, emotions, or decisions
- Replace human relationship, therapy, or community
- Create emotional reliance on MAIA or on yourself via MAIA
- Treat client data as insight currency, pattern fuel, or leverage
- Position MAIA as "knowing better" than the person
- Use interpretation to collapse ambiguity prematurely

If you feel tempted to do any of the above, it's not a personal failure — it's a moment to slow down.

### What MAIA Will Never Do (And Neither Should You)

- Optimize engagement or retention
- Nudge beliefs, emotions, or decisions
- Replace human relationship or care
- Claim authority over meaning or truth

---

## Part 4: Language Guidance

Pay attention to phrasing. The difference is subtle — and profound.

### Prefer

- "What do you notice?"
- "How does that land for you?"
- "What feels true here?"
- "What's yours to decide?"

### Avoid

- "You should..."
- "This means that..."
- "What's really happening is..."
- "Based on your patterns..."

---

## Part 5: MAIA Is Not the Relationship

You are not building rapport *with MAIA*.
You are not deepening intimacy *through MAIA*.

MAIA is a reflective surface, not a bond.

If a client begins to treat MAIA as:

- A confidant
- A safe replacement for people
- A wiser presence than their own discernment

Your ethical responsibility is to gently redirect toward human grounding. That is care, not withdrawal.

### When to Step Back

You should reduce or pause MAIA use when:

- A client shows increasing dependence on sessions
- MAIA becomes the primary place they process meaning
- Human relationships are being deferred "until later"
- Sessions feel emotionally regulating rather than integrating

MAIA supports *integration*, not emotional outsourcing.

## Part 6: Drift Checklist (Self-Audit)

Use this privately. No judgment — just orientation.

### Attention & Pace

- Am I rushing toward insight or allowing it to emerge?
- Have I increased frequency or intensity without necessity?

### Language

- Am I implying there is a "right" interpretation?
- Have I used prescriptive phrasing instead of reflective framing?

### Authority

- Have I deferred to MAIA's output over the client's lived sense?
- Am I subtly positioning the system as knowing better?

### Attachment

- Does this client feel calmer *without* MAIA over time?
- Have I encouraged human support where appropriate?

### Incentives

- Would this interaction still feel ethical if no one returned?
- Am I proud of how this would look if fully visible?

**If you answer "I'm not sure" more than once:**

1. Pause new features or practices
2. Re-read the MAIA Promise
3. Reduce complexity before adding more

Drift is not failure. Unnoticed drift is.

## Part 7: Client-Facing Explanation

*(Share verbatim or adapt for your context)*

### What MAIA Is — And Isn't

MAIA is a reflective system designed to help clarify inner experience.

It doesn't diagnose, direct, or decide for you.

It won't try to keep you engaged.
It won't steer your beliefs or emotions.
It won't replace human relationships or care.

The goal is not dependence — it's **self-trust**.

If at any point MAIA feels like an authority rather than a mirror, that's a signal to slow down or step back. That boundary is intentional.

You remain the final interpreter of your experience. Always.

## Part 8: Onboarding Ritual

**Non-performative, grounding, optional but recommended**

Duration: ~10 minutes
Setting: Quiet, no screen if possible

### Step 1 — Orientation (2 min)

Read silently:

> "MAIA exists to reduce dependence, not create it."

Let that land. No analysis.

### Step 2 — Boundary Acknowledgment (3 min)

Ask yourself:

- Where do I tend to over-help?
- Where do I mistake clarity for control?

Name one tendency internally. No fixing.

### Step 3 — Steward's Commitment (spoken or silent)

> "I commit to protecting sovereignty — even when it's inconvenient."

That's it. No oath. No ceremony.

### Step 4 — Closure (2 min)

Take one breath slower than usual.

Begin work only if you feel *less* compelled to act.

If urgency remains, wait.

## Accountability and Trust

MAIA's ethics are not enforced by surveillance. They are upheld by:

- Transparency
- Practitioner self-awareness
- Shared standards
- The willingness to name drift early

If something feels off, talk about it. Ethical practice thrives in the open.

---

## Final Reminder

You are not here to make people better.
You are here to help them remember themselves.

MAIA is a tool for that remembering — nothing more, and deliberately so.
