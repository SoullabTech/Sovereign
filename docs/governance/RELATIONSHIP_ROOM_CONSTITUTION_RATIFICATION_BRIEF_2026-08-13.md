## 1. Each article in plain language

**I — The Object.** The room is about *my relationship with this person*, not about the person. The
member is inside it as a participant, not outside it reading a file. Nothing may be arranged so the
member's own words appear as a fact about the other person.

**II — Relational Authorship.** The member, not the system, gets to say what condition their
relationship is in. Three kinds of statement — what the member declared, what MAIA observed, and what
a heuristic guessed — must stay visibly distinct, and a guess may never quietly become a declaration.
The member can correct or withdraw anything the system wrote, and may also decline to name anything
at all.

**III — The Between.** "The Between" is the member's own felt sense of what happens between them and
the other person. It is never a window into the other person's mind. The test: any sentence the room
shows must still be true when read as *"In my experience, …"* — otherwise it does not get shown.

**IV — Memory.** Material made inside a room belongs to that relationship. Relational memory has to
carry who said it, where it came from, how certain it is, and when. And what was never recorded
cannot be guessed at later: of 1,139 historical machine-written rows, 1,098 are unknowable and stay
unattributed forever.

**V — Worked and Held.** Some relationships are being actively worked (repair, contact, a hard
conversation); others are simply held (grief, remembrance, distance, completion). Only the member may
say which, it may never be inferred from silence or elapsed time, and no relationship owes the
software a next step.

**VI — Condition of Being.** A relationship has a *condition* — living, distant, ruptured, ended,
unknown — not a score, stage, or rank. Conditions may not be ranked or compared across
relationships, and the system may not derive one. *"I don't know"* is a real answer, not a blank
field.

**VII — Change, Ending and Death.** The room has to stay dignified when things get worse, end, or
when the person dies. Silence is never treated as unfinished business, forward movement is never
required, deleting must not be the only way to close something, and no screen may ask whether things
have changed.

**VIII — MAIA.** The hearth is the member speaking; MAIA is company at the fire, not the fire — the
room must still mean something with MAIA switched off. She may witness, remember, reflect, help
prepare a message, accompany grief. She may not adjudicate, diagnose the other person, claim to know
their inner state, or require movement. Asked *"should I contact them?"*, she visibly declines the
verdict and hands back the member's own earlier words.

**IX — Third-Party Sovereignty.** A person remembering their own marriage privately is entitled to
that memory; a machine asserting a diagnosis or motive about that spouse is a different act
entirely. Consent is the wrong frame — nobody should have to make their spouse open an account to
reflect on their own life.

**X — System Containers.** A bucket the software invented to hold things it could not sort out is
the software's own unfinished work, and must never appear among the member's list of people. Today
29 of 44 production rows are exactly that.

**XI — Time.** A relationship should become *more* representable as years accumulate, not less. No
quiet truncation, no dateless history, no record that just stops without saying where. The system may
show *when* and *how often* the member wrote — never *which period mattered*.

**XII — The Soul Test.** Every article is checked against eight specific people, including an
estranged parent, a teenage child, and someone who has died. Good for six and harmful to two is a
fail. Two articles were already rewritten under this test, and the rewrites are recorded rather than
hidden.

**The Promise (unnumbered closing).** A member-facing paragraph promising, in the present tense, that
the room returns their own words first, never tells them who the other person is, and never asks them
to make progress.

## 6. The rupture question

**The production fact.** `member_relational_signals.rupture_state` holds **44 `ruptured`** and **53
`strained`**, most recent **today (2026-08-13)**. Two schema facts anchor what that means
(code-read, stated as such, no evaluation offered):
`database/migrations/20260409000010_member_relational_signals.sql:49` constrains
`source TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (source IN ('maia_conversation', 'labtool_manual'))` — **there is no
value in which a member declaration could be recorded** — and
`lib/relationships/relationshipSignalService.ts:168` hardcodes `source: 'maia_conversation'` on the
detection write path. Every one of the 97 rows is therefore system-authored by construction.

**These rows are not the frozen historical corpus.** Lane B's 1,139 entries are explicitly
*"written before sanctuary containment was enforced"* (`B-corpus-classification.md:378`) and are
treated throughout the 2026-08-10 audits as a closed past. A `ruptured` row written **today** is the
same act, live.

### The clauses that make an autonomously-written `ruptured` judgement problematic

**Article II — BOUNDARY (primary):**

> **"An observation or an inference may never silently become a declaration. The system may not
> author into the member's relational record any statement of relational condition that the member
> has not made. That MAIA today has a write path for *rupture* which no member-facing surface offers
> is an inversion of authorship, stated here as a constitutional fault"**

The Constitution already names this exact write path, in the exact article that would forbid it. It
is the only place in the document where a live behaviour is called a *constitutional fault* by name.

**Article II — PRINCIPLE:**

> *"The member holds primary authority to name the condition of their own relationship. Three sources
> of statement exist and must remain distinguishable at all times: **member declaration** … **MAIA
> observation** … and **system inference** (a heuristic classification with a confidence value)."*

The `source` CHECK admits only two of the three, and the missing one is member declaration.

**Article VI — BOUNDARY:**

> **"Conditions may not be derived by the system."**

`ruptured` and `strained` are condition-of-being words: both appear in founder ruling R5's own
illustrative list (*"new · growing · close · ordinary · changing · distant · **strained · ruptured** ·
estranged · ended · gone"*, audit §15.1). Under VI they are member-declarable and not system-derivable.

**Article III — BOUNDARY (the render gate):**

> *"every sentence rendered in this room must survive being read aloud with the prefix 'In my
> experience, …' — or it must not be rendered."*

A stored `rupture_state = 'ruptured'` carrying a machine confidence does not survive that prefix. The
clause governs rendering, so it bites the moment such a row reaches a surface — which the FSA reports
it currently does not, and which the UNIT's seam work would change.

**Article II — IMPLICATION (correctability):**

> *"the member must be able to correct or withdraw what the system wrote about their relationship."*

The FSA records withdrawal as *"wholly absent"* — *"not a wiring gap," "no column in which withdrawal
could be recorded."* Ratifying II therefore creates a standing obligation against 97 existing rows.

**Article IX — weaker, noted for completeness.** IX bars *"machine claims about a third party's
psychology… at any confidence value."* Whether `ruptured` is a claim about the **relationship** (II/VI
territory) or about **the other person** (IX territory) is genuinely arguable and this brief does not
resolve it. The Constitution's own correction-of-record leans toward the first: *"no name is inferred,
no bond type is stored… What the machine contributes is detection, classification and confidence —
not characterization of another human."*

⛔ **No remedy is proposed here, and none is implied.** Note only that this is *live behaviour*, not
historical residue, and that Articles II, III and VI each reach it independently.

## 7. Decision list

Six items. Each is one question, with a recommended ruling and its reasoning. Everything else in the
document is either founded or uncontroversial and is disposed of in the closing line.

---

**① Scope "that room" in Article IV, and decide whether a Sanctuary clause enters before ratification.**
*(The reconciliation's formal `R3` blocker, §7 D-1.)*

**Recommended ruling:** scope it **BROAD** — Article IV reaches observer-generated relational material
from MAIA conversation, not only material typed inside a Relationship Room surface — and admit the
Sanctuary text **as an appended paragraph to Article IV's BOUNDARY, not as a new numbered article.**

**Reasoning:** broad is the only reading under which IV's own BOUNDARY is coherent, since that
BOUNDARY discusses precisely the 1,139 observer rows that the narrow reading would place outside the
article; and appending rather than inserting avoids renumbering an article set that shipped code
(`enforceArticleIIIConversational`) and three governance documents already cite by number.

---

**② Does ratifying Article VIII unamended pre-authorize the severed read seam (FSA RU-4)?**

**Recommended ruling:** **No** — adopt the reconciliation's PR-3 at Article VIII, separating the two
authorities, and leave RU-4 expressly open.

**Reasoning:** VIII's grant to *"remember … surface the member's own history"* read alongside IV's
*"belongs to"* is a plausible downstream reading of pre-authorization for the exact seam the FSA
reserved to the founder; separating persistence authority from conversational availability settles
the ambiguity without deciding the reserved question in either direction.

---

**③ Article IV is the one NEW INTERPRETATION article and it is load-bearing. Adopt its belonging rule as law, or hold it as a direction pending its own unit?**

**Recommended ruling:** **adopt**, with the reconciliation's PR-2 wording change (*"preserves"* →
*"**must** preserve"*, plus the note that the present schema does not).

**Reasoning:** Articles X and XI have no custody rule to rest on without it, so holding IV would
hollow two articles that are otherwise sound; PR-2 removes the present-indicative phrasing that
currently reads as a description of a system property the FSA proves absent.

---

**④ Does the unnumbered Promise ratify with Articles I–XII, or is it held out?**

**Recommended ruling:** **hold it out.** Ratify Articles I–XII; keep the Promise as proposed
member-facing copy pending the behaviour it describes.

**Reasoning:** it states in the present tense (*"you will find your own words first"*) behaviour that
Article II simultaneously records as presently inverted; under `MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md` a
ratified promise is an outward claim about today, and the line *"We do not tell tomorrow's story as if
it were today's"* applies directly.

---

**⑤ Does `RELATIONSHIP_MEMORY_V1.md` §8's canon-level portability invariant extend to Relationship Rooms?**

**Recommended ruling:** **yes** — record it as a clause under Article IV rather than as a new article.

**Reasoning:** the principle is already founder-ruled for the sibling relational object and the
synthesis independently verified export absent here; but extending a canon-level invariant across two
distinct constitutional objects changes what the system owes members, which is a governance act and
not an implementer's call — hence it is asked rather than assumed.

---

**⑥ Must the Constitution and its evidentiary basis be reachable from trunk before ratification?**

**Recommended ruling:** **yes** — land the Constitution, the FSA, its mandate, and the
`docs/design/reviews/` tree containing the ratified R1–R9 synthesis on trunk as a precondition of the
ratifying act.

**Reasoning:** all four remain untracked today (re-verified 2026-08-13, three days after the
reconciliation flagged it), so ratified canon would depend for its existence on one uncommitted
feature branch; the reconciliation states the principle but only as *"arguably"* and as a proposed
future unit, so it has never been ruled.
