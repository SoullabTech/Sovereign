## 0. Why this exists before the first sitting

The gate is the last **technical** boundary before a practitioner's sitting. This protocol is the first **human** boundary.

> **Do not bring a practitioner into a system whose first act of receiving their wisdom depends on remembering a caution.**

The first recording becomes the canonical example. Whatever we do the first time is what the second practitioner inherits — including the mistakes. So the protocol is written before the first sitting, not derived from it.

⭐ **A note on what the first sitting actually is.** No Class A (practitioner-authored) artifact has been located anywhere searched. The sitting does not *retrieve* primary material — **it creates the first primary artifact that exists.** The raw recording is not a step toward the source. It *is* the source. Handle it accordingly.

## 1. Before recording

### 1.1 Consent to record

Explicit, verbal, on the recording itself. Not a form. Not implied by showing up.

### 1.2 Purpose, stated plainly

> "This conversation helps us understand your work and how you want MAIA to support it. **Nothing becomes part of your field without your explicit approval.**"

That second sentence is a promise the architecture must be able to keep. It is keepable **only** because the corpus gate refuses by default (§5, and see the deploy note in §7).

### 1.3 Ownership boundaries — asked, not assumed

Three questions, answered before recording starts:

- **What may be captured?**
- **What may remain private?**
- **What requires later review before anyone acts on it?**

⚠️ A fourth, easy to skip and important: **what in this conversation belongs to someone else?** Teachers, trainings, books, clients. The practitioner can license their own work. They cannot license the text that shaped them, and they cannot license their clients' material at all.

### 1.4 What we do not promise

Do not say the material will be used. Do not describe a timeline. Do not say "MAIA will learn this." The honest frame is: *we are recording so that you can later decide.*

---

## 2. During recording

### Capture

- raw audio
- date / time
- participants
- context
- purpose

### ⛔ Do not summarize during the conversation.

A live summary is an interpretation performed without review, and it will be the thing everyone remembers instead of what was said.

### ⛔ Do not translate their language into platform categories.

No "so that's your framework layer," no "that maps to our Practice Model." **Preserve the practitioner's own words, including the ones that don't fit.** The words that don't fit are the most valuable data in the room — they are where our ontology is wrong.

⭐ This is Invariant 14 (cultural sovereignty) applied to a conversation instead of a UI: *are we translating the practitioner's meaning into our vocabulary?*

### ⛔ Do not ask them to fill in our structure.

Not *"here are the categories, put your work into these boxes."* The sitting discovers structure; it does not confirm ours.

## 3. After recording — the derivation chain

```text
Raw recording          ← the SOURCE
      ↓
Transcript             ← derived
      ↓
Source classification  ← derived
      ↓
Practitioner review    ← the human act
      ↓
Approved field contribution
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **This chain must never reverse.**

The raw recording is the source. The transcript is derived. The summary is derived. The model is derived. **A derived artifact never acquires the authority of the thing it was derived from** — and the one prior incident in this system is precisely that reversal: a Soullab-authored candidate document carrying a header claiming it was *"composed in full from Larry Closs's program documents."* Correct on authorship, false on relationship.

### Handling rules

- The raw recording is **immutable** and hash-anchored at capture.
- Every derived artifact **names its parent** with a resolvable pointer — not a description of a parent.
- Every derived artifact is **`still exploring` / not composable** until §4 moves it.
- ⛔ Recordings live **outside the repo**, alongside other unlicensed material (`/Users/soullab/Larry_Corpus/` precedent). Never committed.

## 4. The field status model

Today's states are technical: `uploaded · processed · reviewed · ratified · archived`, and `pending · warning · live`. They answer *"has the pipeline run?"* and *"is the form complete?"*

Neither answers the question that actually matters.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The danger is not a missing file. The danger is a file moving from *"we have something"* to *"the system may speak from this"* without a meaningful human transition.**

### The human status model

```text
Captured
   ↓        we have it, nobody has understood it
Understood
   ↓        someone can say what it is and where it came from
Practitioner reviewed
   ↓        THEY have seen it and confirmed it represents them
Authorized for MAIA use
   ↓        they have said MAIA may speak from it, with attribution
Authorized for collective contribution
            they have said it may leave their field
```

### Rules

| Rule | |
|---|---|
| Every transition is a **human act** | ⛔ never time-based, never accumulative, never inferred |
| Every transition is **recorded**: what moved, from what, when, by whom | |
| Every transition is **reversible** by the practitioner, at any time, without explanation | |
| **Captured → Understood is ours.** The rest is **theirs.** | We may never perform a transition on their behalf |
| MAIA may **show** what sits unadvanced | ⛔ MAIA may **not** say it is ready |

⚠️ **Authorized for MAIA use ≠ Authorized for collective contribution.** These are different consents and must not be bundled into one checkbox. A practitioner may want MAIA to work from their material with their own clients and never want it to enter a shared commons.

### Relationship to the technical states

The technical lifecycle stays — it tracks the pipeline. **It is not the gate.** `ratified` answers *"has someone looked at this?"*; only **Authorized for MAIA use** answers *"may the system speak from this?"* Two questions, two models, and the second is the one wired to composition.

## 6. Applying this to the first sitting

### What the sitting is for

Discover: **his language · his framework · his learning sources · his boundaries · his developmental needs.**

Note the last one. Under the developmental model, *"what do you still want to become as a practitioner?"* is a first-class question, not a courtesy. It is the input to Layer 2, and it is the question a repository-shaped product would never think to ask.

### What the sitting is not for

⛔ Not for confirming our categories. ⛔ Not for populating Attachment A live in the room. ⛔ Not for agreeing what MAIA will do with anything.

### ✅ Leave the field empty

His practice field stays empty through and after the sitting. **The first practitioner corpus must enter through the same gate every future practitioner uses** — not through a hand-placed exception made while the gate was still being built.

### Two things still open that the sitting can settle

- 🔴 The **five-domain language** currently live in `about_practice` on `now-what-demo` (512 chars, composing into every slug-resolved room). Verified present in production 2026-08-03. **Only he can say whether it represents his work.**
- The unsigned materials agreement and its empty Attachment A. §1 of that instrument governs: *"if it's not on the list, it's not in the system"* · *"nothing moves until both versions are signed."*
