## §3 The five required amendments

### A1 — `presence_value` has no positive value *(most mechanical defect)*

§1 requires every assertion to carry `presence_value`, never null. §7 permits only
`zero · none · unknown · stale · unobserved · not_applicable`. **There is no lawful value for
"this thing is currently present and evidenced"** — a normal healthy assertion cannot satisfy the
schema.

Minimum: add `present`. **Preferred: tighten the axes rather than append a seventh value** —
`stale` is temporal and `unobserved` is observational, neither is presence. The contract must make
explicit which field answers which question.

### A2 — §5 mistakes failure of observation for a kind of stillness

`completed`, `equilibrium`, `awaiting` are genuine stillness. `unobserved`, `stale`,
`not_applicable` describe the observer, evidence, time, or jurisdiction; `absent` describes
ontology. The contract currently permits `stillness_kind: unobserved` — but **if the system is
unobserved, JARVIS does not know that it is still.** That is the exact error the contract exists to
eliminate.

> **Only evidenced phenomenon states may receive a `stillness_kind`. Failure to observe a
> phenomenon is represented on the observation / presence / temporal axes and must never itself
> establish stillness.**

§5 retains `completed`, `equilibrium`, `awaiting`, perhaps `failed_silent`; observer/aperture
states move elsewhere. The required realities — not necessarily these field names:

```text
phenomenon condition  ×  observation condition
```

### A3 — M1 `emergence` contradicts the contract's own motion rule

§4 defines motion as a difference between **two** evidenced assertions; §4.2 repeats *"no motion
without two evidenced assertions"*; M1 defines `emergence` as the **first** assertion. These cannot
all hold. Worse, calling a first observation "emergence" commits the error the architecture exists
to prevent:

> **newly observed ≠ newly begun**

A first-ever assertion establishes first *observation*. It cannot establish that the phenomenon
emerged absent a prior competent assertion of its absence/nonexistence. Either M1 becomes **first
observation — not motion**, or `emergence` requires an earlier evidenced `none/absent` followed by
evidenced presence. **The founder strongly prefers preserving that distinction.**

### A4 — `stale` is assigned to an epistemic enum that does not contain it

§9 defines `established · provisional · emerging · ambiguous · contradicted · superseded ·
unknown` — no `stale`. Yet §4.1 drives `established → stale` and F2 requires
`epistemic_status → stale`. **Internal schema violation.**

Staleness belongs primarily to **temporal standing**. The old assertion may remain *established*
about what was true at its `valid_as_of`; it simply no longer establishes the present:

```text
temporal_status → stale
current-state claim → no longer established from this evidence
```

⛔ Do not casually add `stale` to epistemic status. The historic evidence remains real rather than
being epistemically downgraded into something it never was.

### A5 — `authority_blocked` must not redefine a governance pause as completion

§6.1's *"work stopped at an authority boundary is a **completion state**"* is too strong relative to
the governance architecture this contract declares itself downstream of. A governance boundary can
be a truthful stop without being terminal completion of the objective; some gates pause pending
legitimate authority.

> `authority_blocked` is a **non-fault stop condition**. It may be resumable, and must not be
> represented as either failure or completed objective unless the governing mechanism establishes
> that disposition.

Otherwise Living Spiral would quietly alter Run Provenance semantics while claiming not to amend
them.
