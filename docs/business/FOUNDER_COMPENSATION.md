# Founder Compensation: Setting Your Guaranteed Payment

This is the number that protects your family and nervous system.

---

## The Question

**What does baseline family stability cost per month?**

Not thriving. Not comfortable. Just: stable.

---

## How to calculate it

### Fixed costs (non-negotiable)

| Category | Monthly |
|----------|---------|
| Housing (rent/mortgage) | $______ |
| Utilities (electric, gas, water, internet) | $______ |
| Health insurance | $______ |
| Food (groceries, not dining out) | $______ |
| Transportation (car payment, insurance, gas OR transit) | $______ |
| Minimum debt payments | $______ |
| Kids (childcare, school, essentials) | $______ |

**Subtotal fixed:** $______

### Variable buffer (emergencies, unexpected)

Add 10-15% to fixed costs.

**Buffer:** $______

### Total baseline

**Fixed + Buffer = $______ /month**

---

## The rule

Once you have this number:

1. **Pay yourself this amount every month** (as guaranteed payment / owner's draw from the LLC)
2. **Pay it first** - before discretionary business expenses
3. **Don't skip it** - if revenue can't cover it, that's a signal to adjust the business, not your family's stability

---

## What this protects against

- "I'll pay myself later" (you won't)
- Business eating personal savings
- Burnout from financial stress on top of building stress
- Resentment from family members watching savings drain

---

## What this does NOT include

- Savings/retirement (add later when stable)
- Discretionary spending (vacations, entertainment)
- Business reinvestment
- Growth expenses

Those come from what's left after baseline is covered.

## How to pay yourself from CT LLC

For a single-member LLC:

- **Guaranteed payment** (like a salary but simpler) - regular draws from business account to personal
- Track it in your bookkeeping as "Owner's Draw" or "Guaranteed Payment"
- You'll pay self-employment tax on this (15.3% for SS/Medicare) plus income tax
- Set aside ~30% for taxes if you're not doing quarterly estimates

Talk to a CPA if you want to optimize this (some LLCs elect S-corp status to reduce SE tax, but that adds complexity).
