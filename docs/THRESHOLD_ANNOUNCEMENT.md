## For email or direct message (primary version)

**Subject:** Something I've been building. Wanted you to know first.

---

I've been building something for the past year and I want to tell you about it honestly before it becomes public.

It's called Soullab, and the short version is: it's sovereign infrastructure for people who hold others.

Therapists, coaches, facilitators, bodyworkers, grief workers, spiritual directors, educators — people whose job is to carry weight for other people, usually without much structural support of their own.

Most of them are running their practices on a patchwork of platforms that were designed for marketers, not helpers. Calendly. Kajabi. Mailchimp. Instagram. Tools that optimize for engagement, conversion, and scale — none of which have anything to do with care.

I didn't set out to build an alternative to those platforms. I set out to answer a simpler question: what would it look like if the tools a helper uses modeled the same ethics they aspire to?

Here's what exists now:

**MAIA** — a consciousness companion that holds space without claiming it. She remembers what you offer, forgets what you ask her to forget, doesn't optimize for engagement, and doesn't simulate intimacy she can't deliver. She runs on our own servers. No one sits between the person and their data.

**Soullab Studio** — practice management built for helpers. Session recording with real-time reflection. Client and group management. Tools for pacing your work, not scaling it.

**An ethical architecture** — publicly committed prohibitions against the things that make most platforms extractive. No credentialing. No leaderboards. No funnels. No gamification of care work. These aren't aspirations. They're structural constraints.

What's been missing is the front door.

---

**The Threshold** is a six-week passage for helpers who want to use this infrastructure.

It's not training. Not certification. Not onboarding.

It's a period of honest reflection about your relationship to helping — facilitated in small cohorts, supported by MAIA, with a live circle each week.

Six themes, one per week:

1. When not to help
2. What platforms have taught you
3. Containment vs. intervention
4. Who you serve and why
5. Sovereignty for those you serve
6. What you're willing to be held accountable for

No badge at the end. No credential. No title.

The only outcome is clarity — about whether this way of working fits, and what you're actually willing to commit to.

---

I'm inviting 12-24 helpers for the first cohort. Hand-picked. People who are already doing the work, already frustrated by the tools, and ready to look at their practice honestly.

If you know someone like that — or if this is you — I'd like to talk.

This isn't a launch. There's no urgency. The right people will come when they're ready, and some will decide it's not for them, and that's fine.

I'm sharing this with you because you've been close to this work, and I trust your judgment about who might belong here.

Kelly

## Notes on tone and distribution

**What this announcement is:**
- A personal invitation from you to people you trust
- Honest about what exists and what it isn't
- Clear that this is small, quiet, and intentional

**What this announcement is not:**
- A product launch
- A waitlist play
- A hype cycle
- Content marketing

**Distribution guidance:**
- Send the long version to your inner circle first (5-10 people)
- Wait for their responses before broadening
- The shorter versions are for people who already know you
- The social post should wait until the first cohort is confirmed
- Let people come to you. Don't chase.

**What to say when people ask "what is this?":**
> "Soullab is infrastructure for helpers — the people who hold other people.
> We built ethical AI, practice tools, and a community designed to protect
> their capacity rather than extract from it. The Threshold is how you enter."

**What to say when people ask "how is this different from [X]?":**
> "Most platforms are built to scale. We're built to last.
> We don't optimize for engagement. We don't issue credentials.
> We don't treat care work as a conversion pipeline.
> The tools model the ethics the helpers aspire to."

**What to say when people ask "what does it cost?":**
> "Pro membership is $35/month. That gives you MAIA, Studio, and access
> to the Threshold and Practitioner Commons. The Threshold itself is included.
> If cost is a barrier and the work is real, we'll figure it out."
