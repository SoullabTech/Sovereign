## The governing pattern

> **Familiar doorway. Different room.**

The outside must feel familiar — Clients · Sessions · Commitments · Notes · Timeline. The
inside is different — relationship continuity · agency preservation · developmental awareness
· reflective practice. **No new vocabulary at the door.** No "developmental environment," no
"field architecture," no "AI companion." Those are underneath.

### Same nouns, different behaviour

| Noun | Not | But |
|---|---|---|
| **Sessions** | meeting history | *what has happened in the relationship?* |
| **Commitments** | tasks assigned | *what we are tending together* |
| **Notes** | documentation | *what helps continuity* |
| **Timeline** | activity log | *the unfolding story of the work* |

## Prohibited affordances

The walk tests that these **do not appear**. Not because they are impossible — **because they
violate the governing relationship.**

- Progress %
- Completion percentage
- Rankings
- Scores
- "AI insights" presented as truth
- Diagnoses
- Suggested next steps framed as decisions

⭐ **This section fails silently.** A single progress affordance introduced as a convenience
defeats the acceptance question without producing an error.

⚠️ **Related, and load-bearing — pending the Correction 3 ruling:** if a Field declaration
were read as a *completion state* rather than an *act of relationship to material*, **"Keep"
becomes "done"** and the Home becomes a task manager disguised as reflection. That
distinction is not settled here; it is settled by the Correction 3 ruling, which has not been
made.

## The adoption test

Before showing Larry anything about architecture, ask:

> **Would Larry understand what this room is for without someone explaining the philosophy?**

That is the adoption test. Philosophy that must be explained at the door has already failed
it.

## The milestone this walk actually measures

> **Can Larry enter a familiar practice environment and experience that the system makes his
> work deeper without making itself the authority?**

⭐ The harder promise is not *"can Larry manage clients?"* — every CRM does that. It is
**"can a person return to their own development without feeling managed?"**

⭐⭐ **Absence of features is not unfinishedness.** The first version is powerful precisely
because it demonstrates the boundary: *Larry can accompany. The client can author. MAIA can
help. No one becomes the owner of another person's inner process.*
