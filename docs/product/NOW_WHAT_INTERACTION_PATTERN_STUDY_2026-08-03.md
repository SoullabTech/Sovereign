## 0. What this document is

Kelly's proposal: stop designing from abstract principles alone, and study proven interaction
patterns from adjacent categories — not to copy a platform, but to ask *"what did these products
solve about human orientation, trust, attention, and returning to meaningful work?"*

The proposal is sound and the synthesis is largely admissible. This document records it, and does
the one thing a pattern study must do here that a normal design review would not: **check each
borrowed pattern against the rulings already made**, because several of these patterns encode an
ownership model that AIN has explicitly ruled against.

The distinction that organizes the whole study:

> **A pattern is borrowable when it solves orientation. It is not borrowable when it also carries
> an ownership model.** Salesforce solves "who is this person" by making the person a record in the
> practitioner's system. Duolingo solves "what next" by computing it. Both solve real problems.
> Both encode an authority direction AIN has ruled the other way.

## 1. The synthesis, as authored

> AIN OS = Apple simplicity + Linear clarity + Salesforce relationship context + Airbnb trust +

Both stand. The second sentence is the load-bearing one, and §5 below is where it gets its teeth.

## 2. Pattern-by-pattern, with the constitutional check

| # | Source | What it solves | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Linear / Notion / Craft | Progressive disclosure — reduce cognitive load | ✅ with one hard exception (§2.1) |
| 2 | Apple | The interface disappears; the user feels capable | ✅ adopt directly |
| 3 | Salesforce / HubSpot | Person-centered, not module-centered | ◐ borrow the *center*, refuse the *record* (§2.3) |
| 4 | Headspace / Calm | The arrival moment | ◐ admissible only if arrival is authored, not observed (§2.4) |
| 5 | Airbnb | Trust through clear roles and clear authorship | ✅ strongest fit — it is Q-A in interface form |
| 6 | Duolingo | Return loops — the next step is obvious | ⛔ as drawn — collides with E-2 and the Continue prohibition (§2.6) |
| 7 | Figma | Shared canvas, clear authorship, history | ◐ borrow authorship, refuse symmetry (§2.7) |

### 2.1 Progressive disclosure — but authorship is never the thing disclosed

*"The intelligence is underneath"* is right about data volume and wrong if applied to attribution.

Q-A ratified: **the relationship is shared; the claims within it are separately authored, and every
claim carries its author.** If the author line is what progressive disclosure hides behind a tap,
the pattern quietly inverts the ruling — the surface then reads as the system's voice, and the
authorship becomes a detail you can go look up.

> **Rule: complexity is disclosable; authorship is not.** An unattributed claim is not a simplified
> claim, it is a different claim.

The Linear lesson survives intact in its real form: show fewer *objects*, not fewer *attributions*.

### 2.3 Salesforce — the person is the center, but not a record in Larry's system

The pattern to borrow is real and is already ruled in: a person-centered home rather than
`Programs / Sessions / Notes / Resources`. Q-B ratified that a returning person resumes *their own
material*, not "a program."

What does not come along is CRM's ownership model. In a CRM the contact **is** a record inside the
salesperson's account. In AIN the ownership map (Phase 0.5 §5) is generated by a different question:

> *Does this record exist because of a professional relationship, or because the person exists?*

The first gets a `relationship_id`; the second must never acquire one and shares only by creating a
**separate** object. Senja is not a row in Larry's caseload; she is a person the platform already
holds, with whom Larry has a relationship.

**The sharpest consequence for the pattern study:** the two homes are not one template.

### 2.3.1 ⛔ The client-page mockup, as drawn, is a projection

The proposed page —

```
SENJA · Your relationship with Larry
WHERE WE ARE · WHAT IS ALIVE · WHAT LARRY OFFERED · WHAT YOU ARE CARRYING · NEXT
```

— is excellent as **Senja's home**. It is not admissible as **Larry's client page**, and the header
(*"Your relationship with Larry"*) reads as Senja's while the placement in the study reads as
Larry's. That ambiguity is exactly the failure Q-A named: `Larry's model → client's reality` inverts
the Constitutional Direction of Authority.

Specifically, on Larry's side these three bands are ruled unreachable or unbuilt:

| Band | Constraint |
|---|---|
| *What you are carrying · your reflections* | `member_reflections` / `member_field_note_*` are **person-owned**, client-only. Larry must not see them absent an explicit share. Reaching them through a practitioner-scoped query is the exact move Invariant 1 forbids. |
| *What is alive* (Delegation · Confidence · Boundaries) | Nearest substrate is `coach_client_selected_focus` — **unreachable from any practitioner query** — and `coach_current_focus` (the focus in the client's words), which is ⛔ deferred. |
| *What Larry offered* (Practice · Reflection · Resource) | `coach_work_items`, `coach_note_publications`, `coach_resource_recommendations` are all ⛔ deferred, in the encrypted lane. Q-C: their absence is a **protected boundary, not a gap**, asserted by gate check `1d`. Creating them in a UI lane fails the gate by construction. |

**Correct form of the same idea:** one *model* (the relationship), two *views*, each rendering only
what its holder may see, and each labelling every claim with its author. Larry's client page is
mostly the relationship spine plus what Larry himself authored, plus **whatever Senja has elected to
share** — an election that is a separate consent object, not a visibility flag on her material.

### 2.4 The arrival moment — authored, never observed

Headspace's arrival is the right register, and the draft copy is close:

```
Good morning Larry.
8 active clients. Today: 3 conversations ahead · 2 clients have something waiting ·
1 reflection from your own practice
```

*"2 clients have something waiting"* is admissible **only** if "waiting" derives from an authored
act — a client published a share, affirmed a commitment, accepted an invitation. It is not
admissible if it derives from the client having been *active*.

E-3 ruled: **private sovereignty is silent.** No member-Field activity history is visible to anyone
else — *"that makes the platform an observation system."* An arrival line computed from activity is
precisely a rendered activity feed with the feed styling removed.

> **Test for any count on Larry's arrival screen: name the act and its author. If you cannot, the
> number is surveillance with good typography.**

### 2.6 ⛔ Return loops — Duolingo's mechanism is the one AIN ruled against

```
Continue your work
You left off here: Delegation practice
Next step: Reflect on your conversation with Sarah
```

Two collisions, both already ruled:

1. **E-2 — Band ① shows kept, not last.** *The member's gesture determines presence; time may
   describe, but not determine, prominence.* "You left off here" is ordering by recency.
   Recent ≠ important ≠ alive.
2. **The Continue prohibition.** Slice 0 forbids inferring progress from activity, scoring
   development, or turning Continue into a recommendation engine. *Continue lists **named doors**,
   never a computed suggestion.* "Next step: Reflect on your conversation with Sarah" is a computed
   suggestion, authored by the system, about a person's inner work.

**What survives:** Duolingo's actual insight is that *returning should not require a decision*. That
is the same finding as Sitting 001 — *"Home's job is to not lose the intention the member walked in
with."* The admissible form is **kept material, named in the member's own words, as doors** — momentum
without authorship theft.

### 2.7 Figma — authorship yes, symmetry no

*Relationship shared, claims separately authored* maps cleanly onto Figma's shared canvas with named
cursors and history. One hard difference: **co-equal ≠ symmetric write access.** The client never
moves their own stage; Larry never authors the client's focus. The existing precedent in code is
`field_program_positions.stated_by ∈ (member_confirmed, member_stated, practitioner_seeded)` with
`member_confirmed_at` NULL until the member's own gesture — a practitioner may *seed* a position;
only the member may *confirm* it.

So: borrow named authorship and visible history. Do not borrow the single canvas both parties edit.

## 3. The one pattern the study found and did not name

Kelly's own contrast is the most transferable artifact in the document:

```
Larry offered:                            versus       Insight:
"Try noticing where you avoid delegation."             Avoidance pattern detected.
[Save for session] [Discuss next time]
```

This is Airbnb's clear-roles pattern and Q-A's authorship ruling arriving at the same interface, from
two directions. It should be lifted out of the pattern study and treated as the **house rendering
rule for every claim on either surface**:

> **Every claim renders as: who said it · in their words · what you may do with it.**
> Never as a system-voiced finding.

Note also which one is shorter. The unattributed version is more compact *because* it dropped the
author — which is why compactness is not a neutral virtue here.

## 4. Where this study sits against the method already ruled

Two standing constraints apply to the study itself, not to its content:

1. **The unit of design is a moment**, not a screen or a product. This study is organized by
   *product* — a useful gathering pass, but the durable form converts each pattern into the moment
   it serves: **arriving · orienting · resuming · deciding · returning.** Recommend a second pass
   that re-files these seven patterns by moment before any of them reaches a sitting.
2. **Draw first, wire second.** These belong in discardable artifacts, not in a production surface,
   until a design survives a sitting. A wired surface costs revert + redeploy + walk to throw away,
   and that cost silently becomes an argument for keeping it.

## 5. What this does and does not change about the build order

**It does not change it.** Slice 0 remains the ruled next unit, and it is not a homepage:

> **Slice 0 is a trust-boundary demonstration, not a homepage with widgets.** Relationship line +
> kept material + selected focus. **The proof is a negative — the absence is the product.** Larry
> sees relationship, program and stage but **not** the focus, **not** private Field, and gets **no**
> withdrawal signal. Services before UI. No migration.

This study is the answer to *"what should it feel like once the boundary holds"*, and it is
premature as an answer to *"what do we build first."* Read in the right order, the two fit together
exactly — the north star's second sentence is literally describing the negative:

> The navigation should feel like software Larry already knows.
> The experience inside should feel like something he has never had before.

**The unfamiliar part is not a feature. It is the absence.** Larry has used CRMs where the client is
a record he can open. What he has never had is a workspace where the relationship is real, the work
is shared, and part of the person is structurally none of his business — and where that boundary is
legible to him as *integrity* rather than as *missing data*. That is the design problem the pattern
study should be pointed at next, because no adjacent product has solved it: every category studied
here assumes the operator can see everything in their own account.

## 6. Open questions (not answered here — per the method, each study ends with one)

- **Q-P1.** Larry's client page and Senja's home share a model and diverge in view. Are they one
  component with a viewer parameter, or two components? (Q-A says the *model* is shared; it does not
  rule the *implementation*. A viewer parameter is the classic place a projection re-enters.)
- **Q-P2.** How does a practitioner experience a structural absence without reading it as a defect?
  Silence is correct (E-3) and silence is also indistinguishable from "nothing happened." Naming the
  boundary to Larry may itself leak that there is something to know.
- **Q-P3.** Nav as drawn (`Programs · Sessions · Resources`) implies content tables that are
  deliberately deferred. Does nav get sized to what exists — per *"never show disabled verbs"* — or
  does the shape get declared ahead of the content?

**Q-P2 is the one to carry forward.** It is the only question in this document that no adjacent
product's pattern library can answer, and it is the hinge of the north star.
