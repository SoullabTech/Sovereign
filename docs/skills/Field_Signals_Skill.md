# Skill: Field Signals (Feedback Perception)

## Purpose

Read a body of feedback (emails, DMs, notes, app comments, support threads, beta tester responses) and surface what the field is actually telling you — not what it's literally saying, but where the system is working, where it's confusing, and where sovereignty is under pressure.

## Input

User provides:
- Raw feedback (pasted, linked, or described)
- Source context (beta testers / practitioners / public / internal)
- Timeframe (if relevant)

If source or context is missing, ask briefly before proceeding.

## Output Structure

### 1. Confusion Signals
Where mental models don't match the product reality.
- What users expected vs. what happened
- Language that's being misread
- Flows that create wrong assumptions

### 2. Trust Signals
Where resonance is increasing.
- What people return to
- What they quote or reference
- Where they relax, open up, or go deeper

### 3. Friction Signals
Where effort spikes unnecessarily.
- Steps that feel heavy or unclear
- Moments where people pause, retry, or abandon
- Technical friction vs. emotional friction (distinguish)

### 4. Boundary Signals
Where sovereignty feels threatened.
- Privacy concerns (stated or implied)
- Moments of "too much" — too personal, too fast, too presumptuous
- Places where the system feels like it's overreaching

### 5. Top 3 Fixes
Smallest safe changes that address the strongest signals.
- One per signal category (pick the three with highest leverage)
- Framed as experiments, not mandates

### 6. Language Patch
Specific words or phrases to rename, rephrase, or reframe based on what feedback reveals about how language is landing.

## Guardrails

- No over-interpreting. If signal is weak, say so.
- No pathologizing users. Describe behavior, not character.
- Distinguish between: one person's reaction vs. a pattern.
- Protect user identity — never quote in ways that expose individuals unless explicitly asked.
- Label confidence: "strong signal" / "emerging pattern" / "single data point."

## Tone

- Perceptive, not judgmental.
- Specific, not abstract.
- Oriented toward action, not analysis paralysis.
