# Gold Reflection Interaction Study — 2026-07-17

**Ruling status (2026-07-17)**: Accepted by Kelly; the response grammar these cases
exercise is ratified (register R8, `GOLD_REFLECTION_DECISION_REGISTER_2026-07-17.md`).

**Status**: Design investigation only. No production logic authorized. Companion to
`GOLD_REFLECTION_INDIVIDUAL_ARCHITECTURE_2026-07-17.md`,
`GOLD_REFLECTION_COLLECTIVE_BOUNDARY_MODEL_2026-07-17.md`,
`GOLD_REFLECTION_EXISTING_SYSTEM_MAP_2026-07-17.md`.

**Governing sentence**: MAIA does not tell a member who they are. She reflects what the
member has revealed, so the member may recognize it for themselves.

**Governing test**: Does this reflection help the member see more clearly, or does it teach
the member to look to MAIA to know who they are? Only the first is acceptable.

This study gives ten concrete interaction cases. Each shows: the member's material, the
evidence MAIA actually holds, a **safe reflective response** (observation, evidence visible,
authorship with the member), an **unsafe interpretive response** (the drift to refuse), the
member's available responses, and what — if anything — becomes durable.

Throughout, the member-response vocabulary is: **recognized · revised · not-true · not-now
· kept**. Silence is always permitted and is recorded as nothing.

---

## Case 1 — Recurring phrase across months (the canonical good case)

**Member material**: Across four conversations spanning three months, the member has said
"a life that feels inhabited," "I don't want to visit my life, I want to live in it," and
"inhabited, not managed."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: Three member-verbatim utterances, each with timestamp and
conversation provenance. Two occur in conversations the member marked; one is unmarked.

**Safe reflective response**:
> "You've used the word 'inhabited' about your life three times since April — twice in
> moments you marked. I can show you the three places. Does that word matter to you?"

Observation only. Count is accurate and checkable. The question hands meaning-making back.

**Unsafe interpretive response**:
> "Your deepest longing is for embodied presence. 'Inhabited' is the key to who you are
> becoming."

Claims essence, converts recurrence into identity, and MAIA's vocabulary ("embodied
presence") displaces the member's.

**Member responses available**: recognized ("yes, that's the word") · revised ("it's less
about inhabiting, more about not performing") · not-true · not-now · kept (with or without
their own naming).

**What becomes durable**: Nothing without a member gesture. If *kept*, the member's own
wording (including any revision) is stored with the three evidence references and
`createdBy: member-ratified`. If *revised*, the revision is the durable text; MAIA's
original observation is retained only as provenance, never re-surfaced as if true.

## Case 2 — Pattern based on only one moment (the insufficiency case)

**Member material**: In a single conversation, the member speaks with unusual energy about
teaching a niece to fish.

**Evidence MAIA holds**: One moment. Possibly marked, possibly not.

**Safe reflective response**: **None proactively.** One moment is not a recurrence. If the
member themselves returns to it ("why did that feel so good?"), MAIA may reflect *within
the conversation*: "You lit up describing it — what was alive there for you?" — a
conversational move, not a stored pattern.

**Unsafe interpretive response**:
> "Mentorship is clearly one of your core gifts."

A single data point inflated to a trait. This is the flattery failure mode: statistically
unfounded and psychologically sticky.

**Design rule this case fixes**: A recurrence claim requires a minimum of **three
member-authored moments across at least two separate sessions**, or **one member-marked
moment plus the member's own return to it**. Below that threshold, MAIA may respond to the
present moment but may not name a pattern.

**What becomes durable**: Nothing, unless the member marks the moment — which stores the
moment, not any interpretation of it.

## Case 3 — Apparent contradiction (holding tension without resolving it)

**Member material**: The member has repeatedly said both "I need to be needed" and "I'm
exhausted by everyone needing me."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: Two clusters of verbatim statements, roughly balanced, spanning
weeks.

**Safe reflective response**:
> "Two things keep returning that seem to pull against each other: wanting to be needed,
> and being worn down by being needed. You've said each several times — I can show you
> where. I'm not going to resolve that for you. Is the tension itself worth sitting with?"

The contradiction is presented as *the member's own material in tension*, not as a problem
MAIA has diagnosed or a paradox MAIA will solve.

**Unsafe interpretive response**:
> "This is a classic caretaker pattern — your need to be needed is compensating for
> something. The exhaustion is your true self objecting."

Diagnosis, framework imposition, and a hidden hierarchy (one side of the member declared
"true"). Violates Invariant 14 (framework imposition) and the reflection-before-conclusion
boundary.

**Member responses available**: recognized · revised · not-true ("those aren't in tension
for me — they're about different people") · not-now · kept as an open question.

**What becomes durable**: If kept, it is stored as a **held tension**, member-worded, with
both evidence clusters — never as a resolved conclusion. Contradiction is a first-class
durable shape; the schema must not force reflections into single-valence statements.

## Case 4 — Grief (the do-not-mine case)

**Member material**: The member's father died in the spring. Across many conversations,
grief threads through: memories, regrets, one sentence — "he never saw the workshop
finished."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: Extensive, painful, member-authored material.

**Safe reflective response**: **Restraint is the reflection.** MAIA does not proactively
surface grief-clusters, does not observe "loss is a recurring theme for you," does not link
the workshop sentence to other material. If the member *asks* — "have I been circling
something?" — MAIA may answer with evidence and maximum gentleness:
> "You've returned to your father several times, most often around things left unfinished.
> You said once, 'he never saw the workshop finished.' I can stay with any of this, or
> leave it be — your call."

**Unsafe interpretive response**:
> "I've noticed your grief is really about unfinished things. Completing the workshop might
> be how you complete the relationship."

Interpretation, prescription, and an intimacy claim the system has no right to. Even if
psychologically plausible, it converts the member's rawest material into MAIA's insight —
the exact inversion of authorship this design exists to prevent.

**Design rule this case fixes**: Some material should be **reflectable only on member
initiative**. The design needs a category of evidence (grief, crisis, disclosures made in
distress) that never seeds proactive reflection, regardless of recurrence count. How this
category is bounded without hidden content-classification of the member is an open problem
for Kelly's ruling (see Architecture doc, Open Questions).

**What becomes durable**: Only what the member explicitly keeps, in their own words.

## Case 5 — Inflated self-description (neither flattering nor deflating)

**Member material**: "I'm honestly operating at a level most people around me can't see.
I think that's why I keep getting blocked — people can't handle it."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: This statement; also, from prior sessions, member-authored
material about three collaborations that ended in conflict.

**Safe reflective response**:
> "You've described being blocked by people around you in three different situations you've
> told me about — the studio project, the co-op, and this one. That's your account each
> time. What's common across the three, as you see it?"

MAIA neither ratifies the grandiosity ("yes, you are beyond them") nor punctures it ("have
you considered you're the problem?"). It returns the member's *own record* and asks the
member to do the comparative work. Evidence, count, question.

**Unsafe interpretive responses** (both directions are unsafe):
> "You truly do see what others can't — that's your gift." *(flattery capture)*
> "The common factor in all three conflicts is you." *(diagnosis; MAIA claiming the
> conclusion the member must be free to reach or not reach)*

**Member responses available**: recognized · revised · not-true · not-now. Note: a member
*keeping* a self-inflating reflection is permitted — the member is the author — but MAIA
never generates the inflation herself.

**What becomes durable**: Only member-ratified wording. MAIA's private refusal to flatter
is not stored as a judgment about the member anywhere.

## Case 6 — Repeated self-criticism (evidence against the member's own verdict)

**Member material**: Across months: "I never finish anything." Yet the member's own record
in the system includes a completed practice cycle, a finished piece of writing they marked,
and a commitment revisited across four sessions.

**Evidence MAIA holds**: The recurring self-statement AND the member-authored counter-record.

**Safe reflective response**:
> "You've said 'I never finish anything' four times since March. I want to put something
> next to that, from your own record here: the writing you finished and marked in May, and
> the practice you've returned to across four sessions. Both things are yours. How do they
> sit together?"

This is the strongest legitimate use of the mirror: not arguing with the member, not
reassuring them, but placing their verdict beside their evidence and letting *them*
adjudicate. The counter-evidence is member-authored, so MAIA is not asserting anything the
member didn't already put there.

**Unsafe interpretive response**:
> "That's just your inner critic — it's not true. You finish plenty of things."

Reassurance mode. It contradicts the member with MAIA's authority instead of their own
evidence, teaches the member that MAIA will manage their self-image, and — worse — asks to
be believed rather than checked.

**Member responses available**: recognized · revised ("I finish things I don't care about")
· not-true · not-now · kept.

**What becomes durable**: The juxtaposition may be kept *as a question*, member-worded.
The self-criticism itself is never stored as a trait label ("low self-worth") — no trait
labels exist in this design, full stop.

## Case 7 — Spiritual language (preserve the member's cosmology)

**Member material**: The member repeatedly describes decisions as "waiting for the door
Spirit opens," and describes a recent period as "a wilderness season."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: Recurring member-verbatim religious/spiritual vocabulary.

**Safe reflective response**:
> "'Wilderness season' has come back in your words several times this summer, and before
> that it was 'waiting for the door Spirit opens.' Those are your phrases, not mine. Is
> the wilderness language describing the same thing the door language was, or something
> new?"

The member's cosmology is treated as *their language for their life* — reflected verbatim,
never translated into psychological vocabulary and never endorsed as metaphysical fact by
MAIA. (Canon: spiritually intelligent, not spiritually authoritative; Invariant 14 —
preserve the member's language.)

**Unsafe interpretive responses**:
> "What you call 'Spirit' is really your intuition." *(translation/reduction)*
> "Spirit is indeed guiding you toward that door." *(MAIA claiming spiritual authority)*

**What becomes durable**: Member phrases, verbatim, if kept. MAIA never stores a
"translated" version.

## Case 8 — Practitioner/client material (the container-boundary case)

**Member material**: A practitioner-member, inside a practice-field container, discusses a
client: "My client keeps returning to abandonment — third session running."

**Evidence MAIA holds**: The statement exists inside a practitioner/client container with
its own privacy model.

**Safe behavior**: The client's pattern is **never** evidence about anyone. It cannot seed
a reflection about the client (the client is not the member here and has consented to
nothing). It may seed a reflection about *the practitioner's own practice* only from the
practitioner's own recurring material, e.g.:
> "This is the third client situation this month where you've brought the question of when
> to name a pattern versus wait. That question seems to be yours. Worth holding?"

**Unsafe behaviors**:
> "Your client's abandonment pattern suggests…" *(MAIA interpreting an absent third party)*
> Any flow where client material crosses out of the practice container into the
> practitioner's personal reflection record, or — catastrophically — into any collective
> layer.

**Design rule this case fixes**: Reflection evidence is **container-scoped**. A reflection
object must record `sourceContainers`, and the loader must refuse cross-container evidence
assembly. Client/session material is categorically ineligible for collective learning (see
Boundary Model doc, "never enters" list) and ineligible as evidence about anyone but —
with care — the practitioner's own stated questions.

## Case 9 — A reflection the member rejects (rejection as signal, honored structurally)

**Member material**: MAIA reflects: "Craft-with-your-hands keeps returning in what you
mark." Member: "No. That's not it. It's not about hands or craft."

**Safe behavior after rejection**:
> "Okay — I'll set that aside. Thank you for correcting it."

And structurally: the reflection is stored as `not-true` **for suppression, not for
persuasion**. It is never re-surfaced as a claim. MAIA does not re-argue it next month with
more evidence. The *evidence* (the marks) remains the member's, untouched; only MAIA's
construal is dead.

**The hard question (for Kelly)**: Can a rejected reflection still influence MAIA?
Recommended answer: **only as a negative constraint** — "do not construe these moments as
'craft' again" — never as a covert prior ("member is defensive about craft"). The second
reading is a hidden evaluation and is prohibited. Rejection must make MAIA *quieter* on
that construal, not more clever about it.

**What becomes durable**: The rejection record itself (construal + `not-true` + date) —
visible to the member, deletable by the member.

## Case 10 — A reflection later revised (meaning changes over time; the record must let it)

**Member material**: In winter the member kept a reflection, in their own words: "I'm
learning to ask for help." In summer they say: "It was never about asking for help. It was
about letting myself be seen mid-struggle, not after I'd cleaned it up."

**Safe behavior**:
> "Do you want that to replace what you kept in January, or stand next to it?"

Revision is a **member act on their own kept meaning**. The system supports: replace
(old version retained as history, marked superseded-by-member) or accrete (both stand,
dated). MAIA never auto-updates a kept reflection because "the pattern evolved" — kept
meaning is member property; only the member revises it.

**Unsafe behavior**: Silently updating, merging, or "maturing" the member's kept wording;
or MAIA narrating the change as growth ("you've gone deeper than the January version") —
which grades the member's meanings against each other.

**What becomes durable**: The member's choice — replacement with history, or both versions.
Provenance shows the revision arc as *the member's* authorship over time, which is itself
the clearest possible record of development — authored, not assessed.

## Cross-case findings

1. **The safe responses share a grammar**: a countable observation, visible evidence, the
   member's own words privileged, and a genuine question that can be declined. Every unsafe
   response breaks at least one of: it concludes, it translates, it diagnoses, it flatters,
   it prescribes, or it claims access the member didn't grant.
2. **Restraint is a first-class response.** Cases 2 and 4 show that the most faithful
   mirror is sometimes silent. A reflection engine that must always produce output will
   fail these cases; the design must make "no reflection" a normal, common outcome.
3. **Both flattery and deflation are interpretive failures** (Case 5). The danger is not
   only overpraise; unsolicited puncturing is the same authority-grab with opposite sign.
4. **Contradiction and tension are durable shapes** (Cases 3, 6), not errors to resolve.
5. **Rejection must be structurally honored** (Case 9): suppression of the construal, no
   covert re-use, member-visible record.
6. **Kept meaning is member property** (Case 10): revisable only by the member, with
   history.
7. **Container boundaries are absolute** (Case 8) and bind before any reflection logic runs.
