# Hospitality & Trust Principles — Session Frame for Jondi — 2026-07-18

**Status**: PREPARATION DOCUMENT, not the paper. The third commissioned paper
("Hospitality and Trust Principles") is authored **with Jondi** — her witness is its
content, and it cannot be pre-written without becoming exactly the kind of
manufactured meaning the constitution forbids. This frame prepares the session:
role, questions, materials, and what the paper becomes afterward. Week 2 of the
30-day plan.

**Jondi's role, as Kelly named it**: *Hospitality witness and relational translator.*
Not a feature reviewer. She is invited to help discover principles engineers would
never see — the session succeeds if she says things no one in the codebase could
have predicted.

---

## 1. Why now

The platform just crossed from *can MAIA exist?* to *how does MAIA become
trustworthy enough to accompany people over decades?* The incident response answered
the engineering half of trust (promises enforced structurally). Jondi is invited to
the other half: what trust *feels like* from inside a relationship with the system —
before any new feature is built on top of it.

## 2. Kelly's questions (the spine of the session)

1. Does this make MAIA feel more trustworthy?
2. What does Sanctuary mean from a human perspective?
3. What does it mean to be remembered appropriately?
4. What should MAIA never carry?
5. What would make someone feel deeply safe?
6. What would make someone feel subtly violated?

## 3. Derived questions (offered, not required — from this week's findings)

- **On the subtle-violation shape**: the engineering account of the worst failure is
  "MAIA seems to know me differently in a way only the private conversation
  explains." Does that match her clinical intuition? What are the *other*
  subtle-violation shapes engineers haven't imagined?
- **On appropriate remembering**: the platform now distinguishes what a member
  authored from what the system inferred. In her practice, when does *being
  remembered accurately* land as care, and when does it land as surveillance? What
  makes the difference — content, timing, framing, or who speaks first?
- **On the Sanctuary sentence**: the UI says "This session won't be remembered."
  The truth is now per-exchange. What words would she trust? What words would make
  her flinch?
- **On rejection**: the reflection design lets a member tell MAIA "that's not true
  for me" and structurally silences the construal. From an EFT lens — what does a
  person need to *feel* when they refuse an interpretation, for the refusal to be
  strengthening rather than rupturing?
- **On disclosure**: a member will soon be told their Sanctuary promise was broken
  once and repaired. What does she know about repair conversations that the draft
  notification should honor?
- **On arrival** (her standing theme): she has consistently pointed toward
  relationship and arrival rather than features. Where in the current flows does a
  person *arrive* — and where are they merely processed?

## 4. What to walk through with her (per Kelly's Week-2 list)

1. **Implications review** — the incident story told honestly (facts from the
   incident record; her read on what it means relationally).
2. **Hospitality questions** — §2–3 above, unhurried.
3. **Now What? flow** — the walk itself, as a first-time person would meet it.
4. **Onboarding and trust surfaces** — /begin → /maia sequence, the Sanctuary
   toggle, the memory-consent moments; where trust is claimed vs. where it is earned.

Practical note: her walk should be **real-auth, production**, with someone recording
her words verbatim (her language is data — preserve it, don't paraphrase; Invariant
14 applies to witnesses too). Scribe posture: pure witness, no interpretation.

## 5. What the paper becomes

After the session: *Hospitality and Trust Principles* — her principles, in her
language, with provenance (which walk, which moment, her words quoted with consent),
mapped to the surfaces they implicate. It should read as testimony translated into
design constraints, not as engineering with quotes decorating it. Anything she says
that contradicts existing design is a finding, not a problem — route contradictions
to Kelly's rulings, exactly as the register does for code.

## 6. What NOT to do in this session

- Don't demo features at her; let her move and speak.
- Don't defend the incident; report it and listen.
- Don't translate her vocabulary into system vocabulary in the room.
- Don't ask her to validate decisions already made — ask what she sees.
- Don't schedule it tightly; arrival takes the time it takes.
