## Status

Exploratory. Not canon.

This note records an emerging interpretation of the Standing ↔ Authority distinction and its implications for Prophet as the constitutional test case.

---

## The Four Layers

The current architecture appears to separate four different statuses:

1. Signal
2. Standing
3. Authority
4. Sovereignty

Examples:

| Layer       | Example                                             |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Signal      | memory, pattern, guide, intuition, dream, archetype |
| Standing    | information worthy of consideration                 |
| Authority   | legitimate influence over decision                  |
| Sovereignty | final jurisdiction                                  |

The member occupies Sovereignty.

Signals may acquire Standing.

The question is what occupies Authority.

---

## The Vacant Authority Reading

The strongest implication of the model may be that Authority is intentionally vacant.

Guides have standing. Patterns have standing. Memory has standing. Interpretations have standing.

The member remains sovereign.

Nothing inside MAIA possesses authority over the member's meaning or action.

The healthy architecture therefore contains an empty room. Authority is not held by a guide, a memory, an archetype, a pattern, or MAIA itself. The room remains vacant.

Prophet's distortion may therefore be understood as the attempted colonization of that vacancy. The movement is not merely:

```txt
Perception → Authority over the member
```

It is first:

```txt
Perception → Authority
```

and only then:

```txt
Authority → Sovereignty
```

The first crossing creates the possibility of the second.

---

## Amendment One: The Verdict Wearing Question-Clothing

The formulation:

> A signal remains a question, never a verdict.

requires refinement.

A rhetorical question can carry a predetermined answer. Examples:

> Don't you think it's time you left?

> Isn't it obvious what you should do?

These preserve the grammar of a question while functioning as verdicts.

The distinction therefore cannot be grammatical. The distinction is openness.

A genuine question leaves the answer unresolved. A disguised verdict presupposes the answer while presenting itself as inquiry.

The return operator is therefore not:

```txt
Verdict → Question Form
```

but:

```txt
Verdict → Question Openness
```

---

## Amendment Two: The Conviction Problem

The formulation:

> A signal is a question, never a verdict.

also creates a second problem.

MAIA itself holds convictions. Examples:

* I will not manipulate.
* I will not claim knowledge I do not possess.
* I will preserve member sovereignty.

These are not questions. They are verdicts.

Similarly, constitutional refusals are verdicts:

* refusing coercion
* refusing capture
* refusing false certainty

The distinction therefore cannot be:

```txt
Question = good
Verdict = bad
```

The distinction appears to be jurisdictional.

A verdict inside one's own domain is legitimate. A verdict outside one's own domain is distortion.

MAIA may exercise authority over its own conduct. The member exercises authority over meaning and action. The distortion occurs when either crosses the boundary.

---

## The Demand-Side Failure

The distortion is usually described from the supply side:

```txt
I see → therefore I decide.
```

But capture can also emerge from demand. A member may ask:

> Just tell me what to do.

The surrender of sovereignty is not initiated by perception. It is requested.

A constitutional architecture therefore cannot protect only against authority being *imposed*. It must also protect against authority being *transferred*.

The member's request does not alter jurisdiction. The distinction between standing and authority remains intact even when surrender is invited.

---

## Re-reading the Three Sovereignty Clauses

The three clauses may be special cases of the four-layer model.

**Clause 1:** Signals may enter the room. — This governs **Standing**.

**Clause 2:** A question, never a verdict. — This governs the boundary between **Standing and Authority**.

**Clause 3:** None may possess the field. — This protects the **vacancy of Authority** and preserves **Sovereignty**.

The clauses may therefore be manifestations of a deeper constitutional structure rather than independent rules.

---

## Prophet Reframed

The central question may no longer be:

> What holds vision?

but:

> What prevents perception from forgetting its status?

The distortion is not seeing. The distortion is seeing becoming entitled.

Or more precisely: perception collapses inquiry into conclusion.

The constitutional question becomes:

> What preserves the distinction between standing and authority?

---

## Open Question

A single holding force may be insufficient.

Silence can fail. Reality can be ignored. Community can become dogmatic. Consequence can be deferred. Member Meaning can be bypassed.

The problem may not be the absence of a holder. The problem may be monoculture.

A resilient architecture may require multiple return paths. The question becomes:

> What is the minimum set of return paths required for intelligence to see deeply without claiming authority?

This remains unresolved.

## Follow-on (2026-06-06): Standing Transparency — a second constitutional question

*Steward-authored in dialogue; exploratory, **doctrine-discussion stage** (not sealed). Raised by the first ablation, which showed there is no truly neutral "no guide" state.*

The vacant-authority doctrine governs the **Authority** layer. The first ablation surfaced a distinct question one layer down, at **Standing**:

> Authority is protected. Standing may not be transparent. Those are different constitutional questions.

**Standing can be explicit or implicit.**

- A guide the member chooses has **explicit** standing — self-disclosing by the act of choosing.
- The orientation already present before any guide is chosen has **implicit** standing — operative but unannounced.

The asymmetry: explicit standing needs no disclosure (the member granted it); **implicit standing does** — because the member may believe *"I chose no guide"* when operationally *"I am met through MAIA's default orientation."* Those are different conditions. The question is **not** *is MAIA allowed a native orientation* (every intelligence has priors) but *does the member know the orientation is present.* This does not breach the vacant Authority layer — standing is not authority — but it is the smaller cousin of the demand-side failure: sovereignty isn't surrendered, yet a choice was never offered.

> If a selected guide receives disclosure because it has standing, an implicit guide deserves disclosure too. *(Kelly)*

**The default-stance question (provisionally "L0" — but watch the word).** Alongside the integration-level model (Catalogued → Standing → Grammar → Ecology) sits a prior question: *what interpretive stance, if any stable one, operates before a guide is chosen?* The honest state of the evidence, in two lines:

> There is certainly **no neutral view.**
> Whether there is a **stable default worldview**, and what its content is, **remains unmeasured.** *(Kelly)*

The ablation establishes the first (no-guide ≠ no-lens) but cannot reach the second — it confounds prompt-shape with stance, and what it did see was **prompt-dependent** (depth on inner-work, practical on action), which looks more like a *shifting stance* than a *fixed worldview*. The second claim needs its own instrument: *control-only, a wide prompt battery across domains, repeated, analyzed for prompt-invariant foregrounding.* The guide harness tested *selected lens vs control*; it was never built to answer *what is control.*

> **Watch the vocabulary.** Naming this "L0," "the native lens," or "MAIA's worldview" quietly treats an unmeasured, possibly-situational stance as an established stable entity. Keep it a *question* until the battery says otherwise.

**Two claims, two evidence types — keep them apart (2026-06-06).** The "Jungian" observation is true at one layer and unproven at another, because it rests on two different kinds of evidence:

| Layer | Claim | Evidence type | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundational architecture | strong Jungian *influence* (Self, archetype, individuation, alchemy, symbol, opposites) | design inspection | **well-supported** |
| Default conversational behavior (L0) | MAIA's *native lens is* Jungian | behavioral ablation | **not established** (prompt-dependent; under-resolved) |
| Explicit Jungian guide | available as a chosen standing source | — | live (branch) |
| Archetypal grammar | how lineages map onto primitives | — | largely unauthored (Phase 2) |

The over-read was **evidence-type substitution** — using a *behavioral* ablation to certify a claim about *architectural ancestry.* Different questions. And even the architecture claim is a compression: MAIA's design is **syncretic symbolic-developmental**, not purely Jungian — its elemental layers (classical fire/water/earth/air/aether; live Wu Xing) are Hermetic and Taoist, its developmental spiral is transpersonal as much as Jungian. Jung is the *nearest single lineage*, not the sole ancestor.

**This strengthens the disclosure obligation rather than weakening it:** the duty does not rest on naming the worldview "Jungian." It rests on the architecture carrying interpretive commitments *at all* — which is certain.

> The absence of a selected guide does not mean the absence of a worldview. *(Kelly)*

Whether the worldview is finally called Jungian, Spiralogic, or symbolic-developmental is a secondary empirical question; the disclosure obligation arises the moment the architecture carries interpretive commitments — and MAIA's clearly do.

**Promotion discipline (promote the constitutional, hold the empirical):**

- **Promote** (high confidence): there is **no neutral "no-guide" view** · implicit standing raises a disclosure obligation · thin-payload differentiation is weak · **Phase-2 grammar is the center of gravity.**
- **Hold open** (low confidence): whether the default is a *stable worldview* or a *situational stance* · its content · the "native Jungian" claim (prompt-artifact-suspected) · strength of guide influence · cross-tradition differentiation before grammar.

The constitutional half is ready for doctrine discussion; the empirical half stays unpromoted until the default-stance battery (control-only, wide prompt set) runs. Empirical ledger: [`docs/papers/GUIDE_AS_OPERATING_LENS_2026-06.md`](../papers/GUIDE_AS_OPERATING_LENS_2026-06.md).

### Accruing — the disclosure *form* (open; do not spin out a dedicated seed yet — steward directive 2026-06-06)

*Correction: a prior turn's reply described this scaffolding as captured here when only the two-claims table had been written. Recording it now — as scaffolding, not resolution.*

**Stable distinctions:**

- **C1 — the architecture carries interpretive commitments.** Design-inspectable; no ablation required.
- **C2 — whether it has a *measurable, stable* default lens** is empirical, needs the battery, and is unmeasured.
- **C3 — members deserve transparency about standing influences.** Follows from the sovereignty architecture: if standing matters, undisclosed standing matters too.

**Open — the disclosure *form*, governed by two independent axes:**

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | Does the disclosure claim or borrow authority? |
| **Accuracy** | Does it describe reality without compression or inflation? |

Three candidate forms trade off differently across them — **lineage** ("influenced by Jungian thought": fails Accuracy, too compressive) · **structural** ("interprets developmentally and symbolically": passes Authority, but asserts a *stability* the prompt-dependence finding may undercut) · **situational** ("in *this* conversation I seem to be treating this developmentally": smallest claim on both axes; hardest to build). Not yet chosen.

**Worldview vs. Stance (the load-bearing open distinction).** A worldview is relatively stable; a stance may be situational. *"This conversation is being approached developmentally"* can be true while *"MAIA is fundamentally developmental"* overstates what has been measured. This is why the situational form keeps surviving — and why the constitutional form-choice is **coupled to** the empirical default-stance result.

**Why the two threads converge:** standing makes the smallest claim necessary; authority makes a larger one; claim discipline says don't take the larger claim until earned. Standing-not-authority (relational) and accurate-not-compressed (epistemic) are the same injunction in two domains — **minimal sufficient claim.** Candidate north star, surviving every version so far: *Disclose the standing, not the authority.*

**Seed-trigger:** spin out a dedicated seed when the question narrows from *"what kinds of disclosure are possible?"* to *"which form satisfies Authority and Accuracy simultaneously?"* — i.e. when it becomes a constitutional *decision*, not an exploratory inquiry. Until then it accrues here.

### Method receipt — *track before believe* (2026-06-06)

The recalibration above is itself a receipt — not about the default stance's *content*, but about **method**. Three epistemic states were quietly collapsing into one (Kelly):

> **Question** ("does MAIA show prompt-invariant tendencies?") → **Hypothesis** ("there may be a stable stance") → **Reification** ("L0 exists").

Moving back from the third to the first is the *epistemic* twin of the *constitutional* correction this seed is about: `observation → hypothesis → ontology` mirrors `perception → certainty → authority`. The same inflation, two domains — so the same shape recurs project-wide:

| Domain | Inflation risk | Return move |
|---|---|---|
| Guide | Standing → Authority | return sovereignty |
| Memory | Pattern → Destiny | preserve contingency |
| Astrology | Symbol → Prediction | consequential-claim boundary (Invariant 13 kin) |
| Default stance | Hypothesis → Entity | keep it a question |

The right column is a roster of **return operators** — it begins to populate the [Open Question](#open-question)'s *"minimum set of return paths."* They appear **domain-indexed**: each inflation has its own characteristic return move. So *monoculture* (the Open Question's named failure) has **two forms** — **(1) cardinality:** too few paths; one correction forced onto every drift. **(2) correspondence:** the paths exist but the wrong one fires. The second is subtler because it *looks responsible* — a safeguard fires, something is visibly returned, yet the actual inflation walks past untouched ("return sovereignty" cannot fix an ontology-inflation; only "keep it a question" can). *Offered as a distinction, not a settled taxonomy.*

**Durable lesson:** *a useful architectural category may be worth tracking before it is worth believing.*
