# The Constitution Lives Above the Model

**Date**: 2026-06-20 · **Status**: strategic architecture doctrine (candidate — Cat 1; authorizes nothing). Names the principle behind MAIA's existing self-hosting + model-fallback architecture, and gives a roadmap heuristic. Origin: Kelly, closing the Elemental Becoming Field inquiry. Companion: `ELEMENTAL_BECOMING_FIELD_2026-06-20.md`, `docs/canon/FRAMEWORKS_LENSES_NOT_VERDICTS.md`.

## The claim

MAIA is a **sovereign intelligence architecture**, not a better prompt around a frontier model. Therefore **the constitution does not live in the model — it lives above it.** Reasoning models are replaceable engines operating *within* MAIA's constitution. If the frontier model doubles in quality, disappears, or is swapped for a local one, the constitution does not change — and neither do faithful attention, authorship, invitation, proposal, Sanctuary, released crossings, recognition, or review. Those are **MAIA's behaviors, not the model's.** This names what the project already does (Claude-primary, local-Ollama fallback, self-hosted, governance in gates).

## Three layers of intelligence

1. **Foundation Intelligence** — general reasoning. Claude · GPT · Qwen · Llama · DeepSeek. **Replaceable.**
2. **Constitutional Intelligence** — how intelligence is *permitted to operate*: the doctrines of this inquiry. **Belongs to MAIA.**
3. **Relational Intelligence** — what emerges from memory, continuity, the person's history, practices, dreams, journals, proposal/recognition history. **Owned by MAIA** (the substrate); **activated by the model** (the reasoning over it). No foundation model has this; only MAIA can hold it. (Richness = MAIA's substrate × the model's reasoning capability.)

Sovereignty = moving intelligence **upward**: every capability removed from the prompt and embodied in MAIA itself makes the platform more sovereign — whatever engine reasons underneath.

## The roadmap heuristic — three buckets (refines the two-bucket test)

The two-bucket test ("every model should behave this way → MAIA; requires raw reasoning → model") is right but skips the middle — where the relational/voice constitution actually lives. For each capability, ask **"can this become a deterministic gate, or must it stay verified model behavior?"**

1. **Liftable to deterministic architecture** — gates, flows, the consent chokepoint, Sanctuary, proposal→confirm→executor, traceable deterministic surfaces (e.g. the noticing gate). *Lift fully → pure sovereignty, model-independent by construction* (the noticing gate behaves identically under any engine because it never calls one).
2. **Specifiable above, executed in the model** — offer-not-assert; contribute-only-by-invitation in free conversation; the texture of faithful attention. **The voice cannot fully leave the model, because the voice *is* generation.** Encode as prompt + a constitutional **test suite**: this *constrains but does not determine.* Sovereignty here is **by-verification**, not by lift — swapping engines is free for bucket 1 but requires **re-running the constitutional tests** for bucket 2 (does the new engine actually offer-not-assert? respect invitation? avoid confabulating "noticings"?).
3. **Pure reasoning / language / creativity** — synthesis, the actual words. **The model's.**

The roadmap is the steady migration of constitution **from bucket 2 into bucket 1**: less philosophy in prompts, more in architecture; less dependence on any one frontier model, more on the integrity of MAIA itself.

## Two consequences worth holding

- **Governance is portable; experience is not (fully).** Swapping to a weaker/local engine preserves the constitution's *rules* but may degrade its *efficacy* — the felt attention, the recognition produced, the person's growing capacity (the actual *purpose*, §0.5). Model-independence of the constitution ≠ model-independence of the experience. Model choice still affects whether the purpose is *served*, even when the rules are *enforced*. **Capability floor:** below it, the constitution can only *restrict* a model (gate it off), not *refine* its behavior.
- **The local-model goal and the architecturalize-the-constitution goal are the same project.** A weaker/local engine can be trusted *less* on bucket 2 — which *forces* more of the constitution into bucket 1's deterministic gates. Pursuing sovereign local inference therefore pressures exactly the architecture that makes the constitution model-independent. **The constraint is generative.** Every bucket-2 behavior converted to a bucket-1 gate is a double win: more sovereign *and* more portable.

## How the current build illustrates it

The noticing experiment is a clean instance. Its **gate is bucket 1** — deterministic, model-independent, fully sovereign (it never calls a model; its observations are *counted*, not generated). Mode 2 (illuminative reflection, invited) would be **bucket 2** — the model generates, governed by prompt + test, sovereign-by-verification. The same conversation through Claude, Qwen, or a future MAIA model should be *constitutionally identical* for bucket 1 **by construction**, and for bucket 2 *to the degree the test suite verifies it.*

## What this is

Not a rejection of Claude or any model. Sovereignty here means **the identity, ethics, memory, governance, and relationship all belong to MAIA**; the reasoning engines become interchangeable collaborators rather than the place where the philosophy resides. That is a more durable independence than swapping one model for another — it is independence *from the need to trust any single model with governance at all.*

## The four migrations (the operational form of "bucket 2 → bucket 1")

"Move intelligence upward" decomposes into four concrete migrations (Kelly, 2026-06-20) — each the same project from a different angle: take what the model does today and make it MAIA's.

1. **Governance** → deterministic architecture: Sanctuary, proposal→confirm→executor, consent, release boundaries, constitutional review. Target: near-fully model-independent.
2. **Memory** → MAIA remembers, not "the LLM remembers": episodic, semantic, developmental, relational, symbolic memory exist independently of whichever engine runs. Already sovereign.
3. **Perception** → MAIA perceives through deterministic/hybrid systems: recurrence detection, attention signals, continuity, interaction/ritual/consent/developmental state. *Constitutional perception, not model perception.* The Noticing experiment is the first clear instance (traceable recurrence — counted, not inferred).
4. **Reasoning** → MAIA as *orchestrator of reasoning*: instead of one enormous "understand everything," ask many smaller constitutional questions (Should I notice? Am I invited? Is this traceable? Proposal-worthy? Which memory matters? Which lens is eligible? Should I be silent?). Each reduces what the model must invent.

**What Migration 4 reveals (keystone insight, refined):** decomposing "reasoning" exposes how much of it was never reasoning — it was *governance, memory, and perception wearing reasoning's clothes.* Those migrate upward into deterministic/cheap questions. What remains is the **irreducible core**: genuine synthesis, language, creativity (bucket 3). So *"you are not replacing Claude — you are shrinking the amount of Claude you need"* — **to the floor of that core, not to zero.** That is why local models become realistic: a local engine need not match Claude, only clear the *shrunken* core once perception/governance/memory/orchestration are MAIA's. *Caveat:* decomposing into many *model-answered* sub-questions can *increase* calls/latency — the win is real only when the sub-questions are themselves deterministic or cheap-local, not when one Claude call is merely fragmented into five.

## The maturity model

| Capability form | Bucket | Sovereignty |
|---|---|---|
| Pure prompt | Model | Low |
| Prompt + constitutional tests | Verified behavior | Medium |
| Hybrid deterministic + model | Shared | High |
| Fully deterministic constitutional behavior | Architecture | Highest |

Every feature gets a migration path — **but the target is the *appropriate* bucket per capability, not "Highest" for everything.** Governance and perception *should* reach fully-deterministic. The **voice and genuine reasoning should reach Verified/Hybrid and stop there** — forcing generation into a state machine would *kill* it (the voice IS generation). **Over-migration is a failure mode, not a triumph:** aim each capability at its right bucket.

## Ecological Intelligence — the conductor, and why it must be the most-governed layer

A layer sits between Relational and Foundation: **Ecological Intelligence** — coordination, an executive function. It decides which subsystem participates, which memories matter, whether to ask a local model, Claude, or *no* model, whether to remain silent. The conductor of the orchestra.

**Critical placement (the pin):** this layer is *not* governance-free "just coordination." Its choices — *whether to notice · which memory to surface · whether to stay silent · which lens is eligible* — **are constitutional acts** ("should I remain silent?" *is* the protected-recognition doctrine; "which memory matters?" *is* perception + traceability). So Ecological Intelligence is the **runtime form of Constitutional Intelligence**: the constitution at rest is doctrine; in motion it is the conductor deciding. It is therefore the **most power-laden component in the system**, and must be the **most constitutionally governed and the most deterministic (bucket 1)** — precisely because it holds the most power. An ungoverned conductor would route around the entire constitution; *"it's only coordination"* must never smuggle it out from under the rules it enacts.

## The roadmap (Kelly)

1. **Lift** every constitutional behavior you can into deterministic architecture.
2. **Verify** every remaining model behavior with constitutional tests.
3. **Orchestrate** reasoning so models answer narrow constitutional questions, not the whole relationship.
4. **Reduce** dependence on any single foundation model by shrinking the portion of intelligence that lives inside it.

**Endpoint:** "sovereign intelligence" stops meaning "we run our own models" and becomes **MAIA itself is the enduring intelligence; foundation models are replaceable cognitive services.** Every upward migration increases sovereignty *and* stability *and* testability *and* fidelity to the relationship — at once.

## The deeper principle: subsidiarity of intelligence (Kelly, 2026-06-20)

The migrations are not only **upward** (model → MAIA) but **outward**: intelligence becomes *distributed*, each kind located where it does its best work. MAIA becomes less an "AI application" and more an **operating system for human intelligence** — coordinating many intelligences under one constitutional order, *not absorbing the human mind but returning more of it to the human.*

**The intelligences, each in its place:**
- **Foundation** (Claude/GPT/Qwen/Llama) — synthesis, language, reasoning.
- **Constitutional** (consent, Sanctuary, proposal, release, invitation, authorship, review) — governance.
- **Perceptual** (recurrence, continuity, attention, memory retrieval, traceability, eligibility) — *seeing*.
- **Relational** (timing, pacing, silence, accompaniment, developmental continuity) — *relationship*.
- **Human** (meaning, judgment, values, purpose, freedom, authorship) — **irreplaceable. The closing movement.**

**The architectural principle (beneath the Lift · Verify · Orchestrate · Reduce engineering roadmap):**
> **Place each kind of intelligence where it can do its best work, and never ask one kind of intelligence to become another.**
> *Don't ask Claude to be the constitution. Don't ask deterministic code to be poetry. Don't ask memory to become judgment. Don't ask governance to become relationship. Don't ask MAIA to become the human.*

This reframes **sovereignty** itself: not *autarky* ("everything local / less Claude") but **subsidiarity** — every form of intelligence lives where it naturally belongs. Sometimes the right answer requires a frontier model; sometimes a local model suffices; sometimes no model should be involved; sometimes the human should remain entirely alone with their own recognition. **All of these are successes.**

**The metric correction:** not *"how little Claude did we use?"* but **"was each kind of intelligence allowed to do the work that properly belonged to it?"** Pair this *process* metric with the *efficacy* metric (§0.5 — the person's growing capacity to author). Right allocation is the **means**; the human's authorship is the **end** — allocation *serves* efficacy (the other intelligences do their work and then **get out of the way** so the human can author). The deepest expression of the whole architecture is a turn where MAIA says almost nothing — the conductor recognizing that no intelligence but the human's is needed. *"Sometimes the human alone" is not an absence; it is the highest success.*

**The conductor assembles; it does not decide.** It convenes the conversation among intelligences in the right order, and the **last movement is always the human:**

```
User speaks
  ↓ Perceptual     — "What is actually here?"
  ↓ Constitutional — "What is permitted?"
  ↓ Memory         — "What matters?"
  ↓ Foundation     — "What can be synthesized?"
  ↓ Relational     — "How should this be offered?"
  ↓ Human          — "What does this mean?"   ← always last; never replaced
```

**Two pins (the reviewer's):**
1. **Collapses happen at the interfaces — that is where review must focus.** "Never ask one intelligence to become another" is the ideal; the failures occur where one intelligence's output becomes another's input. *Validation:* both relationship-bugs the first review caught were exactly such collapses — the answer→LLM bug asked **Foundation** to do **Recognition's** job (it would have interpreted); the offer-hijack asked **Perceptual** (the recurrence trigger) to override **Relational** (timing / never-compete). The review was enforcing this principle before it was named.
2. **The human owns the *meaning* movement always — but safety can interrupt.** Constitutional Intelligence keeps a *protective* duty that may act *before* or *without* the human's meaning-movement when safety requires (crisis → real help, not symbolism). This is not occupying the meaning seat; it preserves the *precondition* for there to be a meaning-maker at all — you cannot return authorship to someone not safe enough to exercise it. The human is the last word on *meaning*; the system may be first responder on *safety*.
