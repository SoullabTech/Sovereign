# RELATIONSHIPS-UX-01

## Governing law

The Relationships experience is designed from the quality of attention Soullab intends to cultivate, not from a catalogue of features.

**Ontology → Ethics → Attention → Encounter → Interaction → Representation → Visual form.**

If a visual or interaction decision conflicts with this order, the design changes.

## Core principles

- Attention precedes representation.
- Relationship comes before object.
- Process comes before category.
- Presence comes before explanation.
- Phenomenon first; model second.
- The member remains the author of meaning.
- The absent other remains a person, not an object of diagnosis.
- A relationship is a living field and a form-in-motion.
- Every representation must offer a path back to lived experience.
- Depth should be available without being imposed.
- More intelligence, less apparatus.

## Implicit intelligence, explicit humility

MAIA may perceive, remember, compare, infer, and form increasingly sophisticated hypotheses about relational patterns across time. She need not expose all that she perceives.

When she brings a pattern into the relationship, she offers it as a living possibility rather than a verdict, preserves the difference between evidence and inference, remains open to contradiction, and allows the member's encounter with the pattern to transform what she understands.

A previous MAIA hypothesis may tune later attention. It does not become independent evidence merely because it was stored or encountered again.

### Epistemic layers

1. **Member evidence** — what the member said, wrote, selected, marked, or explicitly declared.
2. **Observed structure** — recurrence or relational form derived from member-grounded material with provenance, confidence, and correctability.
3. **MAIA hypothesis** — a provisional interpretation or gestalt-level understanding.
4. **Member-ratified meaning** — a hypothesis the member has recognized, amended, deepened, or rejected.
5. **Gestalt awareness** — MAIA's continually revised implicit sense of the whole, used primarily to tune attention rather than to fill the interface.

## Canonical experience

**Relationships Home**

Who is present for you?

→ **Relationship Space**

A person, inner figure, or larger relationship comes into focus.

→ **Now**

What is alive now? Conversation, voice, live transcription, present experience.

↔ **Story**

Meaningful relational chronology and member-grounded moments.

↔ **Field**

What seems to happen between the participants across time: movement, recurrence, change, contradiction, rupture, repair, and possible pattern.

MAIA inhabits all three. She is not a separate tool the member must leave the relationship to access.

## First-release reconciliation

### Keep

- outer / inner / transpersonal relational ontology
- member relationship records
- member-authored entries and continuity
- structural pattern detection
- confidence, evidence, and decay
- relational signal provenance and containment
- relational context stance

### Integrate

- check-in → Now
- Relational Field → Field
- Dynamics Map → Field / Pattern depth
- Repair Path → contextual relational depth
- Repair Script → contextual action when useful

### Move / reframe

- Timeline → Story
- source lineages → optional Depth / Why this?

### Hide by default

- field tone
- active signal chips
- dominant pattern labels
- developmental theme labels
- system recency/check-in metadata

### Retire as permanent visible sections

- Next Movement
- Open with a tool
- Take this to MAIA

Their underlying capability remains available contextually.

## Acceptance law

A member should be able to enter Relationships, bring someone into view, begin speaking, encounter relevant history, perceive a possible relational movement, reflect with MAIA, and return to life without feeling they moved through a collection of software tools.

The journey should feel like **one inquiry becoming deeper**.
