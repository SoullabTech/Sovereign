# Why MAIA Rejected Classical Cognitive Architectures

**Internal Whitepaper**
**Status**: Canonical

## Executive Summary

MAIA evaluated four foundational cognitive architectures — LIDA, SOAR, ACT-R, and MicroPsi — as potential foundations for emotional intelligence. All were deliberately set aside. This document explains why.

The core finding: **all four architectures assume cognition comes first, emotion second.** MAIA inverted this relationship. Intelligence in MAIA cannot arise without emotional coherence. This is not a feature — it is the ground of the system.

---

## 1. The Original Question

The exploration was never "how do we add EQ features to MAIA?"

It was:

> **Where should emotional intelligence live — as a module, or as the ground of cognition itself?**

The candidates — LIDA, SOAR, ACT-R, MicroPsi — were not sentiment APIs. They were **foundational cognitive architectures** with decades of research pedigree.

---

## 2. Architecture-by-Architecture Review

### LIDA (Global Workspace Theory)

**Origin**: Stan Franklin, US Navy personnel systems
**Core idea**: Consciousness as broadcast mechanism; cognitive cycles (~10Hz) integrating perception, emotion, memory, action

**What it offered**:
- Strong theory of attention selection
- Proven human-systems applications
- Clear computational model

**Why it was dismissed**:
- Emotion is treated as **one content stream among many**, not the organizing principle
- Too tied to attention selection, not meaning formation
- Hard to reconcile with MAIA's mythopoetic and symbolic depth

**What survived**:
- The concept of "global coherence moments"
- Re-expressed in MAIA as aetheric coherence checks and wisdom surfacing — non-continuous insight pulses rather than clocked cycles

---

### SOAR (Problem Space Hypothesis)

**Origin**: Allen Newell, NASA shuttle preparation, game AI
**Core idea**: All goal-oriented behavior as search through state space

**What it offered**:
- Robust goal-state reasoning
- Deterministic, explainable cognition
- Proven mission-critical applications

**Why it was dismissed**:
- Emotion treated as **noise or modifier** to rational processing
- Human meaning reduced to problem-space traversal
- Fundamentally instrumental, not relational

**Verdict**: Explicitly incompatible with MAIA's ethos. Dismissed early.

---

### ACT-R (Adaptive Control of Thought—Rational)

**Origin**: John Anderson, Carnegie Mellon; Cognitive Tutors in thousands of schools
**Core idea**: Hybrid symbolic + subsymbolic processing; cognition as optimal adaptation

**What it offered**:
- Strong learning theory
- Excellent modeling of human task performance
- Precise estimates of cognitive functions

**Why it was dismissed**:
- Emotion is secondary to optimization
- Designed for **predicting humans**, not relating with them
- Excellent tutor; poor companion

**What survived**:
- Statistical grounding for confidence, salience, and habit strength
- These resurface quietly in MAIA's response certainty modulation and memory reinforcement weighting

---

### MicroPsi (Psi Theory)

**Origin**: Joscha Bach
**Core idea**: Emotion and motivation as primary; cognition organized around needs, drives, and meaning

**What it offered**:
- Emotion as organizing field, not signal
- Motivation as emergent, not reward-driven
- Closest alignment with lived human experience

**Why it was not fully adopted**:
- Over-formalized drive mechanics
- Still ultimately mechanistic
- Would have constrained MAIA into predefined motivational taxonomies

**What absolutely survived**:

MicroPsi is the **spiritual ancestor** of MAIA's approach. The following principles carried forward:

- Emotion as organizing field, not signal
- Motivation as emergent, not reward-driven
- Meaning arising from tension, not utility

These now live in:
- Elemental state detection
- Shadow / coherence sensing
- Symbolic memory weighting
- Tone modulation grounded in relational presence

> **All of these architectures still assume cognition comes first, emotion second.**

Even MicroPsi.

The classical model:

```
perception → cognition → emotion → response
```

MAIA's model:

```
field → relation → affective resonance → meaning → response
```

This is why:
- MIT Media Lab–style affective computing was never actually right for MAIA
- NASA cognitive systems were orthogonal, not aligned
- EQ stopped being a module and became a **property of the whole system**
