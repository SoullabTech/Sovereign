# Constitution Map — Design Brief

**Status: Design brief only. The map itself is not yet drawn. Revise this brief only after the underlying constitutional documents stabilize. Write the map only after this brief is stable.**

---

## Purpose

The Constitution Map is the orientation layer for contributors.

It introduces no new doctrine.

It summarizes none of the constitutional documents beyond what is necessary for navigation.

Its purpose is to help contributors find the governing document that answers the question they currently have.

The authority always remains with the constitutional documents themselves.

---

## Governing Principle

Orientation precedes action.

The map performs for contributors what MAIA performs for members:

It provides orientation so that action can occur within an existing structure rather than creating a new one.

---

## Organizing Structure

The map is organized around five questions.

### 1. Why are we here?

Purpose

Directs contributors to the constitutional documents describing the platform's fundamental purpose and relationship to human life.

Timescale: Decades

---

### 2. How do we work?

Method

Directs contributors to documents describing the platform's operating philosophy, including orientation, agency, participation, and related behavioral principles.

Timescale: Years

---

### 3. What must we never do?

Constraints

Directs contributors to constitutional prohibitions and architectural boundaries.

Timescale: Every decision

---

### 4. How do we know it's working?

Evidence

Directs contributors to observational methods, retrospective evaluation, and transferred-capacity criteria.

Timescale: Weeks, months, years

---

### 5. How do we decide what to build next?

Criteria

Directs contributors to engineering filters and feature evaluation principles.

Timescale: Every pull request

---

## Change Discipline

The Constitution Map changes only when the constitutional structure changes.

It is revised after—not before—the underlying constitutional documents stabilize.

The map follows the constitution.

It never leads it.

---

## Non-Authority Constraint

The map possesses no independent authority.

If tension exists between the map and a constitutional document, the constitutional document always governs.

The map is an orientation device.

Never a source of doctrine.

---

## Two-Axis Pattern (Emerging — Not Yet Adopted)

The constitution may have two orthogonal axes worth watching before the map is drawn:

**Vertical:** Purpose → Method → Constraints → Evidence → Criteria.
Answers *which question* is being addressed.

**Horizontal:** Runtime → Session → Rhythm → Longitudinal → Organizational.
Answers *over what span of time* that question is expressed.

For example:
- "Orientation" appears in a single conversation (runtime), across a week of check-ins (rhythm), and in the Constitution Map itself (organizational orientation).
- "Evidence" appears as a response within a session, as patterns emerging across months, and as retrospective evaluation of the platform itself.

If this pattern continues to prove useful across future work, the eventual Constitution Map may become a two-dimensional navigation aid rather than a simple hierarchy.

**Adopt only if earned.** The test is whether it consistently explains the architecture across enough cases — not whether it is elegant. Elegance is not evidence.
