## The unit

The unit is not the feature. The unit is the living thread.

A member may enter through:
- business decision
- creative project
- personal issue
- practitioner session
- dream
- journal
- conversation
- strategy meeting

MAIA treats these as different expressions of one developmental continuity.

Do not design separate primary experiences for each. Design one central experience that can hold all of them.

---

## Primary UI metaphor: The Continuity Field

Not a dashboard.  
Not a chat app.  
Not a journal.  
Not a CRM.

**A field where the member can enter with whatever is alive.**

---

## Main entry point

Replace mode-first UX with one primary invitation:

> **What are you holding today?**

Then offer optional context — doors, not boxes:

- Personal
- Creative
- Business
- Relationship
- Session
- Dream
- Body
- Unknown / I'm not sure

These are optional. Expression can begin without choosing one.

---

## Central flow

1. Member enters what is alive
2. MAIA receives it as expression
3. MAIA orients gently:
   - What movement is present?
   - What continuity does this belong to?
   - What has appeared before?
   - What is asking to be recognized, trusted, or participated in?
4. Member continues in conversation, voice, writing, or session
5. MAIA quietly updates developmental trace candidates
6. Nothing becomes memory without human review or clear consent

---

## Three UI regions

### 1. The Living Thread
The current conversation / reflection / session.  
Where the member speaks, writes, walks, records, or engages.  
This is primary. Everything else is in service of this.

### 2. The Continuity Rail
A subtle side rail. Shows only what helps orientation:
- recurring symbols
- active developmental movements
- open questions
- commitments
- recent related threads

No clutter. No analytics feel. Quiet and available, never demanding.

### 3. The Threshold Review
Appears only after natural pauses or session closing.  
Shows possible developmental shifts — proposals, not declarations:
- "This may belong to the same movement as…"
- "A shift may have occurred here…"
- "This sounds like recognition returning…"
- "Would you like this remembered?"

The Threshold Review is not a notification. It is an invitation after the fact.

---

## Critical UX law

> **Never force the user to classify experience before expressing it.**

Classification, if needed, happens after expression. Not before.

**Design sequence:**
```
Expression first
Orientation second
Continuity third
Memory last
```

Do not ask for structured metadata up front unless absolutely necessary.

---

## Central interactions (all should work)

```
"I need to think through a business decision."
"I'm stuck in my creative process."
"I had a hard session today."
"Something is happening and I don't know what it is."
```

All four enter the same continuity architecture.  
MAIA responds differently by context. The underlying field is the same.

---

## Constitutional constraint

MAIA only works with what has entered expression.

Permitted:
> "In what you've shared, I notice…"

Forbidden:
> "Deep down, you really…"

Expression is the boundary of legitimate system knowledge.

---

## The central design test

> Does this protect the integrity of the person's becoming, or does it force their becoming into the system's structure?

If it forces → simplify.  
If it protects → build.
