## §G The one question that is above the boundary

Everything above resolves without a ruling **except** this, and it is the hinge for directive §4
(founder summary), §12 (leverage points), §13 (cascades) and §25 (dynamic check output) simultaneously.

**Contract §1 states:** ⛔ *"Nothing renders that is not an assertion. Every visual element … must
resolve to an `assertion_id`."*

Every headline the directive asks for is a **derived** statement — *"Mostly operational, one critical
pathway blocked"*, *"the leverage point is disk availability"*, a five-step consequence chain — and
resolves not to one `assertion_id` but to a **set** of them plus a derivation rule. Two competent
implementers, both bound by this contract, would genuinely differ on whether §1 permits that.

### Recommended ruling (carried, not asked open-ended)

> Admit a second unit, `derived_assertion`, subordinate to §1 rather than an exception to it:
>
> ```text
> DERIVED ASSERTION
>   derived_id
>   statement                    the founder-legible sentence
>   derivation_rule              named, versioned, deterministic — never model prose
>   inputs[]                     assertion_id[] — complete, no partial sets
>   epistemic_status             FLOORED at the weakest input; never exceeds it
>   temporal_status              FLOORED at the stalest input
>   ⛔ may never be `established` if any input is provisional, stale, or unknown
>   ⛔ may never introduce a subject/predicate absent from its inputs
> ```
>
> A causal chain (§12/§13) is then a derived assertion whose rule is **an evidenced-edge walk** — each
> edge an assertion in its own right. This keeps §2.5 intact: co-location still cannot become a link,
> because an edge with no assertion cannot enter the walk. "No glow without cause" is preserved; the
> cause is simply allowed to be a named rule over a complete input set.

**Why this and not the alternative:** forbidding derived statements outright leaves the founder view
permanently unable to answer *"is my system functioning?"* — which is the directive's whole purpose.
Permitting free-form synthesis would let inference re-enter through prose, which is the failure class
§2.5 and M1 exist to prevent. The floor rule makes the honest answer structurally unavoidable: with
today's single bound observation, every derived statement would render `provisional`, and the summary
would truthfully read as unestablished rather than confident.

⭐ Status: ~~`AWAITING_AUTHORITY`~~ → **`AUTHORIZED` 2026-08-16** — see §I.
