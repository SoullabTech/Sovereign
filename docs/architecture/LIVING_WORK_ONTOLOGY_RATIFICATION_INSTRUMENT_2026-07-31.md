## The core candidate — the thing to be ratified

> **The Studio remains in relationship with a *Living Work*: a member-authored body
> of material, inquiry, decisions, and expressions that may exist before any single
> form and may give rise to many forms over time.**

## The essential guards — ratified together with the definition

1. **Nothing enters a Living Work without the member's declaration or explicit adoption.**
2. **The Studio may notice what is gathering; it may not pronounce what the work is becoming.**
3. **Observations remain distinct from interpretations.**
4. **A manuscript remains a valid expression of a Living Work — not the governing ontology.**
5. **Existing R1 writing infrastructure is retained, not discarded.**

These are drawn from ratified canon and a recorded constitutional candidate.
**Ratifying this ontology adopts the five guards for the Living Work ontology itself;
it does not independently ratify any referenced constitutional candidate beyond this
instrument's scope.**

## The ten decision questions (candidate answers — rule each, or the whole)

**1. What is the primary object of the Studio?**
The **Living Work** — a member-authored body of material, inquiry, decisions, and
expressions. *Not* the manuscript, the account, or the session. The manuscript
becomes one expression of it.

**2. How does a Living Work differ from a manuscript or a project?**
A *manuscript* is a single form (words, checkpoints). A *project* is task-bounded —
a goal and an end. A *Living Work* is neither: it may exist before any form, outlive
any single form, and give rise to many. It is defined by **continuity of authorship
and inquiry**, not by a deliverable or a deadline.

**3. Can one member hold multiple Living Works?**
**Ruled by the founder (2026-07-31): plural in identity, narrower in the first build.**
The ontology **permits multiple distinct Living Works** — a member may steward more
than one; each remains separate unless the member explicitly relates or combines them.
**The first implementation may support one active Living Work per member; that
implementation limit does not redefine the ontology.** Ruled explicitly rather than
by whole-document incorporation, because it is the single largest model change in the
instrument.

**4. What may belong to a Living Work?**
Any material the member places or adopts into it: drafts and manuscripts, ideas,
journal reflections, kept passages, voice notes, questions, research/sources,
gatherings of related material, and **member-authored references to people,
relationships, collaborators, or conversations relevant to the work** — a person is
never a contained object; only the member's references to them belong. **These
materials are *contents of* a Living Work, which is exactly why they were gated on
this ruling.**

**5. What counts as an expression of a Living Work?**
A form the work takes for an audience or a use — book, workshop, course, talk,
article, facilitator guide. Expressions **share lineage**; they are not separate
projects and not duplicates. One Living Work → many expressions.

**6. How does material enter it?**
Only by a **member act**: writing it, marking/keeping it, importing/uploading it, or
explicitly adopting something offered. Entry is always a member gesture. (Import
already works; it becomes an entry path *into a Living Work* rather than into a lone
manuscript.)

**7. What must never be silently absorbed?**
**Anything.** Nothing enters by inference, proximity, or system decision. The Studio
may *offer* ("this seems related — adopt it?"); it may never *absorb*. No
cross-surface content joins a work without an explicit member act.

**8. What existing manuscript structures remain valid?**
**All of them.** The working draft, its checkpoints, the immutable source,
autosave / exit-guard / revision machinery, `WorkingDraftEditor`, and Returning
(R1.1) remain exactly as built. They become **the Writing expression of a Living
Work** rather than the whole. Nothing is discarded or rewritten by this ruling.

**9. What becomes newly *possible* after ratification?** (possible ≠ authorized-to-build)
(a) a member may hold a Living Work as a first-class thing with its own identity;
(b) the eight held rooms become legitimate design targets as *contents of* that work;
(c) material may gather around one center across surfaces — always by member act;
(d) multiple expressions may share one lineage. **Ratification makes these
*designable*; it builds none of them.** Each is a later Implement act.

**10. What implementation remains explicitly out of scope (under this act)?**
**Everything buildable.** No schema / tables, no migration, no API, no UI, no branch
convergence, no room build. This instrument ratifies *what the Studio's object is*,
not *how it is stored or rendered*.
