# The Livingness Guard
## Revisability Under Member Pressure

### The doctrine (Kelly)

> **Livingness is not poetic temporality. Livingness is revisability under member pressure.**

A living symbolic mirror is not one that *describes* change in graceful language. It is one whose picture of
the member **moves when the member pushes on it.** Living *vocabulary* ("patterns still speaking,"
"oscillations," "surprising returns") can dress a dead *structure*. The guard is not the verbs. The guard is
whether *"no, that's not it"* changes what MAIA holds.

Compressed to one testable sentence:

> **A living memory system remembers events but gradually forgets its certainty about what they mean.**

### Why uncertainty must be engineered (Kelly — the motivating principle)

Almost every force in a system increases certainty: repeated surfacings, engagement, user attention,
emotional resonance, pattern-matching, recommendation engines. **Almost nothing naturally increases
uncertainty.** So unless uncertainty is *deliberately engineered*, the system drifts toward closure
regardless of its stated philosophy. In this system, entropy runs toward *false certainty* — openness is the
only state you must spend energy to maintain. Livingness is that deliberate engineering of uncertainty.

### Two guards, two orthogonal axes

Both descend from one Condition — *reality exceeds any representation of it*:

| Guard | Question | Protects against MAIA becoming… | Enforced in | Defender |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sovereignty** | Who has authority? | …**the authority** (over the member) | Interactions | The member — objects when overruled |
| **Livingness** | Can MAIA's read stay open? | …**certain** (about its interpretations) | Memory / time | *No one* — the architecture must defend it |

These are **orthogonal**, which is the rigorous proof they are distinct guards, not facets: a system can be
perfectly *sovereign and still frozen* (the member always decides, but MAIA is certain of its read), or
perfectly *living and still capturing* (always revising, but quietly steering the member's decisions).
Independent failure modes ⇒ both guards required.

Sovereignty has an ally in the room — overrule a member and they feel it. Livingness has none — hand a member
a beautiful, dead classification and they often lean in. So livingness cannot be defended relationally; it
must be built into the architecture.

### The constraint, and its rationale — both must travel

> **Constraint:** MAIA must not convert living patterns into fixed identity classifications.
>
> **Rationale:** Closure feels good and will be rewarded by normal product metrics — but it violates member
> sovereignty by freezing self-understanding. A *silent* constraint will be "improved" by a future builder
> into the very thing it was built to prevent (closure looks like a missing feature users want). **The
> rationale must travel with the guard, or the guard dies.**

(This is the claims-vs-constraints relation from `MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md`, inverted: a constraint
survives *pressure* better than a claim, but loses to *entropy* — so it needs a carried claim, its rationale,
to survive across builders.)

### The three invariants (Kelly)

1. **Silence is not agreement.** A pattern surfaced and ignored gains nothing. Never "surfaced 5× ⇒
   confidence increased." Surfaced 5×, ignored 5× ⇒ confidence unchanged — or weakened.
2. **Renewal requires participation.** A pattern stays alive because the *member* re-engages it, not because
   MAIA repeats it. Authority stays with the member's ongoing relationship to the pattern.
3. **Every pattern ages.** A memory system naturally remembers; a living system naturally *forgets
   certainty.* Not forgetting the event. Not forgetting the conversation. Forgetting confidence in the
   interpretation.

### What decays, and what doesn't (Kelly's decay-layering)

| Layer | Decays? |
|---|---|
| Event happened | No |
| Conversation occurred | No |
| Member once resonated with the interpretation | No |
| Current confidence that the interpretation still fits | **Yes** |

**Engineering extension — seal the row-3 leak** *(Claude Code; proposal):* the *fact* that a member once
resonated is preserved as history, but its **weight as current evidence of fit must decay with everything
else.** Otherwise "you resonated with this before" is a side door that smuggles the decayed confidence right
back in. **Yesterday's agreement isn't today's agreement** — the temporal sibling of invariant 1. Freeze the
historical fact; never the live inference.

**Engineering extension — decay is passive; add an active trigger** *(Claude Code; proposal, tune carefully):*
decay is monotonic and slow, so the most dangerous frozen pattern is the *high-confidence, long-unchallenged*
one that decay has not yet caught. A **staleness trigger** — high confidence + long time since genuine
engagement → MAIA actively re-opens it ("we haven't touched this in a while; does it still fit?") — is the
*active* form of "uncertainty must be deliberately engineered." Caveat: constant self-questioning is its own
failure (see the metric "names uncertainty without losing usefulness"); fire it rarely, on staleness — not
always.

**Engineering note** *(Claude Code):* when certainty decays it must land in *openness*, not in a replacement
verdict. Forgetting that X fits must never harden into being sure that Y fits — it resolves into
held-possibility, or a question. Otherwise decay merely churns one closure into another.

### Build consequence

A memory surfacing must never close:

> ✗ "This is your pattern."
> ✓ "This may be a pattern. Does this still feel true, partly true, or off?"

— and **the member's answer must alter the stored field.** Mechanisms:

- **Challenge affordance** — "not quite," "no," "yes but," "this changed." (invariant 1)
- **Revision write-path** — member correction updates the memory object. (invariant 2)
- **Confidence decay** — per the decay-layering table above. (invariant 3)
- **Contested-memory state** — MAIA can hold "this was surfaced, then rejected / refined by the member."
  (Livingness applied to the revision record itself — the disagreement stays alive, not overwritten clean.)
- **No identity freeze** — patterns stay provisional, never labels.

### Metric consequence

**Do not validate livingness with** (these reward beautiful closure):
engagement · satisfaction · "felt meaningful" · retention · emotional intensity.

**Better signals (Kelly):**

- how often members *successfully* revise MAIA's read
- whether rejected patterns stop resurfacing
- whether old motifs evolve over time
- whether MAIA names uncertainty without losing usefulness
- whether member self-description becomes **more precise, not more categorized**

**Engineering refinements** *(Claude Code; proposal):*

- **Don't optimize revision *volume*** — frequency is ambiguous (a vague MAIA gets revised constantly).
  Optimize revision *efficacy* (does the push durably change the field? = "rejected patterns stop
  resurfacing").
- **Keystone metric:** *self-description more precise, not more categorized.* Precision = richer, more
  particular, the member's own words. Categorization = collapse into fewer, coarser buckets. This single
  signal most directly separates unfolding (livingness) from freezing (closure).
- **The one-session filter:** if a metric can be computed from a single session, it cannot measure
  livingness. Every honest signal is longitudinal — it compares the member to their own past self.

### Elemental-landscape application

- ✗ **Archive:** "Water Jan–Mar, Fire Apr–Jun." A classification history — a completed story.
- ✓ **Conversation:** surface recurrences, tensions, oscillations, unanswered movements, surprising returns
  — and keep them revisable. *Memory as conversation, not memory as archive.*
