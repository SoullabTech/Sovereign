# The Architecture of Shared Understanding

**How coherent individuals create coherent relationships, coherent gatherings, and eventually a more resonant world.**

> **Design law — Conversation exists to produce durable understanding.**
>
> **Build the explicit substrate first; do not name the collective field before reality earns it.**

The Architecture of Shared Understanding describes how coherent individuals create coherent gatherings — not by maximizing conversation, but by creating durable shared understanding. Conversation is transient; understanding is cumulative. The purpose of the platform is not to increase communication, but to help people **create, preserve, and steward** the understanding that allows relationships, teams, families, therapeutic partnerships, and communities to become increasingly coherent over time.

> **The platform cannot create coherence. It can only cultivate the conditions under which coherence becomes more likely to emerge through authentic relationship and shared work.**

*Renamed from "The Collective Experience" (2026-06-18): that title named the hoped-for outcome; "shared understanding" names the thing actually built — see the naming note. This document is the design constitution for that substrate: it has been run through a generativity audit (below) for every refusal and feature, with its two remaining frontiers named rather than hidden.*

**Status:** Architecture reference. Recovered, synthesized, and promoted from a working session on 2026-06-18 (session `local_ea118459`, which evolved out of "Message deletion and channel membership"). This records the *settled understanding*, not the conversation that produced it.

**Claim discipline:** Under `docs/canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md`, almost everything here is **Designed** or **Vision** — not **Live**. This document does **not** authorize a build. Each roadmap slice is its own explicit go. Where a capability already exists in the repo, it is named with a file/table reference so the claim is answerable to runtime fact.

## The central equation

The platform begins with the **person**, not the collective — not because the collective matters less, but because **coherent collectives cannot be manufactured directly.** They emerge from the quality of the people, and the relationships, that compose them. The whole architecture is one progression:

```
Inner Coherence
        ↓
Authentic Relationship
        ↓
Shared Work
        ↓
Shared Understanding      ← this document
        ↓
Collective Coherence
        ↓
A More Resonant World
```

Read as three distinct kinds of statement — keeping them separate is what makes the architecture easy to reason from:

- **Purpose** — help a person cultivate greater **inner coherence**. *(the individual)*
- **Process** — support relationships in creating **durable shared understanding** through shared work. *(what the platform actually does)*
- **Outcome** — more **collective coherence**, and over time **a more resonant world**. *(what may emerge — never manufactured)*

**Shared Work stays in the chain deliberately** — it is the bridge that keeps the architecture from becoming abstract. People rarely reach shared understanding by talking alone; they reach it *while doing something together* — raising a child, sitting in therapy, building a company, serving a community, making a decision. Shared work gives understanding something to crystallize around.

Nowhere does the system try to *create* community. It creates the conditions from which community can arise — optimizing **quality of being before quantity of interaction.** A person enters MAIA not to join a network but to come into greater alignment with themselves; they become more *coherent*, not more *engaged*. Then something natural follows: they begin meeting others doing the same work, and those meetings are different because neither person is mining the other for validation, attention, or identity. **Shared understanding is the hinge** between the individual-and-relational layers above it and the collective layers below it — which is why this document is named for the hinge, not the destination.

This is a different theory of what human technology should cultivate. The dominant social platforms run a closed, extractive loop:

```
Attention → Engagement → Conversation → More Attention
```

This architecture runs an open, cumulative one:

```
Awareness → Relationship → Understanding → Coherence
```

### Foundational principle — causal honesty

> **MAIA does not attempt to manufacture coherence between people. It helps people cultivate coherence within themselves, steward coherence in relationship, and create the conditions in which coherent gatherings can naturally emerge.**

This keeps the causal direction honest. The platform does not *produce* a collective field; it cultivates the conditions from which one might emerge (§7). Manufacturing coherence between people — inferring consensus, synthesizing a group mind, optimizing connection for its own sake — is precisely the move this architecture refuses.

The boundary, stated as a table — what the platform cultivates, and what it must never manufacture in its place:

| The platform cultivates | The platform does **not** produce |
|---|---|
| Attention (as attentiveness / awareness) | Agreement |
| Relationship | Consensus |
| Understanding | Uniformity |
| Coherence | Conformity |

*"Attention" here means attentiveness — the capacity to attend to oneself and to another — not the captured, monetized attention of the extractive loop above.* The bottom row, **coherence not conformity**, is the one most easily misread; "A resonant world is not uniformity" (below) settles it.

### Foundational principle — reciprocity

> **Every collective capability should strengthen individual coherence, and every individual capability should become more valuable in relationship.**

The two directions reinforce rather than compete:

- Reflection becomes dialogue.
- Personal practice becomes shared practice.
- Individual decisions become team agreements.
- Personal insight becomes collective wisdom — through **explicit contribution, not automatic synthesis**.
- Individual stewardship becomes community stewardship.

**Nothing changes category — it simply expands its sphere of participation.** A personal object and a shared object are the same kind of thing at different radii; the crossing between them is an authored human act (provenance, §4; visibility, §6), never an automatic promotion.

### A resonant world is not uniformity

"A more resonant world" deliberately does **not** mean everyone thinking alike. The aim is that people become *sufficiently coherent that meaningful differences can coexist without fragmentation.* Coherence is the capacity to hold difference without collapse — not its erasure. Any feature that manufactures agreement by flattening difference works against the goal.

The word *resonant* is chosen deliberately, and it is best heard musically: an orchestra is coherent precisely because the instruments keep their distinct voices while playing the same piece. Coherence is not the absence of difference — it is difference held in relationship. A platform optimizing for agreement produces a unison; this one aims for harmony.

> *Claim discipline: the progression above is the orienting telos — Vision, not present capability. Today only its first steps have any live substrate (see "Existing Substrate"). Naming it does not build it.*

## One developmental arc: MAIA and Co-lab

This architecture dissolves the apparent separation between MAIA and Co-lab. They are not two adjacent products; they are two layers of one developmental arc, each with a distinct responsibility — and neither claims to produce what only people can create together.

| Layer | Responsibility | Cultivates |
|---|---|---|
| **MAIA** | the inward work — a person coming into alignment with themselves | **inner coherence** |
| **Co-lab** | the relational work — people creating durable shared understanding through shared work | **shared understanding** |
| **Together** | hold the conditions under which coherent gatherings can arise | the *emergence* of **collective coherence** |

MAIA cultivates the person; Co-lab cultivates the understanding between people; collective coherence is what may emerge when both are healthy — never a feature either one ships. This is why the document can begin with the individual and still be an architecture *of the collective*: the causal direction runs inner → relational → shared → collective, and the platform's responsibility ends at *cultivating the conditions*, not producing the result. (See `docs/canon/` on the MAIA ⇄ AIN relationship, and `memory/project_outer_coherence_studio` on MAIA Assist / Studio as the outward half of one ecology.)

## The center of gravity: an inversion

Within that progression, the **collaboration layer** — where people do shared work together — has its own load-bearing inversion:

> **People do not primarily enter Co-lab through channels. They enter through relationships, shared work, and ongoing commitments.**

Almost every collaboration tool — Slack, Teams, Discord, Mattermost — assumes the **message stream is primary** and everything else hangs off it:

```
Conversation
      ↓
Everything else
```

This architecture inverts that:

```
Relationship
      ↓
Shared work
      ↓
Durable understanding
      ↓
Conversation  (supports it)
```

This is a profound inversion, and it is the whole point. It moves the center of gravity of the platform **from communication to coherence**. Communication tools optimize the flow of messages; this optimizes the *accumulation of shared understanding* between people who are in an actual relationship. Conversation does not disappear — it becomes the *servant* of the relationship and its durable work, not the thing the product is organized around.

This inversion is the collaboration-layer expression of the central equation: it is how "Shared Work" produces "Shared Understanding." Everything below elaborates it and the three substrates beneath it.

## The three substrates

This architecture is built from **three substrates**. Naming them explicitly is what keeps it grounded in observable structure rather than inferred abstraction — and they line up with the central equation: the Human substrate carries *inner coherence and authentic relationship*, the Object substrate carries *shared work and understanding*, the Continuity substrate carries what persists toward *coherent gatherings*.

### 1. Human substrate

**People — not users, not accounts. Actual relationships.**

- therapist ↔ client
- founder ↔ team
- parent ↔ child
- collaborators
- family
- organization

These relationships are what define **permissions, responsibilities, and context**. The system's primary objects are relationships; identity is *who you are in relation to whom*, not *which workspace you logged into*. (Elaborated in §2.)

### 2. Object substrate

**The things people intentionally create together — not messages. Objects.**

- decisions
- agreements
- questions
- commitments
- projects
- sessions
- practices
- experiments

**Messages are merely one way objects come into existence.** A conversation may *produce* a decision; the decision is the durable thing, the conversation is the scaffolding. (Elaborated in §3.)

### 3. Continuity substrate

**What persists — not "memory" in the AI sense. Continuity.**

- decisions remain true
- agreements evolve
- questions remain open
- practices accumulate
- commitments complete

This is the thing Slack loses every Monday morning. The stream resets; nothing carries the relationship's understanding forward except human recollection. The continuity substrate is what carries it forward *as explicit structure*. (Operationalized in §4 as Shared Orientation.)

> The three substrates map onto the themes below: **Human → §2 (Relationship before Channel), Object → §3 (Durable Understanding), Continuity → §4 (Shared Orientation).** §§5–9 govern how they are kept sovereign, organized, bounded, and built.

## 1. First Principles

### What it is trying to become

A place for **calm shared work**: where a relationship or group accumulates *explicit shared understanding* over time — without surveillance, engagement pressure, or inferred claims about what anyone believes. The unit of value is not the message; it is the **durable, authored object** a message sometimes produces and that outlives the conversation.

### How it differs from Slack / Teams / Discord

| Conventional collaboration software | This architecture |
|---|---|
| The **message stream** is the center. | The stream is transient; **authored objects** are the center. |
| Manufactures urgency (unread badges, presence, typing, "active now") to drive return visits. | **Restraint is a feature.** Nothing is engineered to pull you back. |
| Optimizes engagement; success = time-in-app, messages-sent. | Optimizes **attention for life**; success = *decreasing* necessity. |
| Auto-summarizes, infers sentiment, generates "team insights." | **No synthesis without consent and evidence.** It never infers what a group believes. |
| Identity is the channel/workspace you're in. | Identity is the **relationship** you're in; channels merely serve it. |
| Memory is a searchable log of everything said. | Continuity is a small set of **explicitly promoted, provenance-bearing objects**. |

### The litmus test

Whenever there is a temptation to build another messaging feature, apply the design law:

> Does this increase **conversation**, or does this increase **durable understanding**?

Those are different products. This one always chooses durable understanding. A feature that only increases conversation — more reactions, more threads, more presence — must justify itself against the durable-understanding test or it does not ship.

## 2. Relationship before Channel — *the Human substrate*

The load-bearing insight, recovered verbatim from the session brief: *"People do not primarily enter Co-lab through channels."*

**Channels support relationships. Relationships are not created by channels.** A channel is a workspace a relationship may use; it is never the thing itself. This inverts the Slack model, where the workspace is primary and relationships are an emergent side effect of who happens to be in the room.

**Design consequence:** the top-level object is the relationship (or the container/field that holds it), not the channel list. Messaging, decisions, sessions, and agreements all hang off the relationship. Permissions and responsibilities derive from the *relationship*, not from channel membership.

**Refusal (recorded), with its generating principle:** *A new relationship model is justified only by a human relationship-type the existing surfaces cannot serve — the test is an unserved relationship-type, never a target count.* No such unserved type has been demonstrated, so the recorded rule holds: *do not introduce a new relationship model.* The existing relational surfaces are sufficient to serve the human relationships in the Human substrate; Relationship Home (§6) **reorganizes around them — it does not add another model.** Whether the existing set should be *consolidated* is an open question (§Open Questions); the answer is never "add a fifth."

---

## 3. Durable Understanding — *the Object substrate*

**Messages are transient.** Most should be allowed to disappear from attention once their moment passes. The platform's job is to let a relationship **gradually accumulate explicit shared understanding** as authored objects: decisions, agreements, open questions, principles, active experiments.

These are **authored objects, not AI synthesis.** A human writes them, or a human promotes a moment into one. The system stores, surfaces, and searches them; it does not generate them on the group's behalf.

This is the **"Team Object Lifecycle"** recovered from the session: the move from *"what was said this week"* (a transient stream) to *"the decisions made this week"* and *"the still-open questions"* (durable shared understanding). That move — conversation → authored object — is a **human act**, consistent with Reveal → Curate → Commit (`memory/project_studio_reveal_curate_commit.md`): the system may reveal and organize; the crossing into a committed shared object stays a person's decision.

## 4. Shared Orientation — *the Continuity substrate, operationalized*

This is the most important reframing in this document.

The thing a healthy team primarily needs is **not shared memory. It is shared orientation.**

"Shared memory" implies a stored record of the past. "Shared orientation" is the live answer to *where we are and where we're going* — and that is what actually lets people act coherently together. The ledger of decisions and open questions that this architecture builds is, properly understood, an **orientation engine**.

### Orientation primitives

A relationship or group is oriented when it can answer, from explicit objects:

- **What are we trying to do?** (current focus)
- **What decisions currently govern us?** (decisions that remain true)
- **What questions are alive?** (open questions)
- **What experiments are underway?** (active experiments)
- **Where is attention naturally flowing?** — **member-named, never inferred.** This is what the group has *explicitly* declared as current focus, never a heatmap of who-typed-what. Inferring attention from activity is surveillance wearing an orientation costume; it is refused (§8).

### The hard distinction (do not get this wrong)

**Not allowed:** *"What the team believes."*
**Allowed:** *"Recorded decisions and open questions."*

The system must **never** infer beliefs/consensus/mood, generate consensus no one authored, perform hidden synthesis, or auto-promote anything to shared standing. Orientation is composed of *explicitly authored objects*, full stop. This follows `docs/canon/RIGHT_TO_REMAIN_UNPOSSESSED.md` (some inferences are violations *by formation*, not exposure — forming "what the team believes" is itself the harm) and the Consent Gates principle that *future commitments require authorship as their warrant, not evidence* (`docs/canon/MAIA_CONSENT_GATES.md`).

### The provenance contract

Every shared object MUST carry:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **author** | the member (or named role) who created it. Never "the team," never "MAIA." |
| **timestamp** | created_at, and updated_at on revision. |
| **source** | how it came to exist — manually authored, or promoted-from-a-moment-with-consent. Never "inferred." |
| **visibility** | its scope — private / relationship / team. **Nothing is "collective" by default.** Widening scope is an explicit human act. |

An object that cannot state all four does not get shared standing. Continuity over time (decisions stay true, agreements evolve, questions stay open, practices accumulate, commitments complete) is expressed as the *lifecycle* of these objects, not as an accumulating log of messages.

## 5. Member Sovereignty

Shared orientation sits on top of strict personal sovereignty. Four memory domains exist, and **their boundaries are explicit and never silently crossed**:

1. **Personal MAIA memory** — the member's own atoms / developmental memory. Member-owned. Never flows outward without an explicit act.
2. **Collaboration history** — shared objects and messages within a relationship or team. Scoped to its participants.
3. **Practitioner records** — clinical/consultation material a practitioner holds (e.g. `comms_*`, `studio_decisions`, caseload). Held in stewardship, not shared back automatically.
4. **Sanctuary** — material spoken under Sanctuary Mode. **Absolute boundary: never indexed, never surfaced into collaboration or collective memory, under any circumstances, including by request during the session** (project invariant; `docs/canon/RIGHT_TO_REMAIN_UNPOSSESSED.md`).

The cardinal rule: **nothing flows from a more-private domain into a more-shared one without an explicit, authored crossing.** Personal → collaboration is a human act. Sanctuary → anything is *impossible by construction*.

**Member Data Center ("My Data") must make sovereignty visible**, not merely possible. The member can **see** what is held (by domain), **export** it, **delete** it, and **understand the four boundaries** rendered legibly. Governed by data-minimization as a first-class discipline — *the burden of proof rests with collection, not omission* (`memory/feedback_data_minimization_as_governance.md`).

> **Honest grounding:** the export/delete *backend* already exists (`app/api/members/export-data`, `app/api/members/delete-account`) but is buried in `app/studio/settings`, with **no dedicated surface, no memory-visibility inspector, and no domain-boundary view**. Slice 1 makes existing sovereignty *visible and legible* — mostly a surfacing job.

## 6. Relationship Home

The emerging architecture: **messages become one tab, not the home.**

The center of a relationship is not its chat log. It is the living, authored picture of the relationship:

- current focus
- agreements
- sessions
- decisions
- practices
- open questions
- continuity (where we were, where we are)

Messaging is one tab among these — available, but not the gravitational center. This is the structural expression of the inversion, §2, §3, and §4: when you open a relationship, you see *what it has become and where it is oriented*, not *what was most recently typed*.

**Refusals (recorded):**
- Do **not** build **client-facing** relationship views until consent and visibility infrastructure exists. A practitioner seeing a summary the client never consented to share is a sovereignty violation.
- Do **not** merge Relationship Home into Session Room / LiveKit work, except as **context**. The 1:1 sovereign room (`app/studio/session-room`) is relationship *infrastructure* and informs this, but Relationship Home is a separate architectural object and must not be entangled with the realtime-media build.

## 7. The Collective Field

The important conclusion, recovered and preserved:

> **We do not currently have a collective field. Do not build one. Do not claim one.**

Canonically the collective field is **frozen** — Category 1 (preserved direction; held, not authorized), not Category 6 (live). The blocker is not engineering; it is constitutional: a shared field requires an **authority structure to confer collective standing**, and that authority layer is *vacant*. *"The collective is not a missing feature; it is a vacant-authority-layer problem,"* and the wall (`crossing_allowed = FALSE`) blocks automatic promotion from personal to collective standing by default.

Therefore:

- **Build the explicit substrate first** — the member-authored orientation objects, with provenance, scoped and consented (§§3–5).
- **A collective field, if it ever emerges, would emerge *from* shared understanding — not the other way around.** This is the same discipline applied throughout the project (migrations, memory architecture, Session Room): build the explicit, observable structure; let larger phenomena emerge from it; never invert that order.
- Only after long-term evidence that the substrate behaves coherently — **and** the authority question is constitutionally answered — should any "collective-field" concept be *reconsidered*. Until then, "field," "resonance," "what the field knows" remain **metaphorical architecture language**, not runtime claims. Metaphor after measurement, never before.

**Refusal (recorded):** Do not introduce a new collective-field model. (See `docs/canon/FIS_FIELD_STATE_PRIMITIVE.md`, which authorizes no build, no wiring, no surfacing, no inference.)

## 8. Things We Refuse

The permanent "no" list. These are not deferred features; they are commitments *against*. **Restraint is a feature.**

- **presence pressure** — no "active now," no implied availability debt
- **typing indicators**
- **engagement scores** — no metric ranking a person by participation
- **seen-by surveillance** — no read receipts, no "seen by 4"
- **trending algorithms** — no ranking by activity
- **activity nudges** — no "you haven't posted in a while"
- **streaks** — no gamified continuity
- **notification escalation** — no escalating pings to manufacture response
- **manipulative ranking** — no ordering designed to capture attention rather than serve it

Each refusal traces to the Oath (*never make a human life narrower than before the companion arrived*), the Sovereignty Invariants (*reduce the system's psychological centrality over time*), and the Attention Doctrine. The litmus test (§1) is the day-to-day enforcement: *increase conversation, or increase durable understanding?*

### Where current surfaces diverge (reconciliation debt — honest note)

Per the project's reconciliation discipline (`memory/feedback_declarative_drift_reconciliation.md`), the "no" list is only credible if it names where reality currently diverges. A repo audit (2026-06-18) found three instances to reconcile — **not** to quietly grandfather in:

1. **Presence** — `app/fields/[field]/presence/page.tsx`, plus `lastSeen` in the admin substrate monitor and "Online / Last seen" status in the model-studio comms UI. **Action:** review before any reaches the shared surface; presence-as-pressure is refused, an explicit co-presence *ritual* may be a different object — to be decided, not assumed.
2. **Engagement %** — `app/dashboard/overview` computes/displays it but explicitly subordinates it ("Human agency > engagement metrics"). **Status:** admin/ops analytics, out of member-facing scope. Keep it there; never a member-facing or ranking signal.
3. **Activity nudges** — `app/api/studio/field/pulse` surfaces stale draft/casting items; gentle, self-directed ("a living instrument, not an engagement engine"). **Status:** compliant by the boundary test — *a refused nudge is system-initiated pressure to act or return; a person's own parked work shown only when they choose to look is not a nudge.* It stays on the allowed side precisely because it is never pushed; adding notification or escalation would cross the line.

None are in the shared surface today. The refusal is the governing law for what we build next; these three are the reconciliation list for what already exists.

## The generativity audit

A constitution is not valuable because it is internally consistent. It is valuable because it lets people derive *new* decisions without asking the founders — long after today's implementation details have changed. So the test is not merely "does each section follow from the principles?" but **"is the document generative?"** Every feature, refusal, and future proposal is held to three questions:

1. **Can it be derived?** Could a new contributor reach this decision from the principles alone — or does it depend on history that lives only in a founder's head?
2. **Would two people derive the same answer independently?** If two engineers read the document separately, would they make the same choice? If not, something is still implicit.
3. **Does it eliminate more rules than it creates?** A fundamental principle collapses many rules into one reason. A section that adds exceptions instead of reducing them is not yet fundamental.

### Derivation — what passes

Most of the architecture passes cleanly: each decision traces to the opening in one or two steps.

| "Why is there …?" | Derivation | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| **a Decisions / Open-Questions ledger?** | Shared understanding must be *created and preserved*, not inferred → it lives in durable authored objects (§3) → the smallest such object is a decision or an open question (Slice 2). | thesis → §3 → §9 |
| **a Member Data Center?** | Shared understanding rests on individual coherence and sovereignty → the person must see and govern what is held → sovereignty made *visible* (§5, Slice 1). | thesis → §5 |
| **no presence dots / typing indicators?** | The purpose is durable understanding, not maximized conversation → presence manufactures attention and availability-pressure (the extractive loop) → refused (§8). | thesis → litmus → §8 |
| **a Relationship Home, not a channel list?** | People enter through relationships, not channels (Human substrate) → the relationship, not the message stream, is the home (§2, §6). | thesis → §2 → §6 |
| **no claim of a collective field?** | Coherence between people *emerges*; it is not manufactured (causal honesty) → the platform cannot claim to produce what it only creates conditions for → frozen (§7). | thesis → causal honesty → §7 |
| **search only over explicit objects?** | Understanding lives in authored objects, and private/Sanctuary material is never indexed (§5) → search covers explicit shared objects only (Slice 3). | thesis → §5 → §9 |
| **provenance on every shared object?** | Nothing becomes shared by inference, only by an authored crossing (reciprocity; §4) → each object must name author / source / visibility or it has no shared standing. | thesis → reciprocity → §4 |

If a future proposal cannot be derived this way, that is the signal to reject it — or to revise the constitution deliberately and in the open — never to smuggle it in.

### The inversion audit — what a conventional platform would optimize instead

Naming the conventional default each principle refuses makes the philosophy concrete, and shows the architecture differs in *kind*, not degree:

| A conventional platform optimizes | This architecture instead |
|---|---|
| Increase engagement | Increase coherence |
| Maximize conversation | Preserve understanding |
| Capture attention | Cultivate attentiveness |
| Infer people | Let people author themselves |
| Predict behavior | Support reflection |
| Optimize activity | Steward relationships |
| Manufacture consensus | Record authored decisions |
| Retain everything | Steward the minimum |

### Where the document is not yet generative

Honesty is the point of the audit. Four decisions did **not** pass all three questions as originally written; two are now closed in-text, two are named frontiers:

1. **"No fifth relationship model" (§2)** — *failed Q1/Q2: a count is not derivable; two people could not independently arrive at "four."* **Closed:** §2 now states the generating principle — *a new model is justified only by a relationship-type the existing surfaces cannot serve; the test is an unserved type, never a count.* "No new model" now derives.
2. **The activity-nudge boundary (§8)** — *failed Q2: "no activity nudges" left the field-pulse case "arguably compliant," so two people could split.* **Closed:** §8 now carries the boundary — *a refused nudge is system-initiated pressure to act or return; a person's own parked work shown when they choose to look is not a nudge.* The case now resolves identically for everyone.
3. **The four memory domains (§5)** — *Q1 partial, left open.* The *boundary principle* (privacy scopes never silently crossed) is fundamental; the *specific four* (personal / collaboration / practitioner / Sanctuary) are a contingent instantiation of today's product — "practitioner records" exists because the Studio does. A revisable instantiation, not a first principle.
4. **The promotion / authorship gesture (Open Question #3)** — *Q2 open, left open.* "Crossings are authored human acts" is generative about *that* an act is required, but not yet about *what counts as sufficient authorship* — two implementers could build different consent surfaces. This is the gating question for Slice 2.

### Verdict

With gaps 1 and 2 closed, every refusal and feature in this document is derivable from the opening in one or two steps. The two remaining frontiers (the contingent domain list; the authorship gesture) are named, not hidden. That is the threshold the standard names: *a document people can use to make good decisions after today's implementation is gone.* It earns **constitutional architecture** — with its implicit edges marked rather than buried.

### A note on the larger arc

Watching this evolve, *Shared Understanding* reads as the middle volume of a consistent trilogy, whether or not it is ever written as one:

- **Elemental Alchemy** — how an *individual* becomes more coherent (the human developmental model).
- **The Architecture of Shared Understanding** — how coherent people create coherent relationships and communities (the social developmental model).
- **AIN / MAIA** — the technology that supports both without replacing either.

Each stands alone; together they answer progressively larger questions without changing the underlying philosophy: individual coherence → shared understanding → the software that cultivates both.
