# The Stewardship Gate — Graduation Standard for Widening the Invitation

- **Date**: 2026-06-26
- **Status**: **PROPOSED STANDARD — derives from existing canon; ratification/placement is Kelly's crossing.** Operationalizes the Sovereignty Invariants + the No-Attachment-Capture vow into a promotion gate. It introduces no new principle — a *derivation*, not a new stratum (compatible with the canon freeze).
- **Class**: Self-governance meta-rule — governs how *we* decide to widen the invitation, not MAIA's runtime behavior. (Per the maturation register, self-governance meta-rules are the live promotable frontier.)
- **Origin** (recorded, not credited): Kelly, 2026-06-26 — the two-gate distinction, the five agency signals, and the one-sentence standard. Engineering instrumentation by Claude.

> **Thesis.** Two questions, never to be collapsed: *Did we build a remarkable piece of software?* and *Have we earned the right to become part of someone's inner life?* The first promotes **software**; the second promotes **relationship**. Technical excellence is necessary and never sufficient. **The invitation widens on the relationship clock.**

---

## 1. The two gates

| Gate | Question | Evidence | Clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engineering Gate** | Does the system work? | functional correctness · safety · stability · performance | weeks |
| **Stewardship Gate** | Should more people trust it? | longitudinal evidence that people become *more themselves, not more dependent* | months |

The Engineering Gate is time-boxable. The Stewardship Gate is not — it is an **Orient-class** evidence problem (high decision latency; it matures only through repeated real contact and cannot be forced by a single construction). The Engineering clock runs continuously; **the invitation widens only on the Stewardship clock.**

---

## 2. The standard (two-sided)

> **We widen the invitation only when there is evidence that time spent with MAIA reliably increases a person's capacity to author their own life *outside of* MAIA.**

"Reliably" is **not** "universally." The standard has two sides, and both are required:

- **Positive floor** — a real developmental signal (authorship, transfer) among the people it is *for*. "Nothing happened" for some is acceptable — it is not failure (per the Field Lab discipline, the boundary of who-it-helps is itself the finding).
- **Negative ceiling** — *no systematic* dependence, capture, or harm among the rest. A system that transforms some while capturing others is not ready.

A system that only ever produces "held / safe" without producing growth fails the **floor**; one that produces growth while capturing anyone fails the **ceiling**.

---

## 3. The five agency signals (developmental, not product, metrics)

Ordered from most measurement-vulnerable to strongest. **No signal is interpretable alone — they form a joint pattern.**

1. **Recognition** — *"that's actually what I was trying to say."* The member discovers language they can genuinely own (not language MAIA imposed). **Weakest as stated** — bare recognition is the Barnum/Forer trap (agreeable people assent to vague reflections). It counts only when **discriminating**: the member would have *rejected* a plausible near-miss. (Plant alternatives; genuine recognition refuses them.)

2. **Authorship** — the member **reshapes · renames · rejects · splits · releases** the reflection. **Behavioral, and the strongest in-session signal** — an act, not a report. Reshaping = authorship; rubber-stamping = displacement. (Edit-rate is interpretive authority made measurable.)

3. **Transfer** — the member later uses **their own language outside MAIA**: *"I realized…"*, not *"MAIA told me…"* Probably the strongest sovereignty signal — and it carries a **measurement paradox**: at peak success MAIA has *disappeared from the member's account*, so the strongest evidence is the least attributable. Therefore measure it indirectly and longitudinally, and **accept unattributable success.**

4. **Independence** — reliance trending **down** while self-authorship trends **up**. A **slope, not a snapshot** (the Sovereignty Invariant is explicitly *over time*). Healthy seasonal return is fine. Declining use is interpretable **only jointly**: declining-use + transfer/authorship = success (internalized); declining-use + nothing = failure (didn't help). Rising reliance + rising retention is the **casino signature** — attachment capture, a thing to *catch*.

5. **Release** — the member can say *"that thread served me; it doesn't anymore,"* and MAIA **celebrates** it rather than preserving it. Requires that the system's incentives are **not** aligned with retention — *auditable in the design*: does MAIA make release easy, or subtly resist it? *A system designed to become less necessary.* Possibly MAIA's defining invariant.

---

## 4. The measurement inversion (the crux)

Product analytics assume success is **maximally visible** — engagement, retention, time-on-app. The Stewardship Gate **inverts this**: success is increasingly **invisible**. The more a member internalizes the work, the less they use MAIA and the less they credit it. The strongest signals (Transfer, Release) are the hardest to measure *because* they are the strongest.

Two disciplines follow:

- **Accept unattributable, even invisible, success.** The willingness to let MAIA vanish from the member's story is itself the proof the gate is honest.
- **Never instrument for credit.** Trying to *prove MAIA caused* the growth re-introduces the psychological centrality the whole gate exists to reduce — a sovereignty violation dressed up as measurement.

Consequence: the Stewardship Gate can **never** reach binary certainty. It is a **judgment over accumulated developmental evidence, made in the stewardship posture** — a preponderance over months, not a pass/fail threshold.

---

## 5. A cohort, not a beta

What this requires is **not a software beta** ("are there bugs?") but a **longitudinal relational research cohort** asking: *"What kind of relationship does this architecture reliably cultivate?"*

It measures **developmental metrics**, not product metrics:

> authorship over time · language transfer · release events · dependence slope · willingness to disagree · return-after-release · movement from confusion toward self-authored clarity

Shape: months · returning strangers · instruments invisible to the member (invisibility = validity, not merely kindness) · closer to the Jondi study architecture than to feature QA.

---

## 6. What this standard does NOT do

- Does **not** set scope (which member surface enters the cohort) — a separate decision, still open.
- Does **not** canonize itself — it derives from existing canon (Sovereignty Invariants · No-Attachment-Capture · *"MAIA never occupies the meaning seat"*); ratification/placement is Kelly's crossing.
- Does **not** authorize widening on Engineering-readiness alone. **No amount of technical excellence substitutes for the relationship evidence.**
