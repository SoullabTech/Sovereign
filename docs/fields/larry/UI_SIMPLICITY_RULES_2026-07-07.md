# Larry Field — UI Simplicity Rules

**Status:** CANDIDATE design rules. 2026-07-07. Artifact 2 of 3 (with [Experience Map + Build Phases](./LARRY_FIELD_SPEC_2026-07-07.md)).
**One-line compass:** *Build the smallest inhabitable version of Larry's Field that feels simple, elegant, intuitive, and in harmony with his evolving work.*

## Governing principles

1. **World Design principle:** *The world presents what people have chosen to bring into relationship. It does not narrate who they are becoming.* Show evidence (shared / chose / brought), never an interpretation of who someone *is*.
2. **Harmony:** every part embodies the same way of working. If the practice values reflection over prescription and emergence over rigid plans, the interface must too.
3. **The standing heuristic for every feature:** *Does this make the world simpler, deeper, and more harmonious — or merely more capable?* A mature developmental world often becomes **more powerful because it becomes quieter.**
4. **Unitive, not all-in-one.** *The participant never experiences platform capabilities — they experience a continuous developmental relationship. The practitioner never experiences platform management — they experience stewardship of their practice.* Every capability (video, messaging, scheduling, transcripts, practices, MAIA) must disappear into one of those two experiences. Simple = one continuous experience, not few features.
5. **Transitions are the product.** The magic is not the Portal, the Co-Lab, or the Studio — it is how effortlessly someone moves among them without feeling they've left the relationship. Design the *seams*, not the rooms. (Portal → *Who am I becoming?* · Co-Lab → *What is becoming possible between us?* · Studio → *How do I steward this relationship?* — one ecology, three responsibilities.)

**THE PRIMARY TEST (elevated above all others):** *Does the person feel they're continuing one relationship, or entering another application?* If they feel a boundary, the architecture has leaked. Corollary failure modes: a participant asking *"which module do I open?"*, or Larry feeling he is *managing software rather than entering his practice*.

**Learning Journey — artifact, not narrative.** A Learning Journey is a *shared memory of lived recognitions*, authored by the participant's own choices — never an AI-authored developmental story. Show counts, dates, and saved moments: *"you've returned to this practice six times," "you noticed this in March, May, and today," "these are the moments you chose to save."* Never *"Stage 4," "your attachment style is…," "you've integrated forgiveness."*

## What appears first (by responsibility, never a dashboard)

- **Member/student:** *their own practice.* On open — the thread they're living, the return question ("what happened?"), the next reflection. Not a menu, not "Larry's app," not analytics. They should think *"I'm returning to my practice,"* not *"I'm opening software."*
- **Larry:** *his living body of work.* On open — the people and practices alive in the field, the consent-shaped view of what members chose to share. He should think *"I'm entering my practice,"* not *"I need to manage my platform."*
- **Facilitator:** *only consented shares,* scoped to their stewardship. Nothing a member didn't put there.

## What stays hidden (progressive disclosure)

The **architecture is never the UI.** These exist but are revealed on demand / by responsibility, never as the front door:
- The module inventory (Soul Portrait, MAIA, Studio, Resources, Community, Courses, Practices, Cohorts, Journals, Events) surfaced as a named system. The member experiences a *coherent journey*, not a feature list — otherwise it reads as an LMS or an all-in-one business suite.
- Settings, permissions, composition controls (Larry's, in Pro Studio — not in the member's world).
- Analytics and field-movement views (Larry's, opt-in, consent-bounded — never ambient).

## What never appears (hard prohibitions)

- **Interpretations of a person's internal state or development.** No *"you've integrated,"* *"Michael recognized the pattern himself,"* *"you're at stage 3."* These claim to know what the system cannot and must not. (Facts-not-interpretation edge; Cultural Sovereignty / Invariant 14.)
- **Engagement / retention mechanics.** No streaks, no attention-demanding notifications, no *"needs outreach,"* no activity telemetry from private sessions. Care that originates in metrics is attachment-capture in a care costume; the *"I'd welcome a check-in"* signal must originate from the member.
- **MAIA impersonating Larry**, or reading a member *through* Larry's developmental map.
- **AIN / the architecture as the subject.** The member is in *Larry's world*, not "on a platform."

## Harmony rules (the interface as teaching)

- Values reflection → the UI does not push notifications demanding attention.
- Values emergence → the journey is not a checklist everyone completes in the same order.
- Values relationship over performance → no leaderboards, scores, or performance framing.
- The Field is **never "finished"** — it matures as Larry's work evolves; someone joining in five years meets accumulated wisdom, not replaced material.

## The success test (higher than "the UI was intuitive")

> *"I never really thought about the software. It just felt like Larry's work was always there when I needed it."*

The technology has disappeared into the relationship, the practice, and the rhythm of life. The person remembers the journey, not the interface.
