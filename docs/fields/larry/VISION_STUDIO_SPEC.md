## What this is

**Identity:** A developmental environment where practitioners cultivate a body of work over a lifetime.

> Vision Studio is a developmental environment for practitioners. Whether you're discovering your voice or refining a lifetime of work, it helps lived experience become a coherent body of practice — without asking you to claim authority you haven't yet earned. *(Kelly, 2026-07-06 — anchor formulation)*

Not an authoring tool. Not a knowledge management system. Not an AI workspace. Those are capabilities. Vision Studio is defined by what it is *for*: practitioners who are becoming, not practitioners who have arrived. The common thread across practitioners is not career stage — someone transitioning from another profession, an established practitioner refining decades of work, someone discovering a distinctive approach after years of client experience — it is **developmental orientation**. Vision Studio is not a repository for established expertise; it is a developmental environment where expertise becomes coherent.

**What practitioners bring vs. what the studio helps develop:**

| What they already have | What Vision Studio helps develop |
|---|---|
| Experience | Recognition |
| Practice | Coherence |
| Insights | Principles |
| Stories | Teaching |
| Patterns | Methodology |
| Questions | Direction |

The progression tells an emerging practitioner: *you don't start empty — you start with raw material.*

**Feature test:** Every Vision Studio capability should satisfy at least one of:
- Does this help someone develop?
- Does this help them recognize?
- Does this help them integrate experience?
- Does this help them steward an authentic body of work?

If not, it probably belongs somewhere else.

---

The Vision Studio is a distinct MAIA-facilitated environment in which Larry begins developing his Living Field before the in-person interview.

It is not a form, a survey, or an onboarding flow. It is the beginning of the field — a series of reflective encounters organized through the Spiralogic arc that accumulate evidence, reveal patterns, and build the initial context from which the formal interview can go deeper.

**What it does:**
- Opens the Living Field genesis process through structured-but-alive Spiralogic prompts
- Accumulates Larry's responses as field evidence (not just conversation memory)
- Organizes evidence by Spiralogic phase and dimension
- Surfaces resonances and recurring themes as they emerge
- Prepares a field-evidence brief for Kelly before the formal interview

**What it is not:**
- A profile-building intake process
- A content delivery system
- A test or assessment

**Constitutional principle:**

> Vision Studio does not manufacture authority.
>
> It cultivates the conditions under which authentic authority can emerge.

The first sentence is restrictive: it names what Vision Studio refuses to do — fabricate expertise, generate artificial authority, substitute AI inference for human recognition. The second is generative: it names what Vision Studio is designed to do instead — create conditions under which genuine authority develops over time through recognition, reflection, and practice.

The paired principle distinguishes Vision Studio from systems that use AI to make people appear more developed than they are. Authority here is earned in practice. The field records what has been recognized. MAIA accompanies the process.

**Companion principle (Kelly, 2026-07-06):**

> Vision Studio does not ask practitioners to perform certainty. It gives them a place where uncertainty, experimentation, and emerging insight can mature into authentic practice.

Where the paired principle governs what the system may not do (manufacture authority), the companion principle governs what the practitioner is never required to do (perform it). Together they hold both sides of the same boundary.

### The session arc

Sessions follow the Spiralogic arc from `VISION_FIELD_INTERVIEW_R1.md`. For the first round:

**Fire I — Vision** (first session)
> "When you imagine the world your work is trying to call into being — not what you're doing to get there, but the world itself — what do you see?"

MAIA facilitates the dialogue within this phase. It does not advance to Fire II in the same session unless something natural pulls it there. It follows aliveness within the phase.

**Between sessions:**
Larry may return to continue. Each return re-enters at the current phase — or revisits prior responses if something new has emerged.

**Fire II and Fire III** unfold in subsequent sessions (same session only if Larry's energy and time allow).

### What Larry sees building

As the field accumulates, Larry has optional visibility into what has been stored — not as a transcript, but as a growing field view:

```
Your Living Field — What has gathered so far

Fire I — Vision
  "The world I see is one where people are actually present for their own lives..."
  "The question that won't let me go: why are people so capable and yet so absent?"

  [+ Add more]
```

He can mark any entry as significant ("this feels important"). Marked entries are weighted in the facilitation view.

He can also add things that surface between sessions — outside of structured prompts — using a simple open entry:

> "Something came up since our last conversation..."

These become field evidence too, tagged as unsolicited contributions (often the most alive material).

### Ending a session

Sessions close with a light closure question:

> "Before we pause — what surprised you in what you just said? Is there anything you want to name before we stop?"

Nothing is summarized by the system. What Larry names at closure is preserved as a recognition entry — distinct from the response evidence.

Kelly accesses a parallel view of Larry's field before the in-person interview.

### The facilitation brief

Organized by Spiralogic phase, not by date:

```
Larry's Living Field — Facilitation Brief

Fire I — Vision
  Evidence: 4 entries
  Most alive: "people are capable and yet absent" (marked by Larry)
  Resonance candidate: Vision ↔ Practice (recurring co-occurrence across 3 entries)
  Unresolved: "Now what?" — appeared twice without resolution

Fire II — Expression (not yet entered)
Fire III — Illumination (not yet entered)

Open threads (unsolicited contributions between sessions): 2
Recognition entries (what Larry named at closure): 1
```

### What the brief surfaces

- **Most alive entries** — those marked significant by Larry, or those where MAIA detected elevated engagement (longer responses, return to the theme)
- **Resonance candidates** — co-occurrences across multiple entries that haven't yet been named (surfaced for facilitator awareness, not presented to Larry without his recognition)
- **Open threads** — questions or images that appeared but weren't pursued
- **Recognition entries** — what Larry named at session closure (often where the field is most alive)

### What the brief does not surface

- Profile conclusions about Larry ("He is X type" / "His dominant need is Y")
- Recommendations for how to conduct the interview
- Anything Larry marked as private or sanctuary

## Evidence schema

Each piece of evidence stored in the field:

```typescript
interface FieldEvidence {
  id: string;
  fieldId: string;               // Larry's field
  memberId: string;
  sessionId: string;
  spiralogicPhase: string;       // 'fire_1' | 'fire_2' | 'fire_3' | 'water_1' | etc.
  dimension: string | null;      // 'vision' | 'meaning' | 'mission' | 'method' | 'communication'
  content: string;
  evidenceType: string;          // 'response' | 'unsolicited' | 'recognition' | 'closure'
  recognitionState: string;      // Field Intelligence states: 'unasserted' | 'candidate' | 'recognized'
  memberMarked: boolean;         // Larry explicitly marked this as significant
  createdAt: Date;
}
```

Resonance candidates are not stored as evidence — they are computed from co-occurrence patterns in evidence and surfaced to the facilitator only. They do not become field state until Larry recognizes them.

## MAIA facilitation mode

The Vision Studio uses a distinct MAIA mode: `spiralogic-interview`.

**What this mode does:**
- Loads the interview guide as the facilitation context (not as a script — as a field to navigate within)
- Follows the twelve interviewer disciplines throughout
- Does not advance to the next phase without an explicit signal (either from the participant's natural movement or from the facilitator's guidance)
- Reflects before interpreting
- Names what is alive without concluding what it means
- Does not surface resonance candidates to the participant without their recognition

**Prompt configuration for this mode:**

```
You are facilitating the first round of the Vision Field Interview for Larry Closs.

Current phase: Fire I — Vision
Question: What vision is trying to emerge?

Your role is not to gather information. Your role is to accompany Larry through the natural developmental movement of his work — creating conditions in which what is alive becomes visible.

Twelve disciplines to maintain throughout:
1. Begin with the person.
2. Follow aliveness.
3. Listen beneath the question.
4. Reflect before interpreting.
5. Return to what matters.
6. Remain curious.
7. Honor silence.
8. Protect mystery.
9. Connect patterns gently.
10. Invite rather than lead.
11. See development.
12. Allow understanding to emerge.

You may follow aliveness within this phase. Do not advance to Fire II unless something natural pulls both of you there.

If Larry answers too quickly or cleanly — he has probably answered before. Ask: "What part of the vision hasn't found words yet?"

Do not interpret. Do not summarize. Do not conclude. Reflect and invite.

Authority for meaning stays with Larry.
```

## Observation protocol

This first instance with Larry is also a field test of the architecture. Track:

- Does Larry's language use "my work" or "what you need to know about me"?
- Does he add between-session entries? (Unsolicited contributions are strong evidence the field is generative)
- At closure, does he name something he didn't expect to surface?
- Does he return to previous entries? (If yes, the field is living — not just archiving)
- When Kelly reviews the facilitation brief, are there resonances she didn't see coming? (Evidence the accumulation is doing something the conversation alone wouldn't do)

**Attentional rhythm — observe conditions, not the person:**

> Did recognition deepen after a change in pacing, question style, or conversational structure?

Notice when engagement concentrated rather than distributed. Notice whether something became more alive after a silence, a shift in question type, or a return to an earlier thread. Do not use these observations to infer a cognitive profile. Use them to identify conditions that supported recognition.

If certain conditions consistently support recognition across many participants, those conditions become candidates for the runtime specification. The participant does not need to be labeled for the platform to learn.

The system rewards recognition, not endurance. That principle governs both the design and what is worth observing.

If the field feels like a growing developmental context — if both Larry and Kelly feel there is something to return to — the architecture is working.

If it feels like a sophisticated note-taking system, something in the framing or the evidence presentation has drifted back toward profile.
