## Verdicts

`VERIFIED EXACT` · `VERIFIED VARIANT/TRANSLATION` · `PARAPHRASE/ADAPTED` ·
`MISATTRIBUTED` · `UNVERIFIED`

## Evidence standard

Primary text or authoritative edition first; official artist/source pages where
applicable; reputable scholarly/provenance research second. Quote sites may
locate a lead but never close a finding.


## Governing doctrine for misattributed quotations

Authorial ruling, 2026-09-01. Do not automatically remove, and do not
automatically reattribute. Apply in order:

1. True source found + manuscript wording is genuinely that source's wording +
   the quotation still does the same editorial work → **REATTRIBUTE**.
2. True source found, but changing the author materially changes why the
   quotation is there → **AUTHORIAL JUDGMENT**.
3. The familiar wording is a mash-up, distortion, quote-site compression, or
   later paraphrase → **REMOVE as a quotation**. Do not "repair" it by attaching
   a prestigious source to approximately similar language.
4. Underlying idea is legitimate but the wording is not → optionally recast as
   prose or as a clear `Adapted from…`, only where the idea is load-bearing.
5. No defensible source → **REMOVE**.

The audit stays findings-only until the full census is complete. Do not repair
the manuscript one quotation at a time: the census may reveal duplicates,
shared-source patterns, competing variants, or cases where one ruling changes
another. Sequence is census → authorial adjudication batch → one application of
all approved repairs.

## Adjudicated

### MISATTRIBUTED

**L852 · Ch5 · Einstein — "Reality is merely an illusion, albeit a very persistent one."**
Distortion of the verified 21 March 1955 Besso condolence letter, which reads:
"For us believing physicists, the separation between past, present and future
has only the meaning of an illusion, albeit a tenacious one." The circulating
version changes the subject from *time* to *reality* — a material change of
meaning, and the meaning Ch5 appears to be drawing on. Popular form unverified
to any Einstein text.

**L1253 · Ch6 · Buddha — "You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love and affection."**
Traced to **Sharon Salzberg**, *Woman of Power* (1989); in print 2001. It
reverses the sense of the Udāna passage, where the point is that others hold
themselves dear too, so we should extend our regard *outward*. The popular
version turns it inward.
*Consequential note:* Salzberg is already quoted in Ch5, and *Lovingkindness*
already appears in the Ch5 bibliography. The actual author is an established
source in this manuscript.

**L1758 · Ch8 · Socrates — "To know, is to know that you know nothing. That is the meaning of true knowledge."**
The second sentence is **Robert C. Solomon**, *Introducing Philosophy* (1989) —
his gloss on Socrates, not Socrates. *Apology* 21d reads closer to "I don't
pretend to know what I don't know." The epigraph fuses a paraphrase with a
modern commentator's description and credits both to Socrates.

**L1939 · Ch9 · Buddha — "When you realize how perfect everything is, you will tilt your head back and laugh at the sky."**
No canonical source. Earliest traces are a 2004 forum post and a 2005 blog post.
Possible distant derivation from **Longchenpa** (1308–1364), *The Great
Perfection's Self-Liberation in the Nature of Mind*, ch. 1: "Since everything is
but an illusion… one might as well burst out laughing!"

**L1531 · Ch7 · "Chinese Proverb" — "The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago…"**
Not Chinese in any documented sense. Earliest strong match: Cleveland city
councilman George W. White, *Cleveland Plain Dealer*, 1967 (who disclaimed
credit). The "Chinese proverb" label first appears in a Nebraska newspaper,
1985. Variants circulated through North American newspapers 1967–1985.

### PARAPHRASE/ADAPTED

**L1129 · Ch6 · Aristotle — "The whole is greater than the sum of its parts."**
Not Aristotle's wording. *Metaphysics* VIII.6 (1045a) holds that the whole is
something *beside* the parts. "Greater than the sum" is a modern gestalt
formulation.

### VERIFIED VARIANT/TRANSLATION

**L1437 · Ch7 · Emerson — "Adopt the pace of nature: her secret is patience."**
Genuine. "Education," in *Lectures and Biographical Sketches*. The fuller
sentence begins "Leave this military hurry…"

### UNVERIFIED — open

**L1340 · Ch6 AND L1770 · Ch8 · Aristotle — "Knowing yourself is the beginning of all wisdom."**
*Appears twice, verbatim, same attribution.* Not located in the Aristotelian
corpus; attested only on quote sites, which variously credit Aristotle, Plato,
Socrates and Laozi — a signature of an unsourced attribution. The underlying
maxim is the Delphic **"Know thyself,"** inscribed at the Temple of Apollo and
traditionally associated with the Seven Sages; it predates Aristotle.
Trending MISATTRIBUTED. Not yet closed to standard.
**Provenance supersedes the duplication question** — do not choose between the
Water and Air placements until the attribution is settled.

**L1684 · Ch8 · Mark Twain — "An open mind leaves a chance for someone to drop a worthwhile thought in it."**
Absent from twainquotes.com and from Wikiquote's sourced Twain sections. Twain
is a magnet for unsourced attribution.

**L1754 · Ch8 · Gandhi — "Our ability to reach unity in diversity will be the beauty and the test of our civilization."**
Quote-site attestation only; "Young India" is cited without volume or date.
Needs checking against the *Collected Works of Mahatma Gandhi*.

## Chapter 5 — Fire (30 quotations; 9 adjudicated)

### MISATTRIBUTED

**L998 · Peter Drucker — "The best way to predict the future is to create it."**
Origin is **Dennis Gabor**, *Inventing the Future* (1963): "The future cannot be
predicted, but futures can be invented." **Alan Kay** states he coined the
popular form — "the best way to predict the future is to invent it" — and began
using it in 1971. The Drucker ascription, with *create* replacing *invent*,
appears by 1986 and spread through Scripps Howard News Service in 1991.
Manuscript wording matches neither true source → **doctrine rule 3**.

### PARAPHRASE/ADAPTED

**L1028 · Joseph Campbell — "The cave you fear to enter holds the treasure you seek."**
Quote Investigator judges the thought genuinely Campbell's but the pithy form a
later telescoping. The recorded wording, in Diane K. Osbon's *Reflections on the
Art of Living: A Joseph Campbell Companion* (1991) — collected by Osbon directly
from Campbell — reads: "The very cave you are afraid to enter turns out to be
the source of what you are looking for." → **doctrine rule 4**, authorial
judgment on whether the recorded wording earns the placement.

### UNVERIFIED — open

**L776 · Rumi — "Your task is not to seek for love, but merely to seek and find all the barriers within yourself that you have built against it."**
Quote-site attestation only. No scholarly source located; not yet checked
against the Persian corpus or against Barks's renderings. Note the manuscript
elsewhere (L1052) labels a Barks-derived line honestly as "Adapted from Rumi,
after Coleman Barks" — this one carries a bare "— Rumi".

**L1098 · Ferdinand Foch — "The most powerful weapon on earth is the human soul on fire."**
Wikiquote's earliest documented ascription is Harold Whittle Blakeley, *The 32d
Infantry Division in World War II* (1956), p. 3 — an American divisional history
published 27 years after Foch's death. No French primary source located.

**L1074 · Ken Wilber — "Soulplay is the act of expressing ourselves authentically and intuitively in the world…"**
No source located. "Soulplay" does not appear to be a Wilber term, and the
register is unlike his prose. **High suspicion** — see the open question below.

**L846 · Carl Jung — "Our senses provide a subjective view of our surroundings, not an objective accounting…"**
Not located in the Collected Works. Jung holds a compatible epistemological
position (he doubted the possibility of "pure observation"), but the wording is
modern and reads as a secondary description of Jung's view rather than Jung.

**L812 · Thich Nhat Hanh — "From a spark to a flame, fire is life."**
No source located. Thich Nhat Hanh used flame and candle imagery extensively,
but this sentence is not among his located writings or talks.

**L828 · Ram Dass — "The flame that burns within each of us is the light of the world."**
No source located, including in the Ram Dass archives. Related genuine material
exists ("In each of us there once was a fire"; "As you find the light in you,
you begin to see the light in everyone else") but not this sentence.

### Bibliography citation error — found in passing

**L976 · William Shakespeare — "Out of this nettle, danger, we pluck this flower, safety."**
The quotation is **VERIFIED EXACT** — Hotspur, *Henry IV, Part 1*, Act II sc. iii.
But the Chapter 5 bibliography (L2697) cites ***Henry IV, Part 2***. Wrong play.
Belongs to the bibliography-completeness audit; recorded here because provenance
work surfaced it.

## Open question for the author

Five Chapter 5 epigraphs (Wilber, Jung, Thich Nhat Hanh, Ram Dass, and the bare
Rumi) return no locatable source at all — not merely a disputed one. The Wilber
line is the sharpest case: "Soulplay" reads as a coinage, not a quotation.

Before ruling these under doctrine rule 5, the author should consider whether
some of this language is **his own**, written in a teacher's voice and later
hardened into an attributed epigraph. That is a question only the author can
answer, and it would change the remedy from removal to reclaiming authorship.
This connects to the standing Chapter 4 item on the Hermes "one thing, many"
quotation.
