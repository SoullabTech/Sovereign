## The room

A dedicated reading environment at **`/read/elemental-alchemy`**, outside the Book
Studio entirely. Verified structurally (a "conditions changed" claim — *not* an
experiential one): no Studio chrome, no publisher front matter in the stream, the book
begins at `# Preface` with the cosmogram plate as its doorway, cover and single title
block present, 200, no console errors.

## The change (the only two variables)

Versus `/book-studio/read`, exactly two conditions differ:

1. **Studio chrome removed** — no editorial nav, no masthead, no "Read Flow" label, no
   workshop language.
2. **Front matter lifted out of the reading stream** — copyright, ISBN, disclaimer, and
   table of contents are no longer in the reader's path (they remain in the canonical
   manuscript and the print editions; only the *experiential* order changed).

**Everything else is held constant** — same renderer, typography, measure, cover, the
single long scroll, the in-prose plates. This is deliberate: a reader's report is then
attributable to these two changes and not to a dozen confounds. *The ability to learn
outranks the desire to optimize* — improvements that would weaken attribution are queued,
not abandoned, and become eligible only after this observation window closes.

## Unit of analysis

**Transitions, not objects.** A reader will not report "the copyright page interrupted
me"; they report movement — "I never settled," "at some point I forgot I was reading a
screen." The question the Lab asks is *across which transition did the reader's mode of
attention change* — including transitions that were offered but never crossed.

## Predictions (expectations, not findings)

Held as falsifiable predictions about what readers may report. They are *predictions*,
authored from the structure of the page — not experience, and not claims.

- **P1** — No reader reports being addressed by *software or a workshop* before the book;
  the first thing that speaks is the book itself (cover → title → Preface).
- **P2** — The book "takes the floor" *earlier* than in the Studio view — at the cover and
  Preface, rather than after a wall of publisher apparatus.
- **P3** — The cosmogram reads as a *threshold/doorway* into the Preface, not as an
  interruption.
- **P4** — Any residual "this is a webpage/document" report implicates a *held-constant*
  condition (the wide measure, the single long scroll, or — in production — the global
  Beta pill), **not** the two changes. Such a report is informative precisely because it
  cannot be attributed to what we changed.

## Disconfirmation criteria (what would surprise us)

- Readers settle *no earlier* than in the Studio view → **P2 disconfirmed** (the
  front-matter lift did not matter).
- The cosmogram is reported as jarring or premature → **P3 disconfirmed**.
- "Software / not-a-book" is still reported before the prose → **P1 disconfirmed** (some
  other voice speaks first; find it).
- The reading feels *more* clinical or disorienting without the Studio frame → a genuine
  surprise: the frame may have provided orientation whose loss costs more than the
  interruption it caused.

## Jurisdictional limits (what this experiment may NOT conclude, regardless of outcome)

The most this experiment can ever conclude is: **"these readers, under these conditions,
reported X."** It may **not** conclude:

- that the Preface is a universally superior threshold,
- that all books should begin this way,
- that lifting front matter universally helps,
- that "inhabitation" has been demonstrated or solved,
- that the design "works."

**Confounds held alive (not retired):** prose quality (the Preface may simply be
well-written — "the book worked" is not "the reading room worked"), prior mood, device,
time, subject familiarity, the unchanged measure, the long scroll. This experiment cannot
separate these from the two changes without variation across readers and contexts. They
are *dormant, not dead* — reinstated the moment an observation needs them.

**On the reader's report:** the *occurrence* ("I never settled") is sovereign and is never
disputed; the *description* ("it felt restless") may be clarified, never contradicted; the
*explanation* ("because of the copyright page") is a hypothesis — a mapping between object
and transition — fully open to test, and never authoritative simply because the reader
offered it. Authority over an experience is not authority over its explanation.
