## 1 · 2 — the Buddha · L47 and L190 *(two occurrences, one saying)*

> *"If you knew how perfect it all is you'd throw your head up at the clouds and laugh."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **`MISATTRIBUTED`** |
| **Origin** | `EXTERNAL SOURCE` — but not the Buddha |
| **Rights** | `NONE` as it stands |

**A documented fake-Buddha quotation.** No Pali canon source; nothing resembling it
in the suttas. **Earliest trace is a Usenet post of 27 November 2001**, where it was
posted as *"Buddhist"* — **not attributed to the Buddha.** The attribution accreted
afterward through quotation sites. *(Pattern 1 — `SOURCE DISPLACEMENT`.)*

**Two-layer defect.** The commonly circulated form is *"When you realize how
perfect everything is, you will tilt your head back and laugh at the sky."* **The
manuscript carries a further variant of an already-spurious saying.**

**`SOURCE RESTORATION OPPORTUNITY`** — a genuine ancestor is available: a stanza
attributed to the 14th-century Dzogchen master **Longchenpa** — *"Since everything
is but an illusion, perfect in being what it is, having nothing to do with good or
bad, acceptance or rejection, one might as well burst out laughing!"* **Lead, not
verdict** — the Longchenpa attribution needs its own check, and any modern English
rendering carries translation rights.

**Inline form:** L47 sits in the Preface's statement of purpose; L190 stands alone.
**Neither is load-bearing** — no surrounding sentence depends on it.

**Bibliography:** no Buddha entry expected. Confirm at Stage 7.

## 3 — Carl Jung · L1125 — **`LOAD-BEARING`**

> *"The meeting of two personalities is like the contact of two chemical substances:
> if there is any reaction, both are transformed."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **`VERIFIED`** — Jung, *Modern Man in Search of a Soul* (1933); CW 16 |
| **Mediation** | **English is a translation** — W. S. Dell and Cary F. Baynes |
| **Rights** | `TERM UNCERTAIN` · **`STANDARD`** — the 1933 translation is itself protected |

**⚠️ The most structurally dependent quotation found in this census.** It is
immediately followed by: *"**This quote beautifully encapsulates** the
transformative power of engaging with our emotional depths…"* — **the surrounding
argument explicitly names the quotation as what carries it.** This is the exact
object 4B exists to adjudicate, and it survived Stage 2 unexamined.

**`WRONG TRANSLATOR RISK`** — the translation is unnamed in the text. Under the
printed-English rule, naming Jung without naming Dell/Baynes leaves the mediation
invisible.

## 6 — Carl Jung · L1746 — **`HOST-ALIGNED ALTERATION`**

> *"I don't have beliefs, I have experiences."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **`PARAPHRASE/ADAPTED`** — not Jung's wording |
| **Origin** | `EXTERNAL SOURCE` — a real Jung position, altered |
| **Rights** | `NONE` as it stands |

**Jung's genuine position is documented and the ancestor is recoverable:** BBC
*Face to Face* (1959) — ***"I don't need to believe. I know"*** — and the
corresponding *Collected Works* position that *if we have the experience, we do not
need to believe.*

**⚠️ The alteration is not neutral.** Jung's distinction is **belief versus
knowledge**. The manuscript's version substitutes **experience** for **knowledge** —
**and the paragraph it sits in is precisely an argument for experience over
belief**: *"Information leads to knowledge; experience leads to wisdom."*

**The quotation has been moved toward the claim it is being used to support.** That
is the `HOST-ALIGNED ALTERATION` signature from Stage 2, and this is its clearest
instance in the book. **`SOURCE RESTORATION OPPORTUNITY`** — but restoring *"I
don't need to believe. I know"* **would not serve the host argument**, which is
itself the diagnostic.

## 7 — John Milton · L1752 — **`SPEAKER MISATTRIBUTION`**

> *"The mind is its own place, and in itself, can make a heaven of hell, a hell of heaven."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **`VERIFIED VARIANT`** — *Paradise Lost* I.254–255 |
| **Rights** | **`PUBLIC DOMAIN`** |

Original: *"The mind is its own place, and in it self / Can make a Heav'n of Hell, a
Hell of Heav'n."* The manuscript modernises spelling and adds a comma and *"can."*
**Minor, and defensible for a general readership** — but it is a variant, not exact.

**⚠️ The substantive finding is the speaker.** The line is **spoken by Satan**,
newly fallen, insisting he can make his own heaven of hell. The manuscript
introduces it as *"John Milton aptly noted"* — **as if it were the poet's
observation rather than his fallen angel's self-persuasion.**

**Structurally identical to the Polonius finding in 4A**, and sharper: **the
section is "The Dark Side of Air," about the ego ruled by cold logic and lost in
"thoughtland."** Satan's line is *exactly* that pathology speaking. **Attributing
it to Milton as wisdom inverts what the passage is diagnosing** — while attributing
it correctly would make the example far stronger. **Recorded for 4B; not repaired.**

## 8 — Lao Tzu · L1867 — **`WRONG TRANSLATOR RISK` · longest inline item**

> *"We put thirty spokes together and call it a wheel… we should recognize the
> usefulness of what is not."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | **`VERIFIED`** — *Tao Te Ching* 11 |
| **Mediation** | **the English is Arthur Waley's**, *The Way and Its Power* (1934) |
| **Rights** | **`TERM UNCERTAIN` · `HIGH`** — Waley died 1966; the 1934 translation is protected in most jurisdictions |

**The one Lao Tzu attribution in the manuscript that is genuinely Lao Tzu**, and
the second such after the *Tao Te Ching* 78 epigraph kept in 4A. *(Contrast with
the four "Adapted from Lao Tzu" items dissolved in Stage 3.)*

**Two defects.** The **translator is not named** — a provenance claim under the
printed-English rule. And the text reads *"usefulness"* where **Waley has
*"utility"***, so it is a **slightly altered protected translation**, which is the
least defensible position to occupy: long enough to matter, changed enough to
notice, unattributed as to translator.

## What this population proves about the gap

**Inline quotations are a materially different object from block quotations.**

- **1 of 9 is explicitly load-bearing** (Jung/chemical — *"This quote beautifully
  encapsulates…"*), and **two more lean.** Across 45 block epigraphs in 4A, **none**
  did.
- **3 of 9 carry unnamed translators.** Long inline quotations are where mediated
  text hides, because a block epigraph invites an attribution line and an inline
  quotation does not.
- **The one genuinely long quotation in the manuscript is inline** (Lao Tzu, ~90
  words) — **the highest rights exposure in this census sat outside it.**

# ⚠️ THIRD SCOPE DISCOVERY — 2026-09-01, before the eight adjudications

**The eight inline adjudications are held.** Adjudicating on a field now known to
be incomplete would repeat, for a third time, the exact error already diagnosed
twice.

## What happened

The speaker sweep surfaced **"Be in the world but not of it" — John 17:16**, which
**was not among the Stage 2c nine.** That prompted a re-scan of my own detector
rather than a re-scan of the book — and the detector had **two further gaps.**

**Three typographic forms of quotation exist in this manuscript. The census has now
been extended twice and was wrong both times.**

| Form | Example | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| **Block** — line begins `*"` | most epigraphs | Stage 2 (137) |
| **Inline, emphasis closes on the quote** | *"…" * | Stage 2c (9) |
| **Inline, attribution inside the emphasis** | *"…" — John 17:16* | **missed — 1** |
| **Plain quotation marks, no emphasis at all** | "…" | **missed — 3** |

## What this changes about the lesson

**The first correction was substantive — block versus inline is a real distinction
between kinds of object.** The second and third are not: **they are the same object
in different punctuation.** My detector pattern was wrong twice in ways that had
nothing to do with the ontology.

> **Fixing a pattern once does not make it right. A pattern must be tested against
> the corpus, not against the case that exposed it.**

**Both new gaps were found by a probe aimed at something else** — the speaker sweep,
looking for dramatic context, returned a census defect. **That is now three
consecutive times a downstream operation has exposed an upstream inventory**, and
the strongest available argument for the *"run a thin downstream probe"* principle.

## Immediate consequences

1. **Stage 2c reopens as 2c-ii** for the four newly found occurrences, at the same
   evidence standard. Small.
2. **The eight adjudications wait** until the inline field is closed. **The Buddha
   family is the precedent**: a ruling that looked complete at two occurrences was
   wrong until the third was linked.
3. **The Blake item joins the dramatic-speaker set** — Polonius, Satan, Hotspur, and
   now a devil in Blake's *Proverbs of Hell*. **Four specimens.**
4. **A structural fix is owed, not just a recount:** the manuscript should adopt
   **one consistent typographic convention for quotations**, so that no future pass
   can miss a form. Carried to final QA.

**No adjudication, no repair, no verification performed in this entry.**
