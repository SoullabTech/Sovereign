# Starter Case Workflow — Conversational Urgency / Interruption

## Purpose

This is a reference implementation of the Practitioner Loop using a concrete case. It demonstrates how each stage of the workflow functions in practice, with realistic field data, inquiry responses, council inputs, and intervention design.

Use this as a template for onboarding new practitioners and for testing the loop end-to-end.

---

## Case Frame

**Client:** Lena (fictional)
**Presenting pattern:** Interrupts conversation partners, particularly intimate partners and authority figures. Reports feeling an urgency to speak before the thought disappears or the moment closes. Has awareness of the pattern but cannot yet interrupt it in the moment.

**Change type:** Threshold (crossing into new relational capacity)
**Situation type:** Individual
**Urgency:** Medium

---

## Stage 2 — Client Inquiry

**Prompt set used:** Conversational Urgency / Interruption

**Lena's responses:**

> Q: Think of a recent moment where you felt the urge to speak before the other person finished. Describe what was happening in your body just before you spoke.
> A: A tightening across my chest, kind of like someone pressing from the inside. My throat got ready to speak before I consciously decided to. It was already happening.

> Q: What was your fear in that moment? What did you believe would be lost if you waited?
> A: That the thought would evaporate. Like it existed only in that window and if I didn't grab it, it would be gone. Also that he would move on and my point would be irrelevant. Like I would miss the moment and it would be as if I never had the thought.

> Q: What were you doing internally while the other person was still speaking?
> A: Half listening. Mostly rehearsing. I was running my response in my head, making sure it was right, making sure I wouldn't forget it. I wasn't really with him.

> Q: Is there an earlier memory of trying to be heard and not being able to?
> A: Dinner table. Seven or eight. My father would be talking, my two older brothers would talk over each other. I could never get in. I would sit there with this thing I wanted to say and it would never come out. Eventually I stopped trying and then I guess I started forcing it.

> Q: When you imagine waiting — staying fully present until someone finishes — what happens inside you?
> A: Anxiety. A kind of panic that I will disappear. That if I don't stake my claim in the conversation I will stop existing in it. Which is ridiculous when I say it but it's what happens.

```
[interruption] Client interrupted twice within our session when I offered reflections about waiting.
  Both interruptions occurred when the reframe touched on tolerating delay.

[relational_field] Pattern strongest with intimate partner and father figure types.
  Absent or much reduced with peers and subordinates.

[somatic_shift] Breath deepened and slowed briefly when she named the dinner table memory.

[pattern_notice] Client can name the precursor (chest tightening) but reports no gap between
  noticing and speaking. The signal and the action appear simultaneous.
```

## Stage 4 — Council Synthesis Inputs (Decisions)

What the council now receives, assembled from the evidence bundle:

**CLIENT INQUIRY:** Conversational Urgency / Interruption prompt set
→ Primary evidence: panic that the thought will evaporate; fear of disappearing in conversation; dinner table memory; "I wasn't really with him — I was rehearsing"

**FIELD SIGNALS:**
→ Somatic precursor: chest tightening, 8/10, 10 seconds before interruption
→ Behavioral: 2+ interruptions per observed day
→ Emotional: shame followed by defensiveness (protective of the pattern)

**PRACTITIONER OBSERVATIONS:**
→ Pattern active within the therapeutic relationship (practitioner interrupted twice)
→ Strongest with attachment figures and authority types
→ Breath shift at memory of dinner table (possible access point)
→ No gap between precursor signal and action (simultaneity — no window yet)

**What the council can now work with that practitioner summary alone could not provide:**
- The exact phenomenology of the urgency ("like I will disappear")
- The specific fear driving it (annihilation of the thought, irrelevance)
- The internal reality during others' speech (rehearsing, not listening)
- The origin scene (dinner table, age 7-8)
- The somatic marker (10-second chest tightening)
- The simultaneity (no current gap between signal and action)

## Stage 5 — Intervention Design (Changes)

**Experiment 1: Somatic Precursor Mapping (witness-first)**

- **Title:** Light trance — observing the chest signal
- **Hypothesis:** Lena can name the precursor but has no gap between signal and action. Before installing any technique, she needs to experience the signal as separate from the behavioral impulse. The intervention is observation, not interruption.
- **Modality:** Hypnosis (light trance)
- **Instructions:** (use `light-trance-precursor-mapping` template) Settle with breathing. Recall a mild activation moment — not the worst episode. Observe from inside: where is the very first signal? What does it need her to know? Debrief.
- **Observation window:** This session only. Review in next session before proceeding.
- **Success signals:**
  - Lena can distinguish the precursor signal from the behavioral impulse as separate moments
  - Lena reports any moment of increased time between signal and action in the week following
- **Risk / caution:** Do not move to pattern interrupt or anchor installation in this session. The simultaneity is important data — moving to technique before the phenomenology is clear risks bypassing the work.

**Experiment 2: Relational Pause Tolerance (between-session practice)**

- **Title:** 3-second pause experiment with low-stakes partner
- **Hypothesis:** Lena has no current practice of tolerating the micro-pause. This cannot be installed in session without first building tolerance in low-stakes contexts.
- **Modality:** Relational experiment
- **Instructions:** (use `relational-pause-tolerance` template) Choose one low-stakes conversation before next session. After the other person finishes, pause 3 full seconds before responding. During the pause: breathe, feel feet, stay curious about what just landed. Journal afterward: what happened in the pause?
- **Observation window:** Between this session and next.
- **Success signals:**
  - Lena completes the practice at least once
  - Something new is discovered in the pause (about herself or the other person)
  - The pause does not feel like disappearing
- **Risk / caution:** Do not attempt with intimate partner or father figure yet. Starts with a friend or neutral colleague.

---

## What This Case Demonstrates

1. **First-person phenomenology is irreplaceable.** "I felt like I would disappear" and "I was rehearsing, not listening" are not things the practitioner could have provided. They came from the inquiry.

2. **Somatic precision matters.** The 10-second chest tightening becomes an access point. The council can recommend working specifically with that signal — not "anxiety" in general.

3. **The simultaneity finding changes the intervention.** Without the field signals and observations, the practitioner might have jumped to pattern interrupt. With them, the council correctly identifies that no gap yet exists — witness-first is the right next step.

4. **The intervention is the smallest useful one.** Not a complete treatment plan. One session of witnessing. One week of pause practice. Review.

5. **Honest naming of risk.** "Do not move to technique before phenomenology is clear" is a constraint, not a qualification. The experiment design holds this.
