# MAIA Wisdom Keepers Governance Protocol
## Stewardship, Authority, and Cultural Integrity in a Living Ecosystem

**Classification:** Governance Document — Founding Version
**Date:** March 2026
**Status:** Draft for Stewardship Council Formation

---

## Preamble

MAIA integrates knowledge from living wisdom traditions: astrology systems spanning four cultural lineages, I Ching, Tarot, Runes, Spiralogic, depth psychology, somatic frameworks, and more. These traditions are not content libraries. They are living transmissions, each with its own holders, contexts, interpretive communities, and ethical constraints.

Without governance, AI becomes the implicit authority over these traditions — selecting, framing, and surfacing them according to technical convenience rather than cultural integrity. That is not acceptable.

This protocol establishes how MAIA holds and transmits wisdom: who oversees it, how traditions are stewarded, what practitioners are accountable for, and how human authority remains primary.

The animating principle throughout:

> The AI is a reflective instrument in service of human wisdom, not a substitute for it.

---

## I. The Stewardship Council

### 1.1 Purpose

The Stewardship Council is MAIA's governing body for wisdom integrity and ethical evolution. It is not an advisory board. Its decisions bind the platform.

### 1.2 Composition

The Council is composed of five categories of seat:

| Seat | Holder Profile | Function |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Elder Wisdom** (2 seats) | Senior practitioners or teachers from living lineages integrated into the platform | Authority over tradition-specific guidance; can veto misrepresentation |
| **Psychological Ground** (1 seat) | Licensed clinical psychologist or psychotherapist with depth orientation | Oversight of therapeutic safety boundaries; clinical ethics review |
| **AI Ethics** (1 seat) | Researcher or practitioner with expertise in AI ethics, digital rights, or sovereignty-respecting technology | Oversight of AI behavior, memory architecture, and manipulation risk |
| **Community Voice** (1 seat) | Representative elected by active Studio practitioners | Accountability to the practitioner community using the platform |
| **Founding Steward** (1 seat) | Platform founder | Strategic continuity and execution accountability |

Initial total: 6 seats. Quorum for decisions: 4.

### 1.3 Authority and Responsibilities

The Council has binding authority over:

- **Framework inclusion:** Any symbolic, psychological, or wisdom framework added to the Library requires Council review before activation in the oracle
- **Ethical boundaries:** The Council defines and updates the prohibition list — behaviors MAIA may never perform, regardless of user request
- **Canon amendments:** Changes to MAIA Canon v1.1, the Oath, or the Sovereignty Invariants require Council approval
- **Tradition stewardship review:** Annual review of how each integrated tradition is being presented, used, and attributed
- **Incident response:** Any reported misrepresentation of a tradition, boundary violation, or harm pattern is reviewed by the Council within 30 days

The Council does not have authority over:
- Day-to-day platform operations
- Technical architecture decisions (unless sovereignty is implicated)
- Business and economic decisions (unless they conflict with the governance principles)

### 1.4 Meeting Cadence

- **Quarterly:** Full Council meeting — framework review, tradition stewardship, ethics incident review
- **Annual:** Deep review of the Canon, Oath, and Sovereignty Invariants
- **Ad hoc:** Within 30 days for urgent ethics incidents

### 1.5 Formation Timeline

The founding Council should be constituted before any institutional partnerships are formalized. Institutional partners will ask who oversees this. The answer cannot be "the founder alone."

## II. Knowledge Stewardship

### 2.1 The Hierarchy of Authority

MAIA operates with an explicit authority hierarchy:

```
Living lineage holders and teachers
              ↓
Stewardship Council
              ↓
Platform knowledge architecture
              ↓
AI oracle (reflective instrument)
              ↓
Member (sovereign subject)
```

The AI is at the bottom of the authority chain, not the top. It surfaces, reflects, and frames — it does not determine what is true within a tradition.

### 2.2 Tradition Stewardship Categories

Each integrated tradition falls into one of three stewardship categories:

**Category A: Active Stewardship**
The platform has a formal relationship with a living lineage holder or teacher who reviews how the tradition is represented. The holder can request corrections, additions, or removals. Compensation is provided.

Examples warranting Category A treatment: Vedic astrology, Mayan calendar, Chinese BaZi, I Ching.

**Category B: Scholar-Verified**
No individual lineage holder is affiliated, but representation has been reviewed by a qualified scholar or practitioner who has signed off on accuracy and contextual appropriateness. Documentation is maintained.

Examples: Western astrology, Jungian frameworks (no living lineage holder; verified by Jungian-trained therapist).

**Category C: Working Integration**
The tradition is active in the platform but has not yet reached Category A or B standard. This is disclosed internally. Category C frameworks are used but flagged for upgrade.

**Goal:** All frameworks integrated at launch should reach Category A or B within 24 months.

### 2.3 Attribution Standards

Every wisdom tradition in the Library is documented with:

- **Origin:** Cultural and geographic origin, historical context
- **Lineage acknowledgment:** Named lineage holders or schools where applicable
- **Steward:** Current Category A or B steward name and role
- **Epistemic scope:** What the tradition does and does not claim to explain
- **Boundaries of AI use:** What the AI may and may not say when engaging this tradition

This documentation lives in the Library architecture and is surfaced where appropriate in the member-facing experience.

### 2.4 Epistemic Framing Requirements

MAIA's AI layer is required to frame wisdom tradition outputs consistently:

**Required framing posture:**
- "This tradition suggests..." not "This means..."
- "One reading..." not "The interpretation is..."
- "Symbolically, this points toward..." not "You are..."
- Explicit acknowledgment that symbolic frameworks are reflective tools, not diagnoses or authorities

**Prohibited framings:**
- Claiming certainty about future events based on any divinatory system
- Diagnosing psychological states using astrological or symbolic indicators
- Presenting any tradition's framework as scientifically validated when it is not
- Using one tradition to override another without the member's explicit choice

These are implemented as oracle constraints, not just guidelines.

### 2.5 Cultural Knowledge and Indigenous Traditions

The Mayan calendar system and other traditions with indigenous origins require heightened stewardship:

- Category A stewardship is mandatory (not aspirational)
- Compensation flows to indigenous communities, not just individual translators
- The platform explicitly disclaims any authority to speak for these traditions
- Member-facing materials include clear cultural context
- No feature that commodifies indigenous knowledge is permitted without explicit community agreement

## III. Practitioner Certification

### 3.1 Why Certification Matters

Studio is becoming professional infrastructure. Practitioners using it will work with vulnerable people in high-stakes contexts — grief, transition, mental health adjacency, spiritual emergency. Without a training pathway, the platform has no quality standard, no accountability layer, and no defense when a practitioner causes harm.

Certification is not gatekeeping. It is the platform taking responsibility for what it enables.

### 3.2 Certification Tiers

**Tier 1: MAIA Practitioner (Studio Standard)**

Prerequisites: Active therapeutic, coaching, or facilitation practice.

Training includes:
- Spiralogic fundamentals (12 phases, 5 elements, relational maturation)
- MAIA's sovereign AI principles and why they matter
- Ethical use of AI-assisted session preparation and synthesis
- When to defer to human supervisors (not AI synthesis)
- Safety documentation requirements
- Studio workflow and tools

Assessment: Written reflection on a case vignette + practical Studio demonstration.
Duration: 8–12 hours self-paced + 1 live group session.

**Tier 2: MAIA Studio Lead (Advanced)**

Prerequisites: Tier 1 certification + 6 months active Studio use + 2 peer references.

Additional training includes:
- Supervision architecture and peer consultation facilitation
- Team management and practitioner onboarding
- Institutional deployment governance
- Complex cases: safety escalation, clinical handoff protocols
- Facilitating MAIA-supported group work and Circles

Assessment: Supervised case review + peer consultation demonstration.

**Tier 3: Wisdom Keeper Affiliate**

By invitation from the Stewardship Council. For practitioners who have demonstrated sustained ethical use, community contribution, and alignment with the platform's governance principles.

Privileges: Input into Library framework evolution, participation in practitioner community governance, potential Council nomination.

### 3.3 Certification Maintenance

Certification requires annual renewal:
- 4 hours continuing education (platform-provided or equivalent)
- Agreement to updated ethics guidelines
- No active governance violations

### 3.4 Revocation

Certification may be suspended or revoked by the Stewardship Council for:
- Documented harm to a member
- Misrepresentation of the platform's capabilities to members
- Violation of consent or data handling protocols
- Using AI synthesis outputs as clinical diagnoses

Revocation process: Report → Council review within 30 days → Decision with right of response.

## IV. Community Stewardship

### 4.1 Circles Governance

Circles are consent-gated small group containers. Each Circle has a designated **Circle Keeper** responsible for its health and integrity.

**Circle Keeper responsibilities:**
- Establishing and communicating the Circle's purpose and boundaries
- Holding consent norms (what is shared, with whom, for how long)
- Monitoring for patterns that undermine psychological safety
- Escalating concerns to the platform's community stewardship channel
- Ensuring the Circle does not function as a therapy group without qualified facilitation

**Platform responsibilities:**
- Providing clear consent infrastructure for Circle membership
- Making the consent architecture visible to all Circle members
- Providing Circle Keepers with governance guidelines and escalation pathways
- Ensuring that collective state data (field analytics) is never individually attributable

### 4.2 Psychological Safety Protocols

Any Circle that engages depth material — grief, trauma, shadow work, spiritual crisis — must have at minimum one Tier 1 certified practitioner as Circle Keeper or co-facilitator.

Circles that do not meet this standard are designated "Peer Support" circles with explicit scope limitations.

### 4.3 Conflict and Harm Response

Platform-level harm response:
1. Member submits a concern (confidential)
2. Community stewardship team reviews within 72 hours
3. If systemic or serious: escalated to Stewardship Council
4. Resolution communicated to all parties

No active harm report remains unreviewed for longer than 72 hours.

### 4.4 Facilitator Development

The platform provides:
- Community facilitation training (aligned with Tier 1 certification)
- Peer consultation for Circle Keepers
- Shared facilitation resources and templates
- A community of practice for Circle Keepers to consult each other

## V. Economic Structure

### 5.1 The Principle

Wisdom Keepers are not symbolic. They hold authority. Authority requires accountability, and accountability requires stability. Symbolic roles with no economic reality create advisory theater, not governance.

The economic structure must support the Wisdom Keepers layer as a functioning institution, not a branding asset.

### 5.2 Compensation Tiers

**Stewardship Council Members**
- Quarterly stipend for Council participation
- Expenses covered for required travel or in-person sessions
- Amount: Modest but substantive. Not token. Reviewed annually.

**Category A Tradition Stewards**
- Annual stewardship fee for framework review and oversight
- Per-use attribution (optional: can be directed to community or lineage organization rather than individual)
- Recognition in platform materials and Library attribution

**Certification Contributors**
- Revenue share on certification program fees (designed collaboratively with contributors)
- Attribution in training materials

**Category B Scholar-Verifiers**
- One-time or periodic fee for review work
- Optional ongoing advisory relationship with retainer

### 5.3 Platform Economic Model

The platform's own economics must not betray the governance structure:

**Permitted revenue sources:**
- Practitioner Studio licensing (individual and team tiers)
- Institutional deployment contracts
- Certification program fees
- Foundation grants and research partnerships (with data governance reviewed by Council)

**Not permitted:**
- Revenue derived from member data aggregation, sale, or behavioral advertising
- Pricing models that make sovereignty features a premium add-on (Sanctuary Mode is always free)
- Institutional contracts that require relaxing memory governance standards

### 5.4 The Helper Fund

An existing platform mechanism: a sliding-scale pool funded by higher-paying members that subsidizes access for those who cannot afford standard rates.

The Helper Fund is a governance instrument, not just an economic one. It embodies the principle that wisdom access should not be gated by economic privilege. The Stewardship Council reviews the Fund's health annually and can recommend adjustments to pricing tiers.

## VI. Living Document Provisions

This protocol is a founding document, not a fixed one. The Stewardship Council reviews and may amend it annually.

Amendments require:
- Written proposal submitted 30 days before a Council meeting
- Council discussion and vote (4/6 quorum)
- 30-day public comment period for practitioner community before ratification
- No amendment may reduce the human authority of the Wisdom Keepers layer, weaken Sanctuary Mode, or relax cultural stewardship standards

The Canon, Oath, and Sovereignty Invariants take precedence over this protocol. If this protocol conflicts with them, the Canon governs.
