# AIN Studio — Business Model (Working Draft)

**Date:** 2026-07-08
**Status:** Cat 1 — Strategic direction, held for learning. Not authorized as pricing. Projections are Vision, not Live (see §7).
**Related:** [[project_living_studio_developmental_os]], [[project_ain_os_field_tenancy_candidate]], [[project_context_assembly_canon]] (ONE MAIA, many Fields), `docs/canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md`, [[project_studio_north_star_relationship_sovereignty]]

> **Governing sentence.** The platform does not contain the practitioner's judgment. It supports the ongoing *formation, expression, and stewardship* of that judgment. The judgment stays theirs; the platform preserves its context, connects it across time, reveals its patterns, and provides an environment in which that authority can mature over a lifetime. Every layer, price, and claim below is subordinate to this sentence. It is the deepest differentiator and the constitutional line at once — a violation of it is invalid regardless of commercial merit (cf. the Oath, [[project_reveal_not_manufacture]], [[project_conditions_protection_family]]).

**The real product is not the Studio.** The Studio is what people buy. The product is **continuity of professional intelligence** — today fragmented across books, notebooks, PDFs, slides, client notes, recordings, journals, memory. The job is not to *organize* those; it is to preserve and cultivate the *relationships among them* so understanding keeps developing instead of freezing.

---

## 1. The core reframe

You are not building custom apps. You license **one platform (AIN OS)**, and each practitioner receives their **embodiment (Studio)** of it.

```
AIN OS (one platform, one MAIA)
   ├── Kelly Studio (Soullab)        ← flagship / horizon
   ├── Larry Studio (What Now?)      ← first reference implementation
   ├── Heather Studio
   ├── Physician Studio
   └── Executive Coach Studio ...
```

What varies per Studio: **MAIA's Field Configuration · the practitioner's corpus · body of work · branding · workflows · developmental language · permissions.** The platform, the constitutional architecture, and MAIA-the-flagship stay one.

You are not selling *What Now?*. You are licensing *AIN Studio, configured as What Now?*

## 2. This is downstream of ratified architecture, not invented for pricing

The business model is not bolted on. It follows directly from what the architecture already decided:

- **ONE MAIA, many Fields** (ADR-013): a Studio is a *Field Configuration*, not a cloned MAIA. Configuration, not fork.
- **Field = unit of tenancy** ([[project_ain_os_field_tenancy_candidate]]): the falsifier is that many Fields run without new primitives. If that holds, each Studio is a tenant, not a rebuild — which is exactly what makes the economics work.

Commercial consequence: marginal cost of a new Studio is **configuration + stewardship**, not net-new engineering. That is the whole business.

## 3. Commercial structure — three components

Keep these separate; they have different cost bases and different value stories.

| Component | What it is | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Platform** | The evolving AIN OS Studio substrate. Every practitioner benefits from every improvement. | Shared, continuous, near-zero marginal cost |
| **2. Studio Configuration** | Making the Studio uniquely theirs: branding, MAIA Field Configuration, corpus ingestion, workflows, developmental language, practice setup. | Substantial one-time consulting + implementation |
| **3. Ongoing Stewardship** | *Not support.* Continually helping them expand their body of work, refine MAIA, evolve frameworks, add capabilities, onboard facilitators, grow the practice. | Ongoing partnership |

**Framing rule (keep):** value reflects *the transformation in the practitioner's capability and the stewardship of their lifetime body of work* — not feature counts or seats. Never "charge appropriately"; always anchor in the value created.

## 4. The value stack

Five deepening layers. Only the top two are defensible long-term; the bottom is commoditizing fast.

1. **Productivity** — organize, search, prepare, retrieve. (Commoditized.)
2. **Professional intelligence** — connect ideas across years; surface forgotten insight; recurring themes.
3. **Professional development** — refine methods, strengthen facilitation, evolve frameworks, reflective practice.
4. **Legacy stewardship** — books, workshops, research, IP, methodologies kept *active and interconnected*, not archived.
5. **Continuation** — professional judgment continues to mature rather than going static.

**The sharpening (this is the real thesis):** information is abundant; the scarce asset is **judgment**. The platform's job is to preserve, cultivate, and extend the *conditions* under which a practitioner's judgment lives — it does **not** manufacture judgment, and it does not replace it. (See §6.)

## 5. Category and market progression

**Do not anchor the category on coaching.** The architecture is practitioner-agnostic. Larry is an excellent first exemplar precisely because his work spans research, writing, facilitation, teaching, and executive leadership — but psychologists, physicians, professors, clergy, attorneys, architects, designers, researchers, nonprofit leaders, and consultants share the relevant thing. It is not a *profession*; it is a **practice**. Internal name: *Practitioner Intelligence Platform*. External category, over time: *a platform for stewarding professional practice.*

Do not serve every market at once. Incremental:

- **Practitioner Studio** — one practitioner (coach, psychologist, educator, clergy, physician, author, researcher).
- **Practice Studio** — small org: multiple practitioners, supervision, shared methodology, internal training.
- **Institute Platform** — org whose primary asset *is* its methodology: coaching schools, consultancies, certification bodies, research institutes.

**The enterprise story (Vision — label it).** When a renowned surgeon retires, what made them exceptional isn't in the procedure manuals; it lives in judgment accrued across thousands of cases. Same for consulting firms, universities, law firms. The problem — *retain, cultivate, and transmit expert judgment across generations without reducing it to static documentation* — is far larger than knowledge management. **Claim-discipline caution:** state this as the problem you are *oriented toward*, never as a delivered capability, and hold the governing sentence — you steward the *conditions and artifacts* of that judgment, you do not preserve or continue the person's judgment itself. Told loosely, this story becomes an Oath violation.

## 6. Constitutional guardrails (this project only)

A generic advisor would skip these. They are non-optional here.

**A. Stewardship ≠ continuation of the person.** Layers 4–5 must be framed as stewardship of a *body of work*, never as MAIA continuing the practitioner's *judgment* or authority. "Judgment continues to mature" is a claim about the *practitioner's own ongoing work supported by the environment* — not MAIA thinking for them, and not a digital afterlife. Cross-check against the Oath: MAIA offers reflection and framing, never authority or substitution.

**B. Two different sovereignty jurisdictions — don't blur them.**
- The **practitioner** is a *business customer* buying professional infrastructure. Pricing around the centrality of their Studio to their working life is legitimate.
- Their **clients/members** are *persons*, and the full sovereignty invariants apply to them — including *reduce the system's psychological centrality over time* and *no attachment capture.* A business model that is healthy at the practitioner layer must not push dependency-capture down onto members. Design so Studio economics never reward member dependence.

**C. Marketing Claim Discipline governs every number and promise here.** Run each outward claim through Live / Designed / Vision. Projections in §7 are **Vision**. The Larry pilot is **Live**. Do not tell tomorrow's story as if it were today's.

## 7. Pricing — near-term vs. later

**Near-term (Live / do this now):**
- Larry's **$2,000 advance** is the right beginning. It makes him the first pilot and buys you the thing you actually need: *learning what the platform must be.*
- **Do not lock long-term pricing until the pilot teaches you.** This mirrors the project's own epistemics: observation authorizes; earn before you name.

**Founding engagement (Designed):**

| Phase | Fee | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Founding Build | $10k–25k one-time | Discovery, architecture, corpus import, Studio configuration, MAIA methodology training, launch |
| Stewardship | $300–600/mo | Hosting, AI, updates, backups, security, new capabilities, support |
| Evolution | As needed | New modules, workshop/certification systems, community, research tools, custom work |

**Mature tiers (Vision — label as such, do not quote as current offers):**
- Practitioner Studio ~$249/mo · Professional Studio ~$499/mo · Institute $1,500–5,000+/mo.

**Lease-to-own (recommended — sovereignty-aligned):** e.g. $5,000 setup + $495/mo for 36 months → they **own their Studio, data, and knowledge base**, then pay only for hosting/AI/updates if they choose. This is not a pricing gimmick; it is the *only* model consistent with "sovereignty first, no cloud lock-in, self-hosted by design." It is also a moat competitors structurally cannot copy without abandoning their SaaS lock-in.

**Vision economics (Vision, not forecast):** 100 × $350/mo ≈ $420k/yr; 250 ≈ $1.05M; 500 ≈ $2.1M — before Institute deployments, implementation engagements, or marketplace. Stated only to show the *shape* of the long-tail relationship, not as a plan of record.

## 8. Positioning

Not "AI software." Not another subscription to a bundle of features.

> **A professional practice platform that grows with you across your career — the infrastructure for a professional life, and the steward of a lifetime body of work.**

People already invest thousands in certifications, conferences, CRMs, and coaching to support their practice. If the Studio becomes where they think, teach, write, coach, and develop, the monthly fee buys *continuity and stewardship of a professional life* — a categorically stronger proposition than feature-count SaaS.

2. **Instrument what configuration actually costs** (corpus ingestion, Field Configuration, workflow setup) — this sets Component 2 pricing honestly.

## 10. Economics — the first Studio is the expensive one

Two years of effort didn't build one Studio; it discovered the *primitives*: What is a Field? What is Context Assembly? Why one MAIA? What belongs in a Body of Work? What is a Recognition? Where does consent live? Constitutional vs. ecological? Those are architectural discoveries, not implementation tasks. Once stable, a new Studio is **configuration, not invention** — marginal cost falls sharply.

```
before:  2 years → one Studio
after:   2 years → Platform → N Studios   (marginal cost ≈ configuration + accompaniment)
```

**Pricing consequence.** The Founding Build ($10–25k) is therefore **not** buying engineering. It is buying *professional accompaniment*: understanding the practitioner's philosophy, ingesting the corpus, shaping developmental language, configuring MAIA's Field, cultivating the Body of Work. Price and describe it as that. Larry's job is to prove the primitives generalize — that his Studio feels *authentically Larry*, not "Kelly with different branding." That proof is worth more than ten similar Studios you build yourself.

## 11. The strategic inversion — accompaniment is the moat *and* the ceiling

This is the point that decides the shape of the company, and it cuts both ways:

- **As the technology gets faster, the human partnership becomes *more* valuable, not less.** Many companies will generate AI apps in a day. Far fewer will have an architecture that can faithfully embody a practitioner's lifetime of work, preserve its integrity, and help it keep evolving. That fidelity — not speed of app generation — is the hard-to-copy moat.
- **But the same accompaniment that is the moat is also the scaling constraint.** If every Studio needs deep relational onboarding, the business does *not* scale like SaaS. "We can make Studios in a day" is a technical truth that hides a business falsehood: the relationship work doesn't compress.

**The unresolved decision this forces** (surface it, don't bury it): how does accompaniment scale? The honest options are (a) deliberately slow, high-touch growth where you personally steward each Studio; or (b) build a **cadre of trained Studio stewards / facilitators** who can do accompaniment to standard — which makes *facilitator training and certification* a product line, not a side effect. You cannot have SaaS-curve growth *and* full accompaniment with one team. Pick knowingly. This is likely the single most important business-model choice after the Larry pilot.

## 12. Product principle — full Studio, configured down

> Every practitioner should **begin with the full Studio** and configure it into their own way of working — not begin with a minimal product and hope it becomes their practice.

This inverts standard SaaS (ship stripped-down, add complexity). The point is to let them experience the *coherence of an integrated professional environment* first, then hide, defer, or disable what isn't theirs yet.

**Reconciliation with the repo's own simplicity rules** (`docs/fields/larry/UI_SIMPLICITY_RULES_2026-07-07.md`, "one thing that matters"): these only *look* opposed. Resolve it as **capability-complete, surface-progressive** — the whole Studio is *present* and coherent underneath; the *surface* is progressively disclosed (see the `progressive-disclosure` pattern). Full capability is not the same as full UI. Larry meets simplicity; the architecture stays whole.
