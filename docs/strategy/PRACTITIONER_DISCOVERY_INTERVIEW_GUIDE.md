# Practitioner Discovery Interview Guide

_Companion to `SOULLAB_MARKETING_ARCHITECTURE.md` — 2026-06-03_

> The market is not waiting for a slogan. It is waiting for **language that accurately describes an experience it already has.** This guide exists to find that language — in practitioners' own words.

## Purpose

**Recognition, not validation.** We are not testing whether people like Soullab. We are listening for the phrase a practitioner already uses for a pain they already live with — the sentence that makes them stop and say _"Yes. That's exactly the problem I've been living with."_ Somewhere in 10–20 conversations is probably the line that becomes the campaign — not because it's clever, because it's **already true.**

This is the Living Field's ethic applied to discovery: **observation over evaluation.** Listen and capture; do not interpret or pitch.

## The one rule

**Do not lead with MAIA. Do not pitch. Spend almost no time on the product.** If the practitioner asks what you're building, defer: _"I'll show you at the end — right now I just want to understand your experience."_ The moment you describe the solution, you contaminate the language you came to find.

## Sample

10–20 structured conversations across candidate segments — therapists, coaches, executive coaches, facilitators, healers, educators. Track which segment recognizes the pain fastest and most viscerally; **segment signal is a finding, not a side effect.**

## The conversation

**Open (warm, no agenda):** _"Tell me a bit about who you work with and what a typical arc with a client looks like."_

**Core questions — the pain of lost continuity** (ask, then stay silent and let them fill it):
- Tell me about a client who lost momentum between sessions.
- Tell me about a breakthrough that disappeared.
- Tell me about a time you wished you had more continuity.
- What happens between appointments that you currently cannot see?

**Follow the charge** — when their voice changes or they reach for a metaphor, slow down: _"Say more about that."_ _"What word would you use for that?"_ The emotionally charged, reached-for phrase is the gold.

**Only at the very end (optional), test recognition** — never as a leading prompt earlier. Read the candidate phrasings flat and ask which, if any, they recognize:
- A — The most important part of transformation happens between sessions.
- B — Development has memory.
- C — Don't lose the thread.
- D — A living record of becoming.

Note which one lands _unprompted_ vs. only when offered. Unprompted recurrence beats prompted agreement every time. _Per `LANGUAGE_LOG_GOVERNANCE.md`, anything said in this end-test is `tested` (recognition testing), not `cold` discovery — log it separately; it never counts toward the five._

## What to listen for

- **Verbatim recurring language** — capture exact words, not paraphrases. The campaign line will be a phrase several people used independently.
- **The moment of recognition** — visible relief, "yes, exactly," leaning in.
- **The shape of the pain** — what's invisible to them between sessions; what they reconstruct each time; what they grieve losing.

## Signal vs. noise

- **Signal:** unprompted recurring phrases · strong felt-charge · the same pain named by different segments · spontaneous "I wish I had…".
- **Noise:** polite agreement · feature requests · praise for the idea · interest in the technology. (Interest is not recognition.)

## How it feeds the one motion

This can run **on the Living Field itself**, so discovery is captured as durable signal, not scattered notes:

```text
Living Question (which pain do you recognize?) → Practitioner Response
→ Observation Stream (sealed, verbatim) → recurring language
→ Shared Learning → Field Pulse reading → campaign language → product direction
```

- Frame the message test as a **Living Question**; "describe the moment you lost the thread with a client" as an **Experiment**; replies land in the sealed **Observation Stream**; recurring truths surface as **Shared Learnings** and a curated **Field Pulse** reading.

## Output — the Language Log

A living document: **`THINGS_PRACTITIONERS_KEEP_SAYING.md`** — verbatim practitioner phrases tagged by segment and felt-charge.

**Definition of done for the round** (narrow it hard — sentiment is not signal):
- ❌ "positive feedback" · ❌ "they liked the vision" · ❌ "concept validated"
- ✅ **five independent practitioners use nearly the same phrase for the same pain.**

The recognized story is the phrase that recurs _unprompted_. Hand _that_ to copy; the promise line ("Don't Lose the Thread") and the campaign get built on language the market already speaks.

## Guardrail

When the product does come up: MAIA **supports** continuity, reflection, integration — it does **not** replace wisdom, relationship, practice, or human judgment. And promise only the continuity that is **live and verified** (see the verified-capability map in the architecture doc). Recognition earns trust; an over-claim spends it.
