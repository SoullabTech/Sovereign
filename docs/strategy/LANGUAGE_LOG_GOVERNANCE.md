# Language Log Governance

_The constitution of the discovery engine. Companion to `THINGS_PRACTITIONERS_KEEP_SAYING.md`, `PRACTITIONER_DISCOVERY_INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md`, `SOULLAB_MARKETING_ARCHITECTURE.md` — 2026-06-03_

> The empty document is doing work. These rules keep it empty until practitioners fill it — and keep the founding cohort a **listening instrument**, not a marketing funnel.

## Why this exists

Most organizations secretly run:

```text
Desired Message → Research → Confirmation
```

We run:

```text
Observation → Language Log → Convergence → Category → Campaign
```

The emptiness of the Language Log protects that sequence. This governance protects the emptiness.

## Purpose

The Language Log captures language practitioners **independently** use to describe **their own experience.** It is an **observation document** — not a brainstorming document, not a positioning document, not a copywriting document.

## The rules

**1. Inclusion.** A phrase may enter the Log only when it was **spoken by a practitioner.** Not imagined by the team. Not proposed in strategy. Not because it sounds compelling.

**2. Signal.** A phrase becomes _signal_ when it appears **independently across multiple practitioners who could not reasonably have borrowed it from one another.** Threshold: **five independent occurrences describing substantially the same experience.**

**3. Campaign.** No campaign line may graduate into active use unless it **originates from the Log.** Marketing may refine, shorten, and clarify wording. Marketing may **not invent the underlying recognition.**

**4. Contamination.** Once a phrase is introduced into interviews, workshops, landing pages, or any marketing material, it is **no longer eligible as discovery evidence.** From that point it is **recognition testing, not category discovery.** (Enforced via provenance, below.)

**5. The Question.** Every entry must answer: **"What experience is this person trying to describe?"** The goal is not collecting phrases — it is discovering **recurring realities.**

## Provenance — how Rule 4 is enforced, not just stated

Every logged phrase carries a provenance tag:

- **`cold`** — heard _before_ the practitioner was exposed to any Soullab language. Counts toward discovery and the five-occurrence signal threshold.
- **`tested`** — said _after_ exposure (we read a candidate line, they saw a landing page, a peer repeated it). Counts only as **recognition testing** — never toward category discovery.

Practically:
- The candidate phrases in the Language Log are **never read to interviewees.** Doing so converts the rest of that conversation to `tested`.
- The optional end-of-interview A/B/C/D recognition check is, by definition, `tested`. Log it separately. It can confirm resonance; it can never establish that the category was _discovered_ rather than _suggested_.
- **Only `cold` occurrences count toward the five.**

## Success

Success is not positive feedback. Not excitement. Not agreement. **Success is when multiple practitioners independently describe the same problem using nearly the same language _before being given the category_.** At that point the category is being **discovered, not invented.**

## The principle — for the canon

This is the discovery-layer member of the project's discipline lineage:

- _built ≠ verified_ — product
- _observation before interpretation_ — field
- _fix the footgun, don't explain it_ — engineering
- **recognition before positioning** — _don't invent the language before you've heard the language_ — discovery / go-to-market

One epistemics, every layer: **do not run ahead of the evidence.** Here, the evidence is the practitioner's own unprompted words.
