# Beta Steward Program

## The Shift

Soullab beta testers are not users evaluating software.
They are stewards entering a living environment and helping it grow.

This distinction governs everything: language, structure, rhythm, and relationship.

The frame is not: "Here's what the system does."
The frame is: "Here are a few doors. Tell us what happens when you walk through them."

---

## Core Rhythm

**Orientation → Conversation → Reflection**

This is the pattern that creates intimacy between stewards and the field.

- **Orientation**: Simple map of what to explore (not documentation)
- **Conversation**: Direct experience with MAIA and the community
- **Reflection**: Structured feedback that feels collaborative, not clinical

---

## What Stewards Need (and What They Don't)

### They do not need
- Long documentation
- Feature lists
- Architecture explanations
- Bug report forms
- Technical onboarding guides

### They need three things

**1. A simple map**

Not technical — experiential. Three to five "doors" to try each week.

Example:

> **Three ways to explore Soullab this week**
>
> - Have a conversation with MAIA about something real
> - Try a daily check-in (Soul Mirror)
> - Visit a Field Room and respond to one person

That's it. People learn by doing.

**2. A place to respond emotionally**

Not bug reports. Felt experience.

- What surprised you?
- Where did it feel meaningful?
- Where did it feel confusing?
- Where did it feel flat or mechanical?

You're not testing function. You're testing aliveness.

**3. A sense of proximity**

Stewards should feel: "Kelly is actually listening."

Not through support tickets. Through presence. Through the Field Letter. Through "here's what we're learning from you."

## The Exploration Menu

A simple orientation for new stewards. One sentence per door. No documentation dump.

**Ways to explore Soullab right now**

| Door | What it is |
|------|-----------|
| Conversation with MAIA | Talk about something real — a decision, a feeling, a question |
| Soul Mirror | A quick daily reflection to see where you are elementally |
| Journal / Dream Upload | Bring something from your inner life into the field |
| Field Rooms | See what others are experiencing and respond |
| Practitioner Studio | For practitioners — explore how this integrates with your work |

Each door is an invitation, not a feature description.

## Field Rooms Structure (Beta)

### Room: Beta Field

The primary gathering space for stewards. Four persistent threads:

**Weekly Field Letter** (posted by Kelly)
The heartbeat of the program. See "Field Letter" section below.

**Meaningful Moments**
"When did this help you?"
For sharing experiences where MAIA, Soul Mirror, or the community felt alive, surprising, or genuinely useful. This is where signal lives.

**Confusing or Friction**
"What didn't make sense?"
For honest accounts of where things broke, felt mechanical, or lost coherence. This is where pain surfaces — and pain is information.

**Wishes and Possibilities**
"What do you wish it could do?"
For vision. What stewards imagine Soullab becoming. This is where the collective field begins to dream forward.

This three-thread structure separates **signal**, **pain**, and **vision** — and psychologically, it feels collaborative rather than critical.

## The Field Letter

**Frequency**: Weekly
**Tone**: Short. Personal. Human.
**Delivery**: Posted in Beta Field room + emailed as "The Field Update"

### Structure

**1. What's evolving this week**

Not release notes. Narrative. What the system is learning, what shifted, what's being explored.

Example tone:

> This week the system is learning to listen differently around emotional intensity. Some of you may notice responses feel quieter and more reflective. We're curious whether that lands differently.

**2. One way to explore**

A single focus for the week. One door. One experiment.

> Try asking MAIA about something you're uncertain about, and notice whether it asks questions back — or whether it jumps to answers.

**3. One question for the field**

An invitation to reflect together.

> Where did the system feel most human this week?
> Where did it feel least human?

Link to: **Beta Field → Meaningful Moments** or **Confusing / Friction** thread as appropriate.

---

## The Intimacy Multiplier

**Frequency**: Weekly or biweekly
**Format**: "Here's what we're learning from you"

This closes the loop. Stewards see their experience reflected back — proof that their presence shapes the system.

Examples:

> - Many of you are using MAIA for decision reflection rather than advice. That wasn't something we designed for — you taught us that.
> - Several people said the Soul Mirror helped them notice emotional patterns they'd been missing. We're exploring how to deepen that.
> - A number of you felt responses were too long. We're experimenting with brevity this week.

This builds:

- **Psychological ownership** — "my experience matters"
- **Trust** — "they're actually listening"
- **Early culture** — "this is how we relate here"
- **Steward identity** — "I'm not a user, I'm a co-creator"

---

## What This Builds (Strategically)

This program is not just feedback collection. It produces:

1. **Psychological ownership** — stewards feel the system is partly theirs
2. **Trust** — the foundation for everything that follows
3. **Early culture** — the norms and tone that shape all future community
4. **Narrative gravity** — stories that become the mythology of Soullab's origin
5. **Steward identity** — people who relate to the system reflectively, not transactionally
6. **Developmental data** — genuine signals about what creates aliveness vs. what falls flat

Most importantly: you're training people to interact with the system **reflectively**. That shapes your entire user base from the beginning.

## Shepherding the 90% (The Silent Majority)

Most of your 48 beta members haven't engaged yet. This is normal. The instinct is to increase outreach, add reminders, sharpen the message. Resist that. Silence doesn't mean disengagement. It usually means one of three quiet states:

- "I don't know where to begin."
- "I don't have time to learn a system right now."
- "This feels meaningful... I just haven't found my way in yet."

The question is not: how do we get them to log in?
The question is: how do we reduce the emotional distance between them and the living thing that's forming?

### The Shepherding Principle

For the 90%, you are not trying to drive behavior. You are doing three things:

1. **Remove guilt** — gratitude for their presence, even if they've done nothing
2. **Keep the connection warm** — the Field Letter creates narrative continuity
3. **Let curiosity ripen** — belonging precedes activity, always

Think of it as tending a fire at the edge of their attention.

### Phase 1: The Gratitude Re-Invitation (Week 0)

The first email after the silence. Leads with thank you, not "come back." Acknowledges that the earlier emails were product-shaped (features, bug fixes, passkey reminders) and that what's emerged since is something different — a living environment, not a tool.

Key emotional move: *"Whether you've explored it yet or simply kept the invitation, your presence matters."*

Script: `scripts/send-steward-invitation.ts`

### Phase 2: The Heartbeat (Weeks 1-6)

The weekly Field Letter becomes the primary relationship. Not updates. Not features. Three parts only:

1. What's quietly happening
2. One way to explore
3. One reflective question

**Critical truth**: Many people will read Field Letters for weeks before logging in. That's healthy. They're moving from curiosity to trust to readiness.

In Field Letter #2 or #3, include this line:

> Some of you are active inside the Field. Many of you are simply reading these notes. Both ways of being here matter.

That single sentence removes invisible shame — and often triggers engagement.

### Phase 3: Segmentation Without Pressure (Week 3-4)

After two Field Letters, the 48 naturally divide:

| Group | Signal | What to do |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| **Active** (~5-10) | Logging in, posting | Invite deeper reflection |
| **Watchers** (~15-20) | Opening emails | Just keep sending Field Letters |
| **Silent** (~20-25) | No opens | One personal note, then respect the silence |

The personal note for the silent group should be very light:

> "Hey — just wanted to make sure my emails are reaching you. No pressure at all — if the timing isn't right, that's completely fine."

Then stop. Respecting silence increases trust more than repeated outreach.

### Phase 4: Field Gravity

The system is working when:

- People reply to Field Letters
- Someone posts without being prompted
- Members respond to each other

That's when engagement stops being something you drive. The field begins to hold itself.

### The Mindset Shift

You don't have 48 disengaged testers. You have:

- ~10 early stewards
- ~20 quiet observers
- ~20 people holding a dormant connection

Observers are extremely valuable. In community dynamics, watchers often become the most committed members once they cross the threshold. They just need time to feel safe.

### The Intimacy Multiplier: "From the Field"

Once a week or biweekly, post short anonymous observations:

> - Someone said they used MAIA during a difficult conversation and felt less reactive.
> - Several people noticed the Soul Mirror made emotional patterns visible over days.
> - One person said the system felt most alive when it asked questions instead of giving advice.

This creates shared experience, collective identity, and emotional safety. People start feeling: "Others are going through this with me." That's when a community becomes a field.

### What Not to Do

- Do not survey
- Do not score
- Do not run feature votes or roadmap polls
- These pull the energy back into product evaluation mode

Instead, keep asking open, reflective questions:
- Where did this feel alive?
- Where did it feel mechanical?
- When did it feel useful?
- When did it feel like too much?

---

## Anti-Patterns

- Do not create a bug tracker and call it community
- Do not overwhelm with features, documentation, or options
- Do not gamify participation (no badges, no streaks, no points)
- Do not treat feedback as metrics to optimize
- Do not create distance between Kelly and the stewards
- Do not rush past confusion — confusion is information
- Do not aggregate away individual experience into statistics

---

## From Beta to Movement

The beta period ends when:

1. Stewards feel ownership, not obligation
2. The Field Letter rhythm is self-sustaining
3. Community threads have organic conversation (not just prompted responses)
4. The first steward invites someone else — not because asked, but because they want to share what they've found

At that point, the beta is over. Not because the software is finished — but because the field is alive.
