# Soullab Press — Witness Protocol (one page)

**Date:** 2026-07-22 · **Status:** active instrument · **Serves:** [Creator/World Studio Direction Candidate](../architecture/CREATOR_WORLD_STUDIO_DIRECTION_CANDIDATE_2026-07-22.md)

This is experimental discipline, not architecture. It exists to prevent three errors:

- mistaking **enthusiasm** for **fit**;
- treating **any useful experience** as confirmation of the proposed dynamic;
- **interpreting the participant's experience for them** afterward.

The four relational dynamics (self-recognition, emergence, relationship, transmission) are *hypotheses about people who have not yet walked*. This protocol is how they earn — or lose — the right to be believed.

---

## How to run one walk

1. **Pre-register before the person arrives.** Fill *Working hypothesis · Primary question · Fit observable · Disconfirming observable · Non-evidence* in advance, and lock them. Behavior is defined *before* the walk so the walk can surprise you. Never "watch for recognition" — name the visible act that would count.
2. **Observer stays silent on meaning.** Help with mechanics (how a control works); never suggest a grouping, name a theme, or offer an interpretation. If the observer names the pattern, that observation is **void** — the observer is bound by the same non-interpretation constraint as the system.
3. **Fill the post-walk fields from behavior**, in the participant's own words — quote, do not paraphrase.
4. **Later consequence needs a pre-decided, non-leading checkpoint.** Set the follow-up window in advance (e.g. *did they return to the material within ~a week, unprompted?*). Ask what they did, not whether it felt meaningful.
5. **Watch for the Missing Object Signal.** Objects are discovered *behaviorally* before they are named — record what the participant acted as though existed that the system couldn't represent. This is the primitive-discovery instrument: a signal that **recurs across walks** is what turns a missing object into a candidate primitive.
6. **Keep three layers separate in the notes.** (1) *Observed behavior* — what happened. (2) *Immediate language* — what it was called in the moment. (3) *Later interpretation* — what it may imply. Only layer 1 is raw evidence. Fast conceptual intelligence collapses observation and ontology into one sentence — the layers keep them apart. (E.g. layer 1: *"looked repeatedly for a way to order twelve talks into a participant sequence"* — **not** layer 3's *"needs a Curriculum object."*)

## Capture per walk (only this)

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Working hypothesis** | The dynamic presently suspected |
| **Primary question** | What this walk is actually testing |
| **Fit observable** | Behavior that would support the hypothesis *(pre-registered)* |
| **Disconfirming observable** | Behavior that would weaken or overturn it *(pre-registered)* |
| **Non-evidence** | Reactions that sound positive but prove little *(pre-registered)* |
| **Unprompted reach** | What the person tries to do without suggestion |
| **Missing Object Signal** | What object the participant behaved as though existed, though the system had no representation for it ("these belong together" · "can we both hold this" · "this isn't a chapter" · "somewhere for these images") |
| **Missing Object Signal** | An object the participant behaved as though existed, though the system had no representation for it |
| **Member-authored account** | What the participant says happened, in their language (quoted) |
| **Later consequence** | Whether the experience changes behavior after leaving |

**On the Missing Object Signal (the primitive-discovery field).** Objects are discovered *behaviorally* before they are named. During the walk, record any moment the participant acted as though an object existed that the system cannot represent — e.g. *"these belong together"* · *"this should live beside this"* · *"can we both hold this?"* · *"I need somewhere for these images"* · *"this isn't a chapter."* Record the behavior and the participant's words; do **not** name the object for them, and do **not** update the ontology from a single signal (a real primitive recurs across walks). This field is the direct instrument for the four open questions in the [Substrate & Open Questions](../architecture/SOULLAB_PRESS_SUBSTRATE_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS_2026-07-22.md) page.

## Pre-registered falsification frames

### Kelly — founder walk (self-recognition + body-of-teachings)
**Possibility:** The Room becomes a place for stewarding a lifetime body of teachings across many forms — and encountering his own older material changes his relationship to it.
- **Supports:** he keeps passages he did not expect · names a relationship among passages himself · **reaches for an object not presently in the ontology** (Missing Object Signal) · returns later to reopen, reorganize, or write from the material.
- **Disconfirmation:** despite broad experience, the work **repeatedly collapses back toward manuscript-shaped objects** · he keeps only passages he already knew mattered · leaves without seeing or doing anything differently.
- **Doesn't count:** "the room looks beautiful" · "this is promising" · mechanical completion.
- **⚠ Unique risk — pre-registered explicitly:** *founder knowledge unconsciously authors outcomes.* Kelly knows the paths, frameworks, and intended structure; his walk can confirm what he built rather than discover what reality does. **Guard:** walk toward what surprises; treat any outcome you could have predicted as suspect.
- **Scope: Discovery first** (not mechanical-path testing — that confirms known paths). Discovery uses an LLM to pre-rank his passages by "resonance" (a recorded crossing), so Kelly walks it *knowing the model curated*, and watches the constitutional question directly: does the pre-selection **support** his noticing or **displace** it? That firsthand read is the evidence for his later ruling on the extractor.

**Pre-registered walk questions (answer from what happened, not what it means):**
- What did I attempt without planning to?
- Where did I stop because the available object didn't match the work?
- What did I repeatedly try to gather, connect, sequence, or return to?
- Did Discovery surface material I genuinely **recognized**, or merely material that **sounded resonant**?
- Did the model's selection **expand** my noticing, **narrow** it, or **substitute** for it?
- At what moment, if any, did the Room stop feeling manuscript-shaped?
- What did I expect to want but never reached for? *(disconfirmation — check against the availability rule before counting it as evidence)*

## Three rules that protect the experiment

1. **Absence is evidence — but only when the object was reasonably available.** A walk that produces no recognition, no unexpected behavior, and no later consequence is a valid result, not a failed observation. *However:* absence is category evidence only if the material and the affordance were actually within reach. A participant who never met a capability, or whose material the system couldn't even ingest, produces an **infrastructure** finding, not a verdict on the category. (See the interpretation hierarchy below.)
2. **The participant names what happened.** The record may say: *"Jason grouped these six items and called the relationship 'food as remembering.'"* It may **not** say: *"The platform revealed that Jason's work is really about ancestral memory."* That distinction protects both the experiment and the member.
3. **Surprise is evidence — preserve it before interpreting.** Not every surprise should become theory, but every surprise deserves to be recorded raw (note-layer 1) before interpretation begins. The instinct to *explain* a surprise is the same instinct that *erases* it.

## Interpretation hierarchy — separate infrastructure from category

Before reading any walk as evidence, classify what happened:

1. **Material unavailable to the Room** (couldn't ingest it) — *infrastructure finding.*
2. **Material available but awkward** (held it, clumsy fit) — *infrastructure finding.*
3. **Material available and naturally adopted** — *category evidence.*
4. **Material available and new objects emerged** — *category evidence (strongest).*

**Only (3) and (4) speak to the direction.** (1) and (2) tell us about the tool, not the category — *containment failure ≠ ontology failure.* Larry is the walk most likely to sit at (1): most of his corpus is slides/recordings/video the text-based Room cannot ingest. Guard hardest there against reading a format failure as a verdict on whether a lifetime body of teachings can be stewarded here.
