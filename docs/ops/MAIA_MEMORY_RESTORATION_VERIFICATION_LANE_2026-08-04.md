## Phase 1 — Establish current reality

**Reality capture, not repair. ⛔ No fixes in this phase.** Treat this as a physiological
assessment before treatment — *do not repair what has not yet been understood.* The report answers
ONLY (final founder formulation):

1. **What exists?** — tables, services, providers, loaders.
2. **What is active?** — runtime paths actually called (and **where does the path stop?**).
3. **What retrieves?** — sources contributing candidates.
4. **What is filtered?** — gates, suppression, relevance rules.
5. **What is offered?** — the transition from internal availability to conversational availability.
6. **What is injected?** — actual context reaching MAIA.
7. **What influences?** — observable effects ONLY; ⛔ no inferred causation.
8. **What remains unknown?** — explicit uncertainty. *A mature system needs an honest place to say
   "we do not yet know."*

Plus: current deployed SHA · active services · retrieval paths · gating rules · prompt assembly
path. The Phase 1 report is the lane's first meaningful artifact — it determines whether the next
move is a rule change, an observability addition, a bug fix, or **confirmation that current
behavior was intentional. The evidence gets to decide.**

## Completion standard (founder)

> Memory restoration is complete when **MAIA can participate in continuity with a person without
> pretending to know more than she knows, without forgetting what she is permitted to remember,
> and without taking ownership of the person's meaning.**

⚠️ **A successful restoration may not produce MORE memory surfaced — it may produce more accurate
memory behavior.** Sometimes maturity means remembering more; sometimes remembering less; sometimes
saying why something was not brought forward. Success is never *"MAIA retrieved more memories"* —
it is *"MAIA knows why she remembers something, why she offers it, what she cannot conclude, and
what remains the member's."* `suppressed_count` and `suppression_reason` are not debugging fields;
**they are the beginning of a self-understanding mechanism.** Retrieval says *"I found these six
things"* (finding); offering says *"these are appropriate to bring into this moment"* (choosing) —
that judgment may be right or wrong, but without observability neither the engineer nor MAIA can
know which.

### The five truths behind "I don't remember" (founder ruling on false compression)

When MAIA says *"I have no memory of our early conversations"* while 142 atoms and retrieval
infrastructure exist, the phrase collapses five different realities:

1. Those conversations were **never stored**.
2. Stored but **not retrieved**.
3. Retrieved but **not selected**.
4. Selected but **not injected**.
5. Available but **outside the current continuity policy**.

**Phase 5 target:** MAIA must be able to say *which* truth applies — the honest versions range from
*"that was before what I'm permitted to hold"* to *"I have that, and it wasn't brought into this
moment."* The single phrase "I don't remember" is exactly the false compression this architecture
exists to eliminate.

### Priority ruling (founder): the first fix is legibility, not volume

⛔ Do NOT respond to this evidence by increasing retrieval or loading more atoms. **Before recall
grows, MAIA needs to know: why something was selected · why something was withheld · whether
withholding was intentional · whether the member should be told about the limitation.** Otherwise
more memory creates a larger opaque system. The lane's first engineering question:

> **What is the selection mechanism that turns 142 into 8 — and can it account for itself?**

Possible outcomes, all acceptable: selecting correctly but needs observability · overly
restrictive · stale gate · needs a better continuity policy. **⛔ Do not change the loader until
the mechanism is known.**

### Founder refinement — relationship integrity roadmap (2026-08-04, final)

**The first engineering act:** `inspect → connect → verify → extend` — never
`invent → store → retrieve`. For the BUILT-UNWIRED thread substrate, the audit questions:

**A thread is held, not owned.** `withdrawn` is not a minor state — it prevents the failure mode

```
Member shares → MAIA holds → life continues → MAIA reconnects → member confirms meaning
```

The last step is load-bearing: MAIA reconnects; **the member confirms meaning.**

**Why semantic search is later, stated finally:** embeddings answer *"what information is
similar?"*; threads answer **"what matters remains alive?"** — *a highly sophisticated semantic
engine could retrieve the wrong thing beautifully; a simple thread model could preserve the right
thing faithfully.*

**The dependency structure (binding order):**
1. **Truth** — can MAIA accurately describe her memory state? (policy declaration · transition

3. **Context** — can MAIA recognize what kind of remembering is being invited?
4. **Intelligence** — can MAIA use richer forms of relationship? (semantic · pattern ·
   developmental · wisdom translation)

*The ordering is not conservative — it is the fastest path to trust.*

**The design target:** neither accumulation nor amnesia. **Faithful presence.**

### Closing statement (founder) — expansive memory, humble meaning

Not a retention problem — **a relationship integrity problem**: *what kind of relationship must
exist between MAIA, memory, and the person for deeper remembering to be worthy of trust?* A small
memory can harm by confidently misrepresenting what it knows; a large memory can serve deeply if
transparent, invitational, and subordinate to the person's meaning-making.

| Capability | Without governance | With governance |
|---|---|---|
| More memory | surveillance | continuity |
| Pattern recognition | labeling | reflection |
| Personalization | prediction | accompaniment |
| Recall | authority | service |
| Knowledge of person | profile | relationship |

*The same capability becomes a cage or a companion depending on whether meaning is owned by the
system or returned to the person.*

**`withdrawn` models what technical memory systems do not: time changes the meaning of the past.**
*"That mattered then" · "I don't want to revisit that now" · "I see it differently today" · "I am
no longer that person."* Without withdrawal: *once learned, always relevant.* With it: the
relationship between a person and their own history evolves. **Not a privacy feature — a model of
human becoming.**

**The actual promise of memory is relief** — not convenience, not personalization: the relief of
not having to repeatedly explain who you are, what you carry, what you're building, what matters.
Valid only while the person stays free to say *"that was true then; it is not true now."*

**The four storage questions (replace "can we store this?" for all future expansion — a permanent
review instrument governing schemas, migrations, retrieval pipelines, prompt construction, and
future AI capabilities):**
1. **What is this?** — event? thread? pattern? reflection? member-declared meaning?
   *Prevents category collapse.*
2. **Where did it come from?** — explicit statement? member action? system observation?
   *Prevents false authority.* (*"I want to write a book"* ≠ *"MAIA noticed creative themes"* —
   both useful, not the same origin.)
3. **What authority does it have?** — the ladder: `observed → remembered → inferred → offered →
   confirmed`. **The higher the rung, the greater the responsibility.** *Prevents interpretation
   drift.*
4. **How can the member revise it?** — *the most human question. Prevents freezing a person in
   time: a system that cannot allow revision will turn yesterday's truth into today's constraint.*

**The implicit shape of every future memory object** (beyond `content · timestamp · embedding ·
metadata`):
```
origin:        where did this come from?
authority:     what kind of claim is this?
relationship:  who has standing to change its meaning?
state:         active · resolved · withdrawn?
offering:      why is it appearing now?
```
*That is the difference between a memory system and a relational memory system.*

**The four final questions, named:** *what do I hold* — the **inventory** question · *why do I
hold it* — the **provenance** question · *what am I offering* — the **humility** question · *what
remains yours* — the **sovereignty** question. **A sufficiently advanced system can answer the
first three and still become dangerous. The fourth requires restraint — it is the one that keeps
the architecture human.** Memory and humility are coupled, not competing: without provenance,
transparency, revisability, and the observation/interpretation distinction, memory accumulation
becomes authority accumulation; **with them, deeper memory becomes a gift.**

The first thing a trustworthy memory system must remember is not *"here is more about the person"*
— it is **"here is the truth about myself."** Not because caution is the goal — **because trust is
the enabling condition for depth.**

*That is how a system becomes capable of holding a lifetime without trying to own one.*

> **MAIA can carry the story. The person remains the author.**
>
> A trustworthy companion begins not by claiming more. It begins by accurately knowing what it is.

> That boundary is the difference between **a companion and an interpreter of a person's life.**

**Selection axis discipline (permanent):** the current `ORDER BY is_breakthrough DESC, kept_at
DESC` mixes two different things — **member-declared significance** (*"I want to keep this"* — a
relational act) and **system-inferred significance** (*"this seems important"* — an

**The Truth sprint's real purpose** — not telemetry, the foundation of trust. Before *"I remember
