# The Representation Authority Law

**Status:** CANON — adjudicated by founder act 2026-09-17 (`docs/programme/J9_REPRESENTATION_AUTHORITY_ADJUDICATION_2026-09-17.md`).
**Scope:** General. ⛔ Not restricted to AIN or to knowledge retrieval — J9 is its proving ground, not its semantic boundary.
**Confers:** no implementation authority.
**Converges with, does not absorb:** `INTERFACE_HUMILITY.md` (see §2).
**Kin:** `RECOGNITION_INTEGRITY.md` · `PATTERN_PRIMITIVE.md` · `RIGHT_TO_REMAIN_UNPOSSESSED.md` ·
`MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md` · `CONSTITUTIONAL_DIRECTION_OF_AUTHORITY.md`

> ⚠️ **Renamed 2026-09-17** from *"The AIN Representation Law"*. Representation is not the constitutional
> problem — MAIA, AIN, retrieval, memory, interfaces and ordinary cognition all necessarily create
> representations. **The problem is a representation silently acquiring authority over the thing represented.**
> The name now says what the law governs. The `AIN` prefix is dropped because J9 exposed the law; it does not
> bound it.

## 1. The law

> **A representation is descriptive by default.**
>
> **A representation may determine or alter identity, validity, eligibility, admission, inclusion, exclusion,
> crossing, or other authoritative standing only when:**
>
> 1. **a governed act explicitly grants that representation such authority; and**
> 2. **an attestation exists that can fail when the authority-bearing condition becomes stale, absent,
>    substituted, or invalid.**

Both conditions. A grant without an attestation is an authority nobody can audit; an attestation without a
grant is instrumentation around an authority nobody conferred.

### What does not grant authority

```text
Computation does not grant authority.
Presentation does not grant authority.
Similarity  does not grant authority.
Salience    does not grant authority.
Classification does not grant authority.
Table membership does not grant authority.

Authority comes from the governed act that grants it.
```

`INTERFACE_HUMILITY.md` is **retained in its present doctrinal scope.** It is neither withdrawn, nor widened
into a general computational law, nor reduced to a child of this one.

therefore the stronger instrument.

## 3. The defeater — Substitution → Grant → Attestation

**RATIFIED. The law is not complete without it.** For any representation that affects an authoritative
outcome, ask in order:

### A — SUBSTITUTION
Hold the represented thing constant. Change, remove, stale, relabel, recompute or substitute the
representation. **Does an authoritative result change?**
→ **No:** the representation may remain DESCRIPTIVE. Stop. → **Yes:** continue.

### B — GRANT
**What exact governed act granted this representation authority to cause that change?**
The answer must identify an actual act, contract, rule, member instruction, admission decision, rights
decision, or other governed authority. ⛔ **It may not be inferred merely because the implementation currently
uses the representation.**
→ **No grant can be named:** the representation is exercising **spurious authority**.

### C — ATTESTATION
**What becomes red when the representation is wrong, stale, missing, improperly substituted, or no longer
entitled to that authority?**
→ **Nothing can become red:** the claimed authority is **not adequately attested**.

## 4. Authority classes

**DESCRIPTIVE** — the default. May inform, orient, rank, display, suggest or assist **without independently
determining authoritative standing.**

**SELECTIVE** — may **narrow a valid candidate set** because a governed act has explicitly authorized that
form of selection.

**AUTHORITATIVE** — may **establish or alter** identity, validity, admission, eligibility, rights, crossing,
or equivalent governed state.

> **Promotion from DESCRIPTIVE is never inferred from usefulness, accuracy, confidence, salience,
> implementation convenience, or repeated use. It requires a governed grant.**

⭐ **SELECTIVE is the class that makes the law usable rather than absolutist.** Most real systems need a
representation to narrow a candidate set; the law does not forbid that. It requires the narrowing to be
*authorized as narrowing* — and, critically, **a SELECTIVE representation may not decide that something does
not exist.** Narrowing operates inside an already-established corpus; it never establishes one.

⚠️ **Open vocabulary item, not resolved here.** `J8-R3 §VII` proposes a **four**-class taxonomy on this same
axis (AUTHORITATIVE / DERIVED-GOVERNED / DESCRIPTIVE / EXPERIMENTAL). These three are ratified; those four are
a candidate contract. `DERIVED-GOVERNED` and `SELECTIVE` are not obviously the same class, and `EXPERIMENTAL`
has no counterpart here. **One canonical term, one canonical referent** — the reconciliation is owed before
both vocabularies reach implementation. ⛔ Not reconciled by silent merge.

## 7. What would show this law is wrong

A law that explains everything decides nothing. Recorded so it can fail:

- **Over-breadth.** *Everything* computed is a representation. Read as *"no computed value may decide
  anything"*, the law forbids computing and **is defeated by that reading.** §3 exists to prevent it: the law
  binds only where substitution changes an authoritative result **and** no grant can be named.
- **The genuine counter-case.** A representation may legitimately be the best available authority — a checksum
  *is* content identity; a member's own label *is* their meaning. ⭐ Both survive **GRANT**: an act conferred
  it. If a case is found where authority is clearly correct and clearly *unconferrable*, the law is too strong
  and must be narrowed.
- **Cost.** If §3 applied across a real lane yields mostly DESCRIPTIVE reclassifications nobody acts on, the
  law is producing distinctions rather than decisions and should be withdrawn.

## 8. Direction of authority

This law is the representational form of `CONSTITUTIONAL_DIRECTION_OF_AUTHORITY.md`: authority moves upward
through authored experience and may never be manufactured at a higher layer. A representation acquiring
authority **is** manufacture — a label standing in for admission, a score for existence, a match for a
member's act, a portrait for a person.

> **Before asking "which knowledge is relevant?", the system must know "which knowledge is legitimately here?"**

> Representation helps the system find what matters. It does not get to decide what matters,
> what exists, or what you meant.
