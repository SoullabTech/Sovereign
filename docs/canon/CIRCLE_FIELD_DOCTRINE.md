# Circle Field Build Doctrine

_Governing principles for all Circle development. Companion to MAIA_CANON_v1.1 and MAIA_SOVEREIGNTY_INVARIANTS._

---

## Core Law

The field is primary. Content is secondary.

---

## Governing Invariants

### The Membrane Invariant

The boundary between Personal Field (MAIA) and Shared Field (Circle) must remain:
- **Permeable by choice** — manual sharing, intentional acts only
- **Opaque by default** — nothing crosses without deliberate action
- **Reversible at all times** — revocation cascades, no hostage data

What would break it: auto-suggestions, previews before contribution, "recommended to share" nudges, cross-circle leakage. Even small convenience features can dissolve the membrane.

### The Coherence Function

A system decision is correct if it increases:
- **Continuity** — helps a person track themselves over time
- **Integration** — helps them hold tension without premature resolution
- **Relational integrity** — they can stay themselves while participating

A system decision is incorrect if it increases speed over reflection, visibility over truth, agreement over differentiation, or expression without integration.

### Qualities of Being

Not structure — felt quality the system carries:
- **MAIA Voice Shift**: Alone = reflective, interpretive, intimate. In Circle = orienting, spacious, non-centralizing. MAIA must never become the center of meaning inside a circle.
- **Threshold Moment**: Circle entry is crossing into a field, not loading a page. Brief pause, field language first, no immediate input focus.
- **Emergent Identity**: Circles develop distinct character through accumulated memory and inquiry — never through configuration.

---

## Principles

1. **Every screen must orient before it informs.** A person entering a circle should feel where they are before they see what others have said.

2. **No mechanic should reward frequency, speed, or performance.** If a design choice incentivizes volume, it distorts the field.

3. **MAIA inside a circle is a steward of coherence, not a content generator.** Her role shifts from "I'm with you" (individual) to "we're in a field together" (collective). She does not summarize, moderate, or direct. She reflects the field back to itself.

4. **A circle should feel more like entering weather than opening a chat room.** Atmospheric, not informational. The pulse is felt, not read.

5. **Preserve sovereignty even when optimizing usability.** If making something easier to use erodes the consent boundary, the intentional weight, or the contribute-before-you-see constraint — reject the optimization.

6. **If a design choice makes the system more legible but less alive, reject it.** Dashboards, metrics, activity graphs, and engagement indicators flatten the field into data. The field is not data.

7. **Coherence is felt and reflected, never scored.** The moment coherence becomes a number, people perform coherence instead of developing it.

---

## Interaction Order (Non-Negotiable)

```
Feel → Contribute → Browse
```

Never reversed. Never collapsed.

---

## Structural Constraints (Inherited from Implementation)

- 2-member minimum for theme surfacing (anonymity threshold)
- One inquiry at a time per circle (prevents fragmentation)
- One response per member per inquiry (prevents thread collapse)
- Responses hidden until contributed (prevents convergence)
- No counts, percentages, or scores in any circle surface
- No notification badges or urgency mechanics
- Manual sharing only (consent is structural)
- "Add to the field" requires an orienting step

---

## Open Questions (Require Founder Input)

These cannot be answered by architecture. They define the felt quality of the system.

### 1. Field Tone

- How quiet should circle entry feel? (Meditative pause? Gentle shift? Simply different?)
- Should the language be sacred, clinical, poetic, or restrained?
- What is the right amount of MAIA's presence inside a circle — visible guide, quiet witness, or near-invisible infrastructure?

### 2. Facilitator Philosophy

- What is a facilitator *for* in this system?
- What should a facilitator never do?
- How does a facilitator close an inquiry without collapsing the multiplicity of responses into a single narrative?
- How does the system support facilitator development as a first-class pathway?

### 3. MAIA's Voice Inside Circles

- More witnessing, more facilitative, or less interpretive than individual sessions?
- Does MAIA speak to the circle as a whole, or to individuals within the context of the circle?
- When does MAIA offer a field synthesis vs. leaving synthesis to the facilitator?
- What is MAIA's relationship to tension in the field — does she name it, hold it, or stay silent?

### 4. Observation Protocol

How do we evaluate whether the living experience matches the vision?

- Where do people speed up? (Indicates performance pressure)
- Where do they get confused? (Indicates orientation failure)
- Where do they perform rather than participate? (Indicates social media gravity)
- Where does the field actually deepen? (Indicates the system is working)

These observations will come from the Soullab Dream Team circle. They cannot be anticipated — only watched for.

## The Test

> Does this help a person stay in contact with their own process while participating in something larger than themselves?

If yes → keep.
If no → it will distort the field.
