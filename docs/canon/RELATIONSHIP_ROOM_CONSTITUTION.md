# The Relationship Room Constitution

> **Status: PROPOSED — awaiting founder ratification. Recorded 2026-08-10.**
> Recording is not a state change. Ratification occurs only through an explicit act of the
> designated authority (`RATIFICATION_BOUNDARY_PRECEDENT_2026-07-26.md`). Articles II–IV,
> VIII and IX restate or extend founder rulings R3–R7 already ratified into
> `docs/design/reviews/RELATIONSHIP_PAGE_RELATIONAL_EXPERIENCE_AUDIT_2026-08-10.md`; the
> remainder is proposed law.
>
> ⛔ **This document authorizes no code, no schema, no migration, no UI.** It is law about
> what a Relationship Room *is*, written before design resumes. It is not a specification.

**Evidence base** (read; not restated): the Relational Experience Audit of 2026-08-10 and
its three discovery lanes. Where those conflict, the synthesis governs.

## The three things this document keeps apart

Conflating them is the error this instrument exists to prevent.

1. **Member experience** — the member's own words about what happened. Legitimate personal
   memory.
2. **Machine classification** — *"this appears relational"*: detection, categorization,
   confidence. Observer provenance, never the member's voice.
3. **Third-party characterization** — a claim about who the other person *is*. This corpus
   does not do it, and must not begin.

⚠️ **Correction of record.** An earlier intermediate reading described the historical
observer corpus as a machine-authored psychological dossier about third parties. That was
verified wrong: no name is inferred, no bond type is stored, the container is a single
fixed catch-all string, and stored content is the member's own language, truncated, with
no model interpretation. What the machine contributes is detection, classification and
confidence — not characterization of another human. No sentence in this document may imply
otherwise.

## I — THE OBJECT

**PRINCIPLE.** The object of a Relationship Room is **my relationship with this person** —
not the person, and not a record about the person. The member is *inside* the relationship,
as a participant, not outside it as an analyst reviewing a file.

**BOUNDARY.** The room may not be organized as a profile of the other human being. No
element may be structured so that the member's own authored material appears as an
attribute of the other person. Where the grammar of a surface makes the member disappear,
the object has been lost regardless of how the surface looks.

**IMPLICATION.** **ME** — the member must be present and primary; their words are the
substance, not a caption. **YOU** — the other person appears as a participant, named and
respected, never modeled. **US** — the pair has standing as a shared history the member
recorded. **THE BETWEEN** — see Article III; it is a place, and it belongs to the member's
experience.

## II — RELATIONAL AUTHORSHIP

**PRINCIPLE.** The member holds primary authority to name the condition of their own
relationship. Three sources of statement exist and must remain distinguishable at all
times: **member declaration** (*"something between us broke"*), **MAIA observation**
(*"something here sounds ruptured"*), and **system inference** (a heuristic classification
with a confidence value).

**BOUNDARY.** An observation or an inference may never silently become a declaration. The
system may not author into the member's relational record any statement of relational
condition that the member has not made. That MAIA today has a write path for *rupture*
which no member-facing surface offers is an inversion of authorship, stated here as a
constitutional fault — the remedy is not designed here.

**IMPLICATION.** Every rendered statement carries its author. Member-authored and
system-authored material must be visibly distinguishable, and the member must be able to
correct or withdraw what the system wrote about their relationship. Authorship includes
the authority to decline to name anything at all.

## III — THE BETWEEN

**PRINCIPLE.** **The Between is phenomenological before it is inferential.** It is where
the member experiences what happens between them: their lived sense of the relationship,
their own sensing, shared history, interaction patterns as they name them, relational
events, ambiguity, tension, tenderness, distance, movement.

**BOUNDARY.** The Between is never privileged access to the other person's interiority.
MAIA may help the member perceive what is between them; MAIA may not convert perception
into certainty about the other person. The operative test: every sentence rendered in this
room must survive being read aloud with the prefix *"In my experience, …"* — or it must not
be rendered.

**IMPLICATION.** The restraint is structural, not tonal. Pressure to let the system explain
the other person will increase as the room becomes more alive, because that is what members
often want and what comparable products supply. The refusal must be built into where things
can be placed and how they may be phrased, not left to the quality of a prompt.

## IV — MEMORY

**PRINCIPLE.** A Relationship Room accumulates relationship-specific life. Where explicit
relationship identity exists, material generated in that room belongs to that relationship
unless the member explicitly places it elsewhere. Relational memory preserves: relationship
identity · source · authorship · provenance · epistemic status · time.

**BOUNDARY.** **UNKNOWN REMAINS UNKNOWN.** Historical material whose relationship identity
was never captured may not be assigned to a person by guessing, by name matching, by
inference, or by model judgment. Of 1,139 historical observer rows, none is explicitly
attributable, 5 are derivable, 15 possible, 21 unresolved, and 1,098 unknowable. Most of
that past cannot be safely attributed from retained evidence, and it therefore stays
unattributed.

**IMPLICATION.** The line is drawn at the present: the past remains unresolved with no
retroactive person assignment; from the moment explicit room identity exists and survives,
history begins compounding. A thin true history is worth more than a full invented one.

## V — WORKED AND HELD

**PRINCIPLE.** Relational life divides two ways. **WORKED** — something presently invites
movement: contact · repair · conversation · boundary · discernment · clarification.
**HELD** — the relationship remains meaningful without requiring action: remembrance ·
grief · honoring · witnessing · acceptance · distance · completion · mystery.

**BOUNDARY.** These are not fixed taxonomies and not a partition. A relationship may be
both at once, neither, or move between them without announcement. The distinction may only
be member-declared — never inferred from silence, cadence, tone, or elapsed time — and it
must be available without ever being demanded. **No relationship owes the software a next
step.**

**IMPLICATION.** A room that merely *omits* a next step is not a room that *knows nothing
is owed*. Where a relationship is held, the machinery that produces prompts, elapsed-time
signals and unresolved threads should be incapable of firing rather than merely quiet.

## VI — CONDITION OF BEING

**PRINCIPLE.** A relationship has a **condition of being** — not a status, not a score, not
a stage in a funnel, not a developmental rank. Illustrative only, and explicitly **not a
required enumeration**: living · close · changing · distant · ruptured · estranged · ended ·
gone · held · unknown.

**BOUNDARY.** No condition may be ordered, ranked, aggregated, or compared across
relationships. Conditions may not be derived by the system. Any fixed vocabulary is itself
a framework imposed on the member's meaning, and words like *close*, *ordinary* and
*estranged* do not carry the same sense in every family or culture.

**IMPLICATION.** **"I don't know" is legitimate relational knowledge.** It is not missing
data, not an unset field, and not a prompt to resolve. Unknowing is the default; it must
render as a real condition rather than as emptiness or deficit, and a room in an unknown
condition receives the same dignity, hearth and history as any other.

## VII — CHANGE, ENDING AND DEATH

**PRINCIPLE.** A Relationship Room must remain dignified when the relationship changes,
grows distant, ruptures, ends, becomes estranged, cannot be repaired, or involves someone
who has died. A relationship with someone who has died remains a relationship in the
member's lived world — not an archived contact.

**BOUNDARY.** Forward movement is never structurally mandatory. Nothing in the room may
treat silence as unfinished business, absence of action as a deficit, or an ending as
disappearance. Deletion may not be the only available form of completion, and no surface
may ask whether a condition has changed.

**IMPLICATION.** The room must be able to hold what cannot be improved. The measure is not
whether the member acts, but whether the room remains a place they can bear to enter on the
worst day the relationship has.

## VIII — MAIA

**PRINCIPLE.** The hearth is **the member speaking about the relationship**; MAIA is
present at it. She is company at the fire, not the fire. The room must therefore retain its
full meaning when MAIA is absent, unavailable, or declined.

**BOUNDARY.** MAIA **may**: witness · remember · reflect · help discern · notice patterns
tentatively and revisably · help prepare a communication · accompany repair · accompany
grief · surface the member's own history back to them. MAIA **may not**: become the
relationship · adjudicate who is right · diagnose the other person · claim knowledge of
another's interior state · manufacture relational certainty · require movement.

**IMPLICATION.** On the hardest question — *should I contact them?* — MAIA visibly declines
the verdict and returns the member's own prior words, because a recommendation to contact
or not contact another human is a consequential prediction about that person. A visible
refusal is a feature of the room; a silent absence is not.

## IX — THIRD-PARTY SOVEREIGNTY

**PRINCIPLE.** Only the boundary Relationship Rooms require is settled here. **Private
human memory** — *"I felt abandoned when he left"* — is the member's own relational life,
and a person has always been entitled to remember it. **System characterization of a third
party** — an attachment style, a disposition, a motive, a diagnosis — is a claim about
another human being. They are not points on one scale.

**BOUNDARY.** The member's remembered experience is admissible. Machine claims about a
third party's psychology are not, at any confidence value. *"Do we have consent?"* is the
wrong question: a person reflecting privately on their marriage cannot be required to make
their spouse create an account.

**IMPLICATION.** The genuinely open question — under what conditions AIN may detect,
organize and retain a member's own relational memories when those memories necessarily
concern another human being — belongs to its own governed unit, and is narrowed by the
record correction above: what is retained is the member's own speech plus machine
detection, not a characterization of anyone.

## X — SYSTEM CONTAINERS

**PRINCIPLE.** Infrastructure is not relationship. A container the system created to hold
what it could not resolve is an artifact of the system's own uncertainty, and must be
legible as such.

**BOUNDARY.** No internal container may masquerade as a human relationship. A system-named
catch-all currently sits in the outer realm and renders among the member's people for 29 of
44 rows in production; that is a category error, not a display bug. System organization and
lived relational ontology must stay distinguishable at every layer.

**IMPLICATION.** The member's list of people contains people. What the system has not
resolved is shown as what it is — the system's own unfinished work — or not shown at all.
No schema remedy is prescribed here.

## XI — TIME

**PRINCIPLE.** A relationship must become **more** representable as history accumulates,
not less. History is part of relationship identity, not a log attached to it.

**BOUNDARY.** No silent truncation of relational significance. A room must accommodate
days, years, decades, death, long absence and return. Time must be legible — a date without
a year is not a history — and a record that quietly stops is worse than one that says where
it stops.

**IMPLICATION.** Depth is the design target, not recency. Significance within time stays
member-declared; the system may show *when* and *how often the member wrote*, never *which
period mattered*.

## XII — THE SOUL TEST

**PRINCIPLE.** Every article above is tested against eight people: a spouse of 25 years · a
closest friend · a teenage child · an estranged parent · a former partner · a difficult
colleague · someone newly loved · someone who has died. The questions: does it preserve
dignity · tolerate ambiguity · avoid coercing movement · preserve member authority · avoid
claiming the other's interiority · can the relationship change, end, remain, or simply be
held?

**BOUNDARY.** A principle that is beautiful for six of the eight and harmful to two has not
passed. Harm at the edges is the governing case, not the exception.

**IMPLICATION.** Two revisions were made under this test and are recorded rather than
hidden. **Article V** originally read as a binary partition; the teenage child and the
difficult colleague are simultaneously worked and held, so V now states that the two are
not a partition and that a relationship may be both or neither. **Article II** originally
vested authority in the member's act of naming; the estranged parent and the newly bereaved
cannot be asked to name, so II now states that authorship includes the authority to decline
to name.

# THE RELATIONSHIP ROOM PROMISE

When you enter a room for someone in your life, you will find your own words first, kept as
you wrote them. Nothing here will tell you who that person is — we have never met them, and
we will not pretend otherwise. You will never be asked to make progress on a relationship,
to explain a silence, or to name something you are not ready to name. If this relationship
is one you are working, the room will help you work it. If it is one you are simply holding
— because it is over, because they are gone, because it is enough as it is — the room will
hold it with you and ask nothing.
