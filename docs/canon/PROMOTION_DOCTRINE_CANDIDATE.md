# Promotion Doctrine — Constitutional Evaluation

**Status:** DRAFT canon candidate. Not yet promoted. Promotion into constitutional doctrine
is an authored crossing reserved to Kelly.

**Authored by:** Kelly Nezat (2026-06-19/20). Drafted to file by Claude; wording preserved.

This doctrine states a constitutional principle and the discipline that follows from it. It
does **not** define the evaluation procedure. The tests by which candidates are judged
(Test A/B/C, the classification axis, the maturation pipeline) have one authoritative home —
the **Constitutional Maturation Method** — which this doctrine *invokes*, never restates. A
doctrine must not carry the bespoke version of the test that admitted it.

---

## 1. Constitutional Principle

> **Constitutional evaluation is consequence-governed, not origin-governed.**

A principle's standing is determined by what it changes in the architecture — what it closes,
constrains, or makes newly possible — not by who proposed it, where it originated, or how
compelling it feels. Origins are not inert everywhere; the line is precise:

> **Ideas are origin-neutral. Evidence is origin-weighted.**

Provenance and reliability rightly count when judging what is *true* (evidence); a claim's source
neither grants nor docks its *constitutional standing* (ideas). Everything below derives from this.

---

## 2. Structural Constraint

> **Retain provenance while excluding it from constitutional evaluation.**

The genuinely additive move — the *retain **and** exclude* pairing. It holds two obligations
in productive tension:

- **Retain** — preserve authorship, chronology, evidence, traceability. Erase nothing.
- **Exclude** — refuse to let any of them determine constitutional standing. Privilege nothing.

The *exclude* half alone is entailed by §1; the *retain* half is the separate, load-bearing
constraint. Document everything; allow only consequence to confer membership.

---

## 3. Operational Guardrails

Not additional principles — the predictable ways evaluators accidentally violate §1, named so
they can be checked.

**Affective checks** — none carries constitutional weight:
liking it · disliking it · ownership (it came from us) · elegance · familiarity ·
worldview fit · profundity · novelty · authority · tradition.

**Register discipline** — the prohibition is against *substituting social evaluation for
architectural evaluation.* "That's exactly right," "Excellent insight," "A better outcome" are
evaluation errors: none names an architectural consequence. Replace with —
*What constraint changes? What failure mode disappears? What new capability becomes possible?*
That moves the discussion from agreement to structure.

---

## 4. The generalized norm

Stated at full generality — beyond AI, founders, or promotion:

> **Preserve every fact needed for accountability. Allow only consequences to determine
> constitutional membership.**

This simultaneously protects traceability **and** prevents authority, charisma, novelty,
familiarity, or origin from becoming hidden constitutional privileges. The doctrine is less a
rule about promotion than a constitutional norm from which promotion procedures follow.

---

## 5. The promotion norm — procedure by reference

> **Ratification requires a necessary and generative architectural constraint.**

A principle is ratified only when it changes what the architecture can faithfully *do* or must
*refuse* to do — not by elegance, depth, moral appeal, or rhetoric. The reducing question:

> **What does this principle force the architecture to become incapable of doing —
> or newly capable of doing?**

No structural change → it remains **orientation**. The architecture itself must change →
ratification is earned. This doctrine states the standard; the **procedure** that tests for it —
classification, the kind-specific instruments, the adversarial pipeline — lives in the
Constitutional Maturation Method. The doctrine invokes that procedure; it does not restate it.
