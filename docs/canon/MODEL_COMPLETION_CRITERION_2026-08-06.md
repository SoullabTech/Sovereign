## The criterion

> ⭐⭐⭐ **A model is complete enough when every remaining blocker is owned by governance rather than
> modeling.**

⭐ It is an **objective stopping condition** — it does not depend on the model feeling finished, on
every question being answered, or on anyone's judgment that enough has been done.

## How to apply it

Enumerate every remaining blocker and assign each an owner. There are only three:

| Owner | The blocker is… | Resolved by |
|---|---|---|
| **Measurement** | a question about what exists | going and looking — ⚠️ at the right scope |
| **Modeling** | a question about how concepts relate | more design |
| **Governance** | ⭐ a decision about authority, policy, or institutional intent | **a ruling** |

**The test that assigns the owner:**

> ⭐ **Would any amount of additional modeling change the answer?** If no, the blocker is
> governance-owned, and modeling it further is ⛔ displacement activity.

When **no blocker remains in the *modeling* column**, the model is complete enough. ⛔ Not perfect —
**complete enough**, which is the only completeness a model can have.

## The failure mode it prevents

⭐ Design work is productive and pleasant; rulings are neither. So the common failure is to keep
elaborating an **already coherent** model in the hope of resolving questions that are, in fact,
decisions about authority, policy, or intent.

⛔ That never works — and the reason is structural, not motivational: **more modeling cannot produce
a decision, because a decision is not a missing piece of reasoning.** Additional diagrams, schemas,
and taxonomies accumulate, the model becomes ornate rather than more true, and the blockers are
exactly where they were.

⚠️ **The tell:** new artifacts stop changing what anyone would *do*. ⭐ At that point the next
correct action is not another diagram or schema. It is a ruling.

## Two cautions

1. ⛔ **Do not use it to stop early.** A blocker is not governance-owned because it is hard, or
   because someone senior would have to decide. It is governance-owned only when the *would-more-
   modeling-change-the-answer* test says no.
2. ⚠️ **Re-run it after every ruling.** A ruling can return a blocker to the modeling column — it
   settles authority and may open a design question that could not previously be asked.

## Worked example — the lane that produced it

At close, the publishing lane held: **4 governance rulings** (two lane-scoped, two platform-wide
instruments) · **2 ontological questions** · **5 measured implementation prerequisites**.

⭐ **Zero blockers in the modeling column.** The ontological questions carry a **test** that decides
them (`ACT_GRAMMAR_FACT_CLASS_TEST`), so they await application, ⛔ not more design. The
prerequisites are measured facts, ⛔ not open questions. Hence: stop modeling, begin ruling.
