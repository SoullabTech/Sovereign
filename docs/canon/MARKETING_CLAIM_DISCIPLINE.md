## The question this canon protects

> **What must be true for this statement to remain honest?**

That question is bigger than marketing. It governs every place MAIA is represented: website copy, case studies, podcasts, investor decks, roadmap discussions, demos, onboarding, and internal planning. This is **representation discipline**, not pitch polish.

---

## Why this is canon

Canon documents exist because a recurring drift was observed. This one protects against three distinct, already-present failure modes — and earns its place because all three converge on the same verdict, which is what makes it hard to fool:

| Drift | What inflates | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Capability inflation** | claiming a capability we don't have live | Live / Designed / Vision |
| **Emotional inflation** | resting the payload on a capability we don't have live | Center of Gravity |
| **Narrative inflation** | telling a story that collapses without unbuilt layers | Failure Test |

---

## The three instruments

### 1. Live / Designed / Vision — *the capability layer*
- **Live** — verified in production; a member experiences it today. (Internal anchor: Cat 6; answerable to `built ≠ wired ≠ surfacing ≠ verified`.)
- **Designed** — specified and partially built; not verified-surfacing (not deployed, not wired end-to-end, or unverified that it reaches the member).
- **Vision** — depends on infrastructure — and often a consent/ethics architecture — that does not yet exist.

A "Live" claim must be answerable to a runtime fact. The marketing word is the public face of the engineering ladder; **it may not outrun it.**

### 2. Center of Gravity — *the honesty locator*
Not *"which capabilities does this touch?"* but **"what must be true for the emotional payload to hold?"** A story may *touch* Vision capability and still *be* a Live story — if the payload does not depend on it. Locate where the load sits; that layer sets the verdict.

### 3. Failure Test — *the subtractive check*
**Strip the Designed and Vision layers. Does the story survive?** If no, it is not publishable as Live. This reaches the same verdict as Center of Gravity from the opposite direction. Run both: agreement is confidence; disagreement means the Center of Gravity was mis-located — trust the Failure Test and find the hidden dependency.

## Relationship to the Oath and the Sovereignty Invariants

This canon is the vows applied to *speech*. Its deepest finding is a convergence: **who carries the thread sets the layer.** "MAIA reflects; *you* carry it" is both Live and sovereignty-aligned. "MAIA remembers *for* you" is both Designed and centralizing. The most honest framing and the most sovereign framing are the same framing — so representation discipline and the Sovereignty Invariants protect each other.

---

## True-North Preservation

A **Vision** story must not be demoted into a publishable **Designed** story in order to ship it. The strongest narratives are sometimes the least shippable — and that is correct. They *orient* the organization; they do not *sell* this quarter. Strip-mining a north star for near-term copy is itself a drift.

---

## How to use

Every outward statement: (1) declares its **Layer**, (2) names its **Center of Gravity**, (3) passes the **Failure Test**. **Drift check:** if a claim's center of gravity creeps toward Designed/Vision across revisions, or a Designed beat quietly loses its forward voice — re-tag. Do not smooth.

---

## Validation

The standard is sound if **two people can independently classify the same statement and roughly agree.** Disagreement is data: it locates an ambiguous claim, not a broken instrument.

---

## Maturity

> The signal that the framework is mature is that it stopped producing new distinctions and started producing decisions. That is when it crossed from theory into practice.

**Resist adding new instruments until the standard fails in use.**
