# Federated Relational Architecture

**Status:** Canon — binding structural law
**Canonized:** 2026-05-19
**Key term:** *distinct gravitational centers*

---

## What this document is

A structural law of the Soullab/MAIA ecosystem. It protects the architecture from a specific predictable failure mode: **centralization drift** — the gradual collapse of a plural, practitioner-amplifying ecosystem into a founder-voiced, platform-centric monolith.

This is not preference. It is not branding. It is the structural discriminator that determines whether this system remains a federated relational architecture or becomes another centralized creator platform.

---

## The structural law

> Cultural production and practitioner enablement are distinct gravitational centers. Collapsing them into a single system produces centralization drift, founder-voice gravity, and eventual reduction of practitioners into downstream amplifiers rather than sovereign participants.

This is the operative principle. Every other rule in this document derives from it.

---

## Why this is doctrine-grade

Most rules describe how to do something well. This one describes what protects the architecture from a specific, highly likely failure that would otherwise occur regardless of intention.

Diagnostic threshold marker: **its absence permits a future structural failure.** That is the signal for canon-grade material, distinct from positioning or preference.

---

## Why the collapse happens

The collapse is not driven by bad intention. It is gravitational.

- **Cultural production** (Media Studio, documentary, book, salon, public discourse) optimizes for resonance, authorship, narrative continuity, voice coherence.
- **Practitioner enablement** (Practitioner Portal, Session Room, Care Orchestration, Studio operational layer) optimizes for sovereignty, local context, distributed wisdom, plural authorship.

These two functions pull toward different centers. When recombined "for coherence" — usually under simplification pressure — the cultural production center dominates, because it has stronger gravitational pull (clearer voice, public audience, narrative arc, founder visibility).

What looks like consolidation produces collapse. Coherence is precisely what the collapse destroys.

---

## What this protects

- **Plurality** — many practitioners, many lineages, many ways of holding.
- **Distributed wisdom** — knowledge that lives in the field, not centralized in media.
- **Practitioner sovereignty** — practitioners as sovereign participants, not downstream amplifiers.
- **Ecosystem aliveness** — emergence rather than broadcast.

---

## Operational consequences

This law organizes:

1. **Product boundaries** — Media Studio and Practitioner Portal are distinct surfaces. They may share infrastructure but never share gravitational center.
2. **UI architecture** — Practitioner-facing UI carries practitioner-shaped vocabulary (Co-lab, Session Room, Care). Media-facing UI carries cultural-production vocabulary. They are not unified.
3. **Platform posture** — Soullab/MAIA does not posture as a single voice. It hosts plural voices.
4. **Media governance** — Cultural production decisions (documentary, series, public publishing) do not propagate into practitioner workflows.
5. **Practitioner relationships** — Practitioners are sovereign participants; their work is not automatically aggregated into the platform's media surface.
6. **Branding decisions** — Founder-voice (Kelly) lives in cultural production. Practitioner-facing surfaces remain neutral, plural, field-shaped.
7. **Authorship expectations** — MAIA does not author practitioner work. Practitioners do not author central narrative. Each lineage holds its own.
8. **Expansion sequencing** — New surfaces are classified as cultural-production or practitioner-enablement *before* implementation. Hybrid surfaces require explicit gravitational-center decision; hybrid is not a third center.

---

## Anti-patterns (collapse signatures)

These are the textures of drift. Watch for them:

- A practitioner UI feature that begins quoting central narrative.
- A media surface that begins gating, scoring, or directing practitioner behavior.
- Vocabulary leak — SaaS/productivity nouns appearing in practitioner UI ("Teams," "Workspaces," "Projects"), or cultural-production vocabulary appearing in practitioner tooling.
- Founder-voice creeping into practitioner-facing copy.
- A unified dashboard "for everything" — coherence-flavored consolidation is the most common collapse vector.
- The simplification argument: *"Why don't we just combine these — it would be simpler."* Simplification pressure is the gravitational force this doctrine resists. Same operational reality, opposite ecosystem effect.

---

## How to apply

When designing or evaluating any new surface:

1. **Name its gravitational center.** Cultural production, or practitioner enablement? If you cannot answer, you do not ship yet.
2. **If hybrid, decide which center it primarily belongs to.** Hybrid is not a third center.
3. **Inspect vocabulary** — does the surface speak the language of its center?
4. **Inspect authorship** — who is positioned as the author? If the answer is "the platform" for a practitioner surface, the collapse has already begun.
5. **Inspect direction of gravity** — does this surface pull plural work toward a center, or hold it distributed?
