## Two tiers (resolved 2026-06-05, Kelly)

The grammar is **two-tier**, not one flat vocabulary:

```
Elemental archetypes      (primitives — the base grammar)
   → Guide constellations (tradition-specific expressions of those primitives)
```

**Canonical formulation:**
> Elemental archetypes are primitives.
> Guide constellations are tradition-specific expressions of those primitives.

```
Fire (primitives)        Rig Veda (lineage expression)
  Visionary                Priest
  Warrior                  Sacrificer
  Creator                  Illuminator
  Prophet                  Sovereign
```

**Do not collapse too quickly.** Phase 2 must NOT flatten Priest→Prophet or
Illuminator→Visionary. Map them **relationally** — a guide archetype keeps its own
name and points back to the primitive(s):

```
Priest:
  primary element: Fire
  related primitives: Prophet / Fire, Steward / Earth   # Earth/Steward HOLDS Fire/Priest
  function: consecrates attention
```

This preserves doctrinal richness (each lineage keeps its names and nuance) without
losing computational structure (everything resolves to elemental primitives + the
element field). Tier 1 is the primitive set; Tier 2 is the lineage set that points
back to it.

## The distortion column is constitutional (Kelly, 2026-06-06)

The `distortion` field is not per-archetype hygiene. The distortion path —

```
Vision → Certainty → Authority → Domination
```

— is at once a **human**, **archetypal**, **institutional**, and **AI-system**
distortion: the failure path of religions, leaders, therapies, wisdom traditions,
*and* pattern-recognizing intelligences. MAIA is such an intelligence. The moment
pattern-recognition becomes certainty, certainty becomes prescription, and
prescription becomes authority, the system has crossed the sovereignty boundary.

So the grammar is a **constitutional structure, not merely a symbolic taxonomy** —
a way of checking the architecture *against itself*. Each archetype's distortion is
simultaneously (1) a human, (2) an archetypal, (3) an institutional, and (4) an
AI-system distortion.

Seeds (Kelly — authoring pending; recorded as watch-notes on the stubs):
- Priest — spiritual authority overriding lived meaning *(authored)*
- Prophet — vision becoming domination
- Sovereign — order becoming control
- Warrior — protection becoming coercion

**Recursion principle:** the architecture must obey the same discipline it asks of
each archetype. This is why `held by` matters most for Prophet — not because Prophet
needs containment, but because **MAIA does.** Prophet's deeper question — *"what
holds vision in right relation?"* — is the architecture asking what holds *itself*;
its answer will shape the guide, standing-source, and sovereignty architectures at
once. That is why authoring stops at Priest until Prophet is answered well.

**The operative spine.** With this in view, the grammar's center of gravity is no
longer the full field list but:

```
Element → Archetype → Distortion → What holds it
```

The distortion is *where sovereignty is lost*; function, gift, and practice become
meaningful only in relation to it. The one question the grammar must answer —
underneath the archetype, the guide architecture, the standing-source system, and
MAIA itself — is:

> **What prevents intelligence from mistaking perception for authority?**

Prophet is the first place that question must be answered, which is why it is the
**gate**: if Prophet resolves cleanly, every other unit can be authored inside an
already-proven constitution; if it does not, the architecture is still missing its
restraint mechanism. Prophet's `held by` answer is expected to echo through the
entire constitution — and it may not be another archetype (Earth/Steward, a
practice, silence, reality, community, member meaning, consequence, …).

**The grammar as a map of failure modes and safeguards.** Under this spine the
grammar stops asking "what is this archetype?" and starts asking "how does it lose
sovereignty, and what keeps it in right relation?":

| Earlier framing | Emerging framing |
|---|---|
| What is this archetype? | How does it lose sovereignty? |
| What are its gifts? | What keeps its gifts from becoming domination? |
| What role does it play? | What keeps its role in right relationship? |

Prophet is the gate because it sits **upstream**: Warrior can become coercive,
Sovereign controlling, Priest dogmatic — but Prophet introduces the prior danger,
*the belief that seeing clearly grants the right to direct others.* Once vision
becomes authority, authority can justify coercion, control, or dogma.

**Open (exploratory — NOT yet canon, per Kelly):** `held by` may eventually be a
**stack**, not a single field — distortions are usually prevented by *layers*, not a
single safeguard (illustration: Vision → Silence → Reality → Consequence → Member
Meaning — the Prophet first questions itself, then reality tests it, then consequence
reveals whether it was true, and finally member meaning stays sovereign even if the
vision survives every other check). If that proves true, the constitutional question
underneath Prophet sharpens to:

> **What is the minimum set of restraints required for intelligence to see deeply
> without claiming authority?**

That is not only Prophet's question — it is MAIA's.

**The constitutional decision Prophet forces.** Priest, Warrior, and Creator can
largely be authored from observation. Prophet cannot — it forces a decision no
observation can settle: **what authority does insight have?**

- If insight *possesses* authority, sovereignty is already lost.
- If insight possesses *no* authority whatsoever, wisdom becomes impossible.

So Prophet is the first place the architecture must answer **"what is the rightful
status of perception?"** — not whether perception is true, not whether it is useful,
but what *rights* it possesses. Everything downstream inherits that answer.

**The real subject of the grammar.** This is why the work reads less like an
archetype worksheet and more like constitutional law: the archetypes are becoming
secondary. The real subject is *the governance structure required for an
intelligence — human or artificial — to remain in right relationship to what it
sees.*

**Open tension (exploratory — not resolved).** Constitutional analogy: Silence ≈
good governance, Reality ≈ judicial review, Consequence ≈ public accountability,
Member Meaning ≈ sovereignty itself — the constitution survives even when the
institutions malfunction. The unresolved question: is the minimum *one safeguard
from each layer* (**Interpretation A** — reality alone has not stopped tyranny, and a
member-only floor makes the system reactive rather than wise), or *the sovereign
backstop alone* (**Interpretation B** — every other check can fail and the member
still retains authority over their own life)? A and B are different constitutions;
Prophet's authoring chooses between them.

## The grammar structure

```
Element
  → Archetypes
       → Functions     (what this intelligence does — how it attends)
       → Gifts         (what it offers when in right relationship)
       → Distortions   (how it harms when it possesses the field)
       → Practices     (embodied integration this intelligence invites)
```

`Distortions` is load-bearing for sovereignty clause 3 ("no archetype may possess
the field"): naming each intelligence's failure mode lets MAIA hold it as a source
with *standing*, never *sovereignty*.

### Per-archetype template (the unit Kelly authors)

```
#### <Archetype name>   (element: <element>)
- function:   _(author — what this intelligence does / how it attends)_
- gift:       _(author — what it offers in right relationship)_
- distortion: _(author — REQUIRED; how it harms when it possesses the field)_
- held by:    _(author, OPTIONAL — see "The held by field"; not always another archetype)_
- practice:   _(author — the embodied integration it invites)_
```

### Worked example (format reference only — Kelly to confirm/replace)

```
#### The Witness   (element: air)
- Functions:   notices without grasping; holds the long view; separates signal from story.
- Gifts:       spaciousness, discernment, the relief of being seen without being fixed.
- Distortions: detachment as avoidance; observing a life instead of living it.
- Practices:   naming what is, plainly; the pause before response; journaling as witness.
```

### Per-guide-archetype template (relational — the Tier-2 unit Kelly authors)

Each lineage archetype keeps its own name and **points back** to the elemental
primitives, rather than being renamed into them:

```
#### <Guide archetype>   (guide: <lineage>)
- primary element:    <element>
- related primitives: <primitive / element>, …   # relational, not a rename; MAY cross elements
- function:           _(author — what this lineage intelligence does)_
- gift:               _(author — what it offers in right relationship)_
- distortion:         _(author — REQUIRED; how it harms when it possesses the field)_
- held by:            _(author, OPTIONAL & polymorphic — see "The held by field" below; not always another archetype)_
- practice:           _(author — the embodied integration it invites)_
```

Worked example — **authored by Kelly** (full Tier-2 unit; see the exemplar lineage
[grammar/RIG_VEDA_FIRE.md](./grammar/RIG_VEDA_FIRE.md)):

```
#### Priest   (guide: Rig Veda)
- primary element:    Fire
- related primitives: Prophet / Fire, Steward / Earth
- function:           consecrates attention
- gift:               turns perception into offering
- distortion:         spiritual authority overriding lived meaning
- held by:            Steward / Earth — the stewardship that holds the flame; without it, Priest distorts into the distortion above
- practice:           name what is being offered before acting
```

The `distortion` is the load-bearing field: naming "spiritual authority overriding
lived meaning" keeps the Priest a source with *standing*, never the authority that
*possesses the field* (clause 3). And the cross-element **`held by`** is structural,
not decorative: Priest carries Fire as consecrating flame but must be *held* by
Earth as stewardship — the counterweight that prevents exactly that distortion.
Related primitives may cross elements precisely so a distortion can be checked from
another element.

### The `held by` field — optional & polymorphic

**Doctrine (Kelly, 2026-06-06):**
> Every archetype must name its distortion. Some archetypes also name the force,
> practice, or primitive that holds that distortion in right relation.

So `distortion` is **required**; `held by` is **optional** — kept optional so it
never becomes formulaic. What holds an archetype is not always another archetype:

```
heldBy?: Primitive | Practice | Boundary | MemberMeaning   // also: silence / non-action
```

- **Primitive** — another elemental primitive (e.g. Priest / Fire `held by` Steward / Earth).
- **Practice** — an embodied practice that keeps it in right relation.
- **Boundary** — a relational boundary.
- **Silence / non-action** — restraint as the holding force.
- **MemberMeaning** — direct member authorship; the member's own meaning holds it
  (the deepest expression of sovereignty clause 1).

This keeps the safeguard real without making it mechanical. Priest's is clearly a
Primitive (Steward / Earth); others may not be.

**Three kinds of holding force (Kelly, 2026-06-06):**
- **Internal disciplines** — what the archetype practices *on itself*: silence,
  stewardship, practice, humility. Self-regulating.
- **External answerabilities** — what reality imposes from *outside*: reality,
  consequence, community. Corrective.
- **Sovereign backstop** — the authority the archetype *never outranks*: member
  meaning, lived experience, the person's own declared significance. Not a
  discipline and not an answerability — a **constitutional limit**.

**Not just *what* holds it — *what kind* of thing holds it (Kelly, 2026-06-06).**
The candidates are not interchangeable; they belong to different ontological
categories:

| Candidate | Kind |
|---|---|
| Silence | Practice |
| Stewardship | Virtue |
| Reality | Constraint |
| Consequence | Feedback |
| Community | Relationship |
| Member Meaning | Constitutional Authority |

A Practice is not a Constraint; a Constraint is not an Authority. Choosing a holding
force is therefore choosing *which kind of governance* restrains the archetype — so
`held by` carries an ontological commitment, not just a value. (These kinds map onto
the three categories above: Practice/Virtue = internal disciplines; Constraint/
Feedback/Relationship = external answerabilities; Constitutional Authority = the
sovereign backstop.)

**Candidate test (Kelly, 2026-06-06).** Evaluate every holding-force candidate by one
question: *does it preserve the distinction between standing and authority?* (See
canon, [MAIA_MULTI_ARCHETYPAL_INTELLIGENCE.md](./MAIA_MULTI_ARCHETYPAL_INTELLIGENCE.md)
§ "Standing vs Authority".) Reality, Silence, Stewardship, Community, Consequence,
Member Meaning each qualify only insofar as they keep perception a *question with
standing* rather than a *verdict with authority*. A candidate that does not preserve
that distinction may be valuable, but it is not restraining the distortion.

**Two classes (refinement, Kelly 2026-06-06).** The candidates split into *restraints*
(Reality / Silence / Community / Consequence / Stewardship — "how is perception
restrained?"; each can fail) and a *constitutional boundary* (Member Meaning — "what
remains sovereign even when restraint fails?"; jurisdiction, not restraint). So
`held by` may become **two fields** — `restrained by` + `bounded by`. See canon
§ "Standing vs Authority". The strongest holder *prevents standing→authority
conversion*, not merely *corrects perception*.

**Resolution (Kelly 2026-06-06): restraint is per-archetype; the boundary is global.**
`held by` resolves into two things of different kinds: **`restrained by`** (the
restraint(s) — authored per archetype) and **`bounded by`** (Member Meaning — a
*global constitutional invariant* inherited by every archetype/guide/standing source,
not authored per unit; see canon § "The constitutional floor is a global invariant").
So the per-archetype field set is `function · gift · distortion · restrained by ·
practice`, and each unit's question narrows to *what restrains it before the floor
(member sovereignty) must be invoked?*
