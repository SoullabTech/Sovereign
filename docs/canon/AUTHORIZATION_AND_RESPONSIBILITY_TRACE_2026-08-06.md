# The Authorization-and-Responsibility Trace — candidate method instrument

**Status: CANDIDATE.** ⛔ Not ratified. A **method rule** governing design artifacts, ⛔ not a
constitutional principle and ⛔ not an authority-granting instrument. Recorded at founder observation
2026-08-06 as a general discipline rather than a publishing-lane note, because the conflation it
prevents is not specific to this project.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **PROVENANCE OF AUTHORITY — cite it this way, ⛔ never as a general principle.**
> *"This artifact discipline emerged from two opposite failures observed in the practitioner
> publishing lane (2026-08-06): design that manufactured authority no instrument had granted, and
> conceptual work that answered no implementation responsibility."*
>
> ⛔ It was **not** derived from theory. Its authority is **earned by the two failures in §3 and
> extends no further than they do.** ⛔ Do not strip the failures out to leave only the rule — without
> them it reads as a tidy abstraction and a future reader will treat it as optional.

---

## 1. The rule

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Every design artifact must declare, for each decision it makes, both an authorizing ruling
> and an implementation responsibility — each exactly one.**

| Column | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Authorizing ruling** | ⭐ *Why is this permitted?* |
| **Implementation responsibility** | ⭐ *Who is responsible for making it real?* |

⭐ These are **independent questions**. An artifact can be fully authorized and answer nothing; it can
answer a real need and be entirely unauthorized. ⛔ They are routinely conflated because both feel
like "justification" — and the conflation hides whichever one is missing.

*Exactly one*, in both columns, is load-bearing. A decision tracing to two rulings has not been
decomposed far enough; a decision tracing to two responsibilities will acquire two implementation
homes.

## 2. The two failure modes — ⛔ not interchangeable

| Symptom | Diagnosis | ⭐ Required response |
|---|---|---|
| cannot point **upward** to an authorizing ruling | it is **inventing governance** | ⛔ stop — refer it to whoever holds the ruling; ⛔ never resolve it by choosing a schema |
| cannot point **downward** to an implementation responsibility | it is **modeling for its own sake** | ⛔ cut it — the model is not the deliverable |

⛔ **Applying the wrong response is the deeper error.** Answering a governance absence with modeling
*looks* like progress and produces authority no one granted.

## 3. Worked examples (practitioner-publishing lane, 2026-08-06)

**Upward failure.** Four blocked rows — custodial mandate, delegation grant, attestation-content
erasure, organization rights-holder — were **missing instruments**, a constitutional absence.
Inventing an "organization" entity or a "delegation" table would have satisfied the modeling instinct
and manufactured authority no instrument granted. ⭐ Recorded as: *a missing instrument is not a
schema gap.*
→ [Implementation Map §"Three kinds of gap"](../design/practitioner-portal/PRACTITIONER_PUBLISHING_IMPLEMENTATION_MAP_2026-08-06.md)

**Downward failure.** The lane's own next-work direction had to open with ⛔ *"Not another conceptual
document."* Every remaining blocked row asked *who has authority to do this, and how is it enforced?*
— ⛔ no further ontology was indicated, so additional conceptual modeling would have answered no
implementation responsibility.
→ [Phase Record §4–§6](../design/practitioner-portal/PUBLISHING_PHASE_RECORD_2026-08-06.md)

## 4. Enforcement

⭐ **Documentary, not automated.** The artifact carries the table; a row with a blank cell does not
ship. ⛔ The rule is not satisfied by a prose paragraph gesturing at both — the columns must be
separately fillable, or the conflation survives inside the prose.

⛔ **A filled cell is a claim, not a proof.** The trace makes the authorization *legible*; it does
not confer it. A cited ruling that does not exist yet is an upward failure wearing a citation.

⭐ Sequentially: those three ask whether the thing should exist. This one asks, of a design already
judged worth making, ⛔ *by what authority* and ⛔ *toward what responsibility.*
