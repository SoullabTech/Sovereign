# MAIA Demo Script — Mark

## The 60-Second Moment

**Purpose:** Make the epistemic difference visceral, not intellectual
**Duration:** 60 seconds total
**Goal:** The room goes quiet

---

## Setup (10 seconds)

You say:

> "Let me show you the difference between memory as extraction and memory as collaboration."

No philosophy yet. Just framing.

---

## Demo A: Conventional AI (15 seconds)

You (or a baseline system) prompt:

> "Last time we talked, you said you were struggling with commitment."

The response:

> "Yes, previously you mentioned difficulty committing to long-term projects..."

**Pause.**

Let Mark feel it.

What he'll register (even if he doesn't say it):
- Clever
- Efficient
- Slightly invasive
- Flattening

---

## Demo B: MAIA (25 seconds)

Now MAIA.

She says:

> "You marked a decision last week as important."

Pause.
Do **not** rush.

Then:

> "As you think about what you're facing now... what do you notice about how that decision is showing up?"

**Silence.**

This is the moment.

---

## The Explanation (10 seconds)

Only *after* he feels it, you say:

> "MAIA didn't decide that mattered. The member did."

Optional follow-up:

> "She remembers what you told her mattered — not everything you ever said."

That's it.

**Stop talking.**

---

## Demo C: Pattern Recognition (Optional — If He's Tracking)

If Mark is engaged and you have another 30 seconds, show the other side of the coin.

You say:

> "But MAIA isn't passive. She sees across the whole field."

Then MAIA:

> "I'm noticing something — the word 'permission' keeps showing up when you talk about creative work. Different contexts, several weeks. I don't know if it means anything to you, but I wanted to name it. Does that land?"

**Pause.**

Then explain:

> "She noticed a pattern the member couldn't see. But she offered it — she didn't claim it was significant. The member decides if it matters."

This is the full picture:
- MAIA can see what you can't see
- But you still decide what it means

**That's the architecture of trust with intelligence.**

---

## Why This Lands

Because Mark will immediately see:
- Reduced liability
- Reduced hallucinated intimacy
- Increased trust surface
- A new category of human-AI relationship

This is not personalization.

This is **authorship-preserving intelligence**.

---

## Technical Notes

### What MAIA is actually doing

1. **Curated Recall Register** — Only surfaces member-marked significance
2. **Pattern Recognition Register** — Offers observations the member couldn't see
3. **Epistemic Source Tagging** — Acknowledges authorship explicitly
4. **Consent Gate** — Invites exploration rather than asserting interpretation
5. **Present-Moment Sensing** — Asks what's alive now, not what was recorded

### The contrast

| Conventional AI | MAIA |
|----------------|------|
| "I remember you struggled with..." | "You marked this as important." |
| "I've noticed you have a pattern of..." | "I'm noticing something — does this land?" |
| Implies discovery | Acknowledges authorship |
| Claims interpretive authority | Offers observations |
| Flattens to data | Preserves meaning sovereignty |

---

## If Mark Asks Follow-Up Questions

### "How does this reduce liability?"

> "MAIA doesn't claim to know what matters to you. For member-marked content, she returns what you authored. For patterns she notices, she offers them tentatively — never asserting they're significant. The member always has the final word on meaning."

### "But she does notice patterns — isn't that the same thing?"

> "No. There's a difference between 'I've noticed you have a commitment problem' and 'I'm noticing the word permission keeps showing up — does that land?' One claims diagnostic authority. The other offers an observation. The member decides if it's meaningful."

### "What's the business model?"

> "Sovereignty-as-a-service. People pay for a system that genuinely serves them rather than extracting from them. The trust surface *is* the value proposition."

### "How is this different from privacy settings?"

> "Privacy settings control who sees your data. This controls who *authors your meaning*. The member decides what's significant. MAIA is the custodian, not the interpreter."

### "Can this scale?"

> "The architecture is simpler, not more complex. Less inference, less risk, less compute for personalization. More trust, more retention, more willingness to share what actually matters."
