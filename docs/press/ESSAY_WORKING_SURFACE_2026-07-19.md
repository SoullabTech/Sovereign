## Opening — TWO CANDIDATES, you pick at red-line

### Candidate 1 — [KELLY — verbatim, fourth-pass draft, "most alive version"]

> Most meaningful experiences do not fail while they are happening.
> Retreats are meaningful. Coaching sessions are meaningful. Books are meaningful.
> Conversations are meaningful.
> The problem comes afterward.
> People return home. Email returns. Children return. Meetings return. Laundry
> returns. Life returns.
> And slowly, often without anyone noticing, the thing that mattered begins to fade.
> Not because it was false. Not because it lacked depth.
> But because there was nowhere for it to live.
> This question has quietly guided much of our work:
> What happens after people return home?
> And perhaps the deeper question:
> What allows certain experiences, relationships, questions, and recognitions to
> become part of a life rather than simply beautiful memories?

### Candidate 2 — [COUNCIL — draft for red-line, 07-19, ask-don't-claim register]

> I have found myself sitting with a very simple question.
> The College creates extraordinary encounters.
> But what happens after people return home?
> What remains six months later? One year later?
> Which conversations continue living? Which relationships deepen?
> Which moments become part of someone's life?
> I don't know the answer.
> I only know that I have become increasingly curious about whether extraordinary
> experiences might have extraordinary afterlives.
> And if they do, how might we become better stewards of them?

**Fresh coinage worth keeping either way**: **"extraordinary afterlives"** — the

## Ending — [KELLY — verbatim, third pass; "earned rather than asserted"]

> We increasingly suspect that what carries transformation forward is not
> information, insight, or even extraordinary experiences themselves.
> What carries transformation forward are relationships.
> Relationships with ourselves. Relationships with others. Relationships with
> practices, questions, communities, and ways of serving.
> Human flourishing may be fundamentally relational.
> If so, then the task is not merely creating extraordinary moments.
> It is helping the relationships born within those moments endure across time.

**Closing image candidate [KELLY — verbatim, "the porch made prose"]** — pairs with
or follows the relational ending:

> We build places for continuity not because continuity can be engineered into
> existence, but because when someone returns, there should be a light on and
> somewhere for what mattered to still be waiting.

## Fragments added 2026-07-22 [session: sacred play / registers / inhabitation — provenance-labeled, draft-for-red-line]

**The inhabitation frame [DIALOGUE, Kelly-ratified in conversation]** — may be the
essay's deeper altitude beneath "what carries transformation forward":

> Optimization treats life as a problem to solve.
> Inhabitation treats life as a world to participate in.

Question form: *How do humans flourish when life is treated as a world to inhabit
rather than a system to optimize?* — an afterlife of an extraordinary experience is
what inhabitation looks like stretched across time.

**Registers principle [KELLY, verbatim seed]**: *"For some it is sanctuary, for some
it is an adventure, for some it's a lab, for others it is a leap into the unknown.
All will have their unique experiences."* — Refined in dialogue: *"The vows are
singular; the experiences are plural."* · *"Registers are discovered, not
assigned."* · *"A good house is recognizable; a meaningful house is never
experienced identically."* The house remains itself; the relationship changes with
the person's season.

**Kinship evidence from the College's own page [CLAUDE observation, 2026-07-22]**:
their testimonial wall is unreconciled plural registers ("coolest school" /
"leadership lab disguised as a castle" / "portal" / "rabbit hole") — years-later
memories used as the credibility surface. And Barnet Bain: "The College has this
sneaky way of expanding your thinking without announcing itself as
'transformational.'" The College already practices the restraint the essay
articulates — the complement-never-compete stance has a root deeper than strategy:
shared restraint. (Use lightly, if at all — recognition, not flattery.)

**Authored-restraint sentence [DIALOGUE, this week's convergence]** — candidate for

because it is with you — not because it knows what you are."* (may be too internal

**Measure line [DIALOGUE, 2026-07-22 — candidate for the essay's turn]**:

> Perhaps the deepest measure of an extraordinary experience is not what happened
> there, but what kind of life became possible afterward.

Companion sharpening of the anchor distinction: the College's question is
event-centered (how do extraordinary experiences change people?); ours is
continuity-centered (how do people inhabit their ordinary lives more deeply after
meaningful encounters?). Cousins, not twins — the essay lives in the difference.
