# Preface Quotation / Attribution Audit — provisional

**Case:** KELLY-NEZAT-EA-KDP-REFINEMENT-01  
**Scope:** Preface only  
**Purpose:** separate source / attribution / permissions risk from literary editing.

This document records research findings, not legal advice. A quotation can be accurately sourced yet still require a permissions decision for commercial publication.

## Q1 — Zhuangzi butterfly epigraph

**Manuscript attribution:** Zhuangzi

**Finding:** The English wording in the Preface is traceable to Herbert A. Giles's English translation of *Chuang Tzŭ*, first published in the nineteenth century. The wording includes the closing sentence, "The transition is called Metempsychosis"; it is present in Giles's translation itself rather than being an unexplained sentence introduced by the manuscript.

**Current disposition:** attribution lineage substantially verified. Before final KDP proof, cite the Giles translation/edition rather than presenting the wording as an unspecified translation. Because the translation is from the nineteenth century, its U.S. copyright status appears materially different from the modern translations below; final production should still record the edition used.

Sources consulted:
- Wikisource, Herbert Giles 1889 translation, Chapter II: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Chuang_Tz%C5%AD_%28Giles%29/Chapter_2
- Wikisource facsimile page: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AZhuang_Zi_-_translation_Giles_1889.djvu/66

## Q2 — "If your heart is truly open..."

**Manuscript attribution:** Michael A. Singer

**Finding:** The exact line appears as an **Anonymous** epigraph in Wayne Teasdale's *The Mystic Heart: Discovering a Universal Spirituality in the World's Religions*. The source found does not support attribution to Michael A. Singer.

**Current disposition:** probable attribution correction required. Preserve the line only after deciding whether to attribute it as Anonymous / via Teasdale or replace it with a verified Singer passage.

## Q3 — Rainer Maria Rilke, "May what I do flow from me like a river..."

**Finding:** The wording is from *Rilke's Book of Hours: Love Poems to God*, Book I, poem 12, translated by Anita Barrows and Joanna Macy (Riverhead, 1996), p. 58 in a cited secondary source.

**Current disposition:** source verified; modern English translation is a permissions/copyright review item for commercial KDP use. Do not treat Rilke's original publication date as automatically resolving rights in the 1996 translation.

Sources consulted:

## Q4 — Wendell Berry, "It may be that when we no longer know what to do..."

**Finding:** Traceable to Wendell Berry, *Standing by Words* (1983), commonly titled/circulated as "The Real Work." A reputable reprint explicitly states copyright ©1983 and "Reprinted by permission of Counterpoint."

**Current disposition:** source verified; permissions/copyright review required before commercial publication unless an applicable licensing/fair-use determination is made.

## Q6 — "As you start to walk on the way, the way appears."

**Manuscript attribution:** Rumi

**Finding:** No authoritative Rumi source has yet been established. Modern attribution research flags the line as likely misattributed; one investigation traces the familiar wording through twentieth-century Heidegger/Rilke commentary rather than Rumi. Quote aggregators themselves often say a source is needed.

**Current disposition:** high attribution-risk. Do not retain "— Rumi" for final KDP merely because the line is widely circulated. Verify from a scholarly Rumi source or reattribute/remove.

Sources consulted:
- Attribution investigation: https://getsmalltalk.com/blog/as-you-start-to-walk-on-the-way-the-way-appears-real-rumi-or-not

## Q7 — Kalama Sutta passage beginning "Do not believe in anything simply because you have heard it..."

**Manuscript presentation:** "As the Buddha advises in the Kalama Sutta"

**Finding:** The circulating English passage is a later loose paraphrase, not a reliable verbatim rendering of AN 3.65. It materially changes the canonical logic by elevating "reason" in a way that standard translations do not.

**Current disposition:** conceptual teaching may remain relevant, but the Preface should not present this wording as an unqualified direct Buddha quotation without identifying the paraphrase/source. Prefer a reliable published translation or clear paraphrase.

Sources consulted:
- Detailed comparison with Thanissaro translation: https://fakebuddhaquotes.com/do-not-believe-in-anything-simply-because-you-have-heard-it/
- Modern sutta overview noting it is a loose paraphrase: https://dailydharmapractice.com/suttas/kalama-sutta

## Q8 — "If you knew how perfect it all is you'd throw your head up at the clouds and laugh."

**Manuscript attribution:** Buddha

**Finding:** No canonical Buddhist source has yet been verified. Modern quote collections repeat variants such as "When you realize how perfect everything is..." and attribute them to Buddha, but the research found so far points only to recent secondary inspirational books rather than a sutta or early textual source.

**Current disposition:** unresolved / high attribution-risk. Do not state as a direct Buddha quotation in final KDP without a canonical or scholarly source.

## Editorial consequence

These findings do **not** justify rewriting the Preface's dream, autobiographical material, or elemental teaching.

They support a separate source-cleanup pass:
- correct or qualify attribution;
- choose verified translations;
- determine permissions/licensing for modern translations and modern authors;
- preserve Kelly's surrounding prose whenever possible.

The quotation layer should serve the manuscript rather than force unnecessary changes to it.
