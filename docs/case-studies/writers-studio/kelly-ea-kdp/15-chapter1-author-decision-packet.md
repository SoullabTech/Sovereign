## Literary decision A — grammar micro

### Original

> The more conscious we are of the fluid nature of reality and the fact that we are ‘making it up’ allows us to begin to choose what we create and how we create it.

### Problem

The grammatical subject does not agree cleanly with “allows us.” This is a mechanical stumble, independent of the metaphysical claim.

### Recommended Micro

> Being more conscious of the fluid nature of reality and the fact that we are ‘making it up’ allows us to begin to choose what we create and how we create it.

This changes only the opening construction and preserves the proposition, cadence, and surrounding paragraph.

**Recommendation:** ACCEPT this Micro repair.

## Literary decision B — Augusten at the cymatics hinge

Overview suggested that the movement from Augusten’s smiling-face ember into pareidolia / cymatics might need a bridge.

The exact text already contains one:

> I explained to him that this phenomenon is called pareidolia…

followed by:

> This led to discussions about cymatics…

The narrower question is whether Augusten should remain visibly present after that moment.

### Keep case

The passage is a reverie. The mind moves from son → canyon → client room → Louisiana and back through the fire. Augusten fading from the immediate scene can be part of that reverie structure.

### Change case

Keeping him present for one more sentence could make the chapter’s only other character feel less like a launching device.

**Council recommendation:** KEEP.

**Our recommendation:** KEEP. Do not conventionalize the scene merely for scene continuity.

## Source decision C — Emily Dickinson epigraph

### Current manuscript

> “The Soul should always stand ajar, ready to welcome the ecstatic experience.” — Emily Dickinson

Dickinson’s actual poem begins “The Soul should always stand ajar,” but the added “ready to welcome the ecstatic experience” wording is not in the poem.

### Recommended smallest correction

Use the exact public-domain Dickinson line:

> “The Soul should always stand ajar.” — Emily Dickinson

This preserves the epigraph’s opening function while restoring source accuracy.

**Recommendation:** ACCEPT exact-source correction.

## Epistemic decision E — frequencies / cymatics

The chapter currently says:

> all of life is rooted in frequencies. All matter, at its base, is the dance of frequencies…

The two sentences are adjacent restatements, but the council recommends **not** spending a separate craft edit here because the source/epistemic decision will likely resolve the repetition naturally.

### Recommended standing

Treat this as **metaphysical / imaginal language drawing on scientific imagery**, not as a literal empirical definition of matter.

Then make the smallest wording adjustment needed to keep that standing clear.

**Recommendation:** preserve the image; precision pass later in the source/factual lane.

## Epistemic decision F — neuroplasticity

The current uses of “neuroplasticity” exceed the ordinary scientific definition.

**Recommendation:** preserve the experiential claims about flexible perspective and coherence, but revise the scientific term only where needed so “neuroplasticity” refers accurately to nervous-system adaptation/reorganization rather than serving as a synonym for perspective-taking or coherence.

This is source/factual precision, not a literary rewrite.
