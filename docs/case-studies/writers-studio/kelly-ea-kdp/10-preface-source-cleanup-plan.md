# Preface Source / Quotation Cleanup Plan — R1

**Case:** KELLY-NEZAT-EA-KDP-REFINEMENT-01
**Scope:** Preface source layer only
**Status:** proposal; no manuscript changes applied
**Principle:** protect Kelly’s prose first; change the external quotation layer before rewriting surrounding language.

This is an editorial/source-risk plan, not legal advice.

## Preferred KDP route

The lowest-friction publication path is to preserve Kelly’s surrounding prose and make the quotation layer more conservative:

1. keep quotations whose source and rights standing are sufficiently clear;
2. identify modern quotations that require permission;
3. where permission is not worth the delay, remove or replace the external quotation rather than rewriting Kelly’s prose;
4. replace inaccurate direct quotations with a clearly identified paraphrase when the teaching matters more than the exact wording.

## Q1 — Zhuangzi butterfly epigraph

**Current state:** source lineage substantially established through Herbert A. Giles’s nineteenth-century English translation.

**Preferred action:** KEEP the epigraph; identify the Giles translation/edition in the source notes.

**Kelly prose affected:** none.

## Q2 — “If your heart is truly open…”

**Current manuscript attribution:** Michael A. Singer.

**Evidence:** the exact wording appears as an **Anonymous** epigraph in Wayne Teasdale’s *The Mystic Heart*. The current Singer attribution is unsupported.

**Preferred publication action:** do not retain the Singer attribution.

Two viable routes:
- **Preservation route:** retain the line only if its reuse standing is cleared, crediting it as Anonymous with source note through Teasdale.
- **Lowest-risk route:** remove the external epigraph. Kelly’s surrounding prose already carries the experience of communion and remains intact.

**Kelly prose affected:** none under either route.

## Q3 — Rilke / “flow from me like a river…”

**Current state:** source identified as *Rilke’s Book of Hours: Love Poems to God*, translated by Anita Barrows and Joanna Macy (1996).

**Risk:** the modern English translation has its own copyright standing.

**Preferred publication action:** seek permission only if this exact translation is important enough to justify the process; otherwise remove or substitute a rights-cleared translation in a separate source act.

**Kelly prose affected:** none if the quotation alone is removed/replaced.

## Q4 — Wendell Berry / “our real work”

**Current state:** source traceable to Wendell Berry, *Standing by Words* (1983); modern copyrighted work.

**Preferred publication action:** permission or removal. The autobiographical paragraph that follows is strong enough to stand without the quotation.

**Kelly prose affected:** none if the quotation is removed.

## Q5 — Sadhguru / five ingredients

**Current state:** source traceable to *Inner Engineering*; quotation is comparatively long and modern.

**Preferred publication action:** permission if essential; otherwise remove the long quotation or replace it with a brief Kelly-authored paraphrase of the elemental idea in a later author-approved act.

**Kelly prose affected:** none if removed; one small authorial sentence if paraphrased.

## Q6 — “As you start to walk on the way, the way appears.”

**Current manuscript attribution:** Rumi.

**Evidence:** no reliable Rumi source has been established; current research points toward a modern Heidegger/Rilke-related lineage rather than Rumi.

**Preferred publication action:** remove the Rumi attribution and, unless a rights-cleared source is established, remove the epigraph itself.

The Call to Adventure remains structurally intelligible without it; the next sentence already opens the book-reader contract.

**Kelly prose affected:** none.

## Q7 — Kalama Sutta passage

**Current state:** the long circulating English passage is a loose modern paraphrase and should not be presented as an unqualified verbatim Buddha quotation.

**Preferred publication action:** preserve the teaching, not the inaccurate quotation.

Preferred replacement class:
- a concise Kelly-authored paraphrase of the discourse, explicitly introduced as a paraphrase; or
- a short excerpt from a rights-clear translation with exact source/translation identified.

A rights-clear modern reference is Bhikkhu Sujato’s AN 3.65 translation on SuttaCentral, distributed CC0.

**Kelly prose affected:** the current quoted block only; surrounding authorial argument remains.

## Q8 — “If you knew how perfect it all is…”

**Current manuscript attribution:** Buddha.

**Evidence:** no canonical source has been established; modern attribution research traces the familiar form only to recent secondary circulation and possible resemblance to a later Tibetan Buddhist text.

**Preferred publication action:** remove the direct Buddha attribution. Lowest-risk route: remove the quotation.

The closing sentence “Take what resonates, leave what doesn’t…” already carries the tonal function.

**Kelly prose affected:** none if the quotation is removed.

## Preservation effect of the preferred route

If Kelly chooses the lowest-friction KDP route:

- **100% of Kelly’s existing literary prose can remain intact** at this stage;
- the changes occur almost entirely in externally sourced quotation blocks and attributions;
- the only likely new authorial sentence would be a short Kalama Sutta paraphrase if Kelly wants to retain that teaching explicitly.

## Studio product learning

Writer’s Studio should model **Source / Attribution / Permissions** as a separate editorial layer from Development, Voice, Structure, and prose revision. A source-risk finding should be able to recommend:
- verify source;
- correct attribution;
- request permission;
- replace with rights-safe source;
- paraphrase;
- remove external quotation;

without implying that the writer’s surrounding prose is weak or should be rewritten.
