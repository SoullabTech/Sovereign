## Quotations

### Q1 — Emily Dickinson

**Manuscript:** “The Soul should always stand ajar, ready to welcome the ecstatic experience.” — Emily Dickinson

**Finding:** Dickinson did write a poem beginning “The Soul should always stand ajar,” but the original poem continues with “That if the Heaven inquire…” and does not contain the phrase “ready to welcome the ecstatic experience.”

Primary/public-domain text sources consulted include University of Michigan’s American Verse Project and public-domain Dickinson editions.

**Disposition:** the current sentence should not remain presented as an exact Dickinson quotation.

**Preservation-first options:**
1. replace it with an exact, short line from the public-domain Dickinson poem;
2. identify the current wording as a later paraphrase/adaptation if a defensible source is established;
3. remove the epigraph.

Kelly’s surrounding prose need not change.

### Q2 — “The privilege of a lifetime…”

**Manuscript attribution:** Carl Jung

**Finding:** the traceable published wording is Joseph Campbell: “The privilege of a lifetime is being who you are,” in *A Joseph Campbell Companion: Reflections on the Art of Living*. The manuscript wording (“to become who you truly are”) is also altered.

**Disposition:** current Jung attribution is not supported.

**Preservation-first options:**
1. use Campbell’s exact wording and attribute Campbell;
2. remove the epigraph;
3. retain the concept in Kelly’s own prose without presenting it as a direct quotation.

### Q3 — Henry Miller

**Manuscript:** “The aim of life is to live, and to live means to be aware, joyously, drunkenly, serenely, divinely aware.”

**Finding:** source is substantially verified in Henry Miller’s *The Wisdom of the Heart* / later Miller collections.

**Disposition:** attribution is supportable. Because this is a modern copyrighted author, commercial-use permissions/fair-use review remains separate.

## Scientific / empirical-language boundary

### F1 — “all of life is rooted in frequencies” / “All matter, at its base, is the dance of frequencies”

These sentences move from a discussion of cymatics into universal claims about life and matter.

**Issue:** the language reads as scientific assertion even though its function in the chapter is metaphysical/symbolic. Modern physics does not reduce the scientific description of all matter to the simple proposition “matter is frequency.”

**Preservation-first treatment:** do not remove the elemental/metaphysical insight. Consider either:
- clearly marking the sentence as metaphor/interpretation;
- narrowing the scientific wording;
- or separating the cymatics observation from the larger metaphysical claim.

### F2 — imagination and tangible outcomes

The chapter says imagination and beliefs shape reality and that positive imagined scenarios can be invoked into the tangible world.

**Evidence boundary:** mental imagery has empirical support for influencing motivation, intention, behavior, and some outcomes. That does not establish that imagined scenarios become externally real merely by imagining them.

**Preservation-first treatment:** retain the clinical/psychological truth about imagery shaping attention, expectation, behavior, and lived experience; avoid wording that accidentally converts that into an unsupported physical-causation claim unless Kelly explicitly intends a metaphysical claim and labels it accordingly.

### F3 — “objective world experienced by an objective witness is an illusion”

**Issue:** this is a philosophical/epistemological proposition, not an ordinary empirical finding.

**Preservation-first treatment:** preserve it if it is part of Kelly’s worldview, but signal its epistemic standing as interpretation/philosophy rather than presenting it as settled science.

### F4 — neuroplasticity

The chapter currently says:
- seeing reality from multiple vantage points “is the neuroplastic ability to perceive reality from different vantage points”;
- coherence is “a state of neuroplasticity, flexibility, and adaptability.”

**Evidence boundary:** neuroplasticity refers to adaptive structural and functional changes in the nervous system and its connections. Perspective-taking may involve learning and adaptable cognition, but it is not itself a definition of neuroplasticity. Likewise, coherence is not scientifically synonymous with neuroplasticity.

**Preservation-first treatment:** keep the experiential point, but use “neuroplasticity” only where it accurately names nervous-system change or learning-related reorganization.

### F5 — coherence / energy language

The chapter uses “coherence,” “energetically balanced,” “negative energies,” and “field” language.

**Issue:** these terms can function symbolically, phenomenologically, spiritually, or scientifically. Their standing should be clear enough that readers are not asked to treat one register as proof of another.

**Preservation-first treatment:** preserve the language where it belongs to the book’s ontology; add precision only where surrounding wording makes an empirical claim.

## Product learning for Writer’s Studio

Studio needs an **Epistemic Standing** layer alongside Source / Attribution / Permissions.

A finding should be able to say:
- poetic / imaginal;
- personal experience;
- clinical observation;
- philosophical proposition;
- traditional teaching;
- empirical claim;
- empirical analogy;
- metaphor drawing on science;

without ranking one way of knowing above another.

The purpose is not to police metaphysics. It is to prevent accidental category drift—especially when scientific vocabulary is used to carry a spiritual or philosophical meaning.
