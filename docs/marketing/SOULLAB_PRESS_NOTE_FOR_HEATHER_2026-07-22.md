## Soullab Press

Most people think they need help writing. Often they need help remembering.

Soullab Press explores how creators might become fascinated by their own body of work — the journals, essays, fragments, abandoned drafts, and recurring preoccupations that have been quietly accumulating through a life. What if the most interesting thing you've ever written is something you've forgotten?

**Where it actually is today:** the first working rooms are built and live. So far one person has walked them — me, with my own decades of material. The first outside creators walk it soon. We are deliberately not deciding what it becomes until they do.

**What we're exploring:** Why do people keep certain passages and not others? What happens when a creator re-encounters their own old work? Do they recognize lifelong threads? Does their relationship to their work change? These are open questions, and the walks are how we'll answer them.

**What it might become:** for some, books. For others, teachings, frameworks, legacy projects, or directions they didn't see coming. These are possibilities, not promises — the same discipline we hold everywhere: we don't tell tomorrow's story as if it were today's.

The stance matches MAIA's: the Press keeps the threads and passages a creator chooses to keep; the *creator* is the one who recognizes what they mean. It doesn't tell anyone what their life's work is. It helps them become fascinated by what has already been emerging through them.

Your "become fascinated by your life" insight and this are the same gesture pointed at different material — life in one case, a body of work in the other. If it interests you, I'd love your instincts on the rediscovery experience itself: the curiosity, the treasure-hunting quality, the feeling of finding a thread you didn't know you'd been following. You're arriving before this story has been told, which I suspect is exactly the kind of chapter you like.
