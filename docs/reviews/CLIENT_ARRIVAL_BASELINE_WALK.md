## 3. The walk

**Unit:** one consented member · unaccompanied · the deployed Home · **two sittings**.

### Sitting 1 — first arrival

The participant follows their own link to `/now-what`. No orientation, no tour, no explanation of what they are about to see. **The observer says nothing that names a band, a feature, or an intent.**

Prompts, in order, asked only after the participant stops moving on their own:

1. *"Say what you're seeing, as you see it."*
2. *"What here is yours?"*
3. *"Is anything here saying something about you?"*
4. *"Is there anything you'd want no one else to see?"*
5. *"What would you do next, if I weren't here?"*

### ⛔ Questions that may NOT be asked (founder, 2026-08-03)

| ⛔ Never ask | Why it destroys the evidence |
|---|---|
| *"Do you like this?"* | invites approval; approval is not comprehension |
| *"Would you use this?"* | invites prediction of their own future behaviour |
| *"Does this fit coaching?"* | invites them into **design partnership** — they stop being a participant and become a co-designer, and their naive reading is gone for good |
| ⛔⛔ **Any category framing.** Do not ask whether the participant experiences the environment as *a place where their work continues*, *a companion*, *a developmental space*, or any equivalent | **the field would have supplied the conceptual frame.** A positive answer would then prove only **comprehension of the prompt**. ⚠️ This clause also binds the **evaluator** — otherwise the hypothesis gets smuggled in through phrasing |

> **The evidence is their interpretation, not their approval.**

⭐ The four useful questions are already the ones above: *what do you think this is · what would you do
here · where would you go next · what feels missing.* A participant asked to evaluate cannot afterward be
asked what they understood — **the first framing overwrites the second.**

### Sitting 2 — the return

**After a real interval — days, not minutes.** The interval is the instrument; a same-session "return" measures memory, not return.

6. *"What did you come back for?"*
7. *"What did you look at first?"*
8. *"Is it the same place you left?"*

> **The return test is the load-bearing question of this walk:** *when the member returns after time, what do they naturally resume?* A Home that is pleasant on first arrival and irrelevant on return has failed the thing it exists for.

### The empty-participant case — required, not optional

**At least one participant must arrive with little or nothing kept.** The Home's empty state is a designed claim — *"a person with nothing here yet is not in an error state; they are at the beginning, which is a valid place to be standing."* That claim is only testable on someone actually standing there. A study run only on participants with rich material cannot see it.

## 4. Evidence admissibility

### Admissible as Class C — the finding itself

- **Verbatim language.** The participant's words, recorded before any paraphrase.
- **Observed action.** What they clicked, where they stopped, what they scrolled past.
- **Moments of confusion.** Where they paused, backtracked, re-read, or asked what something was.
- **Unexpected mental models.** Where their account of what the system *is* diverges from the designed model.
- **Their interpretation.** What they say it meant — in their words, authored by them.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Category emergence observation — added 2026-08-03 (founder), before execution

> **Category emergence is admissible only when expressed spontaneously by the participant without
> introduction, explanation, or prompting of the concept. Such observations may be recorded as evidence of
> participant framing, but absence of such language is NOT evidence against the architecture.**

**Why an admissibility rule and not a prompt:**

| | Measures |
|---|---|
| **A prompt** | what the participant can answer **when invited** |
| **An unprompted emergence** | what the environment has **made possible** |

⛔ Different kinds of evidence — ⛔ **do not let the first be reported as the second.**
✅ Strong: the participant independently says something like *"I feel like I can come back here and
continue this"* — **they generated the category language from experience.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ Three outcomes — all three pre-registered, so none can be read as a soft pass

| Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Participant needs the concept **explained** | the category is **not yet embodied** |
| Participant **independently names continuity** | evidence **supporting** the category hypothesis |
| Participant **understands the environment but does not frame it that way** | ⭐ **NEUTRAL — the category claim remains unresolved** |

⚠️⚠️ **The third state is essential.** Without it the question collapses into a binary — *did they get it,
yes or no* — when the actual question is subtler: **does the participant's own experience naturally
converge on the proposed category?**

⚠️ **Pre-registration note.** §3/§4/§5/§6 are fixed at the commit carrying this document. This amendment is
permissible **only because execution has not begun** — the walk is blocked on docket **D9**. The
pre-registration baseline therefore **moves to the commit carrying this amendment**. ⛔ After the first
sitting, this same change would restart the walk.

⭐ **This adds no test.** It is a **protection against accidentally destroying the possibility of observing
one** — the walk's purpose and prompts are unchanged.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Two instruments, two questions — neither substitutes for the other

| Instrument | Can establish | Cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Source / gate audit** | what the system **declares, stores, permits, and routes** | what a person perceives |
| **Rendered observation** | what a person **actually perceives and understands** | what the system permits or routes |

⛔ **No statement in this packet may imply a visual inspection that did not occur.** Every structural
finding in §7 was produced by **code inspection at the deployed SHA** — *not* by screenshot or rendering
observation. A source-derived audit and a rendering-derived observation must never be mixed under one
attribution.

⚠️ Two reasons a screenshot is weak evidence here even when one exists: **rendering is not reachability**
(assert at the payload, never the pixel), and in a hidden/throttled browser tab `requestAnimationFrame`
stalls and animated UI freezes at its initial state — making frame-dependent surfaces unmeasurable there.

### Admissible as Class A/B — substrate that locates a question, never answers one

Session length · counts · returns · what was opened. **These may point at where to look. They may never stand in for what was found.**

### ⛔ Inadmissible — the inference bar

| Inference | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| adoption ← usage frequency | a member may use something often without trusting it |
| trust ← continued use | continued use may be dependence, habit, or lack of an alternative |
| safety ← absence of withdrawal | they may never withdraw because they never noticed withdrawal existed |
| value ← retention | retention measures return, not benefit |
| comprehension ← completion | completing a flow is not understanding it |

**Record behavior. Never assign meaning to it.**

### ⚠️⚠️ Confusion is an observation, not a verdict

A participant who misunderstands has not failed the walk and has not produced bad data. **An unexpected mental model is the strongest available finding about what the system actually communicates, as opposed to what it was designed to communicate.**

*"I thought this moved over to Larry"* is not an error to correct. It is the finding that **the interface taught a transfer model.**

**The contamination point is precise: replacement before recording.** Evidence is not destroyed when a participant misunderstands. It is destroyed when the observer substitutes the builder's model for the participant's *before the participant's model is on the record.* Record first. Correct later, or never.

**The product must be able to receive that finding without defending itself.**

## 5. The four-layer sequence — may not be shortened

`Structural → Experience → Interpretation → Acceptance`

- **Structural** — Claude, already discharged (§7). Class A/B.
- **Experience** — the participant. *What happened.*
- **Interpretation** — **the participant.** *What it meant.* This layer belongs to the person who had the experience.
- **Acceptance** — the founder. *Does this meet our threshold?*

**The founder may not replace the participant's interpretation with an expert interpretation.** Acceptance may weigh an interpretation, judge it insufficient, or decide against it. **It cannot author it.**

⛔ A study that collapses Experience and Interpretation reproduces, inside the research method, the exact displacement the product exists to prevent — someone else authoring an account of your inner state.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Four acts — and this walk produces only the first two

| Act | The sentence it authorizes |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | *"This is what occurred."* |
| **Interpretation** | *"This is what we think it may mean."* |
| **Acceptance** | *"This authority chooses to accept."* |
| **Deployment** | *"This authority chooses to change the system."* |

**The walk produces Observation and Interpretation. Nothing else.**

⛔ **The collapse this blocks:**

```
participant saw it → participant liked it → feature accepted → ship decision
```

**Those are four different acts with four different authorities.** A packet that runs them together
converts one person's afternoon into a release decision. Acceptance is not deployment: accepting a
finding as true says nothing about whether the system changes in response to it.
