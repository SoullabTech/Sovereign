# The First Wave
### An invitation to practitioners

Something interesting is happening here. We don't think it's finished — and that is exactly why we're
writing to you.

We're forming a small first circle of practitioners — people who hold space for others' becoming — to help
steward something while it is still taking shape. The work is called MAIA, and it is being built by
practitioners, for practitioners. It grew around a single question: *what kind of intelligence actually
helps a person become more themselves — more clear, more capable, more the author of their own life?*

To be among the first here is a real opportunity — not to receive a finished thing, but to help shape what
it becomes, and to be known, by name, as one of the people who tended it early.

> **We are interested in people who can stay with a question long enough for something new to emerge.**

---

## What this is

An exploration of **relational intelligence** — the kind that lives in relationship, dialogue, reflection,
and accompaniment.

MAIA is a companion you think *with.* It rests on a simple commitment: it reflects, frames, and
accompanies, and it leaves the authorship of your life entirely with you. It supports your judgment rather
than replacing it, and deepens your work rather than automating it.

And it is shaped by the practitioners who use it. As a steward, you don't simply use MAIA — you help decide
what it becomes. Designed by us, for us.

Two things are real today, and one is still becoming:

- **MAIA, as your own companion** — a place to think, reflect, and work. Available now.
- **Studio — the practitioner layer** — the tools for bringing this into your work with the people you
  serve. Being built now, and shaped by this first circle.

We'd rather show you exactly where that line falls than blur it.

---

## Why now — and why together

The ground is shifting under every practice. New tools arrive weekly; the landscape of coaching,
facilitation, and healing work is changing faster than anyone can track. Most practitioners are being asked
to navigate all of it alone — holding together a growing collection of disconnected subscriptions and
platforms, and quietly wondering what their work becomes in a world reshaped by AI.

We think there is another way to meet this moment: together.

One of our aims is a more integrated environment, where the essential functions of a practice can live in
one coherent place rather than scattered across a dozen tools. But the deeper aim is simpler — a place
where practitioners can face a changing landscape in good company, and help build what comes next instead
of bracing against it.

That is what stewardship here is meant to protect and strengthen:

- **Your sovereignty** — your work, your data, your judgment, kept yours.
- **Your practice** — strengthened, never outsourced.
- **Your voice** — the thing that makes your work *yours*, preserved.
- **Your resilience** — steadier ground beneath your practice, built together.
- **A hand in what comes next** — helping shape the future of this work, rather than inheriting someone
  else's version of it.

---

## Who it's for

This is less about credentials and more about the work you already do. We have in mind:

- Coaches and facilitators
- Guides, healers, and spiritual directors
- Educators and mentors
- **Emerging practitioners building a practice — you especially**

If you hold space for other people's becoming — whatever your work is called — you'll likely feel at home
here. This first wave is oriented toward developmental, reflective, and facilitative practice.

---

## What resonates here

More than a skill set, it's a disposition. You may recognize yourself in some of these:

- **Curiosity** that outpaces certainty
- **Reflectiveness** — you notice how things land, in yourself and in others
- **Honesty about your own experience** — naming what doesn't work as readily as what does
- **An ease with things unfinished** — a readiness to help shape rather than wait for finished
- **A pull toward relationship** — the human part of your work is the part you most want to keep
- **Care for what you're entrusted with** — you tend things, rather than only use them

If *staying with a question without rushing to close it* sounds like how you already work, you'll feel the
fit.

---

## What you receive as a steward

We want to be concrete, because a vague invitation serves no one:

- **MAIA as your own companion**, from the start — to think and work with, now.
- **A founding steward's hand in how Studio takes shape** — real influence over the practitioner layer
  while it is still being built.
- **Complimentary access through the founding period** — while you help shape the practitioner experience —
  with founding standing recognized as Studio opens to others.
- **Direct conversations with the people building it** — you'll know us, and we'll know you.
- **A small circle of fellow stewards** — kept intentionally small, and known to one another.

---

## What stewardship asks

A stewardship asks a little of its stewards — gently, and simply:

- Spend a few weeks with MAIA, honestly, in or alongside your real work.
- Tell us what you find — the rough edges as much as the moments that land.
- And if it genuinely moves you, you might share a short, unscripted reflection we could pass on. Only ever
  if it's true for you.

---

## Where we actually are

We'd rather be honest than impressive.

The inquiry behind this work did not end in a conclusion. It ended in a better question — something alive,
and still becoming. MAIA is genuinely a companion you can work with today. The practitioner layer is real,
and still forming.

That honesty is part of the opportunity: there is room here to shape things a finished product would never
let you near. We're gathering thoughtful people who'd like to tend it with us while it grows.

*(The thinking behind all this lives in the essay [Relational Intelligence, Not AI →]. If it speaks to you,
you'll recognize yourself in it.)*

---

## The invitation

If something here resonates, there is a door — and it's open.

We can only work closely with a limited number of stewards in this first cohort. That isn't scarcity —
it's care: stewardship is a real relationship, and relationships don't scale. So we're gathering for fit
rather than numbers, chosen with attention.

To be among them is a genuine opportunity: to help shape, from the inside, a practice built by us and for
us.
