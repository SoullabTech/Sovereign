# The Outcomes Question — Vision Ledger Entry

**Status: CANDIDATE — 2026-07-10 — UNCOMMITTED pending Kelly.**
**Layer: VISION. Designed for, not claimed. This entry exists so the question has a named home before it is asked.**

---

## §1 The question

*"Do clients using this flourish more?"*

Any empirically serious reviewer — a positive-psychology faculty foremost — will eventually ask it, and they will be right to. This entry is the platform's standing answer under the claim discipline (`docs/canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md`), so the answer is prepared honesty rather than silence or inflation.

## §2 What can be said today (the whole of it)

**Live:** Nothing about client outcomes. No outcome claim of any kind is authorized on any surface.

**Live, adjacent:** The system's *conduct* is evaluable and evaluated. The What Now eval harness (`docs/specs/WHAT_NOW_EVAL_HARNESS_SPEC_2026-07-10.md`) carries its own scope boundary in its third line: it evaluates *"the system's conduct — provenance, refusals, register, constitutional behaviors — **not coaching efficacy**. Whether the conversations help Larry's clients is a question only his field answers. The eval must never be cited as more than it is."* That boundary travels with every citation. Conduct evidence is real evidence — it is just evidence about a different thing, and conflating the two is the inflation drift this ledger exists to refuse.

**The honest sentence for the review room:** *We can show you, with production evidence, that the system behaves constitutionally. We cannot show you that clients flourish more, because we have not measured it, and we will not claim what we have not measured.*

## §3 Why "designed for, not claimed" is the accurate stamp

The architecture is not neutral on outcomes — several live structures exist *because* of hypotheses about flourishing (agency preservation, member-language sovereignty, anti-dependency design, the practitioner's framework reaching between sessions). Those are design commitments with reasons, not measured effects. "Designed for" names the intention honestly; "not claimed" keeps the intention from impersonating a result.

Center of Gravity: **architecture and constitutional conduct** (where the evidence is), not client transformation (where it is not yet).

## §4 What a real answer would require (Vision — named, not designed, not authorized)

Held here so that when the question matures, the shape is already visible:

1. **Practitioner-owned instruments.** Outcome measurement in a practitioner field belongs to the practitioner's jurisdiction — their framework, their instruments (a positive-psychology coach has validated ones available), their clients' consent. The platform's role would be carrying the instrument, never interpreting it. This respects the same fence as everything else: the field's health, never the clients' interiors, and nothing crosses without a member's explicit act.
2. **Consent as the gate, per member, per instrument.** No ambient outcome telemetry, ever. Measurement would be a member-facing choice with the same standing-consent grammar as anchors and atoms.
3. **The platform never grades flourishing.** Even with instruments live, the platform reports what members reported; synthesis into "this client is flourishing" is exactly the person-modeling the Refusal Registry refuses. Aggregation, if any, is the practitioner's and the researchers' work, in their jurisdiction.
4. **Independent evaluation over self-report.** If institutional partners want efficacy evidence, the credible path is *their* study design on *their* cohort with the platform as instrumented substrate — not platform-authored case studies. The platform's contribution is provenance: it can show exactly what the field said and when, which most coaching-outcomes research cannot.

None of the above is authorized by this entry. Each would need its own spec, its own consent design, and Kelly's gate. The entry's function is to hold the question's home so the answer never has to be improvised or inflated.

## §5 Failure Test

Read in front of the faculty: does any sentence claim an outcome? No — §2 states the null claim plainly. Does any sentence *forbid* the question? No — §4 names the credible path. The entry survives the room it was written for.
