# Case Study Library — Rewrite Brief

*For Heather. The strategic shift, the anchor sentence, the template, and the test.*

---

## What's changing

The audience paper and the existing case-study library answer: *Who is MAIA for?*

They don't yet answer: *What does someone actually do on MAIA?*

Right now most case studies sit at the identity level — Lifelong Practitioner, Eclectic Practitioner, Second-Career Helper, Founder, Seeker. Those help a reader recognize *the type*. They don't yet help a reader imagine herself *inside the product*.

The shift is bigger than "add actions."

The old unit of value was:

> **User → Need → Outcome**

The new unit is:

> **Recognition → Practice → Transformation**

That's a product insight, not a copywriting insight. The case study isn't selling MAIA. It's prototyping the felt experience MAIA wants to evoke.

## The anchor sentence

> **The purpose of the case study is not to help the reader understand MAIA.**
> **The purpose is to help the reader recognize themselves.**

Everything else follows from that.

Once that sentence lands, the questions change. Not:

- What feature does MAIA provide?
- What capability should we describe?
- What outcome should we promise?

But:

- What moment does this person already know?
- What experience keeps recurring in their life?
- What thread do they keep losing?
- What practice would help them stay with it?
- What begins to emerge if they do?

## The new template (five layers per case study)

1. **Who They Are** — a brief recognition, not a biography. One paragraph. Closer to "you might be this person if…" than to "Dan is sixty-two and…"

2. **The Missing Feeling** — what they secretly long for, in their own voice. One or two lines, in quotation marks. The reader must feel they could have said it.

3. **What They Actually Do** — concrete recurring actions on MAIA. Bullets. Not capabilities. Behaviors. A reader should be able to imagine doing them on a Tuesday morning.

4. **What Begins To Happen** — experiences that emerge, not features that triggered them. Prose. Specific moments, not abstract benefits. "A symbol from Tuesday's dream surfaced in a client session three days later" — not "pattern recognition across temporal contexts."

5. **What Changes** — the transformation. The longing softens. The thread holds. Often ends with a sentence the person might actually say.

## Two versions of the same case study

**Old (positioning statement):**
> Dan is a lifelong practitioner. His challenge is integration. MAIA helps him connect present experience to a lifetime of wisdom.

**New (anticipated experience):**
> Dan wakes up with a dream he can't stop thinking about. Instead of losing it, he opens MAIA and spends ten minutes exploring it. Three days later a difficult interaction with a client surfaces the same symbol. A week later he notices the pattern emerging in his marriage. For the first time he sees three seemingly unrelated experiences are part of the same developmental thread. Not because MAIA told him. Because MAIA helped him stay in relationship with them long enough to notice.

The reader is no longer learning *about* Dan. The reader is *becoming* Dan.

## What we're actually selling

| Identity | What it appears to sell | What it's actually selling |
|---|---|---|
| Lifelong Practitioner | Integration | "Everything I've learned still matters." |
| Eclectic Practitioner | Coherence across modalities | "Maybe these aren't twenty unrelated frameworks." |
| Second-Career Helper | Developmental growth | "Maybe I don't need another certification to be more helpful." |
| Founder | Strategic reflection | "I don't have to carry this vision alone." |
| Grief | Reflective continuity | "I don't have to lose this." |
| Therapist | Better session prep | "The important things stop slipping away." |
| Seeker | Self-understanding | "What I've been looking for isn't another answer — it's a way to stay with what I already know." |

The first column is what the brochure says. The second column is what the reader *recognizes*. That second column is the real product.

## Discipline boundary

Every case study must stay honest about what MAIA currently delivers — see [MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md](../canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md). The three instruments apply:

- **Live / Designed / Vision** — label each action by its current status. Dan's morning ten-minute reflection is Live. A formal "Shadow Inquiry" surface is Designed. A monthly auto-generated review is Vision. Write what's Live. Hint at what's Designed. Don't tell tomorrow's story as today's.
- **Center of Gravity** — each case study should orient around one true axis of recognition, not a feature checklist. Dan's axis is *coherence over time*. Others will be *not having to carry it alone*, *not losing what matters*, *staying with what you already know*.
- **Failure Test** — if MAIA stopped working tomorrow, what about Dan's story falls apart? If the answer is "the recognition," you've sold something real. If the answer is "the feature names," you've sold a chatbot.

## Process

1. The Dan case study, written in this template, is the pattern — see [CASE_STUDY_DAN_RECOGNITION.md](./CASE_STUDY_DAN_RECOGNITION.md).
2. Rewrite the existing library through this lens, one at a time. The order doesn't matter as much as the discipline.
3. Each one passes a single test before it ships:
   > **Does a reader of this type read it and think "wait, that's me"?**

If yes, the case study is doing its work. If no — even if every sentence is true — it's still a positioning document.

## The real deliverable

Not fifty rewritten case studies.

The pattern that can produce fifty more.

Once you have the pattern, you won't need further instruction. Each new persona becomes a question about *what moment does this person already know*, and the rest follows.

## Where this leads

The current project focuses on transforming profession-based case studies into recognition-based narratives.

This is not the final form.

The trajectory is:

> **Positioning → Recognition → Practice → Life**

- *Positioning* helps people understand what MAIA is.
- *Recognition* helps them see themselves in it.
- *Practice* helps them understand how it is used.
- *Life* helps them experience what it feels like to live with it.

The first three are still explanatory. The fourth is experiential.

The current case study work occupies the **Recognition** stage. The Dan pattern is the test that the stage works.

The next stage — **Practice** — surfaces the recurring rhythms underneath the recognition. What does someone do on Monday, on Thursday, when they're not looking for a breakthrough? That's a different artifact, not a case study with more bullets.

Eventually — at the **Life** stage — the strongest stories may no longer be case studies at all, but depictions of ordinary life unfolding with MAIA quietly present in the background. A diary. A week. A season. Forms marketers don't usually make, because they don't pitch — they invite.

The goal is not to explain MAIA.

The goal is to make its role in a person's life *recognizable* — and eventually, *familiar*.

The first prototype at the Life stage is `A_WEEK_WITH_MAIA_DAN.md`. If it works, the form generalizes. If it doesn't, we learn something before drafting Emily and Mark in the Recognition register and find out we should have done it the other way.

Either result is the highest-information next move.
