## 1. The Psychotherapist — Fred & Maya *(Stewardship · flagship)*

**WHO.** Fred, 50s, relational/depth psychotherapist, fifteen years in practice, ~20 clients a week. His client **Maya**, 34, came in for anxiety that runs on a pattern: over-function, over-commit, collapse.

**SITUATION.** Maya has real insight *in session* — and loses the thread by Tuesday. The week between sessions is where her actual life happens, and where the work evaporates. Fred can't be in her pocket, and shouldn't be. Meanwhile he carries twenty people's threads in his own head and quietly worries his attunement is thinning under the caseload.

**USAGE FLOW.**
- **Entry (Care voice).** Fred invites Maya to use MAIA between sessions — framed explicitly as *her* reflective space, not a reporting channel back to him.
- **In the moment.** Thursday night, the familiar collapse starts. Maya opens MAIA. It doesn't fix or diagnose — it reflects: *"You said yes to three things this week. Where did the yes come from?"* She catches the pattern **as it's happening**, for the first time outside the therapy room.
- **Privacy beat (Sanctuary).** The night it's rawest — shame about a specific relationship — Maya turns on Sanctuary Mode. That conversation leaves no trace, ever. She speaks freely *because* nothing is kept.
- **Over time [DESIGNED].** Across weeks she brings sharper material to Fred — *"I noticed I collapse on Thursdays"* — because she's been reflecting, not ruminating. *(Longitudinal pattern-surfacing is the relationship MAIA is built toward; today's reliable value is the in-the-moment catch.)*

**PRACTITIONER SIDE.** Fred uses his *own* MAIA — never to surveil Maya (he sees nothing of her sessions) — but to reflect on his practice: where he's over-identifying, where attention thins across a heavy week. *MAIA holds the mirror for the one who holds mirrors.*

**THE LIABILITY LANE (the hook).** MAIA stays in the reflective lane and never enters the clinical one — it does not diagnose, assess, score, or keep a clinical record, and in Sanctuary it retains nothing. It is not a substitute for Fred and never positions itself as one. *(Framed as scope-of-practice clarity, not a regulatory guarantee.)*

**THE TURN.** Maya arrives at her next session already at the threshold — not needing Fred to excavate, ready to go deeper. Between-session time stopped being lost time.

**AUTONOMY ARC.** Six months on, Maya reaches for MAIA less on Thursdays; she's internalized the question. MAIA's success is measured by how little she needs it.

**TRUE TODAY vs DESIGNED.** *Live:* Care-voice reflection, Sanctuary, the in-the-moment catch, Fred's own reflective use. *Designed:* "MAIA tracked Maya's Thursday pattern across months."

## 2. The Life-Transition Navigator — Dana *(Presence → Continuity · the emotional center)*

**WHO.** Dana, 47, divorcing after nineteen years. Holding it together at work, unraveling at home. Friends are kind but tiring of the topic; therapy is once a week.

**SITUATION.** The hardest moments don't schedule themselves — 11pm, the empty side of the bed, the kid's game she's at alone. She needs somewhere to put the weight that isn't a friend she's burning out or a 2am doomscroll.

**USAGE FLOW.**
- **Entry (Presence, free · Care voice).** She just starts talking.
- **In the moment.** MAIA doesn't cheer her up or fix it. It witnesses, and now and then reflects a shape back: *"You keep saying 'I should be over this.' Says who?"* She feels met without being managed.
- **Privacy beat (Sanctuary).** The night she admits the thing she's ashamed of — relief that he's gone, then guilt about the relief — Sanctuary Mode. Said once, to no one and to something, then gone.
- **Over time [DESIGNED].** As weeks pass MAIA reflects the arc — anger turning to grief turning to something quieter. Dana can *see* she's moving, which from inside a divorce is nearly impossible. *(Anchor copy on the nightly holding, which is live; the arc-reflection is the promise.)*

**THE TURN.** Around month four she opens MAIA less to survive the night and more to mark a good day. The tool changed function — from triage to witness.

**AUTONOMY ARC.** No dramatic graduation; she just notices one week she didn't need it. It was scaffolding for a bridge she built herself.

**TRUE TODAY vs DESIGNED.** *Live:* nightly Care-voice holding, Sanctuary, in-the-moment reflection. *Designed:* "MAIA showed Dana her six-month arc."

## 3. The Daily Reflective Practice — Sam *(Presence · safest to publish first, ~100% live)*

**WHO.** Sam, 38, engineering lead. Not in crisis — wants an examined life, and bounces off meditation apps with their streaks and guilt.

**SITUATION.** Journaling works when he does it, but the blank page is intimidating, and apps turn reflection into a chore with a counter — exactly the engagement mechanics MAIA refuses (Anti-Features #2/#7).

**USAGE FLOW.**
- **Entry (Note voice — the witness).** Two minutes most mornings.
- **In the moment.** MAIA reflects without interpreting. Sam: *"Tired. Low-grade dread about the launch."* MAIA: *"The dread has a date on it. What happens after the 15th?"* Not advice — a better question. The blank-page problem dissolves because there's a presence, not a prompt.
- **No-mechanics beat.** No streaks. No "you're falling behind." Skip four days and MAIA doesn't shame him — at most, *"Been a minute. No pressure."* The absence of pressure **is** the design.
- **Over time [DESIGNED].** Months in, a theme he never connected surfaces — the dread clusters *before* he ships, never during. *(Pattern-surfacing is the promise.)*

**THE TURN.** Reflection stops being a task and becomes a place he wants to go — because nothing is extracting from him there.

**AUTONOMY ARC.** MAIA makes itself less necessary by design. The metric that matters is the one most apps would call failure: he needs it *less*.

**TRUE TODAY vs DESIGNED.** *Live:* Note voice, no-mechanics design, in-the-moment reflection — publishable today with near-zero inflation risk. *Designed:* only the "surfaced themes over months" line is forward-looking.
