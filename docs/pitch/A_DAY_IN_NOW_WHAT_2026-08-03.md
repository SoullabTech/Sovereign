# A Day in Now What?

### Larry and Senja, one Thursday

**For:** Larry · **Date:** 2026-08-03 · One page.

> ⚠️ **This is a designed experience, not a demonstration. None of it runs yet.** It is what Stage One
> and Stage Two are being built to produce. Read it as *what this becomes*, never as *what it does*.

> The hardest part of AI is no longer getting a machine to produce answers. The hardest part is
> creating a system where the answers remain in right relationship with the person using them.

---

## Morning — Larry

**8:40 a.m.** He opens Now What?. Not a dashboard. A short line:

> *Good morning, Larry. Three conversations today. Senja at eleven.*

He taps Senja's name.

**What he sees.** Working together since March. The program he placed her in, and the stage she is
at — placed by him, so it is his to see. Their last session, and Thursday's. Two things he offered
her last month, and which of them she affirmed. One passage she chose to share with him, in her own
words, three weeks ago.

**What he does.** He reads the passage again. He writes a private note for himself before the call —
his note, not hers, and it never appears in her world unless he chooses to give it to her.

He closes the laptop knowing what this conversation is about. It took ninety seconds.

## Evening — Senja

**9:15 p.m.** She opens her own Home. Not Larry's view of her. Hers.

**What she sees.** The relationship named. Then her own material — the things *she* marked as worth
keeping, in the order she left them, not the order she happened to touch them. A paragraph she wrote
in June sits at the top because she kept it, not because it was recent.

Below that, what Larry offered, with his name on it: *"Larry offered — where might you experiment
with delegation?"* She can affirm it, set it aside, or bring it to Thursday.

**What she does.** She writes for eleven minutes. She marks one paragraph to carry forward. She
un-shares something she shared in July — she has changed her mind about it.

Then, because she wants to, she asks: *"have I written about this before?"*

> *In March you wrote: "I don't think I can keep holding all of this."*
> *Both of these mention holding too much.*
> — *shown because you asked*

She reads it. **She decides it is not the same thing** — March was exhaustion, June is something
else — and says so. The connection is dismissed and does not come back.

---

## The part that matters is what did not happen

- Larry never saw what she wrote tonight.
- Larry never saw that she un-shared something. **No notification. No gap where it used to be.** His
  Thursday view is identical to what it would have been if she had never shared it at all.
- Nothing told Larry she had been active, how long she wrote, or that she was working late.
- Nothing decided what her paragraph *meant*. It pointed at two things she wrote and named the
  connection out loud — *both mention holding too much* — so she could check it, and refuse it.
- Nothing appeared to her uninvited. She asked, and the answer said so.

**That is the product.** Everything above the line is what it feels like. Everything in this list is
why it can be trusted — and none of it is visible, which is exactly the difficulty of explaining this
category and exactly why the boundary has to be built before the features.

surfaces: **the refusal must persist**, which Article 9 §2.3 leaves unruled — the scene assumes it
