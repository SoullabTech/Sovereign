# DEMO PROTOCOL — What May Be Shown, and How (2026-07-12)

**Status**: Wave 1 artifact, drafted on Kelly's go; pending Kelly's red-line. **This is the constitutional document in Mark's kit** — it stands between enthusiasm and a high-visibility consent slip. It applies to every external showing: screen-shares, live demos, screenshots, recordings, conference rooms, texts to a friend.

## The one test, before anything else

> **Could this moment appear in a screenshot, published, without violating anyone's consent or any claim stamp?**

If the answer needs a paragraph, the answer is no.

## What you demo FROM

1. **Your own member account** — for anything you're personally willing to show of yourself. This is the strongest demo there is: the mirror working on the person presenting it. Show only what you'd show a stranger; assume the room records.
2. **The demo persona** — a seeded, clearly-labeled fictional member account for everything else. It is always introduced as fictional ("this is our demo member, not a real person") — passing fiction as a real member is a claim violation, not a convenience.
3. **Public surfaces as themselves** — landings, thresholds, the Commons' public faces. Public is public.

## The reference fields (Larry's, Jondi's)

- **Per-instance consent from the field's author, every time.** An existing relationship — including your own with Jondi — is not standing consent. Ask before each external showing, name the audience, get the yes.
- What the author has made public (threshold/landing pages) may be shown as public pages. Everything past the threshold is theirs to gate.

## Never — no exceptions, no judgment calls

- **Never real member data.** Not anonymized, not "just the shape of it," not scrolled-past-quickly. The case-study library exists precisely so member stories travel only with consent and stamps — stories come from there or they don't travel.
- **Never live navigation of real material in front of others** — no live search of a journal, portfolio, or field, even your own if you haven't pre-walked the route. **Pre-selected excerpts, never live exploration** — live navigation is how the thing you didn't mean to show gets shown.
- **Never an unstamped number.** Member counts, revenue, growth, engagement: if Kelly didn't hand it to you with a stamp, it doesn't exist in the room.
- **Never admin surfaces, logs, dashboards, or ops tooling on a shared screen.**

## Demo the refusals — they are the product

The strongest three minutes available: turn on **Sanctuary Mode** and show that nothing is remembered. Ask MAIA to psychoanalyze you and show the **decline**. Show a kept memory's **consent controls** — that surfacing is the member's choice. In this house, what the system *won't* do is the differentiator; a demo that only shows features is selling the wrong thing.

## High-visibility rooms (the CAA rule)

The bigger the room, the *tighter* the protocol — never looser. For any high-stakes demo: pre-walked route, demo persona + your own account only, refusals included, no Q&A-driven live navigation ("great question — let me show you that properly afterward" is a complete answer), and a dry run with Kelly before the first one.
