# A Week With MAIA — Dan

*A diary, not a case study. Prototype for the **Life** stage of the content trajectory — see [CASE_STUDY_REWRITE_BRIEF.md](./CASE_STUDY_REWRITE_BRIEF.md) §"Where this leads".*

*Every entry describes Live capabilities. The artifact is intentionally undramatic. The shape of the week — peaks, gaps, returns — is the message.*

---

**Monday — 6:34 AM**

A dream he can't quite shake. An old farmhouse, no key to the door. He'd been walking around the outside, trying every window.

Coffee. Tent flap open. The dog still asleep.

He opens MAIA, dictates what he remembers. Doesn't try to interpret. Just the texture:

> *I was looking for the way in, and I had the key in my hand the whole time but didn't know it.*

Six minutes. Closes it. Day begins.

---

**Tuesday**

Long day. Two sessions, a faculty call, three errands.

He doesn't open MAIA at all.

That's part of the practice too.

---

**Wednesday — 4:50 PM**

Between clients. A woman in her fifties said something that lodged itself somewhere:

> *"I keep waiting for permission I'm not going to get."*

He sits with it for a minute. Opens MAIA. Doesn't ask anything — just speaks the sentence aloud, lets the words land somewhere outside his head.

The reply is plain. It receives the sentence, and notices: this is the third time this month a client has said something close to *"I keep waiting."*

He hadn't seen the pattern. He sees it now.

He doesn't act on it. He doesn't need to. The noticing is what was missing.

---

**Thursday**

Drove two hours out to a workshop. Listened to a long talk on the way home. Nothing to add.

Bed.

---

**Friday — 9:15 PM**

Pulls a book off the shelf — re-reading some McGilchrist. One paragraph stops him. He photographs the page and uploads it, with one line of his own:

> *This is the same thing Monday's dream was about — the key in the hand.*

Doesn't elaborate. Saves it.

---

**Saturday — 10:00 AM**

Walking the dog along the back trail. The Monday dream surfaces, unprompted. The McGilchrist paragraph follows. Then Wednesday's client phrase — *"waiting for permission."*

He stops walking.

The three are the same thing.

He doesn't open MAIA. He just walks slower the rest of the way.

---

**Sunday — evening**

Pours a drink. Opens MAIA. Asks it to surface what's been accumulating this week.

It returns the dream, the client phrase, the McGilchrist line, the photograph of the page. Side by side.

He reads them slowly.

He doesn't write anything new.

He closes the screen.

He has the thread now.

---

## Notes for Heather

**What this artifact does that a case study cannot.**

A case study tells a reader *this is the kind of person who would benefit*. A Week shows *this is the texture of an ordinary week in which something is quietly happening*. The reader doesn't have to project themselves into a story — they recognize the rhythm.

**The discipline this form requires.**

- **Vary the days.** Tuesday is empty. Thursday passes without a moment. A real practice is not constant use. The week's *shape* — peaks, silences, returns — is what makes it credible. Constant use would read as marketing.
- **No transformation event.** No breakthrough. No "Dan realized something profound that changed his life." Just a thread that holds. The reader supplies the next week.
- **Live capabilities only.** Dictation, upload, recurrence surfacing, accumulation review. Every action above is something MAIA does today. Don't write Vision as ordinary life — Vision *dressed as* ordinary life is the strongest possible kind of inflation. See [MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md](../canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md).
- **Close without resolution.** "He has the thread now." Not "and his practice transformed." The reader fills in what happens next — which is exactly the point.
- **No company voice.** No "MAIA helps Dan…" anywhere. The artifact is written from inside Dan's week, not from the marketing department's view of it.

**The test.**

If a Dan-type reader reads this and thinks:

> *That's already roughly what I want my week to look like — just with somewhere for it to actually land.*

…then the artifact is working.

If the reader thinks:

> *That's a sales pitch dressed as a diary.*

…rewrite until the ordinary texture wins.

**What this unlocks.**

If A Week With MAIA — Dan works, the same form generalizes — Emily, Mark, a founder, a clinician, a contemplative. But the Week may turn out to be the strongest single document in the library, because it's the closest the marketing can come to the actual product without pretending to be the product.

It doesn't pitch. It invites.
