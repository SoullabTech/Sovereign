Heather —

You found the real gap. The audience paper and the library answer *"Who is MAIA for?"* They don't yet answer *"What would I actually do with this on a Tuesday?"* — and that's the question every reader is silently asking.

But the shift is bigger than adding an actions list. The current case studies are written **from the company's perspective** (*"Dan is a lifelong practitioner; his challenge is integration; MAIA helps him connect…"*). That's a positioning statement. People don't buy positioning statements — they buy **anticipated experience**. What they need to feel is *"wait… I can do that?"* / *"that's what's been missing."*

## What this actually is

This is the point where a case study stops being marketing and becomes closer to **product design**. We're not describing a user from the outside; we're reproducing a moment of recognition the reader is *already having from the inside*.

**The mechanism is recognition, not persuasion.** Dan-with-the-dream works because the reader has had that exact morning — the story just puts words on something they already half-know. Once recognized, no persuasion is needed. We're not selling integration; we're selling *"finally, the pieces may come together."* We're selling the feeling of finding the missing key.

## The new template (every case study, rewritten)

1. **Who They Are** — a brief recognition (one or two lines).
2. **The Missing Feeling** — the thing they secretly long for, in their own voice.
3. **What They Actually Do** — concrete, recurring actions.
4. **What Begins To Happen** — *experiences, not features.*
5. **What Changes** — the transformation.

## The one rule that keeps it honest *and* makes it land

The felt form is seductive, which makes it the easiest place to over-claim. The discipline that prevents that is the same one that makes it powerful:

> **Keep the carrier on the human.** Every "What Begins To Happen" beat must be something the *member* experiences or notices — never something *MAIA reveals or remembers.*

If MAIA *reveals* the pattern, the reader meets a tool — and you've quietly imported the longitudinal-memory capability that isn't verified yet. If MAIA *keeps you in relationship with the material until you notice it yourself*, the reader meets their own capacity. The most moving version is the most honest version. (This is why the library's verdicts still apply: Grief and Retreat stay **forward voice** even in felt form; Aging Parent stays **vision**.)

## Two cautions on the action vocabulary

- *"Morning Reflection," "Dream Exploration," "Shadow Inquiry," "Monthly Integration Review," "Decision Navigation," "Wisdom Capture"* are evocative and useful — but write them as **things the member does**, not as **named features MAIA ships**. Naming an unbuilt mode is the action-vocabulary form of capability inflation. (Flag any you want treated as real product surfaces and we'll confirm with engineering before they appear as features.)
- The developmental / integration thesis stays in **provisional voice** — *"appears to," "many find."* It's cohort-observed, not measured.

## The promise, at its most honest — and most powerful

Not *"MAIA will transform your life."* Closer to:

> *"You may discover that what you've been looking for isn't another answer. It's a way to stay in relationship with what you already know."*

That's a feeling. And feelings are what people recognize first.

# Worked example (the pattern) — The Lifelong Practitioner: Dan

> `Carrier: Human · Layer: Live` — the noticing is Dan's; MAIA holds the space.

**Who They Are.** Dan, late fifties, thirty years in practice — depth psychology, bodywork, meditation, more retreats than he can count. He isn't looking for a new method. He has plenty.

**The Missing Feeling.** *"Everything I've learned still matters — but it lives in pieces. I want it to cohere, not just keep accumulating."*

**What They Actually Do.**
- Opens MAIA most mornings for ten minutes, before the day starts.
- Brings the dream that won't leave him alone — and stays with it instead of letting it evaporate.
- When he's stuck with a client or a decision, thinks it through out loud.
- Returns, in his own time, to the threads he wants to keep alive.

**What Begins To Happen.** Monday, a dream he can't shake — he brings it to MAIA and lingers for ten minutes. Thursday, a hard moment with a client; the same image is there, and *he* notices it — it's been with him all week. The following week he catches the same shape in a conversation with his wife. He brings the three together, and in the reflecting, **he** sees it: not three unrelated events, but one thread asking for his attention. Not because MAIA told him — because MAIA gave him somewhere to stay in relationship with the material long enough to notice.

**What Changes.** He stops collecting insight and starts integrating it. Thirty years stop feeling like a pile and start feeling like a path.

> *Escalation to avoid:* if MAIA were the one resurfacing *"you mentioned this symbol Monday,"* that's the Designed continuity capability — not verified yet. Keep the connecting work in Dan until it ships. The Live version is also the better story: the reader recognizes **their own** noticing, not a machine's.
