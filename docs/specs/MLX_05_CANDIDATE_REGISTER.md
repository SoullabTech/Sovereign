## C-01 · Relational stance at arrival

**Proposed (Kelly, 2026-08-25). Not in the frozen stimulus.**

A field after the doorway and before MAIA's first turn:

> **What would be most useful from me right now?**
> listen · reflect · explore · challenge · help me act

```
   FROZEN (c137e44)                    CANDIDATE
   sign up                             sign up
     ↓                                   ↓
   name                                name
     ↓                                   ↓
   what is asking for your attention   what is asking for your attention
     ↓                                   ↓
   one doorway                         one doorway
     ↓                                   ↓
   MAIA begins                         [ optional relational stance ]
                                         ↓
                                       MAIA begins
```

### What is genuinely good about it

It is **member-authored instruction about how MAIA should behave** — not a disclosure about the member, not
an inference, not a profile field. It increases agency rather than extracting data, which places it cleanly
inside the sovereignty invariants. And it addresses something the doorway does not: the doorway names *what
the member brings*; the stance names *what they want done with it*. Those are different, and conflating them
is a real design gap.

It also matches the deeper principle already ruled: **the space between authentication and MAIA should not be
profile completion — it should be the beginning of relationship.**

### The tension it must answer before it ships

**R1's safeguard is explicit: one tap, then MAIA starts — never a questionnaire.** The stance field adds a
second choice before value, against a target of under sixty seconds. Two taps is not a questionnaire, but the
mechanism that produced the ten-lens screen was exactly this: each addition defensible on its own.

**The question for MLX-05 is therefore not "is this good?" but "does it earn a second gate?"** Possible
resolutions, none chosen:

- **Optional and skippable** — present but never blocking; MAIA opens if ignored.
- **Offered by MAIA in-turn** rather than as a screen — *"do you want me to just listen, or push back?"* — which
  keeps the threshold at one tap and makes the stance part of the relationship instead of the form.
- **Returning members only** — a first arrival gets one tap; someone with a world may want to steer.
- **Not at all** — if the cohort shows the single doorway already produces specific enough openings.

The second option is the one I would explore first: it satisfies the intent without spending a gate.

### What the cohort can already tell us

MLX-04 was not designed to test this, but three of its instruments bear on it:

| Instrument | What it would indicate |
|---|---|
| **Q2 — what do you think MAIA is?** | if answers cluster on "it listens" or "it advises", the stance may already be over-determined by the doorway |
| **Q8 — different from ChatGPT?** | if people describe MAIA as steerable without being told, the field is redundant |
| **Doorway hesitation timings** | if the single doorway already produces hesitation, a second gate is contraindicated on cost alone |

**Do not add questions to the protocol to test this.** Read what the existing instrument returns.

## C-02 · The signin → MAIA threshold in the live product

**Observation (Kelly, 2026-08-25), about the deployed product rather than the prototype.**

The live sign-in surface goes from credentials directly into an atmospheric MAIA welcome. The MLX work
inserts a **meaningful threshold** between those two states, so MAIA's first encounter can be specific rather
than generic. This is not a new proposal — it is the current product's version of the gap MLX-07 exists to
close — but it is worth holding as a named before/after for that build, and as a reference point when the
implementation lands.

**Disposition:** carry into MLX-07 as context. No action while the cohort runs.
