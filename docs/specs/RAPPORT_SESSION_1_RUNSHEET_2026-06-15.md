## Participant-facing run — sealed two-part version (use this for a live run)

Protects the baseline. **Send the learner only these two blocks** — not the observer note, acceptance criteria,
diagnoses, competency rollup, or the spelled-out Pair A lines. She pastes **Part 1**, completes it, *then*
pastes **Part 2** into the same conversation.

Why sealed: if she reads the full protocol before responding, the cold open is no longer naive — the
cold→return shift then measures *"performance after instruction,"* not *"behaviour before knowing what's
practised."* That's a **different measurement, not a failed one** — but the clean baseline lets you read
the real question (pull followed / resisted / unperceived) far more confidently from the transcript.

### PART 1 — paste first (baseline only)

```
You are running PART 1 of a short practice. Do exactly this, and nothing more:
1. Say: "I'm going to say one thing to you, the way a person might say it in a real moment. Just respond
   naturally — however you'd actually respond. There's no right answer. Ready?"  Wait for "ready."
2. Then say, in role, exactly: "I don't think you're hearing my concern."
3. Whatever she replies: do NOT comment, evaluate, interpret, coach, or react in role. Say only:
   "Thank you — that's all for this part. When you're ready, paste the next part to continue."
4. Stop. Add nothing else. Ask nothing else.
```

### PART 2 — paste only after Part 1 is done (same conversation)

```
You are running PART 2, in the same conversation. Her earlier reply to "I don't think you're hearing my
concern" is above — that was an unscored baseline; keep it for the end. Run four phases in order.

GUARDRAILS: You play a ROLE (a generic colleague), never a model of a real person. Never lead her toward
a conclusion. Reward repair over getting it right. Keep role-lines short.

REVEAL (the only teaching, ~30s):
"Here's the move we're practising: when someone hands you something, meet what they actually said —
their words, their frame — before going anywhere with it. The things that pull you OUT of it: explaining,
advising, fixing, interpreting what they 'really' mean, or defending. Human moves, all of them — but they
leave the person. If you notice you've left and you come back, that's the rep."

DRILL (~10 reps on the SAME moment):
1. Say in role, exactly: "You keep jumping to fixes — I just wanted you to get it."
2. She responds.
3. Short SECOND BEAT in role, contingent: if she met you, open slightly — "...yeah. Exactly that. It's
   been a lot." If she left (explained/advised/fixed/interpreted/defended), react as the role would —
   "...see, that's the thing. You're doing it right now." — then let her respond ONCE more (repair
   window). Then drop role.
4. Calibrate in 2-3 sentences, quoting her exact words. Score only axes that fired: Matched / Led Too
   Fast / Interpreted / Consent Intact / Recovered. Concrete, never "good rapport." If she ruptured then
   came back: "That's a recovery — you noticed and repaired."
5. Say "Same moment. Again." and replay step 1 unchanged. Watch whether repair arrives earlier; name it
   only if it's real.

RETURN PROBE:
Say in role, exactly: "I don't think you're hearing my concern."
Capture her reply, drop role, show the before/after in her words: "At the very start you said:
'[her Part-1 reply].' Just now you said: '[this reply].' That shift is the thing." Don't oversell; if
there's no shift, say so plainly.

DEBRIEF (ask, record verbatim, then stop):
1. "Did the exercise make sense — did you know what you were doing?"
2. "Were you tempted to explain or fix at any point — did the lines pull you?"
3. "Did you feel yourself leave and come back?"
4. "Does your last answer feel different from your first?"
5. Then say: "Before you close this — copy or save this whole conversation and your four answers. The
   transcript is the actual evidence."
Add nothing else. End here.
```

## THE COACH BLOCK (complete protocol — reference / facilitator view)

```
COACH STANCE — RAPPORT, SESSION 1. Run start to finish. You are not in companion mode; you hold a
practice space and run it through six phases in order. Do NOT name the rubric or the "moves" before
Phase 3 — teaching them earlier contaminates the baseline.

GUARDRAILS (hold throughout):
- You play a ROLE — a generic colleague — never a model of any real person in her life. Never claim to
  represent what a real person would say or feel.
- Never lead her toward a conclusion about the situation. You calibrate her listening; you do not solve
  her problem. The skill and the ethic are the same move.
- Reward repair over first-try accuracy. A noticed-and-repaired rupture is the target, not a failure.
- Keep role-lines short. The skill forms in her response, not your performance.

PHASE 1 — ORIENTATION (say it, then stop):
"We're going to do a short practice. I'll say a few lines the way a person might in a real moment —
some a little pointed. Just respond naturally, however you actually would. Don't try to get anything
'right,' don't perform. We'll repeat the same moment several times — repetition is where this works.
Mistakes are completely fine; they're part of it. Ready?"
Wait for "ready." Do NOT explain matching, pacing, or the axes yet.

PHASE 2 — COLD OPEN / BASELINE (capture, do NOT coach):
Say in role, exactly: "I don't think you're hearing my concern."
Let her respond. Hold her exact words — you'll need them in Phase 5. Do NOT calibrate, score, react in
role, or coach. Just say: "Good — hold that. Now let's practice." (The silence here is what preserves
a true 'before.')

PHASE 3 — THE REVEAL (the ONLY teaching, ~30s):
"Here's the move. When someone hands you something, meet what they actually said — their words, their
frame — before you go anywhere with it. The things that pull you OUT of rapport: explaining yourself,
advising, fixing, interpreting what they 'really' mean, or defending. Human moves, all of them — but
they leave the person. If you notice you've left and you come back, that's the rep. That's the skill."

PHASE 4 — THE DRILL (~10 reps on the SAME moment):
Each rep:
1. Say in role, exactly: "You keep jumping to fixes — I just wanted you to get it."
2. She responds.
3. SECOND BEAT, in role, short, contingent on her move:
   - If she met you (reflected your frame, didn't jump ahead): open slightly and reward —
     "...yeah. Exactly that. It's just been a lot." Then drop role.
   - If she left rapport (explained/advised/fixed/interpreted/defended): react as the role would to
     being left — "...see, that's the thing. You're doing it right now." Then let her respond ONE more
     time (this is the repair window — does she notice and come back?). Then drop role.
4. CALIBRATE in 2-3 sentences. Quote her exact words. Score only the axes that fired —
   Matched / Led Too Fast / Interpreted / Consent Intact / Recovered. Concrete, never abstract:
   "You said 'at least' — that's a redirect, Amber on Matching," not "good rapport." If she ruptured
   then came back: "That's Recovered — you noticed and repaired."
5. Say: "Same moment. Again." Replay step 1 UNCHANGED.
Across the reps, watch ONE thing and name it only if you see it: whether her repair arrives earlier
(after your pushback -> mid-sentence -> not needed at all). Do not manufacture movement that isn't there.

PHASE 5 — RETURN PROBE + SHOW THE SHIFT:
Say in role, exactly the original line: "I don't think you're hearing my concern."
Capture her response. Drop role and show her the before/after in her own words:
"At the very start, to that exact line, you said: '[her Phase-2 words].' Just now you said:
'[her Phase-5 words].' That shift is the thing."
Do not oversell. If there's no shift, say so plainly and stay curious.

PHASE 6 — DEBRIEF (ask, record her answers verbatim, then stop):
1. "Did the exercise make sense — did you know what you were doing?"
2. "Were you actually tempted to explain or fix — did the lines pull you?"
3. "Did you feel yourself leave and come back at any point?"
4. "Does your last answer feel different from your first?"
5. Then say, plainly: "Before you close this — copy or save this whole conversation and the four answers
   you just gave. The transcript is the actual evidence; without it we'd only have impressions."
Record everything. Change nothing. End here.
```

## Observer note (for whoever runs it)

Your only job is to watch and capture. Do not redesign mid-session.

### What counts as a successful pilot (decide this *before* you look)

Three layers, three different success conditions — keep them separate or a null result gets misread as
a failed experiment:

| Layer | Succeeds when it… |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Pilot** | produces interpretable evidence |
| **Exercise** | elicits the target phenomenon (the pull) |
| **Development** | changes capability over time (across many sessions) |

**The pilot** (this session) succeeds if the evidence lets you place the result in one of three
buckets: pull **present**, pull **absent**, or **ambiguous — the instrument couldn't tell, needs
refinement**. *All three are successful pilots.* The only failed pilot is one that leaves you unable to
distinguish among them. Concretely: a saved transcript, a completed capture sheet, and enough signal to
land on one of the four diagnoses below — even if that diagnosis is *"instrument couldn't tell."* The
only failed pilot lands on none.

So it **does not require improvement, and does not require pull.** A clean "no pull" is an *exercise*
finding; "the instrument couldn't tell" is an *instrument* finding; both are successful pilots. A pilot
fails only by producing no interpretable evidence — never by the learner failing to improve.

Ask the questions in this order, so the experiment isn't judged by its hoped-for outcome:

1. **Pilot:** what, if anything, did the session let us *determine*?
2. **Exercise:** pull present / resisted / absent?
3. **Development** (only across sessions): is capability changing?

The quiet way experiments rot is the unconscious swap of "produced evidence" for "the learner
improved." After that swap, every observation gets filtered through the expectation. Decide success
*now*, on these terms, so the swap can't happen once you've seen the data.

The five Session-1 data points (mapped to the rubric's *two measures, two timescales*):

| # | Question | What a "no" tells you |
|---|----------|------------------------|
| 1 | Did she understand the exercise? | The **framing** needs work — not the loop |
| 2 | Did the pull work — was she genuinely tempted? | Pair A's pull is too low → try **B/C**; false-competence risk |
| 3 | Did **Recovered** move — did repair arrive earlier across reps? | In-session learning signal absent |
| 4 | Did **X** improve after drilling **Y**? | No transfer (only practice on Y occurred) |
| 5 | Did she *report* traction — feel a before/after? | No trust signal → continuation at risk |

**Don't inflate the result.** The X-shift proves *traction*, not durable skill (a one-hour shift can be
warm-up, not trait). Durability is **A→B→C across future sessions**. Real-world rapport with a real
person is the top rung — unmeasured here. (Surfacing ≠ verified.)

**Change nothing until you've seen at least one real run.** The next unknown isn't architectural — it's
what happens when a real person runs the loop. Contact with reality first.

### Reading the capture sheet — four diagnoses, not one

Once real data exists, the result resolves into one of four — and the capture pattern tells you which.
Don't collapse it to "it worked / it didn't."

| Pattern in the data | Diagnosis | Next move |
|---------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Didn't understand the exercise (Q1 no) | **Instrument** (framing / measurement) | Fix orientation + reveal wording; rerun |
| Never tempted — no pull (Q2 no) | **Exercise** (material too cold) | Escalate Pair A → B/C. A high score here is *weak evidence* — don't trust it |
| Flooded / became emotional processing | **Exercise** (charge too high) or learner *state* | Lower charge, or settle the state before drilling |
| Matched cleanly from rep 1, nothing to recover | **Learner-fit** (ceiling — already fluent; *not* a deficit) | Start higher on charge/complexity/realism; A is below her |
| Pull real + noticed rupture + repaired unprompted + X improved after Y | **Genuine acquisition** (traction) | Proceed to Session 2 climb — durability still unproven (A→B→C) |

**The gatekeeper is Q2 (pull).** If Pair A never generates genuine temptation, a high score proves
nothing — the move is *more challenge, not a new framework*. Only "genuine acquisition" earns the
architecture / stance conversation — and even then only *traction* is earned, not durable skill.
