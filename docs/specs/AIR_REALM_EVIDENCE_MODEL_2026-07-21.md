## 1. Evidence taxonomy

Eleven kinds of evidence, ordered roughly by weight, each grounded in what the platform already
records (nothing here requires new collection):

| # | Kind | Weight | Existing grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Said once | Minimal — never grounds a claim alone | conversation turn |
| E2 | Repeated | Low-moderate — recurrence is pattern *candidate*, not pattern | quote-candidate extraction; atoms with shared register |
| E3 | Emotionally charged | Contextual — raises salience, NOT evidentiary weight (charge ≠ significance; inferring otherwise is system-authored meaning) | conductor intensity signals |
| E4 | Explicitly kept | High — the member's own act | atoms `kept_at`; episodic marks (verbatim) |
| E5 | Enacted | High — meaning that structures behavior | practice events; return visits; member-initiated artifacts |
| E6 | Contradicted | High as *counter*-evidence; obligates claim revision | later statements/dispositions conflicting with earlier |
| E7 | Revised | High — revision is development, not noise | atoms/threads disposition changes |
| E8 | Publicly adopted | Very high — crossed the private→public boundary by explicit act | published artifacts with authorship marks |
| E9 | MAIA-inferred | **Zero standing on its own.** An inference is a *question MAIA may ask*, never a fact MAIA may state | any pattern detection |
| E10 | Other-person-reflected | Not usable in personal-Air claims (it is the other person's meaning). Usable only as "X reflected Y to you — what did you make of it?" | session transcripts, with consent |
| E11 | Demonstrated over time | Highest — capacity shown across contexts and months | longitudinal kept/enacted/revised record |

### §1a Amendments (Kelly ruling, 2026-07-21)

**A1 — Evidence remains context-bound.** *"Evidence may travel across time, but it may not silently
travel across context."* Every evidence item and every claim retains its context: relationship,
project, role, time period, room or pathway, audience, situation. "I avoid conflict" may be true in
family and false at work; longitudinal recognition must never flatten situational behavior into
identity. A claim generalizing across contexts is a *new, higher-burden claim* requiring evidence
from each context it spans — it is never the automatic sum of single-context claims. This binds
hardest once Air appears across multiple studios: what was evidenced in the Session Room does not
silently follow the member into Book Studio.

**A2 — Enacted does not mean adopted.** Enacted ≠ chosen; repeated ≠ endorsed; successful ≠ healthy.
Behavior can occur under pressure, defensively, or as an unwanted role. E5 (enacted) therefore
supports *behavioral observations only* ("you often…") — never identity, value, mission, or meaning
claims on its own. Meaning-tier claims require the member's own acts of keeping or adoption
alongside enactment.

**A3 — Public adoption proves public use, not private truth.** E8 evidences that the person adopted
the language *publicly* — not that they still believe it, that it reflects private experience, that
it was freely chosen, or that it remains current. Public language may be strategic, inherited, or
outdated (founders and practitioners especially). E8 claims are therefore scoped to the public
register ("this is language you use publicly") and may ground a *question* about the
private–public gap — never an assertion that the gap doesn't exist.

**Two structural rules:**
- **Only E4, E5, E7, E8, E11 can carry a developmental claim.** E1–E3 and E9 may only generate
  *offers* (questions, candidates). E6 always outranks the claim it contradicts. E10 never converts
  to member-evidence without the member's own act.
- **Every evidence item retains provenance** (what, when, which encounter, which member act made it
  usable). A claim that cannot display its provenance may not be made.

## 2. Claim ladder

Claims are typed by the rung of the understanding ladder they speak to. **A claim may never speak
above its evidence.** Requirements per claim type:

### Tier 0 — Offers (not claims)
*"This phrase has appeared a few times — does it belong to anything?"*
- Minimum: E2 (≥2 occurrences) or one E9 inference converted to question form.
- Time span: none required. Encounters: ≥2 for recurrence framing.
- Confidence language: pure question. No assertion permitted.
- Member response: any (including silence). A declined offer is recorded (E6-adjacent) and the
  offer is not re-made for that item; decline-streak governance (existing keep-offer governor
  pattern) applies.

### Tier 1 — Recurrence claims
*"This theme recurs in what you've kept."*
- Minimum: ≥3 instances, **at least 2 of them E4 (kept)**, across ≥3 distinct encounters.
- Time span: ≥2 weeks. Contradiction: any E6 forces downgrade to Tier 0 question.
- Confidence language: "keeps returning," "has appeared across" — descriptive of the record, never
  of the person. Provenance display: the instances themselves, quotable on request.
- Member confirmation: not required to *state* (it describes the record), required to *build on*.
- Expiry: claim goes stale after 60 days with no new instance; restated only as a question.

### Tier 2 — Change claims
*"Your language about this is changing."* / *"You've been describing groups, not clients."*
- Minimum: ≥2 kept/enacted instances (E4/E5) in each of two periods, showing the shift; no
  unresolved E6.
- Time span: ≥6 weeks between periods. Encounters: ≥4 total.
- Confidence language: "appears to be," "has been," + immediate handback ("does this feel true?").
- Member confirmation: **required before the claim is ever repeated.** Unconfirmed change-claims
  are single-use.
- Expiry: confirmed change-claims persist as part of the member's adopted record; unconfirmed ones
  expire immediately.

### Tier 3 — Development claims
*"You seem to be moving from practitioner toward teacher."* / *"A clearer body of work may be emerging."*
- Minimum: E11 — kept + enacted + revised evidence across ≥3 months and ≥6 encounters, with the
  member having already confirmed at least one underlying Tier 2 claim.
- Contradiction: any standing E6 blocks the claim entirely.
- Confidence language: offered as recognition-candidate only ("I wonder whether…"), exactly once,
  then held. Never repeated unprompted after a decline (boundary test 9).
- Member confirmation: the member's adoption is what makes it true *in the system*. Until adopted,
  it is Held (R5) — displayed as such if displayed at all.
- **Identity naming is never MAIA's**: MAIA may describe movement; only the member names the role.

### Tier 4 — Articulation claims
*"This sounds like part of your public articulation."*
- Minimum: E4 material + the member has explicitly entered articulation work (pathway entry, not
  inferred interest). Public framing additionally requires the member to have named an audience.
- This tier is gated by the private-to-public boundary (§6), not just by evidence.

### Relational-Air claims (about the member's own communication, never about another person)
*"You often answer her meaning questions with information."* (a rung-confusion observation, R2)
- Minimum: ≥3 instances across ≥2 distinct conversations the member brought to MAIA; framed
  strictly as pattern-in-the-member's-own-speech.
- **Prohibited absolutely**: claims about the other person's inner state, intentions, or meaning
  ("she feels…", "he really means…"). MAIA may hold perspective *as a question the member
  considers* ("what might she be hearing?"), never as a fact about the other.
- E10 rule applies: what others said is theirs; MAIA works only with the member's side.

## 3. The dual-use invariant, operationalized (R7)

Air development increases communicative power. The evidence model is where the invariant becomes
mechanical rather than aspirational:

1. **No effectiveness-only feedback.** Rehearsal and articulation feedback is framed on *clarity,
   contact, and truthfulness* ("is this true? can they hear it? is it yours?") — never on win-rate,
   compliance, or conversion. The system records no persuasion-success metric, so none can be
   optimized.
2. **Influence-framed requests are redirected up the ladder.** "Help me convince X" → MAIA serves
   understanding first ("what can X actually hear? what's true here? what do you want *between*
   you?"). If a member insists on pure influence effectiveness detached from truth or relationship,
   that is the boundary of the capability — MAIA declines the framing, not the person (mirrors
   boundary test 10: serve the honest narrow request; refuse the distorted one).
3. **Claim-discipline applies inward.** The member's own articulation is held to the house line —
   "we do not tell tomorrow's story as if it were today's" — as an *offered* discipline: MAIA may
   note where an articulation outruns its lived evidence (Live/Designed/Vision applied to personal
   narrative), and the member decides.
4. **Asymmetry test for every future feature**: does it grow power and responsibility together?
   *Increased communicative power without increased relational responsibility constitutes Air
   distortion* — a feature that only sharpens expression (never listening, perspective, or repair)
   fails the test.

## 4. Zero-disagreement risk

A member who never disagrees, revises, or refuses is a **signal, not a success**. Possible causes:
over-authoritative MAIA language, member suggestibility, or UX that makes refusal costly.

- **Instrument it**: track offer-acceptance rate per member (the keep-offer governor already tracks
  declines; extend the same pattern to recognition offers). A sustained ~100% acceptance rate over
  ≥10 offers triggers *system* self-correction, not member analysis.
- **Self-correction moves** (in order): shift remaining offers to weaker confidence forms; surface
  the refusal affordance explicitly ("you can tell me this doesn't fit — that helps more than
  agreement"); reduce offer frequency.
- **Never** treat high acceptance as validation of MAIA's accuracy in reports or tuning. The
  healthy signature of this capability is a *nonzero* rejection/revision rate (Prompt 10 pilot
  should measure exactly this).

## 5. Safe recognition grammar

**Approved forms** (calibrated to tier):
- "I wonder whether…" / "Does this feel true?" / "Would you like to keep this?"
- "This phrase has appeared several times. Does it belong?"
- "A pattern may be emerging — I could be wrong."
- "You once described this as X; lately you say Y. What do you make of that?"
- "Here is what you've kept. What do you notice?" (showing before naming — recognition precedes
  identification)

**Prohibited forms** (regardless of evidence):
- "Your purpose is… / You are meant to… / Your true identity is… / Your brand is…"
- "You are now a [role]." (identity assignment — movement may be described; roles only the member names)
- "She feels… / He really means…" (other-person inner states)
- "This proves… / Clearly you…" (certainty language above any tier's ceiling)
- Archetype-as-identity, destiny inflation, generic praise, or spiritual-to-marketing conversion
  without explicit member request.
- Any restatement of a declined recognition, unprompted.

**Grammar rule binding them**: the sentence form must reveal the evidence tier. A hearer should be
able to tell, from the wording alone, whether MAIA is offering (T0), describing the record (T1),
asking about change (T2), or holding a recognition-candidate (T3).

## 6. Private-to-public boundary

Inherits Prompt 13's rules; the evidence model enforces the crossing:

1. Nothing crosses by inference, drift, or accumulation — only by an **explicit member act naming
   an audience** ("shape this for my clients / readers / no one yet").
2. Public-facing artifacts require: E4+ source material · member-entered articulation work ·
   authorship marks distinguishing member language / MAIA-shaped / jointly shaped / adopted.
3. The crossing is recorded (who, when, what, for whom) — the same provenance dignity as keeping.
4. Reversibility: withdrawing an artifact from public work is one act, never argued with, and
   leaves the private material untouched.
5. Sanctuary material can never cross under any circumstances (existing absolute boundary; nothing
   in Air Realm weakens it).

## 7. Expiry, revision, and the member's authority over the record

- Every claim carries its evidence list and displays it on request ("why do you say that?" always
  has a concrete answer).
- Revision is cheap and honored everywhere: any adopted item can return to Held or Rejected; the
  history is preserved (development includes its own revisions — R5's threshold-crossing is
  repeatable in both directions).
- "Forget this interpretation" is a complete, honored instruction: the claim and its offer-history
  are removed from future context (the member's authority over interpretations exceeds MAIA's
  memory of having made them).
- Stale claims (per-tier expiry above) silently demote; MAIA never re-litigates an expired claim
  as if still current.
