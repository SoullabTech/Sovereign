## The canonical core

The deeper invariant is one line:

> **The system has no authority over the meaning of a member's experience.**

Memory is only where it first became visible. The same prohibition appears in every domain — which is why the framing is stable: it never has to know *what meaning is*, only what the system is **forbidden from doing**.

| Domain | The prohibition |
|---|---|
| Sanctuary | no authority to **retain** |
| Remember | no authority to determine **significance** |
| Threshold | no authority to interpret **identity** |
| Authority Return | no authority to define **the person** |
| Cultural representation | no authority to define **a tradition** |

In its smallest holdable form — which, by the principle's own exercisability test, is the form that should *be* canonical — it is five lines:

```
The system may witness.
The system may remember.
The system may not determine significance.

Do not keep this.
Do not flatten this.
```

**Everything below this line is explication.** If those five lines survive, the constitution survives; a member, a reviewer, or a steward who carries only those has carried the whole of it.

## Why this rises to canon

It explains, under one principle, things that otherwise look unrelated: **Sanctuary · Release · Remember · object permanence · ADHD accommodation · sovereignty · witnessed continuity · the Leaving Test · Cultural Sovereignty.** When a single principle accounts for the cognitive, the ethical, the architectural, and the cultural at once, it is structural, not merely philosophical.

## The two accumulations, the two refusals

Systems accumulate power in exactly two ways: they **keep**, and they **flatten** (treat what they keep as undifferentiated background). The member's two sovereign acts are the precise inverse — not an arbitrary pair:

```
Do not keep this.        (refuse retention   → Release)
Do not flatten this.     (refuse flattening  → Remember)
```

Those two lines are likely the only form anyone will actually remember — and that is not incidental. By the principle's own **Exercisability** requirement (below), a principle too elaborate to be held is, at the meta-level, a hidden refusal: a constitution its people cannot carry governs in name, not in practice. **So the compression is not a tagline; it is the canon-compliant statement of the principle**, with the structured form beneath it as explication. If this is ratified, those two lines should arguably *be* the canonical core.

**It explains existing doctrine rather than competing with it** — the signature of a foundational, not additive, principle. Each prior discovery is one surface of the same constraint:

| Surface | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Sanctuary | right to refuse **retention** |
| Remember | right to refuse **flattening** |
| Threshold Principle (Inv 14) | right to refuse **system authorship** |
| Authority Return | right to refuse **external determination** |
| Cultural representation | right to refuse **misrepresentation** |

Different surfaces; same structure.

**Why it scales — the principle is modest.** It does **not** claim the member determines truth, authors meaning, or that every interpretation is equally valid. It draws one narrow line — *the system may not cross this* — and stops. Positive authorities must continually expand (forever enumerating what is permitted); negative rights stay stable (they name only the few lines that may not be crossed). That stability is what keeps the constitution small enough to be held — the Exercisability requirement turned on the constitution itself.

## The Invariant

**Rule.** The system possesses no authority to determine the meaning of a member's experience. The member's primary power is not authorship over meaning but **refusal of system authorship**.

**The two defaults, the two powers.** By default the system (1) **retains**, and (2) **flattens** — treats what it retains as equal background continuity. The member therefore has exactly two powers of refusal:

| System default | Member refusal | Gesture |
|---|---|---|
| Retain | *"Do not keep this."* | **Release** |
| Flatten | *"This is not equal to everything else."* | **Remember** |
| *(neither exercised)* | — | **Witness** |

Witness is the un-exercised middle: continuity forms, no refusal invoked. Everything else follows.

**Operational form.**
- The system may **witness**. The system may **remember**. The system may **not determine significance**.
- ***Atom formation is continuity; significance is sovereignty.*** Continuity may be automatic; significance may not.
- A right that cannot be easily located and exercised is not a meaningful right. Powers of refusal must therefore remain **visible, accessible, and friction-competitive with acceptance.**

**Prohibited patterns.**
- The system **declaring significance** — "this was important," "this was a breakthrough," "this was a synchronicity," "you're becoming X." Significance is the member's.
- The system surfacing **inferred** significance — echoing back what it inferred rather than what the member marked.
- **Manufacturing consent** — collecting a "yes" through nudges or defaults. You can manufacture a yes; you cannot manufacture a no. Consent here is the honored *no*, never the engineered *yes*.
- **Decorative refusal** — the dark-pattern asymmetry where acceptance is one step and refusal is buried (accept: one click; refuse: six). A refusal that is formally available but practically unreachable is a violated right.
- **Enforcing meaning without representation** at the collective scale — a machine imposing "correct" meaning by rule, with no community behind it, is the system authoring meaning under the guise of protection.

**Litmus.**
- *Significance:* take any line the system says — is it a statement of what the member *did* ("you marked this") or of what *is true of them* ("this matters")? The second is authorship — cut it.
- *Friction:* for the most-promoted acceptance path (Continue / keep), each refusal (Release, Remember) must be reachable in **no more steps**. If acceptance is easier than refusal, the constitution is decorative.
- *Visibility:* a power of refusal that a scattered, depleted, or distracted member cannot find in the moment of need does not exist.

**Principle.** *Consent, in this architecture, is the power to refuse authorship by the system* — a **negative right** (a shield), not a positive grant. Negative rights are the only kind that distribute without concentrating: they need no consensus, no representative body, no central authority. They need only someone to say *"not that,"* and the system to stop.

**Relation.**
- **Underlies** Invariants 6 (Mirror Integrity), 11 (Declared Significance), 14 (Threshold / Remembering ≠ Defining). Inv 14's *"remembers what was placed, never decides who the person is"* is this principle at the moment of arrival.
- **Grounds** Sanctuary (the proof that refusal-of-retention exists), the three-gesture model (*Let go / Witness / Keep* = two refusals and a rest), witnessed continuity (remember without authoring), and the Leaving Test (a system that sheds authority returns people to their lives).
- **Scales to Cultural Sovereignty** by distributing *refusal*, never *authority*: no one speaks **for** a tradition; anyone within it may speak **against** a misrepresentation. The collective power is the same negative right, exercised as **distributed veto**. Caution: at the collective scale, enforcement without representation re-creates the authoring it forbids — *Represented* must arrive **with** *Enforceable*, not after.

## Object permanence as a governance requirement (the convergence)

This principle absorbs the Portal's founding design constraint. *Hidden = nonexistent* began as an ADHD accommodation — **"a tool I can't find doesn't exist."** It returns here as governance — **"a refusal I can't find doesn't exist."** Same statement, two levels. The architecture collapses them into one constraint:

> **Important powers must remain visible.**

Which is why the cognitive accommodation and the constitutional requirement are not two design goals but one. (This is also why the *friction-competitive* clause is load-bearing, not decorative: a right that exists formally but not practically has been silently revoked.)

## Exercisability — a co-equal requirement (governance, not UX)

The clause *a right that cannot be easily located and exercised is not a meaningful right* reads at first like a UX preference. It is not — it is a **governance** requirement, and it may matter as much as the negative-rights boundary itself. History is full of systems that **technically allow refusal while practically obstructing it**; a right granted in language and buried in practice has been revoked without being repealed. The object-permanence convergence above is exactly this failure mode at two scales — *hidden = nonexistent* for the member, *hidden refusal = nonexistent refusal* for governance.

**So exercisability is enforceable, not aspirational.** Litmus: each refusal must be reachable in no more steps than the most-promoted acceptance path. *Accept: one click / Refuse: six* is not a smaller right — it is a violated one. The two requirements travel together: **the boundary says what the system may not do; exercisability keeps the member's power to enforce that boundary at hand.** One without the other is decorative.

## From belief to behavior (why #446 matters)

The principle leaves the realm of values and enters the realm of consequences at **enforcement**:

- Before #446: *"the member may refuse retention."* — a **belief**.
- After #446 + its runtime gate: *"the system must stop retaining."* — a **behavior**.

The Negative Rights Principle is canon-candidate as *language*; it becomes real only where code makes the refusal true. That is the maturity ladder — **Declared → Reviewable → Enforceable → Represented** — applied to the principle itself. (Individual domains can rest at Enforceable; collective/cultural domains must reach Represented, or enforcement becomes a new authorship.)

## Boundary vs. mechanism (per the Threshold-Principle precedent)

Canon governs **boundaries**, not mechanisms. The boundary here — *the system has no authority over meaning; the member's power is refusal; refusal must be exercisable* — is the canon candidate. The specific **mechanisms** (the Evening Portal gestures, the equal-reachability of Remember / Release / Continue, the atoms `is_breakthrough` flag, the Sanctuary non-persistence path) are **design doctrine** that may honor the boundary by other means. Every surface must honor the boundary; none is required to honor it the same way.
