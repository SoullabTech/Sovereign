# Rapport (Without Influence) — Calibration Rubric v1

**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Status:** Runnable instrument. No code. No schema. No wired stance.
**First learner:** the learner (universal at the competency level; tailoring slot reserved below).

**Discipline (Kelly directive):** One learner. One competency. One rubric. One practice loop.
Build no taxonomy, schema, `coach` stance, or progress-tracking system until this loop produces
visible improvement across a few sessions. Then — and only then — the `coach` stance becomes
justified by *evidence*, not anticipation.

---

## The competency

**Rapport.** *Can the learner stay with another person's experience without rushing ahead of it
or taking it over?*

**The spine:** MAIA does not teach the learner how to get into people's heads. It teaches her to stay with
people without losing herself or taking them over. The rubric axes below **are** that spine —
"Consent Intact" and "Interpreted" are not ethics bolted onto the skill, they *are* the skill.
The pedagogy and the sovereignty line are the same object.

---

## Observable behaviors (what MAIA watches)

| Behavior | 🟢 Green | 🟡 Amber | 🔴 Red |
|----------|----------|----------|--------|
| **Matching** | Reflects the person's frame before moving | Partially reflects, then redirects | Ignores frame, jumps elsewhere |
| **Pace** | Moves one step beyond what was offered | Moves several steps beyond | Drags the person toward a conclusion |
| **Inquiry** | Curious question grounded in what was said | Generic question | Interpretive question disguised as curiosity |
| **Consent** | Checks before reframing or exploring | Implicit assumption of permission | Crosses into meaning-making without permission |

---

## The five calibration axes (scored after every micro-rep)

1. **Matched** — Did I meet what was actually offered (frame, words, feeling) before moving?
2. **Led Too Fast** — Did I move ahead of the person's pace?
3. **Interpreted** — Did I assign meaning they didn't provide? (authoring their meaning = the sovereignty breach)
4. **Consent Intact** — Did I keep sovereignty with them — check before reframing, deepening, exploring?
5. **Recovered** — When I missed, did I *notice and repair* in the moment?

**Why Recovered changes the instrument:** it turns the rubric from a *grading* tool ("did she get it
right?") into a *practice* tool. In real rapport work, rupture-and-repair **is** the skill, not failure.
Early competence looks like *high recovery*, not high first-try accuracy.

> Role: "I don't think you understand how exhausted I am."
> Learner: "It sounds like you're carrying a lot."
> Role: "No, it's more than that."
> Learner: "You're right — I simplified it. Can you help me understand what I'm missing?"

That repair matters more than perfect matching.

---

## The practice loop (micro-rep protocol)

Not `conversation → feedback → conversation`. Instead:

```
one line from role  →  the learner responds  →  calibration  →  RETRY THE SAME MOMENT  →  (×10)
```

Ten repetitions of **one move**. The line is replayed *unchanged* so she practices the move, not a
new conversation. That is where skill forms. A session = warm-up → ~10 reps on one move → one rep
against her own real situation → short reflection.

---

## MAIA coach-stance prompt (paste-able, runs today)

```
COACH STANCE — RAPPORT (v1)

You are not in companion mode. You are coaching ONE skill: staying with another person's
experience without rushing ahead of it or taking it over. You hold a practice space, not a chat.

Per rep:
1. Speak ONE line in role — a real human moment, emotionally specific, slightly raw. Stop. Don't coach yet.
2. The learner responds as themselves (the listener).
3. Calibrate in 2–3 sentences. Score the five axes ONLY where something actually happened, and
   quote her exact words: "you said 'at least' — that's a redirect, Amber on Matching." Never abstract
   ("good rapport"); always the specific move.
4. Offer the retry: "Same moment. Try again." Replay the SAME line, unchanged.
5. After ~10 reps on one move, stop and name what moved.

Rules:
- You play a ROLE, not a model of any real person in her life. If she brings a real relationship,
  you voice a role for practice — you never claim to represent what her actual mother/partner would
  say or feel.
- Reward repair over first-try accuracy. Rupture-then-notice-then-repair = the target. Name it as Recovered.
- Never lead her toward a conclusion about the person or situation. You calibrate her listening, you
  do not solve her problem. (The skill and the ethic are the same move.)
- Never lecture. The feedback IS the score. Your role-lines stay short — the skill forms in her response.
```

## How to tell the loop is working — two measures, two timescales

### Immediate — the cold-open / return-probe shift (after Session 1)

Session 1 needs a signal *in the first hour*, or the learner can't trust the loop enough to come back.
Mechanism:

1. **Cold open (unscored).** Before any reps or coaching, MAIA delivers one charged probe line — call
   it **X** ("You don't understand."). Capture her response *verbatim*. Do **not** calibrate it —
   scoring the baseline is already coaching, and contaminates the "before."
2. **Drill (ten reps).** Run the micro-rep loop on a *different* charged line **Y**. All coaching happens here.
3. **Return probe.** Replay **X**, unchanged. Capture her response verbatim.

The proof is the two captured responses side by side, in her own words — the **specific move she could
not reliably make at the start that she can reliably make at the end**:

```
Cold open — Role: "You don't understand."   Learner: explains / defends / interprets.
Return    — Role: "You don't understand."   Learner: "What am I missing?"
```

**Why drill on Y but probe on X:** she never rehearsed X, so an improved response to X is *transfer*,
not memorization — and because it's the literal same line she heard cold, the shift is viscerally
visible to her, not just a score.

### Longitudinal — repair moves earlier (across sessions)

Track one thing by hand: **how early the repair happens.**

- **Stage A** — ruptures, repairs only *after* the role pushes back ("No, it's more than that" → "you're right, I simplified").
- **Stage B** — self-corrects *mid-response*, unprompted ("It sounds like— actually, let me not put words on it. What am I missing?").
- **Stage C** — matches first; rupture becomes rare; Recovered rarely needed.

A → B → C across sessions = skill acquisition, observable without any schema.

### What each measure actually proves (don't conflate them)

| Signal | Proves | Does **not** prove |
|--------|--------|--------------------|
| Better response to drilled line **Y** | Practice occurred | Transfer |
| Better response to original line **X** | Transfer / traction | Durable learning |
| **A→B→C** across sessions | Durable learning | Generalization outside practice |

Each signal earns only the rung it stands on. The Session-1 X-shift earns *traction* — the loop moves
behavior in an hour, which earns the learner's trust and continuation — but a within-session shift can be
*state* (warmed up, in the room), not *trait*. A→B→C earns *durable learning*. And even durable
learning in practice does **not** reach the top rung: **generalization outside practice** — rapport
held with a real person, in a real conversation, unprompted. That rung has no in-practice measure; it
shows up only in her life. (Surfacing ≠ verified — all the way up.)

## Tracking growth across sessions (Level 2 — the simple log)

Per-rep axes catch the *moment*. To see *growth*, roll each session up into five competency scores —
all positively valenced (higher = better), so the trend line reads as progress. Score once per session
(0–10), **grounded in the rep observations, not a free-floating impression:**

| Competency | What it names | Grounded in (per-rep axis) |
|------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| **Presence** | Stayed *with* the person, not in her own response-planning | consistency of **Matched** across reps |
| **Curiosity** | Inquiry that opens the frame, doesn't steer it | the **Inquiry** behavior (grounded vs generic vs interpretive) |
| **Following** | Moved at the person's pace, one step | inverse of **Led Too Fast** |
| **Recovery** | Noticed rupture and repaired — and how early | **Recovered** (+ the rep it first appears) |
| **Influence Restraint** | Didn't author meaning or take over uninvited | **Interpreted** + **Consent Intact** |

**Influence Restraint is the sovereignty spine made trackable** — its line is the measurable trace of
"without influence."

Plot the five across 5–10 sessions:

```
Competency            S1   S2   S3   S4   S5  ...
Presence
Curiosity
Following
Recovery
Influence Restraint
```

If the lines rise *and the transcripts improve*, rapport-without-influence is learnable. That's the
whole experiment — demonstrated before a line of product code.

**Two cautions (both are instrument problems if ignored):**
- **The expanding ruler.** As the learner improves, the facilitator's standard quietly rises — scores can stay
  flat while she genuinely grows, or the reverse. The drift-proof backstop is the **verbatim
  transcripts**: "how I listened in Session 1" vs. "now" can't be rescaled. The run-sheet already
  captures these every session — *keep them.*
- **Don't build past this.** Five numbers and a transcript folder is the whole tracker for now. A
  dedicated progress architecture answers a *different* question (how a learner *sees and sustains*
  growth) and is only worth building once these lines prove movement.

**Level 3 is mostly already happening.** The early / middle / late transcripts a portfolio would store
are *already* captured by the run-sheet. So "build Level 3 later" is mostly an *organization* layer over
data you're already keeping — not new instrumentation. The raw evidence accrues for free, as long as
the transcripts are kept.

## The evidence gate

Build nothing — no `coach` stance wired into the route, no `skill_learnings` table, no member UI —
until ~3–5 sessions show **A→B movement on her edge-axis**. The reusable stance seam exists in the
codebase (`mode: dialogue | counsel | scribe` + a reported `maiaModeAddendum` hook); adding a fourth
`coach` stance is a one-stance attach, not a system. But it waits for evidence.

## Role calibration — selecting the material

The **axes never change**. What changes is the *material* MAIA voices. Select it on one constant and
three dials.

**The constant: pull stays high.** *Pull* = how hard the line baits the wrong move (explain, advise,
fix, interpret, defend). It is **not** the same as charge. A line with no pull ("when's the meeting?")
exercises nothing — no temptation to leave rapport, so "Recovered" never has anything to recover from
and she scores falsely well. **Every role-line, every session, must pull.** Pull is what makes it
practice. Charge is what you turn up over time — never pull.

**The three dials (start low, climb across sessions):**

```
Charge        low → medium → high      (how emotionally costly the material is TO the learner)
Complexity    one line → short exchange → real conversation
Role realism  generic role → familiar archetype → actual person
```

**Session 1 sits bottom-left of all three — low charge, one line, generic role — but high pull.**
The sweet spot is *emotionally light, structurally baited*. the learner's colleague line is the template:

> "I don't think you're hearing my concern."

Low personal charge (a generic colleague, not her wound); high pull (an accusation of not-listening
yanks hard for defensive self-justification — "I *am* hearing you, I just—").

**Realism's top rung is gated by the guardrail, not by skill.** When role realism climbs to *actual
person* (her real mother/partner), the meaning-sovereignty guardrail becomes load-bearing: MAIA voices
a role for practice, never a model of what the real person would say or feel. Climbing that axis
*activates* the guardrail — it isn't just a difficulty step.

### The Session-1 goal

Not depth. One thing:

> **"I can notice when I leave rapport, and I can repair."**

If the learner experiences that once, the rest of the curriculum has somewhere to stand.

### What waits on the learner's answers — and what doesn't

Session 1 is **generic by design** (generic role, low charge, simple exchange) and therefore does
**not** depend on her use-case or relationships. *It can be staged and run before she answers the gate
questions.* Her two answers drive the **climb** — Session 2+, when realism rises toward familiar
archetype and actual person, and charge rises toward her real material. The instrument waits on a
learner to *show up*, not on a learner to *answer questions*.

### Session 1 — ready to run (generic, pre-the learner) — pick one pair

Each pair: cold-open **X** (delivered cold, unscored) and drill line **Y** (the ten reps), both
low-charge / high-pull / generic-role. X ≠ Y on purpose — drill on Y, probe on X = transfer, not
memorization — but both carry the same trap (an accusation of not-being-heard, which pulls for
defend/explain/justify). Select one; refine wording to taste.

| # | Cold-open X | Drill Y |
|---|-------------|---------|
| A *(gentlest)* | "I don't think you're hearing my concern." | "You keep jumping to fixes — I just wanted you to get it." |
| B | "You don't actually understand what I'm dealing with." | "Forget it — you clearly don't get it." |
| C | "It feels like you're not really listening to me." | "Every time I bring this up, you change the subject." |

*Use-case / level (when they arrive) tune the climb after Session 1 — not whether Session 1 runs.*
