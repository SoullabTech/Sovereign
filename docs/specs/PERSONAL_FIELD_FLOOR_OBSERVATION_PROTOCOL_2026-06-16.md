# Personal Field — Orientation Floor: Observation Protocol (2026-06-16)

**Status:** the measurement half of the first Personal Field build (Slice 1 — the orientation floor). The floor is the experiment; this is how we learn from it. **Out of band** — real people, real lives, no in-app instrumentation.

Constitution: `PERSONAL_FIELD_REDESIGN_2026-06-15.md` §0.5.

## The hypothesis under test
> A stable floor made of questions supports orientation without authoring attention.

## The experiential bar — anticipatory, not directive
The floor must create a particular *relationship*, not just display particular content:

- **Directive** — "here is what matters." The system authors attention; the person responds to the system's agenda. ✗
- **Anticipatory** — "I have prepared a space for whatever matters." A container that assumes something meaningful may already be present; it *waits* for the person rather than instructing them. ✓ This is the felt target.
- **Responsive** — the field changes in response to what the person actually *does*; importance is **revealed by their attention**, not decided by the system.

Responsive has two depths:
- **Shallow (live in v1):** the floor surfaces only the person's *own authored content* (their changes, decisions, questions, what they flagged). Already responding to revealed attention — they revealed it by creating it.
- **Deep (gated on the continuity substrate):** the field remembers where attention went over time and later surfaces what relates (the person writes about their sister; the field later brings forward related threads). Needs memory. A later slice.

The constitution's guarantee of all this is one line: **questions, not claims. A claim closes; a question opens. The floor does not arrive with content — it arrives with room.**

## The subtle test (the core felt-signal)
When someone leaves the floor, what do they feel?

- Directive drift → **managed / evaluated / corrected / steered.**
- Becoming responsive → **seen / oriented / grounded / more aware of their own priorities.**

This is the single discriminating signal. Note it is **observational** ("what did you feel?"), never **verdict** ("did it work?") — the measurement obeys the floor's own discipline: reveals, doesn't decide. A verdict question is the evaluator authoring the outcome.

## How we observe (out of band)
- A handful of real people live with the floor for ~a week. **No in-app prompt, no instrumentation inside the experience** — a measurement surface changes the thing measured and can induce the very "judged" feeling it tests for.
- Lightweight before-framing (in conversation, not in-app): *how scattered / pressured / lost / disconnected / oriented are you arriving these days?*
- After living with it, **observational questions** (not verdict):
  - What changed, if anything?
  - What did you find yourself returning to?
  - What became more visible? What became less visible?
  - What felt useful? What felt unnecessary?
  - When you left it, did you feel more **seen** — or more **steered**?

## Failure signals — each falsifies a specific link (failure is diagnostic)
- **directive / steered / managed** → it authored attention instead of holding room (the core failure).
- **overwhelming** → it stopped reducing load.
- **repetitive / irrelevant / inert** → the frame is empty or stale; nothing live to encounter through it.
- **subtly prescriptive** → a question started behaving like a claim.

The principle gets no free pass for elegance. It has to survive contact.

## Why this is the right first experiment
If the floor is unhelpful, confusing, or inert, we learn that *before* building relationship spaces, modules, composition, continuity integration, or personal primitives — an inexpensive failure. If it proves useful, every later decision is made in the presence of evidence rather than theory. Small enough to be an experiment; important enough to teach something real.
