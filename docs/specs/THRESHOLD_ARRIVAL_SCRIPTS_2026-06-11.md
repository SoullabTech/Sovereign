# Threshold Arrival Scripts — the two poles

**Date:** 2026-06-11
**Purpose:** Render the arrival, start to finish, for the two poles — an Open Day and an Exhausted Day. The middle states fall out of these. Each script is followed by a **principle check** against the Threshold Principle (`THRESHOLD_PRINCIPLE_CANON_PROPOSAL_2026-06-11.md`): *does this arrival remember and invite — or does it define?*
**Reading note:** `[bracketed]` = choreography / timing / feel. Plain text = what the person sees or hears. Indented = the verbs/doors offered. This is register and rhythm, not final copy.

## Script A — Open Day

```
[ The screen is already quiet when it opens. The center — MaiaCenterField —
  is breathing: a slow, low motion. For about a second and a half, there is
  nothing else. No modules, no counts, no flash. You are received before you
  are informed. ]

Good morning, Kelly.

[ A beat. The name lands first, alone. ]

You're in a season of Fire.

[ Said the way you'd note the weather of a landscape — where you are on the
  map, not how you feel. It does not say "you are fiery," or "you seem driven."
  It marks the terrain, not the person. ]

The manuscript is gathering.

      [ Return to it → ]

[ One thread, foreground. Beneath it, quieter, smaller, not competing: ]

      also nearby —  Sophie, tomorrow  ·  the Session Room

[ Each is its own small door, but they wait below the line. The eye rests on
  one thing. Then, nothing more arrives. The portal goes still and lets you
  choose. You pass through — into the manuscript, toward Sophie, into the
  Session Room, or back out into your day. ]
```

**Principle check.**
- *Remembers what was placed:* the manuscript, Sophie, the Session Room are all things **Kelly named or scheduled** — not significance MAIA assigned. ✔
- *Invites what is present:* "season of Fire" is **positional** (spiral phase), not an emotional verdict. The doors are offers, not instructions. ✔
- *Never defines:* nothing here says who Kelly is or how Kelly feels. ✔
- *One foreground, several beneath* (decision 3): a single resting point; the rest available, collapsed — not a menu. ✔
- *Capacity never asked:* on an open day the person expands the arrival simply by engaging. No mood-check gates the door. ✔

## Script B — Exhausted Day

```
[ The screen opens quiet, the same held beat — perhaps a touch dimmer, a touch
  slower. The center breathes. Presence, and nothing asked. ]

You're here.

[ A beat. That is the whole recognition. ]

That's enough for now.

[ Nothing else appears. No thread. No "what matters today." No "where to next."
  No capacity question. No card. No count. No "you haven't been here since…"
  No streak, intact or broken. The portal is not waiting for an answer. ]

      [ begin gently ]

[ One door, low and optional, with no agenda behind it. It can also be ignored
  entirely. A person can arrive, be met by "you're here," and simply close it —
  and nothing is lost, nothing is marked, nothing is owed. The arrival required
  nothing of them. ]
```

*Gentle-posture variant (decision 2) — if recent input was heavy in tone:*

```
Welcome back.

Let's begin gently.

      [ begin gently ]
```

[ The register softens. MAIA opens more quietly. But it never says why. It does
  not name a heaviness, a struggle, a grief. It adjusts *its own posture* — not
  *its description of you.* The softening is the shape of care, not a conclusion
  about the cared-for. ]

**Principle check.**
- *Zero input required* (decision 1): the floor holds with no tap, no rating, no declaration. ✔
- *Never defines:* the word "exhausted" never appears. MAIA does not know it is an exhausted day, and does not claim to. ✔
- *Tone sets posture, not label* (decision 2): the gentle variant softens MAIA's register and names nothing about the person. ✔
- *Refusal stands:* doing nothing is a complete, penalty-free arrival. ✔

## What the Exhausted-Day script taught — *there is no "exhausted day"*

You guessed this one would teach more than the open day. It did, and the lesson is structural:

**The system never decides it is an exhausted day. There is no exhausted-day mode.** Script B is not "the portal detecting low capacity and rendering the low-capacity screen." It is simply **the floor, left un-expanded.** The exhaustion is never named, never detected, never branched on — the portal defaulted to the floor and the person didn't reach for more. That's all.

This corrects something latent in the earlier capacity matrix. If the matrix were *implemented* as "detect the person's state → render the matching row," the system would be **deciding what kind of day the person is having** — a quiet violation of the very principle we just wrote. The matrix is **descriptive** (what arrival feels like at each level of expansion), not a **control structure** (states the system classifies and selects).

The principle-compliant mechanism is therefore:

> **Floor by default + person-controlled expansion + (optionally) a gently-carried posture from tone — and no state detection anywhere.**

The "six capacity states" are not six branches. They are how far the person opens one floor. The system holds the floor; the person authors the depth.

**This is now folded into the canon proposal** as the *No state branch* clause of the Operational form — the prose found the last place the system could still "decide who the person is," and closed it.

**Implication for Track A (when built):** no mood/state detection, no capacity classifier. Build the floor (zero-input, always holds) and a single expansion gesture the person controls. That is *less* engineering than a state machine, and it is the only version that satisfies Invariant 14. Sovereignty and simplicity point the same way.

## Why only two scripts

The poles bound the range. Fragmented, Beginning, Overloaded, Numb, Nonverbal are all *partial expansions of the floor* (Script B) toward *the full gather* (Script A) — not separate designs. Once arrival is right at both ends, the middle is interpolation, authored by the person's own reach, not by the system's read of them.
