## 1. The axiom

> **The architecture shall preserve the distinction between encounter and interpretation.**

Offered as a constitutional *root*: every principle in §5 reads as a consequence of protecting this one distinction. Its force is that the collapse it forbids is **unrecoverable** — once an interpretation is allowed to stand as an encounter, the system can no longer tell which is which, and the distinction cannot be reconstructed after the fact.

## 2. The invariant (derived)

> No interpretation may be recorded as, or overwrite, an encounter. The steward of an encounter may **withhold, delete, or correct it for fidelity** — but no one, including the steward, may record an interpretation as that encounter.

The **type** distinction is inviolable (always); the **instance** is sovereign (the steward disposes of their own record). Sanctuary is therefore *deletion*, never type-collapse: a steward removing their own encounter is not one way of knowing erasing another's provenance. Sovereignty determines whether an encounter remains in the system; it does not determine what kind of thing it is.

## 3. A claim is validated at three independent levels

Declaration is not liveness. None of these substitutes for another:

| Level | Question | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional invariant | What must always be true? | Coherence with the constitution |
| Structural enforcement | *Can* the system violate it? | Architecture & implementation |
| Behavioral evidence | Does the running system satisfy it? | Observation, testing, audit |

For this invariant:
- **Constitutional:** encounter and interpretation are distinct ontological objects.
- **Structural:** separate object types; append-only provenance; no operation that records or overwrites an interpretation as an encounter. *(design requirement — not yet built)*
- **Behavioral:** audits demonstrate no provenance/type violations across a release. *(not yet established)*

These are three different artifacts — a constitution, an architecture, an audit — and none stands in for another.

## 4. The anti-collapse family

The axiom is the sharpest instance of one recurring move: *a token may not be mistaken for what it points to or depends on.*

- Declaration is not liveness · Ratification is not enforcement · Enforcement is not behavior · Behavior is not evidence · Evidence is not understanding.
- Representation ≠ reality · interpretation ≠ encounter · authority ≠ truth · declaration ≠ operation.

These are not separate insights; they are one principle at different altitudes. The Representation–Participation Covenant is this principle applied to representation; this axiom is it applied to the encounter itself.

## 5. Derivation map (current evidence: compression)

Existing canon reads as consequences of the axiom:

- **Representation–Participation Covenant** — representation serves participation, never substitutes (never let interpretation overwrite encounter).
- **From Observation to Culture** — observation precedes interpretation.
- **Field Intelligence** — evidence, interpretation, and authority are distinct objects; *evidence accumulates, authority does not.*
- **Sanctuary Mode** — steward sovereignty over one's own encounter (deletion, not collapse).
- **Anchor-substitution** (named failure mode) — a representation becoming authoritative over what it represents *is* one knowing erasing another's provenance: the axiom's violation.

A passing derivation test establishes **compression** — strong evidence of a root. It does not, by itself, establish that the principle is generative.

## 7. Corrigibility

Even an axiom answers to reality. If preserving the distinction ever proved incoherent or harmful, it is revisable by governance. This document is subject to the discipline it describes: held as a candidate, earning standing only as the table in the header fills in.
