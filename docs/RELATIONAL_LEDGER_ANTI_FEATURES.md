# Relational Practice Ledger: Anti-Features

**Structural Prohibitions for Practice Stewardship Tools**

This document defines what the Relational Practice Ledger is **not allowed to become**, regardless of demand, convenience, or perceived efficiency.

---

## Why This Document Exists

The moment you say "CRM," three things try to sneak in:

1. **Optimization logic** — leads, funnels, conversion, retention
2. **Surveillance logic** — tracking, scoring, nudging
3. **Scale bias** — more clients = success

These are structurally incompatible with care work.

But practitioners have a legitimate need:

> *Help remembering, organizing, and staying in right relationship with the people they serve.*

That is not a CRM problem. That is a **relational continuity problem**.

This document ensures we solve the right problem without importing the wrong logic.

---

## The Naming Constraint

**We do not build a CRM.**

We build a **Relational Practice Ledger**.

A ledger:
- Records commitments
- Tracks states and transitions
- Supports accountability
- Does *not* optimize or manipulate

This naming is not cosmetic. It is structural.

If a feature makes sense for a CRM but not for a ledger, the feature does not belong.

---

## Anti-Feature Categories

### 1. No Leads

There is no concept of a "lead" in the system.

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Use the word "lead" anywhere in code, UI, or documentation
- Model people as potential conversions
- Track "lead sources" or acquisition channels
- Score inquiry quality or likelihood to convert

**What exists instead:**
- Inquiry (pending, uncommitted)
- Active relationship
- Paused
- Completed
- Referred out

People arrive. They are not acquired.

---

### 2. No Pipelines or Funnels

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Display people in pipeline stages
- Use language like "move to next stage"
- Visualize relationships as a funnel narrowing toward payment
- Track "conversion rates" from inquiry to client
- Suggest actions to "advance" a relationship

**What exists instead:**
- Relationship states (clear, honest, non-directional)
- State transitions (logged, not optimized)
- No implied directionality toward "closed" or "won"

Relationships have phases. They do not have stages toward extraction.

---

### 3. No Scoring or Ranking

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Score clients by value, engagement, or potential
- Rank relationships by revenue or frequency
- Label anyone as "top client" or "high value"
- Calculate lifetime value (LTV)
- Surface "best" or "most engaged" people

**What exists instead:**
- All relationships have equal structural dignity
- Sorting by recency or alphabetical only
- No metrics that create hierarchy among those served

If a view would make someone feel **extractable**, it does not belong.

---

### 4. No Growth Metrics

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Track client acquisition rate
- Display growth charts (more clients over time)
- Calculate or display "churn rate"
- Set growth targets or goals
- Notify practitioners about growth opportunities

**What exists instead:**
- Capacity visibility (how full am I?)
- Sustainability view (am I stable?)
- No implication that more is better

Business support means **sustainability**, not expansion.

---

### 5. No Engagement Optimization

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Track engagement frequency as a health metric
- Alert practitioners to "re-engage" dormant clients
- Suggest outreach based on inactivity
- Automate follow-up sequences
- Nudge practitioners toward contact frequency targets

**What exists instead:**
- Care horizon (what's upcoming that requires attention?)
- Explicit commitments (what did I agree to?)
- No system-initiated prompts to contact people

Silence is sometimes the right relationship. The system does not know better.

---

### 6. No Upsell or Cross-Sell Features

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Suggest additional services to offer existing clients
- Track "upsell opportunities"
- Calculate revenue potential per relationship
- Prompt practitioners to expand scope
- Display "you could also offer..." suggestions

**What exists instead:**
- Agreement tracking (what did we agree to?)
- Scope clarity (what is this relationship for?)
- No expansion pressure

Relationships have boundaries. The system respects them.

---

### 7. No Automation of Relational Actions

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Auto-send messages or emails
- Schedule automated check-ins
- Generate AI-written outreach
- Automate any communication that appears to come from the practitioner
- Suggest templated responses to copy

**What exists instead:**
- Reminders (you may want to...)
- Never actions taken on behalf of the practitioner
- All communication is human-initiated and human-written

Automation erodes relational integrity. Every contact must be a choice.

---

### 8. No Surveillance of Client Behavior

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Track whether clients opened emails
- Log client portal activity
- Monitor client engagement patterns
- Score client "health" based on behavior
- Alert practitioners to client behavior changes

**What exists instead:**
- Session records (what happened when we met)
- Notes (practitioner's reflections)
- No client-side tracking whatsoever

The ledger records the practitioner's commitments, not the client's behavior.

---

### 9. No Comparative Analytics

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Compare this month to last month with growth framing
- Show "you're doing better/worse than..."
- Benchmark against other practitioners
- Display trends with implicit value judgments
- Celebrate milestones tied to volume

**What exists instead:**
- Current state visibility (where am I now?)
- Historical record (what has been?)
- No value judgment on trajectory

A practice with fewer clients is not failing. The system makes no such implication.

---

### 10. No Marketing Integration

The Relational Ledger must not:
- Connect to marketing tools or platforms
- Import leads from external sources
- Export contacts for marketing purposes
- Track campaign effectiveness
- Attribute relationships to marketing efforts

**What exists instead:**
- Relationships begin when someone arrives
- No origin tracking beyond basic context
- No outbound marketing support

Marketing is separate from care. The ledger serves care.

These are permitted because they support **sustainability without extraction**:

| Allowed | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Capacity visibility | "How full am I?" is honest self-knowledge |
| Income clarity | "Am I stable this month?" supports responsible livelihood |
| Time distribution | "Where does my energy go?" supports discernment |
| Obligation review | "Who am I holding responsibility for?" supports integrity |
| Upcoming commitments | "What's on my care horizon?" prevents dropping people |
| Closure hygiene | "What needs a clean ending?" supports relational integrity |

## The Nervous System Test

You've crossed the line if:

- A practitioner asks "how do I get more clients through this?"
- You feel pressure to add automation "to save time"
- The system starts recommending actions
- There's a dashboard you wouldn't want a client to see
- A metric would change how a practitioner feels about a person
- The system implies that growth is health

If any of these occur, the feature has drifted. Remove it.

---

## The Client Visibility Test

For any view or metric, ask:

> Would I be comfortable if every person in this ledger could see exactly what I see?

If no, the feature does not belong.

The ledger is a tool for practitioner integrity. It should never contain anything that would harm trust if seen.

---

## The Core Constraint (Summary)

**Care remains the unit of measure, not success.**

We are building infrastructure for **responsible livelihood**, not influence.

The Relational Practice Ledger helps practitioners:
- Remember commitments
- Maintain continuity
- See their capacity honestly
- Close relationships cleanly

It does not help practitioners:
- Get more clients
- Extract more value
- Optimize their practice for growth
- Automate their relationships

## Final Rule

If a feature would:

- Frame people as opportunities
- Measure relationships by revenue
- Encourage scale over depth
- Automate relational actions
- Create information asymmetry between practitioner and client

...it does not belong in the Relational Practice Ledger.

No exceptions.
