## The invitation

# Welcome to the MAIA Beta

Thank you for being part of this early stage of MAIA.

Your participation matters.

We are not simply testing features or fixing bugs. We are exploring a deeper question:

**Can reflective technology help people notice aspects of their lives, decisions, relationships, and experiences that might otherwise remain unseen?**

We do not yet know the answer.

That is why your experience is so valuable.

Every observation you share helps us understand what MAIA notices well, what it misses, where it helps, and where it gets in the way. Your reflections contribute directly to the evolution of the system and to a broader exploration of human awareness, reflection, learning, and growth.

---

## How to Participate

This exploration unfolds over time.

You do not need to use MAIA every day.

The most valuable interactions often begin with a real question, challenge, decision, relationship, transition, opportunity, or uncertainty that genuinely matters to you.

Over the coming weeks:

* Bring real questions when they arise.
* Notice what stands out.
* Notice what feels incomplete.
* Notice what MAIA may have missed.
* Revisit earlier conversations when something becomes clearer later.

A return to a previous conversation can be just as valuable as starting a new one.

We are not primarily interested in how often you use MAIA.

We are interested in what you notice across time.

Some of the most important observations emerge days or weeks after an interaction.

---

## The Feedback That Helps Most

The most valuable feedback is not:

* "I liked it."
* "I didn't like it."

The most valuable feedback is:

* What surprised me?
* What felt relevant?
* What felt inaccurate?
* What felt incomplete?
* What became clearer later?
* What did MAIA miss entirely?
* Where did MAIA help?
* Where did MAIA get in the way?

There are no right answers.

An honest observation that something fell flat is often more valuable than praise.

---

## Why Your Participation Matters

Most systems are built by deciding in advance what they are supposed to be.

We are trying something different.

We are learning through observation.

Rather than proving a theory, we are exploring what actually happens when people bring real questions, real experiences, and real life into the conversation.

Your participation helps us distinguish between what we hope MAIA can do and what it genuinely does.

That distinction matters.

The future of MAIA will be shaped not by assumptions, but by the observations, experiences, and insights shared by people like you.

For that, we are deeply grateful.

Thank you for helping us learn.

## Design notes (why this departs from earlier comms)

- **No elemental language** — honesty to the Regime-B surface finding; also avoids priming the very thing a future council-surfacing study would need to measure cleanly.
- **No usage quota** — span-based ("return when life gives you something worth bringing"). Protects the sovereignty / centrality vow and avoids effort-justification bias (a tester who invested "15 min/day" is motivated to report value).
- **Recruits disconfirmation** — the feedback prompts privilege what MAIA missed / got wrong: the least-manufacturable signal.
- **Significance anchored to contribution, not flattery** — the copy does foreground the participant ("Your participation matters"; section titled "Why Your Participation Matters"), but grounds that mattering in the observational contribution and the hope-vs-does distinction ("distinguish between what we hope MAIA can do and what it genuinely does"), not in the participant's stature. The mattering lands on what they notice, not on who they are.
- **Symmetric feedback prompts, asymmetric headline (one residual seam — recorded, not smoothed)** — the feedback section gives the null equal billing with success ("What felt inaccurate? What did MAIA miss entirely? Where did MAIA get in the way?" balanced against "Where did MAIA help?"). But the headline question — *"Can reflective technology help people notice ... that might otherwise remain unseen?"* — still leans success-shaped: it frames the inquiry around MAIA helping people see, rather than around whether it mostly reflects back what they already knew. This is a small residual demand-effect seam. **Disposition (Kelly, 2026-05-31): hold as-is.** The headline stays unchanged; the body already recruits disconfirmation (notices / misses / gets-in-the-way / becomes-clearer-later), so the document as a whole is balanced even though the headline is not. Gate to revisit: real tester responses — tighten the sentence only if the returns show the headline is shaping what people report. Closed-with-condition, not an open polish item; recorded rather than over-claimed as clean symmetry.
- **Cohort note** — the existing (elementally-primed) tester pool is *fine* for this purpose: we are not testing differentiation perception, so prior elemental exposure does not bias "is it useful / what does it miss." File responses as unified-voice feedback; do **not** treat them as a clean Cohort A for any future council study.
