# Jondi Sessions — Facilitator Guide (Concierge Run)

**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Status:** Concierge facilitator guide — the human-facing companion to `docs/research/JONDI_RUNNABLE_PROTOCOL_2026-06-25.md`. Read the protocol once for the instruments; then mostly forget it in the room.
**Register:** This is a guide for a **host**, not an operator. Jondi should experience one thing — *a deeply attentive conversation.* Everything else happens underneath, and afterward.

---

## The one instruction that protects all the others

> **Remain more interested in understanding Jondi than in validating the methodology.**

If you are invested in Architectural Translation succeeding, you will steer — subtly, unavoidably. Your visible job is not to make the method work; it is to help her think more clearly about her own practice. If the method is real, it will show *despite* your indifference to its success, not because of your investment in it.

(This is not detachment. Be warmly, fully engaged — with *her*. Be un-invested only in the *outcome of the method*.)

Hold this above every technique below.

---

## Three layers — and the rule between them

- **Conversation** — what Jondi experiences: being genuinely listened to.
- **Observation** — what you notice and, *after the room*, record.
- **Research** — how those notes are later analyzed.

You are the one human holding the first two at once — which is exactly why the prime directive matters: it is *how* you hold Observation without letting it leak into the Conversation as steering.

**The rule is asymmetric.** The layers may inform **downward** — what you hope to learn shapes what you listen for. They must never bend **upward**: **the conversation is never sacrificed to get a data point.** If protecting the session and capturing an observation ever conflict, protect the session. A lost data point is recoverable; a conversation she felt *managed* inside is not. (This is the project's member-facing / builder-facing boundary, at the scale of one session.)

---

## In the room

- **Her language, always.** Never translate *presence, alignment, the work* into our vocabulary. If a word of hers is unfamiliar, ask what she means by it — that question is itself good listening.
- **Offer, never assert.** Reflect a pattern as: *"I might be wrong — does this capture something, miss it, or land only partly?"* Then stop talking. Her correction is worth more than your accuracy.
- **Follow the heat, not the protocol.** If she lights up off-script, go there. The streams (Recovery / Reflection / Emergence) are a map of what to *notice*, never an order to *march*.
- **Silence is allowed.** The most important sentences usually come after the pause you were tempted to fill.
- **The probe, handled gently.** When you offer a deliberately-uncertain reflection (the discrimination check), offer it exactly as sincerely as a real one, and let her reject it with no persuasion. A rejection is a gift, not a setback. (She consented to the *existence* of probes; she learns which ones in the debrief.)
- **Sanctuary on request, instantly.** Anything she wants off-record goes off-record mid-sentence, no friction.

---

## Emergence is usually invisible in the moment

You often cannot tell, *as it happens*, whether she is recovering something old or inventing something new — it nearly always *feels* like recognition. **Don't force the call in the room**, and never ask her to classify it (that pulls her out of thinking). Mark it **candidate** and let time resolve it: weeks later she may say *"actually — I'd never thought that before"* (emergence) or *"I'd always known that"* (recovery). That drift, in both directions, is **data about the phenomenon**, not noise.

---

## "Nothing happened" is a real and valuable session

Not every conversation externalizes anything. **Do not rescue a flat session by fishing for a result** — that is steering. Record it honestly. The strong version of this research doesn't ask *"does it work?"* but *"for whom, and under what conditions, does it — and when does it not?"* A session where nothing surfaced is a coordinate on that map, and may be worth more than an easy success.

## Afterward (out of the room)

Only now do the instruments come out. From memory and notes, fill the logs in `JONDI_RUNNABLE_PROTOCOL` §5 — Recognition, Discrimination, Elaboration, Utility (with provenance), and the *candidate* Externalization/Emergence tags (§5.7). Before the *next* phase, pre-register what would count as a methodology failure vs an instrument failure (§10.1). The room was hers; the analysis is ours; keep them apart.
