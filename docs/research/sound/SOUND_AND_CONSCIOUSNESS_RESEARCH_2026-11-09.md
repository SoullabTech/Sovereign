# Sound shifts state; frequencies don't heal

The full body of evidence says that sound reliably changes how people feel in the moment and only rarely does anything more specific. The strongest findings come from ordinary listening, not from engineered "frequencies." **Music before and during surgery lowers pain and anxiety (73 RCTs). Music therapy improves depressive symptoms, including in dementia. Music listening improves self-reported sleep quality. Small sound cues during deep sleep strengthen lab-trained memories (91 experiments, g ≈ 0.29).** Brainwave entrainment is more mixed. Rhythmic sound does drive measurable, stimulus-locked brain responses, strongest at about 40 Hz and stronger for monaural, amplitude-modulated and isochronic sound than for binaural beats. But the claim that theta or alpha beats put the whole brain into a "meditative state" rests on EEG studies that **contradict it more often than they support it (5 for, 8 against, 1 mixed)**. Behavioural meta-analyses report medium effects (g ≈ 0.40–0.45) that behave more like relaxation, arousal and expectancy than frequency-specific tuning, though no study here measures how much each of those contributes. Solfeggio, 432 Hz, "528 Hz DNA repair" and Schumann-resonance health claims are numerology and invented history, with tiny pilots that cut both ways. The Monroe Institute's Gateway/Hemi-Sync, Source Vibrations, Jonathan Goldman's "Frequency + Intent = Healing" and most "mind-hacking" audio sit at the practitioner/marketing grade: real as contemplative experiences, unproven as outcomes. The clearest hazards are listening volume, sleep disruption from all-night noise, and visual flicker if it is ever added. The clearest regulatory line is that intended use, functionality and risk decide device status: disease and diagnosis claims make a product a device, while the FDA's wellness policy still allows narrow, well-established "living well with" chronic-condition claims. For Soullab's Sound Studio, this evidence supports an honest, experience-first listening product with a fail-closed rights ledger. It does not support any outcome claim. Every unresolved design question is listed at the end as an open question, not a recommendation.

**How to read the grades.** Every claim carries one of five grades: **[MA/RCT]** meta-analysis or randomized trial; **[SMALL]** small or uncontrolled trial; **[MECH]** lab or mechanism only; **[PRACT]** practitioner or marketing only; **[REFUTED]** refuted or contradicted. The source notes also attach provenance flags, and they are kept here as written. **⟨abstract/secondary⟩** means the researcher read only an abstract, a PubMed record or a secondary report. **⟨from memory⟩** means a well-known paper the researcher cited from memory by DOI without re-verifying it in session; treat it as verified at the citation level only. **⟨unverified⟩** means the researcher could not confirm the item. No flagged item has been upgraded. One note on grades: [MA/RCT] describes the *design* of the evidence, not its certainty. Several [MA/RCT] findings below carry "low" or "very low" GRADE certainty, and that is stated each time.

## Rhythmic sound moves the brain; it rarely moves the mind on cue

The entrainment literature divides into two layers, and most marketing blurs them. The first layer is solid. Rhythmic sound produces a brainstem frequency-following response at the carrier tones and a cortical auditory steady-state response (ASSR) at the beat or modulation rate. In a controlled EEG study, binaural beats "weakly entrain the cortex when compared with monaural beat stimuli," **and neither stimulus changed mood [MECH]** ([Orozco Perez et al. 2020, eNeuro](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066611/)). The ranking by cortical drive is therefore amplitude-modulated / isochronic / monaural above binaural. The response is a sensory, stimulus-locked signal, strongest near 40 Hz and weak at theta and delta rates. It is not evidence that the brain as a whole has adopted a "state." Vendor claims that isochronic tones are "the strongest" method are **[PRACT]**; one source admits there is "very little data" comparing them directly with binaural beats ([6thmind](https://6thmind.substack.com/p/brainwave-entrainment-vs-binaural); [Brain.fm blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/binaural-beats-safety)). No peer-reviewed head-to-head EEG comparison of isochronic, binaural and monaural stimuli was found.

The second layer, "theta beats induce meditation, alpha beats induce relaxation," is where the evidence fails. The only systematic review of the mechanism, Ingendoh, Posny & Heine 2023, included 14 quality-screened EEG studies. **Five were consistent with entrainment, eight contradicted it and one was mixed.** The authors concluded the question "cannot be settled" and that applied claims "should be considered with caution" ([PLOS ONE, PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/)). Samples were mostly young students, exposures ran 1–30 minutes, and EEG metrics varied widely. The researchers graded this inconsistently. The entrainment notes treat state-entrainment as **unestablished [MECH, contested]**; the focus and biohacking notes grade it **[REFUTED/contradicted]**. The authors' own "cannot be settled" better supports the first framing. The fairest grade is that the majority of evidence contradicts the claim and the claim is not established. It has not been decisively refuted.

Against that weak mechanism stand two behavioural meta-analyses with medium pooled effects. **Garcia-Argibay et al. 2019 pooled 22 studies (35 effect sizes) at g = 0.45** across memory, attention, anxiety and pain. Exposure before a task worked better than exposure only during it, and masking with noise made no difference **[MA/RCT ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)). **Basu & Banerjee (online 2022, print 2023) pooled 15 studies at g = 0.40** for memory and attention, but found "conflicting results, especially concerning theta and beta" **[MA/RCT ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/)). Their study sets overlap heavily, so they are not independent confirmations. Their heterogeneity and publication-bias statistics were not verified. A later sustained-attention experiment found only a "modest" effect, with Bayes factors that could not tell the models apart **[SMALL]** ([AZ Regents](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-binaural-beat-stimulation-on-sustained-attention/)). A 2025 Bayesian thesis found mixed or null memory effects **[SMALL]** ([UCT](https://open.uct.ac.za/items/3ad08e8f-5d02-4505-8662-c089ab3848c5)). A g of 0.40–0.45 built on small, often unblinded studies is best read as an upper bound. The timing effect (before beats during) and the null masking effect both fit a non-specific arousal or relaxation account at least as well as frequency-specific entrainment.

The most consistent outcome signal is **acute anxiety before a procedure**. Padmanabhan et al. 2005 is the cleanest trial because it used a sound-matched control: anxiety fell about 26% with binaural beats, 11% with the same soundtrack without beats, and 4% with nothing **[MA/RCT]** ([PubMed 16115248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16115248/)). Dental and caesarean trials point the same way **[MA/RCT]** ([Isik 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28325532/); [J Psychosom Obstet Gynecol 2021](https://doaj.org/article/a50cfe89cf0a4dce86392cec613ba0f6)). One 2020 dental RCT found **binaural beats and 432 Hz music equally effective and both better than control** **[MA/RCT]** ([PMC7806348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7806348/)). That points to a general effect of audio rather than a beat-specific one. The researchers disagree on whether Padmanabhan used Monroe's Hemi-Sync. The entrainment notes verify the trial itself through PubMed. The programs notes found Hemi-Sync attribution only in a blog summary and mark it **⟨unverified⟩**. The trial's existence is well supported; its link to Hemi-Sync is not. Pain evidence is low to very low certainty, and a 2024 review judged pooling inappropriate **[MA/RCT-level review; very low certainty] ⟨abstract/secondary⟩** ([PMC11893577](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11893577/)). No Cochrane review of binaural beats exists, and no large preregistered replication was found.

**40 Hz gamma stimulation** is the most serious clinical program in the space, and it is unresolved. Mouse work from the Tsai lab reported reduced amyloid with 40 Hz light **[MECH ⟨from memory⟩]** ([Iaccarino 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20587)), and with 40 Hz *auditory* and combined stimulation **[MECH ⟨from memory⟩]** ([Martorell 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.02.014)). An independent multisite study (Soula et al. 2023, Buzsáki lab) found that 40 Hz *light* **did not entrain native gamma and did not reliably reduce plaques**, and that mice found the flicker aversive **[MECH; contradicts Iaccarino for light]** ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01270-2.pdf)). Soula tested light only. No independent failed replication of the *auditory* arm was found. In humans, 40 Hz stimulation reliably raises 40 Hz EEG power, and two years of daily use in five mild-AD patients was reported safe **[SMALL]** ([PMC9714926](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9714926/)). Cognito's OVERTURE feasibility trial (n = 76) reported a 76–77% slowing of MMSE decline, but those figures come from secondary and post hoc analyses; a critic argues the primary endpoint was not met **[SMALL; company-promoted, post hoc]** ([Cognito](https://www.cognitotx.com/clinical-studies); [Frontiers in Neurology 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fneur.2024.1343588)). The pivotal HOPE trial (NCT05637801, about 670 enrolled) reached primary completion on 22 June 2026 ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05637801?cond=NCT05637801)). **HOPE topline results were not found as of November 2026** in three targeted searches. The readout may have happened without surfacing, so no outcome can be stated. Spectris remains investigational, with Breakthrough Device Designation and no clearance. Consumer "40 Hz gamma" tracks borrow the credibility of an unproven medical program **[PRACT]**.

## "Healing frequencies" are numerology dressed as history

The solfeggio set traces to Joseph Puleo, a naturopath who in the mid-1970s derived the numbers by numerology from Bible verses. Leonard Horowitz popularized them in *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse* (1999). The claimed link to Guido d'Arezzo and Gregorian chant is unsupported, because medieval pitch was not absolute **[REFUTED as history]** ([RationalWiki archive](https://kiwix.lema.org/rationalwiki_en_all_maxi_2021-03/A/Solfeggio_frequencies); [Skeptics SE archive](https://kiwix.lema.org/skeptics.stackexchange.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/8377/does-528-hz-facilitate-dna-repair)). This provenance rests on secondary skeptic sources that agree with one another; the Horowitz and Puleo originals were not read. The claim that **528 Hz repairs DNA** cites nothing beyond Puleo and matches no known laboratory practice **[REFUTED/PRACT]** ([Skeptics SE archive](https://kiwix.lema.org/skeptics.stackexchange.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/8377/does-528-hz-facilitate-dna-repair)). DNA repair is enzymatic, and audible sound at normal intensity has no plausible frequency-specific route to it.

The 432 Hz story fails on documented history. A 1939 London conference set A = 440 as a compromise among many pitches then in use, some above 450 Hz. Historian Jeffrey Herf dismissed the claim that the Nazis imposed 440 Hz to manipulate people **[REFUTED as history]** ([Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/did-nazis-change-the-universal-music-pitch-to-use-against-their-enemies-675733)). Ellis's 1880 survey found historical pitch ranging from 374 to 567 Hz. France fixed A = 435 in 1859. 432 Hz "has NEVER been a National or International standard," and Verdi's interest was in protecting singers' voices ([roelhollander.eu](https://roelhollander.eu/en/432-tuning/432-as-standard/); [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/debunking-432-hz-tuning-standard)). The physical point settles most of the health claims anyway. Retuning from 440 to 432 Hz shifts every pitch by about 1.8%, roughly a third of a semitone, and most listeners cannot detect that without a reference. Few studies control for the recordings used, so tempo, timbre and mastering confound the comparison.

The peer-reviewed frequency studies are tiny and point in different directions. Akimoto 2018 compared 528 Hz with 440 Hz music in **n = 9**, each participant serving as their own control, and reported lower cortisol and higher oxytocin after 528 Hz. Randomization and blinding were not verified, and the study has not been replicated **[SMALL]** ([SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/journal/PaperInformation.aspx?paperID=87146)). Calamassi & Pomponi 2019 was a double-blind crossover pilot reporting a slight heart-rate decrease with 432 Hz **[SMALL ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([Acta Biomedica](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/10755)). Its sample size is reported inconsistently: one blog says 33, and no primary figure was verified. The size is therefore **⟨unverified⟩** and neither figure is better supported. The largest study, Bozok et al. 2026 (n = 162 students before exams), **cut against the marketing**. The 432 Hz arm raised a cellular-stress marker, 528 Hz looked similar to silence on BDNF, and **Stroop performance did not differ across groups**. The author calls it a "small, short-term pilot" **[SMALL ⟨read via PsyPost report; protein names and effect sizes not verified⟩]** ([PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/listening-to-specific-music-frequencies-alters-stress-markers-in-students-facing-exams/)). An EEG comparison found no delta difference between 432 and 528 Hz music **[SMALL]** ([Resital](https://journal.isi.ac.id/index.php/resital/article/view/17472)). None of these studies tested a pure tone against a pitch-matched, blinded control, so none can test the frequency-specific hypothesis.

The Schumann resonance is real geophysics: an Earth–ionosphere cavity resonance near 7.83 Hz, with harmonics. The brain link rests on a historical observation that this overlaps the alpha band. But alpha is a band (about 7.5–12.5 Hz), not one frequency, and claims about hypnosis, growth hormone or cortisol have no support **[physics MECH; health claims PRACT]** ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/science/what-is-the-schumann-resonance); [Metabunk](https://metabunk.org/threads/schumman-resonance-the-supposed-key-to-our-well-being.3062)). The claimed 2006 Persinger/Saroka coherence study could not be verified from a primary source **⟨unverified⟩**.

## Ordinary music carries the healing evidence that exotic sound lacks

The best-evidenced therapeutic finding in the whole field involves ordinary music, not special frequencies. **Hole et al. (Lancet 2015, 73 RCTs)** found perioperative music reduced postoperative pain (SMD −0.77), anxiety (−0.68) and analgesic use (−0.37). Choice of music and timing did not moderate the effect, and it worked even under general anaesthesia **[MA/RCT ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([PubMed 26277246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26277246/)). The Cochrane review of music therapy for depression (Aalbers 2017) found reductions in clinician-rated (SMD −1.12) and patient-reported (−0.98) depression, with moderate-quality evidence and wide confidence intervals **[MA/RCT]** ([Cochrane CD004517](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004517.pub3/full)). In dementia, at least five music-therapy sessions probably reduce depressive symptoms (SMD −0.27). The 2025 update (30 studies, 1,720 participants) keeps that conclusion and finds music unlikely to affect agitation or cognition **[MA/RCT]** ([PMC6513122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513122); [Cochrane news](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/music-based-therapy-may-improve-depressive-symptoms-people-dementia)). A 2025 transdiagnostic review found effects on depression (SMD 0.57) and anxiety (0.47) that **did not persist at follow-up**, with all evidence rated low or very low **[MA/RCT]** ([Augsburg OPUS](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/118018)). In cancer, the Cochrane conclusion on mood changed between the 2016 and 2021 versions; anxiety and pain benefits are of very low certainty **[MA/RCT]** ([Cochrane 2021](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006911.pub4/full)). Blinding is impossible and outcomes are mostly self-reported, so these effects are probably inflated. The realistic expectation is a small-to-moderate benefit while the music is in use. "Music medicine" (passive listening) and "music therapy" (a credentialed therapist and a relationship) are different interventions, and the depression reviews mostly test the second.

Sound baths and bowls are thin and recent. Goldsby et al. 2017 (n = 62) is often cited as proof, but it was an **uncontrolled observational study [SMALL]** ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2156587216668109)). A 2020 systematic review found only four studies of singing bowls **[review of SMALL studies]** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096522991931756X)). The key RCT, Rio-Alamos 2023 (n ≈ 50), compared bowls with progressive muscle relaxation; summaries indicate the two active arms were broadly comparable, and this still needs checking against the full text **[SMALL ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([PMC9955072](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9955072/)). A registered New Zealand trial explicitly hypothesizes *equivalence* between crystal bowls and guided relaxation **[protocol only]** ([NCT07267520](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/20/NCT07267520/Prot_SAP_ICF_000.pdf)). The active ingredients are plausibly non-specific: stillness, setting, expectancy, slow breathing and absorbed attention. Humming raises nasal nitric oxide about 15-fold (n = 10). That is a real but narrow sinus finding, and it did not test therapy **[MECH]** ([PubMed 12119224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12119224/)). Immunity claims built on it are **[PRACT]**. An "OM" fMRI pilot (n = 12) showed limbic deactivation; claims that it "matches vagus nerve stimulation" go beyond the paper **[MECH; overreach PRACT]** ([PMC3099099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3099099/)). Vibroacoustic therapy had no published systematic review on pain as of 2022 **[SMALL]** ([PubMed 35383053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35383053/)).

Clinical "listening programs" need the stricter clinical standard because they make clinical claims. A Cochrane review found **"no evidence that auditory integration therapy or other sound therapies are effective"** for autism, which refutes Guy Bérard's AIT **[REFUTED]** ([Cochrane CD003681](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003681.pub3)). ASHA does not support AIT outside research protocols ([ASHA](https://www.asha.org/policy/TR2004-00260/)). The Tomatis Method's only meta-analysis pooled five small 1980s studies (n = 231), and the copy found is hosted by the company **[SMALL]** ([issuu](https://issuu.com/tomatisdoc/docs/the_efficacy_of_the_tomatis_method_)). Porges's Safe and Sound Protocol has a few developer-run trials **[SMALL]** and registered RCTs with no results posted ([NCT06701578](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06701578)). Unyte's "61% moved to non-clinical" figure is uncontrolled company data **[PRACT]** ([Unyte](https://integratedlistening.com/research/unytes-real-world-evidence)). The polyvagal theory underneath it faces published challenges to all five of its premises **[contested; critics argue REFUTED]** ([secondary summary](https://umwelt-67q.pages.dev/01-science/topic-overviews/polyvagal-theory-and-the-safe-and-sound-protocol)). The protocol's empirical effects could stand or fall independently of the theory.

## Focus aids help some brains and slightly harm others

Focus evidence is unusually clear about who benefits. The 2024 JAACAP meta-analysis (13 studies, 335 participants) found that white or pink noise **improved task performance in youth with ADHD or high attention problems (g = 0.249) and worsened it in typical youth (g = −0.212)**. These were lab tasks only, and the authors warn about hearing hazards **[MA/RCT ⟨abstract/secondary⟩]** ([OHSU](https://ohsu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-do-white-noise-or-pink-noise-); [OHSU news](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows)). The explanatory model is "moderate brain arousal" (stochastic resonance), a theory **[MECH]** ([Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/48262)). Even within ADHD the response varies. A 2024 report says about one third did *worse* under ~80 dB noise, with inattentive traits predicting benefit and hyperactive traits predicting harm **[SMALL ⟨secondary, verify⟩]** ([scienceworkshealth](https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/adhd-background-noise-brown-noise-focus)). **Brown noise has no controlled studies at all**, so brown-noise-for-ADHD claims are **[PRACT]** ([BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/does-pink-noise-really-help-you-focus)). Note that the studies ran at about 80 dB, the adult weekly safe-listening ceiling.

Background music is neutral to mildly distracting on average, and **lyrics are the main source of harm**. Reading comprehension falls (d = −0.19 overall), with much larger distraction from lyrical than instrumental music **[MA/RCT]** ([Vasilev et al. 2018](https://martin-vasilev.github.io/papers/background_music_distraction.pdf)). The researchers' sources disagree on how to summarize Kämpfe 2011: one calls the overall effect null (positives and negatives cancelling), another cites a small negative effect (r ≈ −.11) for a subset ([Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/273)). These are compatible readings of the same paper: null overall, small negative in some subgroups. The "Mozart effect" is **[REFUTED]** as a specific enhancer. Pietschnig 2010 (~40 studies, >3,000 participants) found any music produced similar effects, leaving "little evidence left for a specific, performance-enhancing Mozart effect" ([University of Vienna](https://medienportal.univie.ac.at/media/aktuelle-pressemeldungen/detailansicht/artikel/mozarts-music-does-not-make-you-smarter)).

The one commercial focus product with substantive peer-reviewed work is **Brain.fm**. Woods, Loui et al. (*Communications Biology*, 2024) found that music with added amplitude modulation improved sustained attention, and that 16 Hz beta-range AM helped most in adults with more ADHD symptoms **[SMALL/MECH]** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07026-3)). The participants were symptom-scored adults, not diagnosed patients, and the outcomes were short-term lab tasks. **Two Author Corrections were published, and both were read on 9 Nov 2026.** The 8 September 2025 correction added an omitted author, Bernardo Cordovez ([doi:10.1038/s42003-025-08777-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08777-3)). The 13 April 2026 correction replaced "The authors declare no competing interests" with the authors' actual conflicts: the lead author is a Brain.fm employee holding unexercised equity options, and a co-author was a Brain.fm employee holding equity when the research was done ([doi:10.1038/s42003-026-10015-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10015-3)). Neither correction retracts or alters the experimental findings, but the undisclosed conflict matters when weighing them. Company marketing calls it a "breakthrough … world's first science-backed" ADHD music, which goes beyond the paper **[PRACT]** ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/brainfms-adhd-music-breakthrough-peer-reviewed-study-validates-worlds-first-science-backed-purpose-built-focus-music-302343487.html)). The AM mechanism is better grounded than binaural entrainment, because AM reliably drives the ASSR. Independent replication is missing. Endel has label deals with Warner and Universal but **no independent efficacy study [PRACT]** ([Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/an-algorithmwith-a-record-deal/index.html)). Holosync's evidence is essentially testimonial; one commentator could "never find a randomized controlled study" **[PRACT]** ([Hearing Health Matters](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/?p=5714)). BrainTap's product-specific evidence "is still limited" **[SMALL/PRACT]** ([NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/brain-tap/)). iAwake, Calm/Headspace soundscapes and "Neuro Hacker" were not found or not searched **⟨gap⟩**.

The best-supported "biohack" is not entrainment but **slow breathing paced by sound**. HRV biofeedback at about 6 breaths per minute showed large reductions in self-reported stress and anxiety (g ≈ 0.81–0.83, 24 small studies) **[MA/RCT]** ([Goessl et al. 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effect-of-heart-rate-variability-biofeedback-training-on-stress-and-anxiety-a-metaanalysis/A839E9C968E54774DF5C8FB186764EF0)). Here the audio is a pacer. The active ingredient is the breathing.

## Sleep audio splits into timed cues that work and continuous noise that may not

The sleep literature turns on one technical distinction: whether sound is timed to a detected brain rhythm (closed-loop) or simply played (open-loop). Closed-loop auditory stimulation (CLAS) delivers pink-noise clicks at the up-phase of slow oscillations. It reliably enlarges slow waves, and this is the most replicated finding in the domain. Ngo 2013 also reported better overnight memory **[SMALL ⟨from memory⟩]** ([Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.006)). But a 2023 meta-analysis of 14 studies found **effect sizes declining steadily with publication year, explaining about 92% of heterogeneity**, which suggests the original memory effect was inflated **[MA/RCT]** ([PMC12713896](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12713896/)). Henin 2019 reproduced the slow-wave boost with **no memory benefit [REFUTED for memory ⟨from memory⟩]** ([eNeuro](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0306-19.2019)). The two older-adult studies conflict. Papalambros 2017 reported memory benefit in older adults **[SMALL]** ([PMC5340797](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5340797/)). Schneider, Born, Ngo and colleagues, comparing young and older adults directly, found much weaker responses and **no memory benefit in the older group [SMALL]** ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.15.876847.full.pdf)). Schneider is better supported: it is the more direct comparison, and the meta-analytic decline and a 2020 review reporting "less consistent effects on memory" in the elderly both point the same way ([MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2076-3425/10/5/300)). Consumer closed-loop headbands showed physiological gains only: Dreem +43.9% delta power after stimulation **[SMALL]** ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00088/pdf)), and Philips SmartSleep a company-claimed +6.8% slow-wave activity **[PRACT]** ([Philips](https://www.philips.com/c-dam/corporate/newscenter/global/case-studies/smartsleep/SmartSleep.pdf)). The current commercial status of both is **⟨unverified⟩**. Elemind's alpha-phase sound cut sleep-onset latency by about 10.5 minutes versus sham (n = 21) in a company-funded crossover **[SMALL, conflict of interest]** ([medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.10.24301120v1.full.pdf+html)). Its marketing figures ("76% fell asleep faster," "48% reduction") could not be matched to the trial **[PRACT]**.

Open-loop sleep audio is weaker. A GRADE-rated systematic review (Riedy et al. 2021, ~38 studies) rated certainty that continuous noise improves sleep **very low**, and warned that it may harm sleep and hearing in some cases **[review; very low certainty]** ([EM-Consulte](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1421191/article/noise-as-a-sleep-aid-a-systematic-review)). A 2026 Penn lab study found **continuous 50 dB pink noise reduced REM by about 19 minutes** and did not deepen sleep **[SMALL; contradicts pink-noise marketing]** ([Penn Almanac](https://almanac.upenn.edu/articles/pink-noise-reduces-rem-sleep-and-may-harm-sleep-quality)). "Pink noise for deep sleep" therefore conflates timed lab bursts with continuous app noise. Music is the open-loop exception. The 2022 Cochrane update (13 trials, n = 1,007) found music **probably produces a large improvement in *subjective* sleep quality** in insomnia, with no clear objective improvement, high risk of bias, and no adverse events **[MA/RCT]** ([Cochrane CD010459](https://www.cochrane.org/CD010459)).

Targeted memory reactivation (TMR), which replays a learning-associated sound during NREM sleep, has the strongest pooled sleep evidence: **91 experiments, N = 2,004, g = 0.29**, effective in N2/N3 and **not in REM or wake [MA/RCT]** ([Hu et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7144680)). It is a lab memory paradigm, it requires sleep-stage detection, and it predates the replication-decline concerns raised for CLAS.

For dreaming and lucid dreaming, a 2012 systematic review of 35 low-quality studies concluded that **no induction technique was verified to work "reliably and consistently" [review of SMALL studies]** ([Stumbrys 2012](https://www.pubmed.gov/22841958/)). Targeted Lucidity Reactivation (Carr et al.) pairs a pre-sleep sound or light cue with a lucid mindset and replays it in REM, producing lucidity in **about 50% of cued participants in one lab nap [SMALL]** ([Swansea Cronfa](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa53997)). The notes correct a common error: TLR was published in *Psychology of Consciousness*, not *Current Biology*. Konkoly et al. 2021 showed two-way communication with lucid dreamers across four labs, but **only 18.4% of 158 attempts were answered correctly [SMALL/MECH]** ([Konkoly 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026)). One outlet reports 57 sessions instead. The 158-attempt figure comes from the primary paper and is better supported; the two figures may count different units. A 2024 smartphone TLR raised lucidity but could not detect REM, risking mistimed cues that disrupt sleep **[SMALL, uncontrolled]** ([Northwestern 2024](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2024/10/leveraging-the-power-of-lucid-dreams)). The galantamine + MILD RCT is pharmacological, not sound **[MA/RCT, non-sound ⟨from memory⟩]** ([LaBerge 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201246)). Remee, Neuroon and Prophetic's Halo have **no peer-reviewed efficacy data [PRACT]**. SSILD has no controlled trial **[PRACT]**.

## Consciousness programs sell real experiences on unproven mechanisms

**The Monroe Institute** is a 501(c)(3) founded in 1971 in Faber, Virginia, by radio executive Robert Monroe, who trademarked his binaural method as Hemi-Sync. Its Gateway curriculum is selling out: about **12,500 Gateway Voyage participants since 2022** (35% more than 2016–19), 80 in-person retreats in 2025 at $2,695 each, and an Expand app with about 386,000 installs ([WIRED, via mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular)). The curriculum promises travel "farther, deeper and faster into different dimensions of consciousness" and includes manifestation and remote viewing **[PRACT]**. The viral authority comes from *Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process* (Lt. Col. Wayne McDonnell, US Army, 9 June 1983, 29 pp.), declassified in the CIA's STARGATE collection in 2003, with a missing page 25 surfaced by Vice in 2021 ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/found-page-25-of-the-cias-gateway-report-on-astral-projection/)). **The McDonnell report itself was not read in this research.** The CIA reading-room link redirected, so its quotes come through WIRED and Vice only ([CIA reading room, not opened](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5.pdf)). By those accounts it is a single analyst's speculative, non-experimental assessment built on popular-physics analogies ("our consciousness is the source of all reality"). Its consciousness claims grade **[PRACT]**, and declassification is not endorsement. That Army INSCOM commissioned it is **⟨unverified⟩**, as is the Hemi-Sync patent number (US 3,884,218). Remote viewer Joe McMoneagle trained at Monroe, and WIRED notes "an absence of scientific evidence to support the institute's methods." The 2025 Neuphoria "higher states" study is participant-funded ($897 each) and company-partnered, with **no results found ⟨unverified⟩**. One user reported weeks of "disorienting instability" alongside transformative change **[PRACT, anecdote]**. Monroe's Focus-level vocabulary (F10, F12, F15, F21) comes from background knowledge and is **⟨unverified⟩** in session. Fair standing: experiential value (deep relaxation, hypnagogic states, a structured contemplative curriculum) is plausible and widely reported. Out-of-body and remote-viewing outcome claims are **[PRACT]**, and the entrainment mechanism is **[MECH, contested]**.

**Source Vibrations** is a direct descendant of that lineage. It is an "intentional music" brand ("Music for Evolution," "we think in frequency") with a YouTube and Bandcamp catalogue, a **Harmonic Mind** course promising "access to non-ordinary states of consciousness" (enrollment closed at fetch), and a **Source Vibrations Sound Clinic in Fairfield, Iowa, opened 1 November 2024** by **Jason and Katherine Wild** with Solomon Davis ([sourcevibrations.com](https://www.sourcevibrations.com/); [Southeast Iowa Union](https://www.southeastiowaunion.com/news/source-vibrations-sound-clinic-to-open-in-downtown-fairfield)). Jason is a hypnotherapist and NLP practitioner who came to the work through the Monroe Hemi-Sync tapes. He reportedly developed 18 treatments "using beat frequencies to train the brain and alter states of consciousness" ([Iowa Source](https://www.iowasource.com/2024/12/13/sound/)). The catalogue blends 432 Hz, solfeggio, theta binaural beats, numerology and sacred geometry ([Bandcamp](https://sourcevibrations1.bandcamp.com/album/deep-sound-healing-432hz-theta-meditation)). No independent clinical evidence exists **[PRACT]**, and its frequency claims inherit the refutations above. A Boulder association surfaced in early search tagging and is **⟨unconfirmed⟩**.

**Jonathan Goldman** popularized harmonic overtone chanting and toning with *Healing Sounds* (1992), the source of "Frequency + Intent = Healing" ([Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Healing-Sounds/Jonathan-Goldman/9781644115831)). No controlled test of his methods was found, and the "intent" term makes the formula unfalsifiable as stated **[PRACT]**. Sources disagree on his organization's name ("Sound Healers Association" vs "Healing Sounds Association," reportedly founded 1982) ([303 Magazine](https://303magazine.com/2021/06/sound-healing-and-the-sound-health-that-follows/)). Neither name is better supported in the notes. His toning overlaps practices with some evidence (slow vocalization and breathing, group singing), but that evidence is not specific to his formula. Pauline Oliveros's Deep Listening is best treated as artistic and contemplative practice, well documented as such, with no controlled health research **[PRACT for health outcomes]** ([Rubin Museum](https://rubinmuseum.org/a-legacy-of-sound-sonic-meditations-by-pauline-oliveros/)). Hans Jenny's cymatics is real standing-wave physics **[MECH]** but **[PRACT]** as healing. Tom Kenyon is **[PRACT]**. Steven Halpern had no sources in session **⟨gap⟩**.

On trance and altered states more broadly, the classic claim that 4–7 Hz drumming drives the brain into theta and causes trance (Neher 1962) was **not reliably replicated [REFUTED/contradicted]** ([SFU thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2022-07/b13031028.pdf)). Hove et al. 2016 instead found, in 15 experienced practitioners, a network reconfiguration with "perceptual decoupling," with no non-practitioner control **[MECH]** ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.07.20.212522v1.full.pdf)). Recent reviews frame trance as rhythm interacting with culture, expectation and practice **[MECH, narrative review]** ([Annals NYAS 2025](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/nyas.15403)). In psilocybin therapy, the quality of the music experience predicted depression reduction at one week while drug intensity did not (n = 19, open-label) **[SMALL, correlational]** ([Kaelen 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29396616/)). Music-evoked chills involve striatal dopamine (PET), and a small crossover RCT showed causally that levodopa increases and risperidone decreases musical pleasure **[MA/RCT design, mechanistic outcome]** ([Ferreri 2019](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/dopamine-modulates-the-reward-experiences-elicited-by-music/)). Ganzfeld and sensory-deprivation audio were not searched **⟨gap⟩**. The honest summary for "consciousness development via sound": sound is a powerful set-and-setting variable and a carrier for contemplative practice. No audio product has demonstrated that it develops consciousness.

## Volume, sleep disruption and flicker are the documented hazards

The best-evidenced hazard is noise dose. The WHO–ITU H.870 safe-listening standard allows about **80 dBA for 40 hours a week for adults and 75 dBA for children**, with +3 dB halving the allowed time. It calls for dose tracking, user prompts and automatic volume reduction **[standards consensus; voluntary]** ([WHO](https://www.who.int/publications-detail/safe-listening-devices-and-systems-a-who-itu-standard); [ITU H.870 summary](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/h/T-REC-H.870-202203-I!!SUM-HTM-E.htm)). Two conflicts in the notes resolve cleanly. One source says 80 h/week, which conflicts with every other source and appears to be a typo; 40 h is better supported ([Canadian Audiologist](https://canadianaudiologist.ca/safe-listening/?output=pdf)). One researcher dates H.870 to 2018 and another to a March 2022 version. Both are right: 2018 is the original (T-REC-H.870-201808), and the ITU page lists a current version approved March 2022.

For audio-only entrainment, **no published case of an audio-triggered seizure was found**. The risk is theoretical, with musicogenic epilepsy (about 1 in 10 million) the edge case ([Epilepsy Society](https://epilepsysociety.org.uk/node/3175)). Absence from these searches is evidence about the searches, not proof of safety. Visual flicker is a different class. About 1 in 4,000 people aged 5–24 have light-provoked seizures, with 15–25 Hz most provocative across a 1–65 Hz range **[established review]** ([Fisher et al. 2005](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/photic-and-pattern-induced-seizures-a-review-for-the-epilepsy-fou/)). Any 40 Hz audiovisual feature would move a product into that class. Sleep-specific harms are REM loss from continuous pink noise, arousal from mistimed cues, and infant sleep machines that can exceed 85 dBA at the crib rail **[measurement ⟨from memory⟩]** ([Hugh 2014](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2013-3617)). Sleeping in earbuds carries mostly minor, poorly studied risks: irritation, infection, wax impaction and missed alarms ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sleeping-with-headphones)). Warnings about pregnancy, pacemakers and psychosis rest on vendor and blog sources, not clinical evidence.

The psychological hazard is real but unmeasured for sound specifically. When meditation adverse effects are actively monitored, **58% of participants reported at least one, 37% had effects that impaired functioning and 6% had lasting bad effects [SMALL]** ([Brown University](https://www.brown.edu/news/2021-05-18/adverse-effects)). Immersive, eyes-closed, trance-oriented sound plausibly shares that profile, including depersonalization and trauma reactivation. No trial measured it, so the absence of reported harm in sound-bath literature most likely reflects absence of measurement. The Cochrane music reviews barely report harms. The documented ethical failure in this field is **claim inflation**, not physical injury.

## The legal line sits at intended use, outcome numbers and other people's audio

FDA's **General Wellness guidance (revised 6 January 2026; guidance, not binding)** keeps a two-part test: general-wellness intent and low risk. Sound framed as relaxation, stress management or sleep support generally falls under enforcement discretion. **Correction (verified against the guidance text, 9 Nov 2026):** mentioning a disease does not by itself make a product a device. The guidance has a second wellness category for claims that a healthy lifestyle choice "may help to reduce the risk of" or "may help living well with" certain chronic diseases or conditions, where that link is generally accepted in peer-reviewed literature or professional statements. Its examples are heart disease, high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes ([FDA guidance, Jan 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download)). What decides device status is intended purpose, functionality and risk. Labeling, advertising, interface or functionality that references diagnostic thresholds, prompts clinical action, gives treatment guidance, claims clinical equivalence, or targets diagnosis, screening, monitoring or management of a disease takes a product out of the wellness policy. Claims that sound treats or manages insomnia, ADHD, anxiety disorder, tinnitus, PTSD or Alzheimer's sit on the device side, and whether any condition-adjacent wording qualifies is a question for counsel ([Covington](https://cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)). Every cleared sound or neuro precedent went through De Novo with RCT evidence and clinician channels: EndeavorRx for ADHD (2020) and Lenire for tinnitus (2023) ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/in-fda-1st-game-based-therapeutic-gets-marketing-ok/579839/); [Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-loss/tinnitus/neuromod-devices-lenire-receives-de-novo-approval-from-fda)). FDA discretion does not shield a product from the FTC. Its 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance expects **RCTs on the product itself** for health claims and treats testimonials as insufficient ([Covington 2023](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide)). Lumosity paid $2M in 2016 over cognitive, age-decline, ADHD and PTSD claims ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)). No FTC action against a sound or frequency product specifically was found, though the search was not targeted enough to rule one out. In the EU and UK, intended purpose *and functionality* decide device status (MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1, June 2025; MHRA digital mental health guidance, February 2025). Adding mood scoring or risk stratification can tip a wellbeing app into a regulated device ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-mental-health-technology-qualification-and-classification); [Emergo](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful)). **EU AI Act Art. 50 (binding, applicable 2 August 2026)** requires providers of *AI systems* that generate synthetic audio to mark it in machine-readable form ([Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content)). **Application is case-specific, not automatic.** The duty does not apply where the system performs an assistive function for standard editing or does not substantially alter the input. Recital 12 excludes from the definition of an AI system "systems that are based on the rules defined solely by natural persons to automatically execute operations" ([Recital 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/12/)), so ordinary procedural or rule-based synthesis is not automatically generative AI. Secondary sources report a transitional period to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 **⟨secondary, not verified in the Official Journal⟩**. Whether a given Soullab work is in scope depends on how it was made: the Night Ground manifests describe "AI-assisted procedural" composition, so each work needs its own analysis. EU and UK consumer-law blacklists and the ASA/CAP Code were summarized from background knowledge and are **⟨not verified this session⟩**.

The researchers combined these sources into a claim ladder. "Designed to support relaxation" and "a soundscape for winding down" are generally safer, though they still need a reasonable basis. "Improves sleep quality," "reduces stress," "clinically proven," "changes your brainwaves" and any quantified outcome need product-specific RCT substantiation. Any claim to treat, diagnose or manage a disease or disorder needs clearance; narrow "living well with" wellness wording is possible only where the lifestyle link is well established, and only with counsel. Additional red flags are "entrains your brain to 40 Hz gamma," Alzheimer's references, and 432/528 Hz cure claims. **This ladder is research synthesis, not legal advice**, and the notes flag every disease-adjacent claim for counsel.

On rights, the US Copyright Office's May 2025 pre-publication report says AI training is **not categorically fair use**, with market harm often decisive ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Copyright-Office-Issues-Key-Guidance-on-Fair-Use-in-Generative-AI-Training)). Label litigation is resolving through licensing: UMG–Udio, WMG–Udio and WMG–Suno settled in late 2025, while UMG/Sony v. Suno and a new Sony suit against Udio (July 2026) were reported active **⟨secondary, unconfirmed in dockets⟩** ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/warner-music-settles-lawsuit-with-ai-music-startup-suno); [AI Law Wiki](https://ailawwiki.com/News_UMG_Sony_Suno_Settlement_Impasse_April_2026)). That purely AI-generated output is uncopyrightable (USCO Part 2, *Thaler*) is **⟨background, not verified this session⟩** ([copyright.gov/ai](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)). Spotify's developer policy forbids using its content to train models, and YouTube's API terms forbid isolating or modifying audio ([Spotify](https://developer.spotify.com/policy/); [YouTube](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies-guide)). Layering tones over streamed third-party audio would likely breach both.

## Implications for Soullab's Sound Studio — design input only

This section is design input. **It authorizes no build, no feature and no outcome claim.** It relates the evidence above to the stated premises of Sound Studio: a self-hosted, privacy-first listening product, with no OpenAI or cloud AI, and rights that fail closed. It also touches the project's existing Live / Designed / Vision claim discipline and the cultural-sovereignty invariant. Where the evidence bears directly on a premise, that is stated as an observation. Everything unresolved is listed as an open question, not a recommendation.

Several observations follow from the graded evidence. The claims that survive scrutiny are experiential and non-specific: listening can accompany relaxation, settling before a stressful event, winding down for sleep, and paced slow breathing. Frequency-specific, entrainment-based, DNA, "Earth frequency," 40 Hz and disease-adjacent claims do not survive at any grade the product could cite. The ADHD noise finding (help for some, harm for others, g −0.21 in typical listeners) and the REM loss under continuous pink noise both show the same thing: a sound aid that helps one listener can backfire for another, so "universal benefit" framing is unsupported. Under the privacy premise, closed-loop and EEG-dependent capabilities (CLAS, TMR, TLR) are the only sleep and dream techniques with controlled evidence. They require brain-state sensing, which would bring biometric data, regulatory exposure and mostly unlicensed or copyleft reference code. Under the no-cloud-AI and fail-closed premises, procedural generation plus CC0 recordings is the lowest-rights-risk path in the survey. ML generation adds a training-provenance question that weight licenses cannot answer, and any generated audio served in the EU falls under Art. 50 marking from August 2026. Lineage traditions (Monroe, Goldman, Oliveros, drumming and chant) carry cultural and contemplative meaning that the evidence grades neither confirm nor dismiss. Describing them in Soullab's own vocabulary would raise the cultural-sovereignty questions the project already governs.

## Conclusion

Taken whole, the evidence inverts the field's own hierarchy of prestige. The most mystified interventions — tuned frequencies, Hemi-Sync, declassified CIA reports — have the weakest evidence. The most mundane — a playlist before surgery, music at bedtime, a sound cue during deep sleep, a breathing pacer — carry the strongest. Where engineered sound does show specific effects (amplitude modulation for some attention-impaired listeners, closed-loop cues in slow-wave sleep), those effects are narrower, smaller, more conditional on the person and more conditional on timing than marketing implies. Several have shrunk under replication. The same pattern explains the paradox at the center of entrainment: brains reliably follow rhythm at the sensory level while minds do not reliably follow it into prescribed states. Context, expectation, breathing and meaning do most of the work, and those cannot be reduced to a frequency.

For a product built on sovereignty, that is a usable finding. The listening experience can stand on what is honest — sound as a companion to settling, attention and practice — while the evidence itself rules out the claims that would erode trust and invite regulatory risk. The real frontier questions are not about which hertz to sell. They are about dose, sleep architecture, listener vulnerability, data custody and rights provenance, and every one of them remains open.
