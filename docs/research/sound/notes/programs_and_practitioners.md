# Named Consciousness/Sound Programs, Companies and Practitioners — Profiles and Evidence

**Method and limits (read first).** These notes are from about 20 web searches and page fetches on 2026-11-09. Most product claims were seen through search summaries. Full texts were read for only five sources: the Brain.fm paper (Nature), a WIRED feature on Monroe (read through a mirror domain), the Source Vibrations home page, the Iowa Source profile, and the Ingendoh et al. 2023 systematic review (already indexed). The CIA reading-room URL redirected to the homepage, so **the McDonnell report itself was not read in this session**; its quotes below come through WIRED and Vice. A claim that could not be sourced in-session is placed under *Gaps*.

**Evidence grades used:** **[A]** meta-analysis/RCT · **[B]** small or uncontrolled trial · **[C]** mechanistic/lab only · **[D]** practitioner/marketing only · **[X]** refuted/contradicted.

**Cross-cutting context: binaural-beat and entrainment science, which underlies Monroe, Holosync, iAwake, BrainTap and Source Vibrations.**
- **[A]** In a 2019 meta-analysis, binaural beats had a medium overall effect across memory, attention, anxiety and pain (22 studies, 35 effect sizes, g = 0.45). Exposure *before* a task beat exposure only *during* it. The analysis is not specific to any brand. — [Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales 2019, *Psychological Research* 83(2):357–372, PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)
- **[X]/[C] for the mechanism.** A 2023 systematic review found 14 EEG studies that met its criteria. Five supported the brainwave-entrainment hypothesis, eight contradicted it and one was mixed. The authors concluded the question "cannot … be settled" and that claims of psychological change attributed to entrainment "should be considered with caution." — [Ingendoh et al. 2023, *PLOS ONE* 18(5):e0286023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286023)
- So: there are modest behavioural or relaxation effects at meta-analytic level, but the *mechanism* every brand advertises ("entrains your brainwaves", "synchronizes hemispheres") is unsettled to contradicted.

## Monroe Institute: Robert Monroe, Hemi-Sync, the Gateway Experience, the 1983 McDonnell/CIA report, Stargate context, peer-reviewed Hemi-Sync studies, and current programs and the Expand app

### Takeaway
The Monroe Institute is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 1971 in Faber, Virginia. Its Gateway curriculum, built on its proprietary binaural-beat audio "Hemi-Sync", is selling out post-pandemic. The 1983 McDonnell report is one Army officer's speculative, non-experimental assessment. It is not a validation, and CIA declassification is not endorsement. No peer-reviewed evidence supports out-of-body or remote-viewing claims. Generic binaural beats have modest relaxation, anxiety and attention effects [A], but the brand's entrainment mechanism is unsettled [X/C].

### Cited Findings
**History and method**
- Robert Monroe was a radio-broadcasting executive. He founded the institute in 1971 in Faber, VA. He began experimenting with binaural beats in the 1950s, later trademarked the method as "Hemi-Sync", and wrote of spontaneous OBEs ("I smoothly floated up over the bed…"). — [WIRED (mirror), 2025](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular)
- The Gateway Process claims to be "a voyage of self-discovery" that takes people "farther, deeper and faster into different dimensions of consciousness." Its curriculum also includes manifestation and remote viewing. **[D]** — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular), quoting [Monroe Institute UK](https://www.monroeinstituteuk.org/the-gateway-process/)
- Hemi-Sync was reportedly awarded US Patent 3,884,218 (1975). This number was **not verified** against USPTO. — [search summary of encyclopedia-style page; unverified](https://keyquest.us.com/monroe-institute.html)

**The 1983 report**
- *Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process*, Lt. Col. Wayne M. McDonnell, US Army, dated 9 June 1983, 29 pp. It was released in the CIA's declassified STARGATE collection in 2003. — [Substack explainer](https://a3dpb.substack.com/p/the-gateway-process-cia-document); primary: [CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5.pdf) (not opened in-session)
- WIRED's context: in the early 1980s, amid fears of a Soviet "psychic warfare" edge, "the CIA and the Department of Defense sent" McDonnell to assess the institute's "suitability as a defense contractor." He writes "There is a sound and rational basis … in terms of physical science parameters for considering Gateway to be plausible in terms of its essential objectives" and "our consciousness is the source of all reality." — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular). One source says Army INSCOM commissioned it; this was **not independently confirmed**. — [search summary](https://a3dpb.substack.com/p/the-gateway-process-cia-document)
- Page 25 (conclusions and recommendations) was missing from the 2003 release. Vice located it in 2021, reportedly from Monroe Institute archives. Vice's 2021 coverage drove the viral wave. — [Vice, "Found: Page 25…"](https://www.vice.com/en/article/found-page-25-of-the-cias-gateway-report-on-astral-projection/); [Vice 2021](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-to-escape-the-confines-of-time-and-space-according-to-the-cia/)
- **Standing:** it is an analyst's assessment that leans on popular-physics analogies (holography, Itzhak Bentov's models). It is not peer-reviewed, not an experiment and not a policy decision. — [SpiritClick explainer](https://spiritclick.com/blog/gateway-process-cia-document-explained); [Open Veils](https://openveils.com/cia-gateway-process-explained). Grade for the report's physics and consciousness claims: **[D]** (speculative; no empirical test).
- **Stargate context:** former Army CWO Joe McMoneagle ("Remote Viewer No. 1") was in Project Stargate, the Army's psychic-intelligence unit. He trained in remote viewing at Monroe and became a Monroe speaker. — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular). WIRED adds: "there is still an absence of scientific evidence to support the institute's methods." — same source

**Current programs (2025–2026)**
- Since 2022 about 12,500 people have taken Gateway Voyage, 35% more than in 2016–19. In 2025 there were 80 in-person retreats of 20 people each, and the campus hit "absolute maximum capacity." In-person retreats cost $2,695 and virtual ones $1,150. Retreats now also run in Romania, Italy, Switzerland and Greece. — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular)
- **Expand app:** launched July 2021; about 386,000 installs per company data. It uses proprietary "Monroe Sound Science" audio. — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1552705313)
- A "higher states of consciousness study" with neurofeedback firm Neuphoria was announced in June 2025. About 333 Gateway graduates each pay $897 to track their own EEG for four weeks. This is participant-funded and company-partnered, not independent. — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular); [Neuphoria page](https://neuphoria.io/pages/unlock-your-consciousness-neuphoria-the-monroe-institute)
- **Harm reports:** a user described sleep-paralysis-like episodes and three weeks of "disorienting instability" after the tapes. She also credits them with transformative change. — [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular). Grade: **[D]**, anecdote.
- Business model: nonprofit; paid residential and virtual retreats, the Expand subscription app, and audio sales. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1552705313); [WIRED](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-cia-used-this-psychic-meditation-program-its-never-been-more-popular)

### Inferences
- The report's viral authority comes from its CIA provenance. Its content gives no evidence for OBE, remote viewing or manifestation. Best framing: "a 1983 Army officer found the program plausible by analogy, and the government later declassified it."
- Experiential value (deep relaxation, hypnagogic and altered-state *experiences*, a structured contemplative curriculum) is plausible and widely reported. Outcome or ontological claims (leaving the body, perceiving remote targets) are **[D]**, and the entrainment mechanism is **[X/C]**.

### Gaps
- I did not find in-session a list of peer-reviewed studies *specific to Hemi-Sync*. Historical Hemi-Sync papers exist, e.g. surgical/anaesthesia trials sometimes cited (Padmanabhan et al. 2005 *Anaesthesia*, binaural beats pre-op). Only a blog summary was found; **unverified**.
- The report's primary PDF and page 25 were not read directly.
- Gateway Wave I–VIII and Focus 10/12/15/21 definitions were not sourced in-session. Monroe's own nomenclature: F10 "mind awake/body asleep", F12 "expanded awareness", F15 "no time", F21 "edge of time-space". These are background knowledge, unverified here.
- I could not confirm whether the Neuphoria study has published results by Nov 2026.

## Other entrainment and soundscape products: Centerpointe Holosync, iAwake, Brain.fm, Endel, BrainTap, Calm/Headspace, Hapbee, Apollo Neuro

### Takeaway
Only **Brain.fm** has a substantive peer-reviewed paper. It is company-affiliated, lab-based, and short-term attention, not ADHD treatment. **Apollo Neuro**'s sleep claim rests on an observational, company and Oura-affiliated dataset. Holosync, iAwake, BrainTap and Hapbee rest essentially on marketing and general entrainment literature. Endel's distinguishing feature is commercial (label deals), not evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Brain.fm**
- Woods, Loui and colleagues, "Rapid modulation in music supports attention in listeners with attentional difficulties," *Communications Biology* 7 (Oct 2024). Music with added amplitude modulation (AM) improved sustained attention when it came early in the session. Rapid AM raised attentional-network fMRI activity and stimulus–brain coupling in EEG. In a parametric experiment, beta-range (16 Hz) AM "helped more than other modulation ranges for participants with more ADHD symptoms" (high ASRS score). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07026-3). Grade: **[B/C]**: multi-experiment lab study of short-term task performance in symptom-scored adults, not diagnosed ADHD and not clinical outcomes.
- **Conflicts of interest:** "K.J.P.W. is an employee of Brain.fm, Inc., the company that produced the music used … holds unexercised equity options"; a second author, A.H., was an employee and equity holder at the time. — [Nature, Competing interests](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07026-3)
- **Two Author Corrections** were published, on 8 Sept 2025 and 13 Apr 2026. Their content was not reviewed. — [Nature article page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07026-3); [2025 correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08777-3); [2026 correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10015-3)
- Company marketing frames the study as a "breakthrough … world's first science-backed purpose-built focus music" for ADHD, which goes beyond the paper. **[D]** — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/brainfms-adhd-music-breakthrough-peer-reviewed-study-validates-worlds-first-science-backed-purpose-built-focus-music-302343487.html)
- Brain.fm says it holds patents on amplitude modulations engineered for "neural phase locking." **No patent number was verified.** — [Brain.fm blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/focus-music-for-adhd)

**Endel**
- Signed a distribution deal with Warner Music Group in 2019, described as the first algorithm to sign with a major label. Later deals: Warner's Spinnin' Records (50 AI wellness albums) and Universal Music Group (2023, "AI-powered, artist-driven functional music"). — [Harvard D3](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/an-algorithmwith-a-record-deal/index.html); [MBW Spinnin'](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warners-spinnin-records-partners-with-generative-ai-startup-endel-to-create-50-ai-generated-wellness-albums/embed/); [MBW Universal](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/universal-inks-deal-with-generative-ai-startup-endel-to-create-ai-powered-artist-driven-functional-music/)
- No independent peer-reviewed efficacy study of Endel was found. **[D]**

**Apollo Neuro (vibration wearable)**
- Company press release on a peer-reviewed study: retrospective real-world data from 935 Apollo + Oura users, "an average of 46 more minutes of sleep per night." — [Apollo press release](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/apollo-neuroscience-announces-publication-of-landmark-clinical-study-demonstrating-apollo-wearable-use-yields-an-average-of-46-more-minutes-of-sleep-per-night/); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/apollo-neuro-touch-therapy-wearable-helps-people-sleep-better-oura-affiliated-study/). Grade: **[B]**, observational, self-selected users, company and Oura-affiliated.

**Centerpointe Holosync**
- Results are almost entirely vendor pages and testimonials. A commentator could "never find a randomized controlled study of Holosync." **[D]** — [Holosync vendor/educational page](https://holosync-centerpointe.pages.dev/technology.html); [Hearing Health Matters](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/?p=5714)

**BrainTap**
- A review says product-specific clinical evidence "is still limited"; cited studies are "often pilot studies." Trustpilot complaints focus on billing. **[B/D]** — [NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/brain-tap/); [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/braintap.com)

**Hapbee and the regulatory context**
- No FTC or FDA action against Hapbee was found. — [Trustpilot (only results)](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/hapbee.com)
- Sector context: a 2019 analysis found consumer brain-wearable claims cite "general scientific papers not specific to the device and … even entirely irrelevant." The FTC has acted against brain-training marketers (Lumos Labs 2016; LearningRx), requiring "competent and reliable" substantiation. — [Sapien Labs 2019](https://sapienlabs.org/the-fantastical-claims-of-consumer-brain-wearables/); [Fortune 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/01/10/a-crackdown-on-companies-claiming-they-can-improve-your-brain); [The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/do-devices-that-monitor-or-zap-the-brain-live-up-to-their-claims---66021)

### Inferences
- Brain.fm is the most scientifically serious of these products, but its key paper is industry-authored and has been corrected twice. Independent replication is the missing step. Its claimed mechanism (AM → neural phase-locking) is a different, arguably better-grounded mechanism than binaural "entrainment," since AM reliably drives auditory steady-state responses.
- No FTC enforcement specifically against a *sound/audio* entrainment product was found. The precedent risk comes from the brain-training cases.

### Gaps
- **iAwake Technologies:** no research or profile found.
- **Calm/Headspace soundscapes:** not searched in-session. Their published RCTs are on meditation apps generally, not soundscapes; unverified.
- Brain.fm patent numbers and the content of the two corrections.
- **"Neuro Hacker":** could not be identified as a distinct brand; may conflate with Neurohacker Collective (supplements). Unverified.
- Hapbee's claims (it markets "ultra-low radio frequency energy," not sound) were not sourced.

## Jonathan Goldman: Healing Sounds Association, "Frequency + Intent = Healing", vocal harmonics, books; any evidence?

### Takeaway
Goldman is a foundational popularizer of sound healing (harmonic overtone chanting, toning, "Frequency + Intent = Healing"). His work is practitioner and contemplative. I found no controlled test of his formula or methods, and the "intent" term makes the formula unfalsifiable as stated. **[D]**

### Cited Findings
- *Healing Sounds: The Power of Harmonics* (Healing Arts Press, 1992; 30th-anniversary edition, Inner Traditions) introduces the formula "Frequency + Intent = Healing" and covers mantra and chakra chanting, vowels, toning, conscious listening, cymatics and "sonic shamanism." — [Simon & Schuster listing](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Healing-Sounds/Jonathan-Goldman/9781644115831); [Wikipedia mirror](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.dweb.link/wiki/Jonathan_Goldman.html)
- His organization, the Sound Healers Association, was reportedly founded in 1982. Sources vary on the name ("Healers"/"Healing Sounds Association"). — [303 Magazine](https://303magazine.com/2021/06/sound-healing-and-the-sound-health-that-follows/)
- He describes intent as "the intent or purpose behind the sound." — [Next Level Soul interview](https://nextlevelsoul.com/?p=101238)
- Skeptical commentary likens the formula to Emoto's water-crystal claims. This is a blog, not peer review. **[X-adjacent opinion]** — [Respectful Insolence 2007](https://www.respectfulinsolence.com/2007/01/26/your-friday-dose-of-woo-the-hills-are-al/)

### Inferences
- Goldman's toning and chanting overlap with practices that *do* have some evidence base: slow vocalization, slow breathing, group singing and humming effects on HRV and mood. That evidence is not specific to his formula, and it is not covered here.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of Goldman's methods was found. Current (2026) activity, courses and pricing not checked.

## Source Vibrations: what it is precisely (people, offerings, claims), as of 2026

It is run by **Jason and Katherine Wild** with collaborator Solomon Davis. Jason is a hypnotherapist and NLP practitioner whose route in was the Monroe Hemi-Sync tapes. Claims combine 432 Hz/Solfeggio, binaural and theta entrainment, sacred geometry and numerology. **[D]** throughout. Earlier search tagging associated some tracks with Boulder; this was **not confirmed**.

There are also "Praxis Live Meditation" offerings. — [sourcevibrations.com (fetched 2026-11-09)](https://www.sourcevibrations.com/)

## Brief profiles: Oliveros/Deep Listening, Tom Kenyon, Steven Halpern, Hans Jenny cymatics, Alfred Tomatis, Guy Bérard (AIT), Safe and Sound Protocol (Porges/Unyte)

### Takeaway
Oliveros and Jenny are best treated as artistic, contemplative and phenomenological contributions, not health interventions. Kenyon and Halpern are practitioner and marketing **[D]**. Tomatis is supported only by small, old studies **[B]**. Bérard's AIT has a **negative Cochrane conclusion** **[X]**. The Safe and Sound Protocol has a few small, mostly developer-linked trials **[B]**, while its underlying polyvagal theory faces substantial peer-reviewed challenge.

### Cited Findings
**Pauline Oliveros / Deep Listening**
- Composer (died 2016). Her "Sonic Meditations" are framed as a healing practice. She founded the Deep Listening Institute to "strive for a heightened consciousness of the world of sound and the sound of the world." Scholarship treats it as an experiential and embodied way of knowing (Shanken & Harris, "A Sounding Happens"). — [Rubin Museum](https://rubinmuseum.org/a-legacy-of-sound-sonic-meditations-by-pauline-oliveros/); [SFU journal article](https://journals.lib.sfu.ca/index.php/aer/article/view/6037); [RPI](https://everydaymatters.rpi.edu/healing-through-deep-listening/). No controlled health research found. Grade for health outcomes: **[D]**; contemplative and artistic value is well documented.

**Tom Kenyon**
- Described as a sound practitioner, psychotherapist, and "pioneer in psychoacoustic research." His "Hathor" material (channelled messages) is voice-only multitrack, e.g. "The Cave of Altruin." **[D]** — [Orcasonian](https://theorcasonian.com/tom-kenyon-notes); [Future of Wellness podcast](https://buzzsprout.com/1958873/14738008-sound-healing-neuroscience-expanded-consciousness-with-tom-kenyon?t=0)

**Steven Halpern** — see Gaps (no in-session sources).

**Hans Jenny (cymatics)**
- A Swiss physician who in the 1960s documented sound-induced patterns in matter and coined "Cymatics." This account is via Goldman, a practitioner. — [FindCenter / Goldman](https://findcenter.com/article/29054/jonathan-goldman/everything-else). Grade: **[C]** as physics (Chladni-type standing-wave pattern formation is real). **[D]** as a healing claim. Wikipedia editors dispute online "cymatic healing" claims as pseudoscience. — [Talk:Cymatics](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Cymatics)

**Alfred Tomatis (Tomatis Method)**
- The only meta-analysis (Gilmor 1999) pooled 5 small 1980s studies (n = 231) of children with learning and communication disorders, not autism. — [Tomatis-hosted copy](https://issuu.com/tomatisdoc/docs/the_efficacy_of_the_tomatis_method_). A 2020 integrative review on autism found most studies supportive, but noted RCTs are needed. — [Brbić & Tomić 2020, Hrčak](https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/354581). Grade: **[B]**, with company-hosted dissemination.

**Guy Bérard (Auditory Integration Training)**
- A Cochrane review (Sinha et al. 2011, CD003681): "no evidence that auditory integration therapy or other sound therapies are effective as treatments for ASD." The three better trials (Bettison 1996; Zollweg 1997; Mudford 2000) showed no benefit over control. — [Cochrane](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003681.pub3). ASHA's position: AIT is not supported outside research protocols. — [ASHA TR2004-00260](https://www.asha.org/policy/TR2004-00260/). Grade: **[X]** (refuted for ASD treatment).

**Safe and Sound Protocol (Stephen Porges; Unyte/Integrated Listening)**
- Porges says a 2014 randomized trial showed reduced auditory hypersensitivity and improved spontaneous speech and emotional control in autism. Trials are few and largely from his group. — [The Transmitter/Spectrum](https://thetransmitter.org/spectrum/brain-body-connection-may-ease-autistic-peoples-social-problems). Grade: **[B]**, developer-run.
- Registered RCTs: NCT06701578 (depression/anxiety vs unfiltered music, n ≈ 120, quadruple-masked) and NCT04999852 (PTSD/anxiety). No results were posted in what I found. A 2023 Japanese adult-autism pilot had n = 6. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06701578](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06701578); [NCT04999852](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04999852)
- Unyte's own "real-world evidence," e.g. "61% … moved from clinical to non-clinical level for anxiety," is uncontrolled company data. **[D]** — [Unyte](https://integratedlistening.com/research/unytes-real-world-evidence)
- **Theory under challenge:** Grossman 2023, "Fundamental challenges and likely refutations of the five basic premises of the polyvagal theory," *Biological Psychology* 180:108589. Neuhuber & Berthoud (2022) dispute its anatomical and phylogenetic claims. Proponents published a 2024 rebuttal. — [secondary summary](https://umwelt-67q.pages.dev/01-science/topic-overviews/polyvagal-theory-and-the-safe-and-sound-protocol); [Your Parenting Mojo](https://yourparentingmojo.com/?p=10363). Grade for the theory's physiological premises: **[X]** (contested and likely refuted per critics). The SSP's empirical effects could stand or fall independently of the theory.
- Business model: licensed to clinicians through Unyte subscriptions (provider training plus app delivery). Inferred from the Unyte site; pricing not checked.

### Inferences
- A useful split for the report: AIT, Tomatis and SSP make *clinical* claims, so the [A]/[X] standard applies. Oliveros, Jenny and Kenyon offer *experiential, artistic or contemplative* practice and should be judged accordingly, with health claims flagged separately.

### Gaps
- **Steven Halpern** (Spectrum Suite, 1975; "anti-frantic alternative" music; Grammy nominations): no in-session sources. Background only, unverified.
- Tomatis: no recent (post-2020) RCT located. Company: Tomatis Développement, licensing devices and training to practitioners (unverified).
- Whether any SSP RCT published results in 2025–2026.
- No FTC action against any of these named practitioners or products was found (absence of search evidence only).
