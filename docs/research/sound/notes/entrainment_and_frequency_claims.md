# Auditory Brainwave Entrainment and "Special Frequency" Claims — Evidence-Graded Notes

Researched 2026-11-09. Grade key: **[MA/RCT]** meta-analysis or RCT · **[SMALL]** small or uncontrolled trial · **[MECH]** mechanistic/lab only · **[MKT]** practitioner/marketing only · **[REFUTED]** refuted/contradicted.
Method note: about 17 search/fetch calls. Full text was read for Ingendoh et al. 2023 (PMC) and the PsyPost report on the 2026 Brain and Behavior 528/432 Hz study. Everything else comes from abstracts, PubMed records or secondary reports, and is flagged where that matters. Items recalled from background knowledge but not re-verified in this session are listed under Gaps, not as findings.

## 1. What do the meta-analyses and systematic reviews conclude (effect sizes, methodological quality)?

### Takeaway
Two behavioural meta-analyses report pooled medium effects of binaural beats on cognition, anxiety and pain: g = 0.45 (2019) and g = 0.40 (2022/23, memory and attention only). Both sit on small, heterogeneous, inconsistent study sets. The newer systematic reviews (pain 2024; young adults 2026) mostly decline to pool because of heterogeneity and high risk of bias. The only systematic review of the mechanism (Ingendoh 2023) found that most EEG studies do **not** support entrainment.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA/RCT] Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales, *Psychological Research* 2019;83(2):357–372 (online August 2018).**
  - 22 studies and 35 effect sizes; overall "medium, significant, consistent" g = 0.45 across memory, attention, anxiety and analgesia — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/).
  - The effect depended on beat frequency, exposure duration and timing. Exposure before the task, or before and during it, beat exposure during the task only. Masking with white or pink noise made no difference — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/).
  - Heterogeneity statistics, publication-bias tests and quality ratings were not verified (no full text).
- **[MA/RCT] Basu & Banerjee, *Psychological Research* 2023;87(4):951–963 (online July 2022), "Potential of binaural beats intervention for improving memory and attention."**
  - 15 studies and 31 effect sizes; overall g = 0.40.
  - The accompanying systematic review found "conflicting results, especially concerning theta and beta's efficacy" on recall, recognition and attention tasks, and called for stronger designs — [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/); [BSL record](https://mijn.bsl.nl/potential-of-binaural-beats-intervention-for-improving-memory-an/23276138).
- **[MECH — systematic review of EEG studies] Ingendoh, Posny & Heine, *PLoS ONE* 2023;18(5):e0286023.**
  - 14 studies passed the inclusion criteria and the quality assessment: 5 were consistent with entrainment, 8 contradicted it, and 1 was mixed. The authors concluded the research question "cannot be settled" and that applied claims built on entrainment "should be considered with caution" — [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
  - The samples were mostly young adults (often students). Theta was the band used most (10 of 14 studies). One study used a 57.3 Hz beat, above the roughly 30 Hz perceptual limit. Exposures ran 1–30 minutes. EEG metrics (absolute or relative power, ASSR, FFR, source localisation) and statistics varied widely — [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
- **[SR] Pain, 2024 (*BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies*).** Most studies were at high risk of bias, evidence quality was low to very low, and a meta-analysis was judged inappropriate. This is from search-result summaries only — [PMC11893577 / BMC listing via search](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11893577/).
- **[SR] Young adults aged 19–24, 2015–2025 (Cambridge, published February 2026).** Covered music therapy, binaural beats and other auditory entrainment. Reported small-to-moderate benefits for sleep, anxiety and stress, but did not pool because of heterogeneity — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4282E1152FD3EACA5DF8304935165202). This is a secondary summary; full text not verified.
- **[SR] Anxiety and depression, 2024 (MDPI, PRISMA, 12 articles).** "Promising" for anxiety and depression, but a narrative synthesis with no pooled estimate — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2850398).
- **[SMALL] A later sustained-attention experiment** that cited the meta-analytic attention effect (about 0.58) found its own effect "modest," with Bayes factors that could not distinguish models with and without the effect — [AZ Regents record](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-binaural-beat-stimulation-on-sustained-attention/).

### Inferences
- The two meta-analyses overlap heavily in the studies they include, so they are not independent confirmations. A g of about 0.4–0.45 built on small, heterogeneous, often unblinded studies is consistent with publication bias and expectancy effects. Treat it as an upper bound, not a calibrated estimate.
- The behavioural meta-analyses and the EEG systematic review disagree: medium behavioural effects alongside weak or inconsistent evidence for the proposed mechanism. Either behavioural effects run through something other than frequency-specific entrainment (arousal, relaxation, expectancy, sound as such), or they are inflated.
- Garcia-Argibay is still the most-cited synthesis, but for the mechanism it has been superseded by Ingendoh 2023. Newer reviews (2024–2026) have not produced a higher-quality pooled estimate.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 meta-analysis with GRADE-style certainty ratings that pools cognition and anxiety together. I also found no Cochrane review of binaural beats.
- I found no large preregistered replication. The closest was a 2025 Bayesian thesis (UCT) with mixed or null memory results — [UCT](https://open.uct.ac.za/items/3ad08e8f-5d02-4505-8662-c089ab3848c5).
- Publication-bias statistics (funnel plots, trim-and-fill) for both meta-analyses were not verified.

## 2. Does a frequency-following response / EEG entrainment occur for each stimulus type? At which frequencies, how strongly, and where (cortical vs brainstem)?

### Takeaway
Rhythmic auditory stimuli reliably evoke brain responses: a brainstem frequency-following response (FFR) at the carrier tones, and a cortical auditory steady-state response (ASSR) at the beat or modulation rate, strongest around 40 Hz. **Monaural beats (and by extension amplitude-modulated and isochronic stimuli) drive the cortex more strongly than binaural beats.** Evidence that binaural beats shift ongoing EEG band power in the claimed "theta = meditation, alpha = relaxation" way is inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- **[MECH] Orozco Perez, Dumas & Lehmann, *eNeuro* 2020.** Both binaural and monaural beats produced subcortical FFR at the pure-tone frequencies and entrained the cortex at the beat frequency (ASSR). Binaural beats "weakly entrain the cortex when compared with monaural beat stimuli." Binaural beats alone produced cross-frequency connectivity. Neither stimulus changed mood — [PubMed 32066611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066611/); [PMC7082494](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7082494).
- **[MECH]** Across Ingendoh's 14 studies, power increases consistent with entrainment appeared in theta (after 6–10 minutes of stimulation), alpha (after 5 minutes) and gamma (after 15 minutes) in a minority of studies. Several well-instrumented studies found no band-specific power increase in delta, theta, alpha or beta — [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
- **[MECH]** Binaural beats are perceivable only at roughly 1–30 Hz differences, which overlaps the conventional EEG bands — the premise of the entrainment hypothesis — [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
- **[MECH / SMALL] 40 Hz amplitude-modulated sound (combined with light, the "GENUS" approach).**
  - Reliably evokes 40 Hz steady-state EEG power in people. In an MIT open-label extension (n = 5, about 2 years), 40 Hz power rose in the late-onset AD patients but the response declined in the early-onset patients.
  - The randomized pilot reported safety and exploratory outcomes. The Cognito OVERTURE posters (company-presented) reported preserved corpus callosum structure compared with a natural-history comparison.
  - No large pivotal RCT result was found — [PMC9714926](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9714926/); [MIT news 2025](https://bcs.mit.edu/news/study-suggests-40hz-sensory-stimulation-may-benefit-some-alzheimers-patients-years); [NeurologyLive 2025](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/cognito-gamma-sensory-stimulation-leads-preserved-corpus-callosum-structure-alzheimers).
- **[MKT] Isochronic tones.** Claims that isochronic tones are "the strongest" entrainment method came only from vendor pages. One source noted there is "very little data" directly comparing isochronic tones with binaural beats — [6thmind substack](https://6thmind.substack.com/p/brainwave-entrainment-vs-binaural); [brain.fm blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/binaural-beats-safety).

### Inferences
- Ranking by cortical drive (mechanistic): amplitude-modulated / isochronic / monaural > binaural. The ASSR is a stimulus-locked sensory response, strongest near 40 Hz and weak at theta/delta rates. It is not evidence that the whole brain adopts a "state." A stimulus-locked response does not by itself imply a change in cognition or mood (Orozco Perez: entrainment, but no mood effect).
- The "theta beats induce meditative states" claim rests on the weakest part of the evidence: low beat rates, binaural presentation, and inconsistent power changes.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed head-to-head EEG comparison of isochronic vs binaural vs monaural stimuli in this session. A 2025 *Brain Sciences* review of audio-visual entrainment was mentioned but not retrieved.
- The size of the binaural-beat brainstem response relative to the cortical one could not be quantified from verified sources.

## 3. Which outcomes have RCT support, and which are null or mixed?

### Takeaway
The most consistent RCT signal is **acute pre-procedural or pre-operative anxiety** (several small to moderate RCTs, including ones with active audio controls). Attention and memory are mixed. Pain is low or very low certainty. Mood and focus claims from EEG-linked studies are often null. No outcome reaches high certainty.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA/RCT] Pre-operative anxiety — Padmanabhan et al., *Anaesthesia* 2005.** Day-case general-anaesthesia patients. Anxiety fell about 26% with binaural beats, about 11% with an identical soundtrack without beats, and about 4% with no intervention; the binaural group differed significantly from both — [PubMed 16115248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16115248/). Notable as one of the few trials with a sound-matched control.
- **[MA/RCT] Dental anxiety — Isik et al., *Br J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2017.** 10 minutes of binaural beats before local anaesthesia reduced anxiety; controls did not change — [PubMed 28325532](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28325532/).
- **[MA/RCT] Dental anxiety, binaural beats vs 432 Hz music (2020).** Both reduced anxiety relative to controls, with no difference between them — [PMC7806348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7806348/). This implies a general "audio intervention" effect rather than a beat-specific one.
- **[MA/RCT] Caesarean section, *J Psychosom Obstet Gynecol* 2021.** A novel binaural-beat audio reduced anxiety more than music or standard care — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/a50cfe89cf0a4dce86392cec613ba0f6).
- **[MA/RCT] Pterygium surgery, *JPMA* 2025, n = 111.** Compared superimposed binaural beats, music and silence; results not verified — [JPMA](https://jpma.org.pk/index.php/public_html/article/view/21023).
- **[MA] Dental pain and anxiety meta-analysis (2025, *Int J Clin Pediatr Dent*, 9 studies)** — from search summary only; effect size not verified.
- **Pain:** the 2024 SR rated the evidence low or very low and found pooling inappropriate — see Q1.
- **Attention and memory:** the meta-analytic g of about 0.40 comes with "conflicting" theta/beta results (Basu & Banerjee). An individual sustained-attention study found a modest effect with indeterminate Bayes factors. A 2025 thesis found high-beta beats did not improve memory, while gamma helped immediate recall only with visual presentation — [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/); [AZ Regents](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-binaural-beat-stimulation-on-sustained-attention/); [UCT](https://open.uct.ac.za/items/3ad08e8f-5d02-4505-8662-c089ab3848c5).
- **[SMALL] 40 Hz binaural beats in medical students** (mood and cognition), *PubMed 39535938* — title only seen; design not verified — [PubMed 39535938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39535938/).
- **Mood: null** in a controlled EEG study (Orozco Perez 2020) — [PubMed 32066611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066611/).

### Inferences
- In the anxiety trials, the active ingredient is unresolved. When binaural beats are compared with no-sound or standard-care controls, any relaxing audio is a confound. Padmanabhan is the cleaner exception, but it is single-site and from 2005, and has not been widely replicated with blinding.
- Reasonable current framing: "relaxing audio before a procedure reduces anxiety somewhat (moderate evidence); a specific contribution from the beat itself is plausible but small and uncertain (low certainty)."

### Gaps
- Wiwatwongwana et al. 2016 (cataract surgery, binaural beats vs music) could not be retrieved, so its result is not reported here.
- ADHD, sleep-onset and depression RCTs were not verified in this session (sleep is covered in the sibling file `sleep_dream_lucid_gamma.md`).

## 4. Provenance and peer-reviewed evidence for solfeggio, 432 Hz, "528 Hz DNA repair" and Schumann claims

### Takeaway
The solfeggio set is a 1970s/1990s numerological construction (Puleo → Horowitz), not a medieval tuning. The 432 Hz "natural/Verdi/anti-Nazi" story is historically false. The 528 Hz DNA-repair claim has no supporting evidence. The peer-reviewed literature that does exist is a handful of tiny, poorly controlled pilots plus one 2026 study whose 432 Hz arm went *against* the marketing. Schumann resonance is real geophysics, but the brain-health link is unsupported.

### Cited Findings
- **[MKT → REFUTED as history] Solfeggio provenance.**
  - Joseph Puleo, a naturopath, "discovered" the frequencies in the mid-1970s through numerological calculations on Bible verses (Numbers 7). They were popularised by Leonard Horowitz in *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse* (1999).
  - The claimed link to Guido d'Arezzo's ut-re-mi hymn and Gregorian chant is unsupported, because medieval pitch was not absolute — [RationalWiki](https://kiwix.lema.org/rationalwiki_en_all_maxi_2021-03/A/Solfeggio_frequencies); [Skeptics SE](https://kiwix.lema.org/skeptics.stackexchange.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/8377/does-528-hz-facilitate-dna-repair). These are secondary skeptic sources; Horowitz's text was not read directly.
- **[MKT / REFUTED] "528 Hz repairs DNA."** It traces to Puleo/Horowitz assertions that it is "the exact frequency used by genetic biochemists to repair broken DNA," with no references beyond Puleo — [Women of Grace review](https://womenofgrace.com/blog/75568); [Skeptics SE](https://kiwix.lema.org/skeptics.stackexchange.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/8377/does-528-hz-facilitate-dna-repair). No peer-reviewed DNA-repair study was found. The "biochemists" claim matches no known laboratory practice. (Inference: DNA repair is enzymatic, and audible sound at normal intensities carries no plausible frequency-specific mechanism.)
- **[SMALL] Akimoto et al. 2018, *Health* (SCIRP), 528 Hz vs 440 Hz music.**
  - n = 9 (8 women), each serving as their own control.
  - After 528 Hz: lower tension-anxiety and Total Mood Disturbance, lower salivary cortisol, higher oxytocin; chromogranin A only trended. No such changes after 440 Hz.
  - No randomization detail or blinding verified; not replicated; published in a low-tier journal — [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/journal/PaperInformation.aspx?paperID=87146).
- **[SMALL] Bozok et al., *Brain and Behavior* 2026 (doi 10.1002/brb3.71452).**
  - n = 162 Turkish students, 18–25, tested before exams. Silence vs 528 Hz vs 432 Hz music, 20 minutes on headphones, then salivary BDNF and other proteins, then a Stroop test.
  - 528 Hz: BDNF comparable to the silent control, and the highest level of a "memory-building protein."
  - 432 Hz: significantly higher levels of a cellular-stress marker.
  - **No difference in Stroop performance across groups.**
  - The author calls it a "small, short-term pilot." Salivary proteins are "not a direct window into the brain" — [PsyPost, 19 June 2026](https://www.psypost.org/listening-to-specific-music-frequencies-alters-stress-markers-in-students-facing-exams/).
  - Note: the 432 Hz arm contradicts 432 Hz relaxation marketing.
- **[SMALL] Calamassi & Pomponi, *Explore (NY)* 2019;15(4):283–290.** A double-blind crossover *pilot* of 440 vs 432 Hz music reported a slight heart-rate decrease with 432 Hz; described as underpowered — citation via [Acta Biomedica](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/10755); summary via [slowwavelabs](https://slowwavelabs.pages.dev/learn/is-432-hz-scientifically-proven). Sample size is reported inconsistently across secondary sources (one blog says 33), so it is unverified. The same group published small Acta Biomedica follow-ups: sleep in spinal-cord injury (n = 12, 2020) and nurses' anxiety (2022) — [Acta Biomedica 12915](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/12915).
- **[SMALL] Dental RCT, 2020:** 432 Hz music reduced anxiety no more than binaural beats, and both beat the control — [PMC7806348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7806348/). An EEG comparison found no significant delta-amplitude difference between 432 and 528 Hz music — [Resital journal](https://journal.isi.ac.id/index.php/resital/article/view/17472).
- **[REFUTED as history] 432 Hz / 440 Hz.**
  - A 1939 London conference recommended A = 440 as a compromise among many pitches then in use, some above 450 Hz.
  - Historian Jeffrey Herf (contacted by Reuters) dismissed the claim that Goebbels or the Nazis imposed 440 Hz to manipulate people — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/did-nazis-change-the-universal-music-pitch-to-use-against-their-enemies-675733).
  - Ellis's 1880 survey found historical pitch ranged from 374 to 567 Hz. France fixed A = 435 in 1859.
  - 432 Hz "has NEVER been a National or International standard." Verdi's interest was practical (singers' voices), not cosmic — [roelhollander.eu](https://roelhollander.eu/en/432-tuning/432-as-standard/); [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/debunking-432-hz-tuning-standard); [Jakub Marian](https://jakubmarian.com/the-432-hz-vs-440-hz-conspiracy-theory/).
- **[MECH (physics real) / MKT (health claims)] Schumann resonance.**
  - The Earth–ionosphere cavity resonance is real: about 7.83 Hz, with harmonics near 14.3, 20.8, 27.3 and 33.8 Hz.
  - Herbert König's observation that it overlaps the alpha range is the historical root of the brain claims. But alpha is a band (about 7.5–12.5 Hz), not one frequency, and claims about hypnosis, growth hormone or cortisol have no scientific support — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/science/what-is-the-schumann-resonance); [Metabunk](https://metabunk.org/threads/schumman-resonance-the-supposed-key-to-our-well-being.3062).
  - The claimed 2006 coherence study (Persinger/Saroka line) could not be verified from a primary source.

### Inferences
- A structural point that disposes of most "special frequency" health claims: retuning music from 440 to 432 Hz shifts every pitch by about 1.8% (roughly a third of a semitone). Most listeners cannot identify this without a reference. Any effect is better explained by novelty, expectancy, or differences in the specific recordings used (tempo, timbre, mastering). Few of these studies control for recording identity, and none of those found used blinded, independent replication.
- "Solfeggio" tracks are usually music or drones at those pitches. None of the 2018–2026 studies tested a pure tone against a pitch-matched control with blinding, so these studies cannot test the frequency-specific hypothesis.

### Gaps
- The originals of Horowitz (1999) and Puleo were not read. Provenance relies on secondary skeptic accounts that broadly agree with one another.
- The full text and sample size of Calamassi & Pomponi 2019 were not verified, nor the exact protein names and effect sizes in Bozok 2026.
- No date for ISO 16 (the A = 440 standard) was verified in this session.

## 5. Plausible vs refuted or pseudoscientific mechanisms; safety signals

### Takeaway
**Plausible:**
- stimulus-locked sensory responses (FFR and ASSR, especially near 40 Hz);
- non-specific relaxation, attention capture and expectancy effects of listening to audio;
- arousal modulation by tempo and rhythm.

**Unestablished:**
- that binaural beats at theta/alpha/delta rates produce the corresponding global brain "state" (the evidence is inconsistent).

**Pseudoscientific or refuted:**
- DNA repair by 528 Hz;
- "cosmic/natural" 432 Hz;
- Schumann-resonance health effects;
- ancient-solfeggio history.

**Safety:** the main hazard is headphone volume. A seizure risk is theoretical, not documented in what was found.

### Cited Findings
- **[MECH]** Entrainment without mood change (Orozco Perez 2020) and the 5/8/1 split in Ingendoh 2023 show that the step from "stimulus-locked response" to "psychological state" is not established — [PubMed 32066611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32066611/); [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/).
- **[MECH]** Garcia-Argibay found effects depended on timing (before the task was better than during) and that noise masking made no difference. Both observations fit non-specific arousal or relaxation accounts at least as well as a strict entrainment account — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/).
- **[SMALL] 40 Hz stimulation safety:** 2 years of daily 40 Hz audiovisual stimulation in 5 mild-AD patients was reported as safe; the randomized pilot also assessed safety and compliance — [PMC9714926](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9714926/); [MIT news](https://bcs.mit.edu/news/study-suggests-40hz-sensory-stimulation-may-benefit-some-alzheimers-patients-years).
- **[MKT-level sources only] Safety:**
  - Vendor and wellness sources agree the realistic hazard is volume on headphones. They cite 85 dB as the long-term hearing-risk threshold, but that figure came from a manufacturer page, not a standards body.
  - They describe seizure risk as theoretical, with musicogenic epilepsy as the edge case, and report no documented cases — [brain.fm](https://www.brain.fm/blog/binaural-beats-safety); [binauralbeatsmeditation.com](https://www.binauralbeatsmeditation.com/binaural-beats-epilepsy-seizures/).
  - Photosensitive seizure risk is highest at 15–25 Hz flicker. This is relevant to combined audio-*visual* entrainment devices, not sound alone — [gammaflasher](https://gammaflasher.pages.dev/). This is a low-quality source; confirm with Epilepsy Foundation guidance.

### Inferences
- For a product like MAIA, the defensible claims are:
  - "rhythmic or relaxing audio may help some people settle before a stressful event";
  - "40 Hz modulated sound reliably drives a measurable sensory brain response; clinical benefit is under investigation."
- Indefensible claims: frequency-specific healing, DNA, cellular repair, "Earth frequency," or guaranteed state induction. This lines up with the project's Live / Designed / Vision claim discipline.
- Safety copy should centre on listening level and duration (headphones), and on caution for people with epilepsy when visual flicker is involved.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed case series of seizures triggered by audio-only entrainment, and no formal safety review. Absence from these searches is not evidence of absence.
- No regulatory statements (FDA/FTC actions against frequency-healing marketing) were checked here. See the sibling file `safety_regulation_claims.md`.
