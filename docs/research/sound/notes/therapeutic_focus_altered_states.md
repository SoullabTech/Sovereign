# Sound and Music for Healing, Focus, and Altered States of Consciousness

Evidence grades used throughout (one per claim):
**[MA/RCT]** meta-analysis or RCT · **[SMALL]** small or uncontrolled trial · **[MECH]** mechanistic/lab only · **[PRACT]** practitioner/marketing only · **[REFUTED]** refuted/contradicted.
Research cut-off: searches run 2026-11-09. Most figures come from abstracts and secondary summaries of the primary papers; the full texts were not read unless stated. Check exact numbers against the originals before quoting them in public.

---

## 1. Music therapy and music medicine: depression, anxiety, pain, dementia, perioperative care

### Takeaway
This is the best-evidenced part of the field. Several Cochrane reviews and large meta-analyses report moderate-to-large pooled effects (SMD ≈ 0.3–1.1) on anxiety, pain, and depressive symptoms. Most certainty ratings are low or very low, heterogeneity is high, and gains are usually not shown to last at follow-up. Perioperative music for pain and anxiety and music therapy for depression in dementia are the most robust findings.

### Cited Findings
- **Depression, Cochrane (Aalbers et al., 2017):** music therapy added to treatment as usual reduced clinician-rated depression (SMD −1.12, 95% CI −2.10 to −0.14, n = 159) and patient-reported depression (SMD −0.98). Evidence quality moderate, wide CIs. **[MA/RCT]** — [Cochrane CD004517.pub3](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004517.pub3/full)
- **Transdiagnostic psychiatric meta-review (BJPsych Open, 2025):** pooled music therapy effects were depression SMD 0.57 (0.36–0.78), anxiety SMD 0.47 (0.27–0.66), and quality of life SMD 0.47 (0.24–0.71). Effects were **not maintained at follow-up**, and all the evidence was rated low or very low. The authors call music therapy "promising as an adjunct." **[MA/RCT]** — [Augsburg OPUS record](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/118018); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/3c2a7c94e80b43d69db712c5d8af6d83)
- **Cancer, Cochrane (Bradt et al., 2021 update):** music interventions may reduce anxiety (−7.73 points on STAI-S, 17 studies) and pain (SMD −0.67, 95% CI −1.07 to −0.26; 12 studies, 632 adults). Certainty very low. No effect on distress or spiritual well-being. The **2016 version found no support for an effect on mood or distress**, so the mood conclusion moved between versions. **[MA/RCT]** — [Cochrane 2021](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006911.pub4/full); [Cochrane 2016](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006911.pub3/full)
- **Chronic pain meta-analysis (BMC Psychology, 2025):** music therapy reduced chronic pain (9 RCTs, SMD −0.51, −0.72 to −0.30) and depression (4 RCTs, SMD −0.83). No significant effect on anxiety. **[MA/RCT]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-025-02643-x)
- **Dementia, Cochrane (van der Steen et al., 2018):** at least five sessions of music-based therapy probably reduce depressive symptoms (SMD −0.27, −0.45 to −0.09; 11 studies, 503 participants; moderate quality) and overall behavioural problems (SMD −0.23). Little or no effect on cognition, emotional well-being, or quality of life (low quality). **[MA/RCT]** — [PMC6513122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513122)
- **Dementia, Cochrane 2025 update (30 studies, 1,720 participants):** the same conclusion holds. Depressive symptoms probably improve and behaviour problems may improve at the end of treatment. Music therapy is unlikely to affect agitation, aggression, or cognition. Effects more than 4 weeks after treatment are uncertain. Most trials were in care homes. **[MA/RCT]** — [Cochrane news](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/music-based-therapy-may-improve-depressive-symptoms-people-dementia); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1075723)
- **Perioperative (Hole et al., Lancet 2015; 73 RCTs, n = 20–458 per trial):** music reduced postoperative pain (SMD −0.77), anxiety (SMD −0.68), and analgesic use (SMD −0.37), and raised satisfaction (SMD 1.09). Length of stay did not change (SMD −0.11). Choice and timing of music did not moderate the effect, and music worked even under general anaesthesia. Searches ran to October 2013. **[MA/RCT]** — [PubMed 26277246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26277246/); [QMUL press release](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/news/latest-news/2015/smd/new-study-confirms-listening-to-music-during-surgery-reduces-pain-and-anxiety.html)
- **Procedural settings:** further meta-analyses report music reducing pain and anxiety during outpatient hysteroscopy and emergency suturing. **[MA/RCT]** — [PMC10324128](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10324128/); [PMC12886440](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12886440/)
- **Anxiety, multilevel meta-analysis (eClinicalMedicine, June 2025):** 51 studies and 93 effect sizes, searched to February 2025. The pooled figure was not retrieved here. **[MA/RCT]** — [OU research record](https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/music-therapy-for-the-treatment-of-anxiety-a-systematic-review-wi/)

### Inferences
- Effect sizes are probably inflated. Blinding is impossible, outcomes are largely self-reported, trials are small, and certainty ratings are low. A realistic expectation is a small-to-moderate adjunctive benefit while the music is being used, not a durable cure.
- The perioperative result is the most reproducible and the cheapest to apply. "Music medicine" (passive listening to recorded music) appears to work without a therapist, while "music therapy" (a credentialed therapist and a relationship) is what the depression reviews mostly test. These are different interventions and should not be conflated.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 network meta-analysis comparing music therapy against psychotherapy or medication was found.
- No head-to-head trials of music therapy versus music medicine were found.
- The pooled numbers from the 2025 anxiety meta-analysis were not retrieved.

## 2. Sound baths, singing bowls, gongs, tuning forks, toning and chanting, vibroacoustic therapy

### Takeaway
Evidence here is thin and recent. Goldsby et al. 2017 was an **uncontrolled observational study**, not an RCT. A handful of small RCTs (2023 onward) suggest short-term relaxation and anxiety reduction comparable to other relaxation methods, with no evidence that bowls are uniquely effective. The humming–nitric-oxide finding is a solid but narrow physiological fact, and claims about immunity built on it are extrapolation. Vibroacoustic therapy has scoping reviews but no robust pooled efficacy data.

### Cited Findings
- **Goldsby et al. (2017), n = 62:** after a ~60-minute Tibetan singing-bowl session, participants reported less tension, anger, fatigue, and depressed mood (all p < .001). There was **no control group**, and the authors called for an RCT. **[SMALL]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2156587216668109)
- **Stanhope & Weinstein (2020) systematic review:** only four studies of singing bowls' health effects existed. There was "some evidence" of mental health and cardiovascular benefit, and better designs were needed. **[MA/RCT]** (review of weak primary studies) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096522991931756X)
- **Rio-Alamos et al. (2023) RCT, n ≈ 50 high-anxiety adults:** a single session of singing bowls was compared with progressive muscle relaxation and a waitlist. Bowls produced an acute relaxation response, but the comparison with PMR is the key test, and summaries indicate the active arms were broadly comparable (verify against the full text). **[SMALL]** (small RCT) — [PMC9955072](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9955072/)
- **Structured sound-meditation RCT versus a remotely delivered music control:** greater short-term reductions in anxiety, depression, salivary cortisol, and systolic BP, which the authors call "preliminary." Publication year not confirmed. **[SMALL]** — [PMC13539022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13539022/)
- **Kim & Choi (2023), IJERPH:** EEG study of whether singing-bowl sound shifts listeners toward "meditational" brainwaves. **[MECH]** — cited via [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/less-stress-more-peace/202410/research-reveals-sound-and-frequency-reduce-stress)
- **Registered NZ RCT (NCT07267520):** quartz crystal bowl music versus guided relaxation for mood and sleep in young adults, with 4- and 8-week follow-up, hypothesising **equivalence**. Ongoing. **[SMALL]** (protocol only) — [ClinicalTrials.gov protocol PDF](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/20/NCT07267520/Prot_SAP_ICF_000.pdf)
- **Humming and nitric oxide (Weitzberg & Lundberg, 2002; n = 10):** humming raised nasal NO about **15-fold** compared with quiet exhalation, through rapid gas exchange with the paranasal sinuses. The paper proposed humming as a test of sinus ostial patency. It did not test therapeutic effects. **[MECH]** — [PubMed 12119224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12119224/)
- **Claims that Bhramari boosts immunity or brain blood flow through NO** are not supported by the 2002 study. **[PRACT]** — [yogabharati.org example](https://yogabharati.org/article-blog/bhramari-increases-immunity-through-nitric-oxide)
- **Om chanting fMRI pilot (Kalyani et al., 2011; n = 12):** loud "OM" chanting, compared with "ssss" and rest, produced limbic **deactivation** including the right amygdala. The authors only noted a *similarity* to vagus-nerve-stimulation patterns. **[MECH]** (pilot, uncontrolled for expectancy) — [PMC3099099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3099099/)
- Blog claims that the OM pattern "matches VNS" or that only OM engages the vagal pathway go beyond the paper. **[PRACT]** — same source, compared against secondary blogs.
- **Vibroacoustic therapy (VAT):** a 2022 BMJ Open scoping review on pain found **no published systematic review** on the topic at that time. **[MECH/SMALL]** (scoping only) — [PubMed 35383053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35383053/); [PMC8984038](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8984038)
- **VAT and low-frequency vibration for dementia (Campbell et al., 2022 scoping review):** "more quality research is needed." **[SMALL]** — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.854794/pdf)
- **Methodological fragmentation:** VAT, physioacoustic therapy, and tactile low-frequency vibration use non-standard terms, which makes pooling difficult. **[MECH]** (methods commentary, preprint) — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/be96c420e0ac0455d3ee161390ab9c12/download_pub)
- **Naghdi et al. (2015), "effectiveness of VAT in rehabilitation: a systematic review":** cited in the literature, but its findings were not retrieved. — referenced in [JYX thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/5c253eb7-a63a-414d-9e96-68f487ecdeee/download)

### Inferences
- The plausible active ingredients of sound baths are non-specific: lying still, a calm setting, expectancy, slow breathing, and absorbing attention. The few active-control RCTs (PMR, guided relaxation) point toward equivalence with other relaxation methods, not superiority. Claims about specific frequencies (e.g., "432 Hz," "chakra tones," "cellular resonance") have no support in the evidence found. **[PRACT]**
- The humming–NO effect is real but is a nasal and sinus phenomenon. Whether it matters for systemic health is untested.

### Gaps
- No RCTs of gongs or tuning forks for clinical outcomes were found. Tuning-fork "sound healing" evidence was not located beyond practitioner sources.
- No meta-analysis of chanting or toning outcomes was found. Most work is Indian pilot studies.
- Naghdi 2015 VAT review findings and Olav Skille's original clinical data were not retrieved.

## 3. Focus and attention: noise for ADHD, music for focus, lyrics, the Mozart effect

### Takeaway
White and pink noise give a **small, real, population-specific** benefit. In the 2024 JAACAP meta-analysis it helps youth with ADHD or high attention problems (g ≈ 0.25) and **slightly harms** typical youth (g ≈ −0.21), and it has only been tested on lab tasks. Brown noise is untested. Background music is on average neutral to mildly distracting, and **lyrics** are the main source of harm for verbal tasks. The "Mozart effect" is effectively refuted as a specific IQ enhancer.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigg, Bruton, Kozlowski, Johnstone & Karalunas (JAACAP 2024;63(8):778-788), 13 studies, 335 participants:** in ADHD or elevated attention problems, white or pink noise improved task performance (**g = 0.249**, CI 0.135–0.363). In non-ADHD participants it worsened performance (**g = −0.212**). Lab tasks only, and the authors warn about hearing hazards. **[MA/RCT]** — [OHSU Elsevier Pure](https://ohsu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-do-white-noise-or-pink-noise-); [OHSU news](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows)
- **No brown-noise studies were in the meta-analysis.** Brown-noise-for-ADHD claims are **[PRACT]** — [deepbliss.me (critical blog)](https://www.deepbliss.me/blog/brown-noise-adhd-myth); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/does-pink-noise-really-help-you-focus)
- **Söderlund, Sikström & Smart (2007, JCPP):** white noise improved memory performance in children with ADHD and impaired it in controls. **[SMALL]** — [PubMed 17683456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17683456/)
- **Moderate Brain Arousal model (Sikström & Söderlund, 2007, Psychological Review):** stochastic resonance, meaning moderate noise improves signal detection. Dopamine-deficient systems such as ADHD supposedly need more noise to reach optimal arousal. **[MECH]** (theoretical and computational) — [Southampton ePrints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/48262)
- **Söderlund et al. (2016) pilot, n = 20 ADHD + 20 controls:** 80 dB white noise improved some tasks. Stimulant medication showed no significant effect on those tests, a surprising result given the tiny sample. **[SMALL]** — [Frontiers 2016](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01331/text)
- **Söderlund et al. (2024):** about one third performed *worse* under ~80 dB white noise. Inattentive traits predicted benefit and hyperactive traits predicted harm. **[SMALL]** — reported via [scienceworkshealth.com](https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/adhd-background-noise-brown-noise-focus) (secondary, verify)
- **Background music and cognition (Kämpfe et al., 2011, Psychology of Music):** the overall effect was described as null, with positive and negative effects cancelling out. Another source reports a small negative effect (≈ r −.11) for a subset. The sources disagree on how to summarise it. **[MA/RCT]** — [Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/273); [Vasilev PDF](https://martin-vasilev.github.io/papers/background_music_distraction.pdf)
- **Reading comprehension (Vasilev et al., 2018; 36 studies):** d = −0.19 overall, with **much larger distraction from lyrical** than instrumental music. **[MA/RCT]** — [Vasilev PDF](https://martin-vasilev.github.io/papers/background_music_distraction.pdf)
- **Vasilev et al. (2023):** lyrical music impaired verbal memory, visual memory, and reading comprehension (d ≈ −0.3). The effect on arithmetic was not credible. **[SMALL]** (single experiment) — [PMC10162369](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162369/)
- **Mozart effect, Chabris (1999, Nature; 16 studies, 714 subjects):** about 1.4 IQ points, limited to spatial-temporal tasks, and not a change in general reasoning. **[REFUTED]** as general enhancement — via [Wikipedia: Mozart effect](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Mozart_effect)
- **Pietschnig, Voracek & Formann (2010, Intelligence; ~40 studies, >3,000 subjects):** d = 0.37 [0.23, 0.52], with publication bias and laboratory effects. Any music produced similar effects, and there was "little evidence left for a specific, performance-enhancing Mozart effect." **[REFUTED]** — [University of Vienna press release](https://medienportal.univie.ac.at/media/aktuelle-pressemeldungen/detailansicht/artikel/mozarts-music-does-not-make-you-smarter)
- **A later reanalysis argues even d = 0.37 is inflated** by small, low-quality studies. This is a poster and should be treated as preliminary. **[MA/RCT]** (reanalysis, unpublished) — [ULiège poster](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303783/1/posterBAPS.pdf)
- **Binaural beats (Ingendoh et al., 2023, PLoS One; 14 studies):** entrainment evidence is inconsistent, with 5 supporting, 8 contradicting, and 1 mixed. **[REFUTED/contradicted]** as a reliable entrainment tool — [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)
- **Binaural beats for memory and attention, meta-analysis (Psychological Research, 2023; 15 studies):** g = 0.40 overall, but with conflicting results for theta and beta. This is a counterweight to Ingendoh, and the two reviews cover different outcomes. **[MA/RCT]** — cited in [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/)

### Inferences
- The noise finding fits an inverted-U arousal model. Noise is a **personalised tool to trial, not a universal focus aid**: for neurotypical users the pooled sign is negative.
- For focus work, instrumental or familiar low-arousal music is the safer default, and lyrical music should be avoided for reading or writing tasks.
- "Focus music" products that claim neural phase-locking are extrapolating from mixed binaural-beat and entrainment evidence.

### Gaps
- No real-world (classroom or workplace) trials of noise for ADHD with functional outcomes were found. No long-term or safety data on chronic noise use was found.
- No independent RCTs of commercial "focus music" products (e.g., Brain.fm, Endel) were found in this search.

## 4. Altered states: drumming, trance, psychedelic-therapy music, chills, ganzfeld

### Takeaway
Rhythmic sound **does** entrain auditory cortical responses (lab-robust), but the classic claim that ~4–7 Hz drumming "drives the brain into theta and causes trance" (Neher 1962) was **not reliably replicated**. Modern imaging (Hove 2016) instead describes a network reconfiguration in *experienced practitioners*. In psychedelic therapy, music is plausibly a major active ingredient, but the evidence is small and correlational. Music-evoked chills have strong mechanistic evidence for dopamine involvement.

### Cited Findings
- **Neher (1961, 1962):** hypothesised that ceremonial drumming produces "unusual behaviour" by auditory driving of the EEG. **[MECH]** (small, early) — [Neher 1962 PDF](https://web.uvic.ca/~aschloss/course_mat/MU320/Global%20Rhtyhm%20and%20Human%20Consciousness/ARTICLES%20AND%20REFS%20FOR%20320/A%20Physiological%20Explanation%20of%20Unusual%20Behavior%20in%20Ceremonies%20Involving%20Drums.pdf)
- **Replication critique (SFU thesis):** overt auditory driving of the EEG "cannot be readily produced in a laboratory situation," and photic driving occurs without Neher's behavioural changes. The scanned source is partly garbled. **[REFUTED/contradicted]** — [SFU Summit](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2022-07/b13031028.pdf)
- **Modern EEG on 1–8 Hz drum and click trains:** periodic sound produces a mix of evoked and induced, rate-specific and rate-independent synchronisation. Entrainment is real, but this does not show that it causes trance. **[MECH]** — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00208)
- **Hove et al. (2016), fMRI, 15 neo-shamanic practitioners, ~4 Hz drumming:** trance involved co-activation of the default mode network (PCC) with control and salience nodes (dACC, insula), plus reduced brainstem–auditory-cortex coupling ("perceptual decoupling"). **No non-practitioner control group.** **[MECH]** — [bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.07.20.212522v1.full.pdf); [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.610466/xml/nlm)
- **Reviews of rhythm-induced altered states** (Aparicio-Terrés et al., 2025, Annals NYAS; and a 2026 Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews paper on the "rhythms of trance") frame trance as an interaction of rhythm, culture, expectation, and practice. **[MECH]** (narrative review) — [Wiley](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/nyas.15403); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763426001636)
- **Kaelen et al. (2018), "The hidden therapist," Psychopharmacology; n = 19 TRD patients, psilocybin 25 mg, open-label:** the quality of the music experience (liking, resonance, openness) **significantly predicted depression reduction at 1 week**, while overall drug intensity did not. Concluded that music plays a "central therapeutic function." **[SMALL]** (uncontrolled, correlational) — [PubMed 29396616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29396616/); [Beckley Foundation PDF](http://www.beckleyfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Kaelen-et-al-2018-The-Hidden-Therapist.pdf)
- **Salimpoor et al. (2011), Nature Neuroscience, PET:** peak emotional arousal to music (chills) is associated with dopamine release in the striatum. Release is anatomically distinct during anticipation (dorsal) and experience (ventral/NAcc). **[MECH]** — summarised in [PMC6397567](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6397567)
- **Ferreri et al. (2019), PNAS, n = 27, within-subject pharmacological RCT:** levodopa increased musical pleasure and willingness to pay, while risperidone reduced both and impaired chills. This is **causal** evidence for dopamine in music reward. The author warns that it does not license "taking dopamine to enjoy music more." **[MA/RCT]** (small crossover RCT, mechanistic outcome) — [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/dopamine-modulates-the-reward-experiences-elicited-by-music/); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/dopamine-music-10628/)

### Inferences
- "Drumming at 4–7 Hz induces theta and therefore trance" is a **practitioner simplification [PRACT]**. Better supported is that rhythm plus a cultural or ritual set plus practice reorganises attention networks.
- Music in psychedelic sessions is best treated as a *set-and-setting variable with a strong correlational signal*. That makes curating, consenting to, and allowing patients to reject or change music clinically important.

### Gaps
- Details of the Johns Hopkins and Imperial playlist designs and any **RCTs that manipulate music** in psychedelic therapy (e.g., music versus no music or genre comparisons) were not retrieved.
- Ganzfeld and sensory-deprivation audio (pink noise plus diffuse light; floatation/REST) was not searched within budget. No graded findings to report.
- No quantitative data on the prevalence of trance responses to drumming in naïve listeners was found.

## 5. Consciousness development, "mind hacking," and biohacking: HRV biofeedback, neurofeedback, meditation audio

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence in this "hacking" space is for **HRV/resonance-frequency breathing biofeedback**, where the sound or display is a breathing pacer and the active ingredient is the ~6 breaths/min breathing (meta-analytic g ≈ 0.8 on self-reported stress and anxiety, from small trials). Binaural or "brainwave" audio products rest on inconsistent entrainment evidence. Audio neurofeedback and meditation-support audio were not adequately covered within this search budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Goessl, Curtiss & Hofmann (2017), Psychological Medicine; 24 studies, 484 participants:** HRV biofeedback was associated with a large reduction in self-reported stress and anxiety (within-group g = 0.81; between-group g = 0.83). The authors call for well-controlled studies. **[MA/RCT]** — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effect-of-heart-rate-variability-biofeedback-training-on-stress-and-anxiety-a-metaanalysis/A839E9C968E54774DF5C8FB186764EF0); [BU OpenBU](https://open.bu.edu/handle/2144/26911)
- Secondary sites ranking HRV-BF "among the highest non-pharmacological interventions" overstate the paper. **[PRACT]** — [outliyr.com](https://outliyr.com/reports/hrv-biofeedback)
- **Binaural beats as a brainwave-entrainment "hack":** inconsistent (see §3). **[REFUTED/contradicted]** — [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)

### Inferences
- A defensible "sound for self-regulation" design uses audio as a **pacer or feedback channel for slow breathing**, not as a claimed frequency-entrainment device. Benefits plausibly come from respiratory–baroreflex mechanisms and the practice itself.
- "Consciousness development" claims for audio products should be graded **[PRACT]** unless tied to the above.

### Gaps
- Audio-based neurofeedback (e.g., alpha/theta protocols with tones), closed-loop auditory stimulation during sleep (pink-noise bursts timed to slow oscillations), and meditation-app audio RCTs were **not searched within budget**. The report writer should not infer their evidence grade from this file.

## 6. Risks and ethics: dissociation, trauma triggering, hearing damage, dependence

### Takeaway
Direct harm data for sound and music interventions is scarce because harms are rarely monitored. The clearest quantified risk is **hearing damage** (WHO/ITU safe-listening limits). This matters because the noise-for-ADHD studies used ~80 dB, which sits at the adult weekly limit. Adjacent contemplative-practice research shows adverse effects are **common when actively looked for**. Absence of reported harms in sound-bath literature is likely absence of measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **WHO/ITU safe listening (ITU-T H.870, current version approved March 2022):** adult guideline ≈ **80 dB for up to 40 h/week**, children ≈ 75 dB, based on the equal-energy principle. Adoption is voluntary. WHO estimates over 1 billion young people are at risk of noise-induced hearing loss. **[MA/RCT-equivalent: standards consensus]** — [ITU H.870 summary](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/h/T-REC-H.870-202203-I!!SUM-HTM-E.htm); [Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/inside-hearing/organizations/asha-works-development-global-recommendations-safe-listening); [ITU news](https://itu.int/hub/2020/05/listen-responsibly-new-itu-standard-to-prevent-audio-devices-from-causing-hearing-loss)
- **The Nigg 2024 meta-analysis explicitly warns** that colour-noise use "is not without hazard," with hearing damage a risk if volume is unmonitored. **[MA/RCT]** (author caveat) — [OHSU news](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows)
- **Meditation-related adverse effects (Britton et al., 2021; n = 96, mindfulness programmes):** 58% reported ≥1 adverse effect, 37% had effects that impaired functioning, and **6% had lasting bad effects**. Prevalence estimates range from ~1% to ~two-thirds depending on whether harms are actively assessed. **[SMALL]** (structured harms monitoring in a trial cohort) — [Brown University news](https://www.brown.edu/news/2021-05-18/adverse-effects); [Goldberg et al. 2021 PDF](https://sites.brown.edu/britton/files/2023/10/Goldberg-et-al-2021-Prevalence-of-meditation-related-adverse-effects-in-a-population-based-sample-in-the-United-States.pdf)
- **Sound baths, clinical guidance:** people with psychiatric disorders are advised to consult a doctor first because meditation can worsen symptoms. Some people feel tired or overstimulated, and post-session disorientation may make driving unsafe. **[PRACT]** (clinical advice, no trial data) — [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sound-bath); [Calm](https://www.calm.com/blog/sound-bath)
- **Claims of dramatic sound-bath side effects** (nausea, dizziness) in some blogs are uncited. **[PRACT]** — [Earkick blog](https://blog.earkick.com/risky-sound-healing-side-effects-of-singing-bowls)
- **In the Cochrane cancer and dementia reviews, harms were not a reported focus.** The reviews say little about adverse events. — [Cochrane 2021](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006911.pub4/full)

### Inferences
- **Dissociation and trauma:** practices built to induce absorption, perceptual decoupling (Hove 2016), or reduced exteroception (lying with eyes closed, immersive sound) plausibly carry the same risk profile as intensive meditation: depersonalisation, flashbacks, and panic in trauma survivors. This is inference from adjacent evidence, not a measured finding for sound specifically.
- **Psychedelic contexts:** if music strongly shapes the experience (Kaelen 2018), unwanted or emotionally loaded music is a plausible route to difficult experiences. Participant choice and a veto over the music are ethical safeguards.
- **Dependence:** no studies quantified dependence on sleep or focus noise, or on "sound healing" sessions. Risk is theoretical. The noise-for-ADHD harm signal in non-ADHD users (g −0.21) is a concrete example of an aid that can backfire.
- **Ethics:** the main documented ethical problem is **claim inflation**, meaning frequency-specific healing claims, unsupported NO and immunity claims, and brown-noise ADHD marketing, not physical harm.

### Gaps
- No trials or case series were found that specifically measured **dissociation or trauma triggering from sound baths, drumming, or music therapy**.
- No data on tinnitus or hyperacusis from gongs or loud sound baths was found. Gong sound-pressure levels in live sessions were not located.
- Music-therapy professional guidance on contraindications (e.g., AMTA or BAMT trauma-informed practice) was not retrieved.
