## 0. Why a study, not a beta

The standing directive is that the limiting resource is **contact with reality, not more abstraction.** Framing the engagement as research makes the software an instrument and the evidence the deliverable — the honest form of "beta" for something whose entire value rests on an *unproven* claim about faithful translation. It also makes the partner invitation honest (§4).

**The defining property of a study (vs a demo):** it is designed so that Jondi's engagement produces evidence **whether the idea succeeds or fails.** A protocol that can only confirm is marketing, not research. The gates (§3), the within-subject baseline (§1), and the discriminators exist precisely so a null or negative result is as informative as a positive one.

## 1. The research program — and the study question

**The enduring research-program question** (worth studying whether the platform succeeds spectacularly or only partially — it produces knowledge about *practitioners*, not just *software*):

> **How does reflective dialogue help experienced practitioners discover, articulate, refine, and extend the tacit knowledge developed through years of practice?**

**The first study question** (Jondi) is framed so the AI is *one component of the process*, **not the thing being validated**:

> **Does a structured reflective dialogue help a practitioner externalize tacit knowledge more effectively than her existing approaches — while she retains authorship over every meaningful refinement?**

Two outcome dimensions, both measurable: **efficacy** (tacit knowledge becomes externalized) and **authorship** (it stays *hers* — the two crossings of `LEGACY_SESSIONS_SPEC` §3 hold).

### 1.1 The research object (sharpened)
Beneath both questions is a single object of study — and it is **not AI and not coaching**:
> **How tacit professional knowledge becomes explicit through structured reflective dialogue while preserving practitioner authorship.**

Everything else — MAIA, AIN, Legacy Sessions, reflection, translation, governance, memory — is *infrastructure* for investigating that one question.

### 1.2 Practice is primary; the architecture is our provisional representation
The practitioner does **not** possess "an architecture" waiting to be found. They possess **lived practice.** The architecture is **our representation of that practice** — provisional, never confirmed-as-identity. This keeps authorship clean: the practitioner never has to *agree with our model*; she only ever says *"that captures something important"* (calibration) or *"no — that misses what I'm actually doing"* (discrimination). The practice stays primary; the map stays revisable. This is `REPRESENTATIONS_AS_ORIENTATION` at the scale of a career — and it names what *"Architectural Translation"* actually translates: **lived practice → an explicit, provisional representation.** (The spec already encodes this: M3 is *"a reading, not a verdict."*)

**On the comparison (*"more effectively than her existing approaches"*).** The instinct is right — it isolates the dialogue's contribution from "she'd have articulated it anyway." But at **n=1 this cannot be a controlled comparison.** Operationalize it as a **within-subject baseline**: a free-written articulation of her architecture *before* the dialogue, plus tacit knowledge her decades of existing methods had **not** surfaced. *"More effectively than existing approaches"* as a general claim is earned only as independent practitioners accumulate (the independent-convergence discipline).

## 2. Two studies, two questions, one process

| | **Study A — Jondi** | **Study B — Larry** |
|---|---|---|
| Practitioner state | Mature; decades integrated ("already become") | Still creating ("still building") |
| Question | Can MAIA **reveal** a mature practitioner's architecture such that she recognizes it? | Can MAIA **accelerate the emergence** of a new methodology while preserving authorship? |
| Primary outcome | **Recognition:** *"Yes — that's me; I hadn't seen that pattern until it was reflected back."* | **Authored emergence:** new structure stated in their own words, traceable to their utterances |
| Run order | **First** — a mature practice is a *fixed target*, so faithful-revelation is cleanly testable | Second — a *moving target*: richer co-evolution, but "faithful to what?" is harder |

**The two studies are complementary by design — that is the point.** They sit at opposite ends of one developmental spectrum:

| Larry | Jondi |
|---|---|
| Emerging methodology | Mature methodology |
| Building something | Embodying something |
| Discovery of a representation | Articulation of a representation |
| Future-oriented | Retrospective and living |

If Architectural Translation holds across **both** conditions, it has survived two fundamentally different forms of expertise — far stronger than two similar case studies (a *most-different* design). **Governor caveat:** Larry and Jondi differ on a *bundle* of variables at once (maturity, domain, person), so success across both shows **robustness across very different experts** — **not** that the method is robust along the *maturity axis specifically* (that variable is confounded with the others). The honest claim is bounded generality; isolating any single axis needs more practitioners.

## 3. The validity problem (the governor's core contribution)

Jondi's headline outcome — *"yes, that's me, I never realized"* — is the most powerful result in the program **and** its most seductive false positive. People readily accept plausible statements about themselves (the **Barnum / Forer effect**). *"She said yes"* is not yet evidence. **Four evidence gates** turn assent into evidence:

1. **Recognition** — *"Yes, that's how I work."* Necessary, not sufficient.
2. **Discrimination** — she **rejects** deliberately-planted plausible-but-*wrong* patterns (*"I don't actually work that way"*). Proves the process is not just producing agreeable statements. *(This is Crossing 2 — the self-confirming-loop guard — in measurement form.)*
3. **Elaboration** — the reflection stimulates further articulation: not *"correct"* but *"yes — and what's really happening is…"* Now she is **extending her own architecture** (authorship; Crossing 1 → 2).
4. **Utility** — does it change what she actually **does**? (*"I changed how I begin sessions"; "I built a new exercise."*) Behavioral and prospective — the hardest to fake, because the reflection didn't just describe practice, it informed it.

**Discharge condition:** evidence that survives all four gates — not agreement alone.

### 3.1 Utility cuts both ways — attribute it through authorship
Gate 4 is the strongest evidence **and** the most dangerous. A change in her practice is *discovery informing development* only if it flowed through her **authorship**. The failure mode is a change that flowed from **bare assent** to an *inaccurate* reflection — she changed her sessions because MAIA said so. That is not revelation; it is **the system steering the practitioner** — an authorship violation wearing the costume of success.

- **Utility via Elaboration / Discrimination** (Gates 3/2): even a *wrong* reflection she **corrects** into a useful change is fine — the system provoked *her own* authorship. **Good loop.**
- **Utility via bare assent** (Gate 1 alone) to an inaccurate reflection: **steering / distortion.**

So Gate 4 is read *through* Gates 1–3, with a **predictive-accuracy cross-check** as corroboration (did the surfaced architecture independently predict her choices in **held-out** sessions?). Predictive accuracy stays in the protocol — not as a felt-gate, but as the lens that tells you *which kind* of utility you observed.

### 3.2 Authorship risk scales inversely with practice maturity

Larry (still forming) offers less fixed ground to distinguish *discovered* from *induced* → the self-confirming loop is more dangerous and "faithful to what?" has a moving referent. The **same fact** that makes him a rich co-evolution partner. The discriminators are mandatory for both; weighted harder for Larry.

### 3.3 Three streams — as epistemic functions (revised 2026-06-25)

The streams are not merely data sources; they are **distinct epistemic functions.** Keeping them distinct prevents the subtle error of **confusing recollection with invention** (§10.3).

- **Stream A — Recovery** *(historical):* recover tacit knowledge already embedded in lived practice — stories, origins, ideal sessions, the questions she asks. → feeds **Recognition · Discrimination · Elaboration**; yields **externalization** evidence.
- **Stream B — Reflection** *(interpretive):* examine her *current* practice against that recovered understanding — session prep, post-session reflection. → feeds **Utility** (still requires watching live practice — Gate 4 is invisible otherwise).
- **Stream C — Emergence** *(creative):* notice genuinely **novel** structures/distinctions that arise *through the dialogue itself*; MAIA offers provisional readings, she corrects/expands/rejects. → where authorship is **exercised** (*system proposes; practitioner disposes*); yields **emergence** evidence (scored separately — §10.3).

### 3.4 Gate 5 — Transfer (program-level, long-term)

The four gates above are *within-practitioner.* A fifth operates **across** practitioners and is the strongest validation of all: **Transfer.**

> Can *another* practitioner **understand and make use** of the articulated representation — not imitate it, not copy it, but think more clearly about *their own* work because of it?

If Jondi's externalized framework helps a *different* practitioner, something **communicable** — carrying usable structure beyond its originator — has been externalized. That is stronger than recognition, which never leaves the originator.

- **Discriminator (against Barnum at the framework level):** transfer must be **differential, not universal.** A faithful externalization of *Jondi's* practice should help practitioners whose work *resembles* hers and be **recognized as foreign** by those whose doesn't. If it is "useful to everyone," it is generic — a horoscope, not a faithful representation.
- **Authorship / consent:** running Transfer means sharing Jondi's representation with others → it invokes the practitioner→others crossing (her **consent + attribution**). Transfer is a *measurement*, not a license to circulate her work.
- **Tier:** program-level; requires n>1; **not** part of the first Jondi study's discharge condition.

## 6. The cross-program hypothesis (sharpened candidate)

Refines `AIN_PRACTITIONER_DEPLOYMENT_SEQUENCE` §4. Kelly's narrower, falsifiable form — improved one notch by scoping to the tacit layer:

> **To the extent a practice is tacit, faithful expression requires prior discovery.** Explicit knowledge can be expressed without discovery; **tacit wisdom cannot.** (Maps to the Knowledge / Experience / **Wisdom** triad: the claim is about the Wisdom layer.)

If every successful deployment follows discovery → expression, that is a *law about faithful translation*, not "our roadmap happened to work."

## 7. The defensible outcome (claim what survives)

The headline is **not** *"AIN successfully modeled Jondi."* That claim's truth depends on the model being right — so it dies the moment the architecture is revised. The defensible outcome is more modest and, paradoxically, more valuable:

> **"The reflective dialogue helped Jondi articulate aspects of her own practice that became useful in her ongoing work, while she retained authorship over every meaningful refinement."**

This is **robust to revision** — it holds even if parts of the emerging architecture are later corrected, because it is a claim about the *process* (dialogue helped + authorship retained), not the *product* (the model is correct). It is the claim-discipline's **Failure Test** applied to a research result: strip the unproven layers, and the result still stands.

### 7.1 Mission and research program are different logical types

The **mission** (`LEGACY_FIRST_POSITIONING` §4.7) is a *declared purpose* — *help practitioners discover, preserve, and extend the intelligence of their life's work.* This **research program** is the *open empirical question underneath it* — *how does reflective dialogue do that?* The mission is **chosen and honored**; the program is **investigated and answered.** They do not compete: the program is the **investigable core** of the declared mission, and it produces transferable knowledge about practitioners whether or not the product succeeds commercially. That is the strongest possible foundation — the work cannot fully *lose*, because it generates knowledge either way.

## 8. Three products (Vision-tier organizing frame)

Read as offerings, the program suggests three connected-but-distinct products. **Tier: Vision** — an organizing frame, not three shipped things (today this is a research protocol + specs).

1. **Architectural Translation** — discovery engagements with experienced practitioners; produces an explicit, *provisional* representation of tacit practice. (≈ deployment Stage 1.)
2. **Practitioner Studio** — ongoing reflective companion; continues refinement after the initial translation. (≈ deployment Stage 2.)
3. **Field Lab** — research environment; aggregates evidence across practitioners to improve the *methodology itself*.

Each feeds the next: Translation → Studio (longitudinal observation) → Field Lab (tests and refines the method).

**Recursive discipline:** Field Lab studies the methodology — so the **"designed so failure is informative"** property (§0) applies to it *recursively.* A research environment that can only ever *refine* its method, never find it *failing*, is self-confirmation at the program scale. Field Lab must be able to return *"Architectural Translation did **not** work here"* — or it is not research.

## 9. The load-bearing discipline

> **The system proposes; the practitioner disposes.**

This is the operational definition of authorship — the two crossings of `LEGACY_SESSIONS_SPEC` §3 compressed to four words: *propose* = offer, never assert (Crossing 1); *dispose* = the practitioner authors, never merely assents (Crossing 2). Every reflection is a proposal; every refinement belongs to the practitioner; **every rejection is high-value data, not a failure** (it is the clearest proof the process is not Barnum — Gate 2). Hold this consistently and the beta stops being a software trial: it becomes the first empirical test of whether **Architectural Translation** reliably helps experienced practitioners externalize, examine, and evolve tacit knowledge **without surrendering authorship.**

## 10. Refinements (2026-06-25) — what Jondi actually validates

### 10.1 Phenomenon · methodology · instrument (and how to attribute a failure)
Three levels, not two:
- **Phenomenon** — how tacit knowledge becomes explicit while preserving authorship (the thing in the world).
- **Methodology** — *Architectural Translation*: the structured intervention claimed to reliably produce it.
- **Instrument** — *this protocol* (arc, prompts, logs): how we observe the methodology in one study.

Jondi validates the **methodology**, observed *through* the instrument. So an awkward Session 3 is, by default, an **instrument** problem — not an Architectural-Translation failure. Phenomenon and measuring device are different things.

**Guard (or this turns unfalsifiable):** "blame the instrument" is itself an escape hatch — if every failure is reassigned to the instrument, the methodology can never fail. So **pre-register, before each phase, what would count as a *methodology* failure vs an *instrument* failure vs the *phenomenon not occurring.*** A failure with no pre-declared owner gets charitably absorbed — the exact inflation this program refuses.

### 10.2 Jondi is a collaborator, not a participant
Posture: not *"we have a protocol to test on you"* but *"we're trying to understand how tacit practice becomes articulable — let's investigate together."* This is the authorship principle applied to the **research relationship** itself, and it is *more* likely to produce the authorship we are protecting.

**Governor caveat (cuts both ways):** a collaborator is *motivated for it to work* → higher demand-characteristic / Barnum risk. That makes the **Discrimination probe (Gate 2) more essential, not less**: a collaborator who is motivated to agree and *still rejects* planted-wrong patterns is the strongest evidence in the study. Collaborator-stance and probe **need each other.** (Consequence: findings are co-authored — her *practice* is hers; the *methodology insights* are shared.)

### 10.3 Two phenomena, scored separately: Externalization vs Emergence
Reflection does two different things; conflating them mislabels invention as recollection:
- **Externalization** — what was *already present but implicit* is recovered (Stream A).
- **Emergence** — what *genuinely came into being* during the dialogue (Stream C).

**Discriminator:** does the surfaced pattern **retrodict** — explain her *past* cases (recovered)? Or does she say *"I'd never thought of it this way until now,"* applying only forward (created)? The **Week-0 baseline free-write** is the anchor: present-in-baseline ⇒ externalization; absent ⇒ candidate emergence.

**Why it is also an anti-steering guard:** emergence mislabeled as externalization is *steering wearing the recognition costume* — MAIA introduced something and it got scored as "recovered from her practice." Emergence is also where **authorship is genuinely ambiguous** (it arose *between* them): score it as a distinct, valuable outcome, and let *her* author whether to keep it.

**But emergence is usually retrospective — don't force the call in the moment.** You typically cannot tell, *as it appears*, that something is genuinely new; it nearly always *feels like recognition.* So classify nothing in the room. Tag each surfaced item **Candidate Recovery** or **Candidate Emergence** and **resolve it on a later revisit**: weeks on, some candidate-recoveries reveal themselves as emergence (*"I'd never actually thought that before"*) and some candidate-emergences turn out to have been long-standing intuitions. **That drift, in both directions, is data about the phenomenon — not noise to eliminate.** Forcing premature classification is its own false precision, and asking *her* to classify pulls her out of thinking (a conversation-layer violation, §11).

### 10.4 The candidate cycle (a more fundamental hypothesis — single-source, not yet earned)
Kelly's own AIN development has repeatedly run: **Live practice → Reflection → Representation → Governance → Contact with reality → Revision.** Architectural Translation appears to *instantiate the same cycle* for practitioners. If it persists across Larry, Jondi, and others, it may name something more general than an AI-reflection method: **a repeatable cycle by which complex human practice becomes progressively more explicit without losing its grounding in lived experience** — and it gives Field Lab a long-term purpose beyond software.

**Discipline (the signature caution):** this is currently **single-source** — the *same lens* designed both the development process *and* the methodology, so their matching is **not** independent convergence (it may be the method inheriting its author's habits). It earns "fundamental cycle" standing only if the cycle appears in a practitioner's **own** development that **predates and is independent of** this method — e.g., did Jondi's pre-contact way of maturing her practice already follow it? **Hypothesis, not conclusion;** held as a candidate, indexed to that independence test.

## 11. Protecting the experience (stop adding governance here)

The instrument list is now long — Recognition, Discrimination, Elaboration, Utility, Transfer, Externalization/Emergence, pre-registered failure attribution, consent, authorship, provenance. All are valid **research instruments**, and Jondi should **almost never experience any of them as instruments.** She should experience one thing: **a deeply attentive conversation.** The instrumentation is methodologically valid *only if it stays invisible* — a practitioner who feels tested talks like someone being tested, which suppresses the very tacit knowledge the study exists to surface. **Invisibility is not kindness; it is validity.**

**Three layers, asymmetric rule:**
- **Conversation** — what Jondi experiences.
- **Observation** — what the facilitator notices and records *afterward*.
- **Research** — how those observations are later analyzed.

They may inform **downward** (research shapes what to notice); they must never bend **upward** — *the conversation is never sacrificed to get a data point.* The facilitator is the one human holding Conversation and Observation at once, so the **prime directive** is the safeguard that keeps the lower layer from leaking into the higher as steering: *remain more interested in understanding the practitioner than in validating the methodology.* (Its human-side mirror of *"designed so failure is informative."*) Operationalized in `docs/research/JONDI_CONCIERGE_FACILITATOR_GUIDE_2026-06-25.md`.

**The strong question is not "does it work?"** A robust methodology must answer **who it helps, who it does not, and under what conditions** — and *"nothing happened"* can be the **highest-value datum**, the coordinate that maps a real boundary. *"Works for everyone"* would be Barnum at the methodology scale; **discovering its limits is the Field Lab's most valuable long-term contribution** — the anti-Barnum guard one level up from differential transfer (§3.4).
