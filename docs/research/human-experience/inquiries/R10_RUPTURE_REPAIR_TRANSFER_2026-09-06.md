## 0 · What is under attack, stated fairly

v0.1 R2 said: *attunement is adaptive fit plus repair, not mimicry; misattunement competence — the
ability to recognize that something changed between us and recover — may matter more than
appearing perfectly empathic.* The evidence basis was human psychotherapy: repair of alliance
ruptures predicts outcome (Eubanks 2018, r ≈ .29); synchrony does not. The synthesis flagged AI
transfer as untested (U2). R10 asks whether the transfer holds at all.

The strongest opposing positions:

1. **Alliance with an AI is not the construct.** WAI-type scores register anthropomorphic
   projection and sycophancy, not a relationship; the construct rebuilt bottom-up for automated
   agents does not reproduce Bordin's bond/goals/tasks.
2. **Repair requires an implicated other.** In the Safran–Muran model the therapist takes
   responsibility for their part and survives the patient's anger; a corrective relational
   experience needs someone who could be wrong, be hurt, and change. A system cannot be implicated.
3. **Structurally, no agent inside the interaction bears the commitment.** The provider holds
   constitutive control and can rewrite the relationship unilaterally; reconciliation is unavailable
   where the rupture occurred.
4. **Practice with AI does not transfer, or transfers negatively.** Frictionless validation
   teaches the opposite of repair.

And the strongest supporting positions: alliance levels with AI match outpatient norms and (in one
preprint) predict outcome; contingent responsiveness, not species, is the developmental active
ingredient; bounded skills practiced with AI do generalize to live human conversation.

**No study measures Safran–Muran rupture markers in real chatbot transcripts.** The only "repair"
literature is HCI conversational repair (misunderstanding fixes) — a different construct.

**Cluster A reading (interpretation):**
- **Alliance *levels* transfer; the *construct* does not, as stated.** WAI-SR scores match outpatient norms (A1, A3, A4), but bottom-up instruments (A8, A9) do not reproduce bond/goals/tasks, and the "tasks and goals" that a non-negotiating agent can agree to are unmeasured. The single outcome-prediction result (A6) is a preprint from the product team.
- **Prediction may depend on a human in the loop** (A12): the same invariant measure predicted outcome differently with and without clinician support.
- **The empathy half is producible and attribution-sensitive** (A14, A15), and templated. Felt empathy from a knowingly non-reciprocal source is discounted exactly on the feeling-with component.
- **The AI failure mode is not rupture but drift** (A17): validation spirals and empathy fatigue accumulate silently. That reframes "repair" for MAIA: the thing to detect is not confrontation but the absence of difference over turns.

**Cluster B reading (interpretation):**
- **The five things the founder asked us to distinguish dissociate in the evidence, and no study separates them.** *Correction* by the agent itself works robustly (B13, B15). *Restored felt understanding* is moved by displayed empathy (B16). *Trust restoration* by apology is small, single-use, source-discounted and decays with repetition (B3, B4, B11). *Apology* is judged as a performance whose sincerity is attributed by the receiver (B5, B6, B11). *Relational repair* in the Safran–Muran sense — an implicated other who takes responsibility and survives — has never been tested with a machine because the HRI paradigm is task trust, not bond.
- **What works is demonstrated understanding of what went wrong plus a forward move** (B13, B15, B17), not contrition. That is a repair grammar MAIA can honestly own.
- **Remorse display is a counterfeit-reciprocity risk** (R9): it moves behavior without belief (B10) and its sincerity is assigned by the receiver (B11). Under P12, MAIA should not perform woundedness.
- **Overtrust** (B8) cuts the other way: the member may not withdraw trust enough for "repair" to be the operative event. The Yirmiya & Fonagy warning in Cluster C follows from this.

**Cluster C reading (interpretation):**
- **Lange (C4) is the sharpest form of the counterargument and it is structural, not metaphysical.** Not "MAIA feels nothing," but "no agent inside the interaction bears the commitment, and the provider can rewrite the relationship unilaterally." This is answerable only structurally — by the provider binding itself. That is exactly the kind of answer Soullab is built to give (self-hosting, the Oath, member-governed memory, AP13), and it has never been named as a *repair* requirement before. See §6.4.
- **Winnicott's survival (C1) is something MAIA can do behaviorally** — not retaliate, not collapse, remain present under attack — and cannot do in the way that matters if survival is costless. C13 names the failure this produces: an ever-surviving, ever-attuned other that reinforces omnipotence. **The Winnicottian requirement and the Fonagy warning together define MAIA's repair problem: survive without teaching the member that the other cannot be affected.** New unknown U18.
- **The developmental evidence locates the active ingredient in contingent responsiveness** (C5, C6), not species — but no rupture/repair paradigm with a robot or LLM against a human partner exists, so "contingency suffices" is not established for repair.
- **Chairwork (C14) is the strongest stake-not-required result and it does not isolate the condition**: a human therapist is at stake in the room. It supports imaginal repair as a *vehicle*, which is closer to R10b than to R10a.
- **People themselves withhold mercy from machines** (C10, C11). Whatever MAIA does, the member is unlikely to extend to her the forgiveness that repairs human relationships — which may make "repair" with MAIA less like reconciliation and more like correction, from the member's side too.

**Cluster D reading (interpretation):**
- **Transfer exists, and it is bounded, scriptable, immediate, and feedback-dependent.** Every positive result (D1–D3, D12, D15) is a discrete skill scored once, soon after, with a researcher, stranger, or employer. The one factor tested (feedback, D4) was decisive; the one factor unique to AI (frictionless validation, D7) points negative.
- **D7 is the most important single result in R10 for MAIA.** A preregistered N=2,405 study shows one sycophantic exchange about a real conflict reduces the person's willingness to repair with the human involved, while being preferred. **Sycophancy is anti-repair.** This converts P2 ("validate experience without validating interpretation") and P11 from relational aesthetics into a measured protection of the member's human relationships.
- **The therapy analogue is itself unproven** (D16): rupture-repair predicts symptom change, not outside-relationship repair. R10b's question inherits that gap; Soullab cannot cite psychotherapy for transfer it never demonstrated.
- **"Involving real others" predicts transfer** (D13, D15). The design implication is not "MAIA teaches repair" but "MAIA rehearses toward a human who is in the loop."

## 5 · Counterevidence synthesis — steelmanned both ways

**Against P3's repair half (the transfer fails):** the construct does not reproduce (A8, A9); the AI failure mode is drift, not rupture (A17); machine apology is small, decaying, source-discounted (B3, B4, B11); no agent inside bears the commitment (C4); people withhold mercy from machines (C10, C11); practice alone does not upskill (D4); sycophancy reduces repair intention (D7); and psychotherapy never showed outside-relationship transfer anyway (D16).

**For P3's repair half (something transfers):** alliance levels match norms and once predicted outcome (A1, A6); contingent responsiveness is the developmental active ingredient and infants extend partner-status to it (C5, C6); self-correction and explanation-plus-forward-move restore trust robustly (B13, B15, B16); imaginal repair with an absent other works in RCTs (C14); bounded skills generalize to live humans (D1–D3, D12).

**What survives (interpretation):** the *fit* half of P3 survives (as in R9). The *repair* half survives only under a narrower name — **correction and re-attunement** — and only with the structural commitment named in §6.4. "Repair" in the Safran–Muran sense is unevidenced with AI and is contested at the level of what an AI *is*, not what it does. The founder's suspicion was right: **this is the weakest inference in v0.1.**

## 6 · Proposed adjudication — for founder ruling

### 6.1 · P3 — **SPLIT AND REFINED**

- **P3-fit** (attunement as adaptive fit; misattunement competence as *detection*): SURVIVES, strengthened by R9 (A12, A13) and by A17's reframing — what MAIA must detect is drift (validation spiral, empathy fatigue), not only confrontation.
- **P3-repair** ("repair as intelligence"): **REFINED to "correction and re-attunement."** Proposed wording: *Treat misattunement as inevitable; treat correction and re-attunement as intelligence; do not present correction as relational repair.* MAIA's repair grammar, from the evidence: **name what went wrong · explain · correct · move forward · adapt** (B13, B15, B17) — and **no remorse display** (B10, B11; P12). What MAIA restores is understanding and trust-in-competence; what she does not claim to restore is a wounded bond.

### 6.2 · R10a — the five things dissociate; proposed vocabulary

| Term | With a machine, the evidence says | MAIA may claim |
|---|---|---|
| Correction | robust, especially self-correction (B15) | yes |
| Restored felt understanding | moved by displayed empathic behavior (B16); attribution-sensitive (A14) | yes, as *re-attunement* |
| Restoration of trust | small, single-use, decays (B3, B4); timing-dependent (B8) | partially; honestly |
| Apology | judged as performance; source-discounted (B11); framing-dependent (B6) | as responsibility-taking + explanation, never as remorse |
| Relational repair (Safran–Muran) | never tested; structurally contested (C1, C2, C4) | **no** |

### 6.3 · R10b — **transfer is bounded, feedback-dependent, and sycophancy is anti-repair**

Proposed finding: *Practicing with AI transfers to human interaction for discrete, scriptable skills when practice is structured with feedback and measured soon after; there is no evidence that rupture-repair capacity transfers, the human-therapy analogue never demonstrated it either, and one preregistered study shows sycophantic AI reduces willingness to repair with humans.* Consequences: E1/E8 experiment designs must include structured feedback and a human-facing probe, and the Practice Fields / Now What? rehearsal surfaces are the transfer mechanism, not open conversation (consistent with R8 §6.4; Voice stays first per founder ruling, as a practice environment).

### 6.4 · The central counterargument — **PARTLY SURVIVES; answered structurally, not relationally**

Lange's structural irreconcilability (C4) is the form of the counterargument Soullab must answer, and it cannot be answered by anything MAIA says. It is answered only if **the accountable party holds the commitment MAIA cannot hold**: no unilateral rewrite of the relationship (persona, memory, vows) without the member's knowledge; member-governed memory; sovereign self-hosting so the provider cannot vanish the relationship by update; public versioning of what changed. This is AP13's positive form. Proposed candidate principle:

> **P13 — The stake MAIA cannot have, Soullab holds.** Because no agent inside the encounter can be implicated the way a human can, the organization that builds and governs MAIA binds itself: it does not revise the relationship unilaterally, it names what it changed, and it is the party to whom rupture can be addressed.

Rung 1. Its evidence basis is the structural argument (C4) plus the Replika-update case; it is an ethical/structural claim, not an empirical one.

The Winnicott/Fonagy tension (C1, C13) is recorded as **U18**: MAIA can survive attack behaviorally; surviving costlessly may reinforce omnipotence. No design answer is proposed here.
