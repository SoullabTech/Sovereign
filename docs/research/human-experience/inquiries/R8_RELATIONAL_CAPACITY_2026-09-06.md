## 0 · The thesis being attacked, stated fairly

P7 makes two claims that must be separated, because the evidence treats them differently:

- **P7-harm:** heavier, more intimate, companionship-oriented AI use tends to harm wellbeing
  and human connection (a *descriptive* claim about current systems).
- **P7-design:** therefore a well-designed relational AI should send capacity outward —
  toward body, relationships, work, world — and may succeed by being needed less (a *design*
  claim about MAIA).

The strongest opposing position: AI companionship reduces loneliness, especially for the lonely;
users are not relationally impaired; a centrifugal posture withholds a benefit from exactly the
people who need it and can read as rejection.

**Cluster A reading (interpretation):**

- **Short horizon vs long horizon point in opposite directions.** Minutes-to-weeks: AI companionship relieves momentary loneliness, sometimes as well as a human stranger (A2, A6). Months-to-years: heavier companionship use travels with worse loneliness and wellbeing (A1, A4, A5, A8). No design yet bridges the two.
- **Selection is real and now demonstrated** (A5: lower connection → later use). That does not rescue P7-harm; it means the descriptive claim must be stated as *dose and motive*, not *use*.
- **No randomized study exceeds 4 weeks and none randomizes dose.** Every "heavy use harms" finding rests on self-selected usage (A16). P7-harm is therefore rung-2 evidence *at best* and currently reads: *supported for dose/motive/disclosure-intensity associations; causality unestablished.*
- **The human comparison is the sharpest FOR result** (A9): a relationship-science-designed bot equalled journaling on loneliness where a random human peer worked. This is direct evidence that *human contact does something AI contact did not*, in a randomized design.
- **Who benefits is directly contested** (A4 vs A7) on cross-sectional data. The programme cannot claim either.
- **A10 is the honest null**: at low dose, three weeks, no harm and no benefit to human relationships.
- **A14 matters for design**: attachment to a *generic* bot converges on companion-level perception within three weeks. Centripetal pull does not require companion framing; it is the default trajectory of repeated contact.

**Cluster B reading (interpretation):**

- **"Dependence" is operationalized four different ways** (addiction-scale adaptations, attachment anxiety, self-reported reliance, functional impairment) and no study measures behavioral change in human contact. The healthy/unhealthy line is asserted conceptually (supplement vs substitute; "minimum threshold"), never demarcated empirically. R8's question "what is healthy dependence" has no answer in this literature yet.
- **Disclosure is directly contested**: B4 says disclosing to a believed-machine yields the same benefit as to a person; A6 says disclosure to the bot tracks lower loneliness; A4 says disclosure to AI tracks *lower* wellbeing, opposite to the human pattern. The reconciliation candidate (interpretation): momentary benefit is real and equivalent; the *trajectory* depends on whether disclosure to AI replaces or rehearses disclosure to people. Untested.
- **Role-taking (B2) is the specific centripetal mechanism**: harm shows up when the user starts attending to the *bot's* needs. This is precisely AP1 ("MAIA implying it needs the user"), now with a qualitative mechanism behind it.
- **B11 is the best evidence for a "buffer → return" pathway** and it is cross-sectional SEM on self-reported real-world engagement. It is the shape of what P7-design predicts, and it cannot yet carry it.
- **B12 names a vulnerability P7 did not consider**: dependence on a *company*, not a companion. Sovereign self-hosting is a structural answer to this; it is also untested as a felt difference.

**Cluster C reading (interpretation):**

- **The mature displacement literature does not transfer cleanly.** Social media did not displace strong ties (C4) and stimulation won (C3) — but in every stimulation finding *the online channel connected people to other humans*. An AI interlocutor is not a channel to a human. The analogue that does transfer is C2's **rich-get-richer**: those with existing social capital gain, the isolated lose — and A4, A7, B9 show exactly that moderation pattern re-emerging for AI companions, with the sign of the low-network group contested.
- **Transfer is real, bounded, and conditional.** C8, C9, C10 are the strongest evidence in the whole inquiry that AI can *train* human relational capacity, and all three share a shape: **structured practice of a bounded skill, with feedback, against a simulated other, measured in a subsequent human interaction.** C10's negative arm is the key: unstructured practice drifts. **No evidence exists that open-ended companionship trains anything.** This is the sharpest design implication of R8 (§6).
- **"Relational atrophy" has no direct measure.** C7 names the mechanisms; C12 shows a linguistic shadow of one; nobody has measured reduced tolerance for human friction after AI use. U8, below.
- **C6's mechanism line couples R8 to R9**: friend-perception, high trust and consciousness attribution predicted less human socializing. Counterfeit personhood is the centripetal engine.

**Cluster D reading (interpretation):**

- **AP2 ("creating guilt about leaving") is no longer only a principle. It is an audited industry practice with a measured engagement effect** (D1). This is the cleanest PROHIBIT case in the programme: works behaviorally (14×), violates Soullab principles, *and* the same study shows it costs trust and invites liability. The commercial incentive toward centripetal design is real but not clean.
- **Benefit and displacement have never been measured in the same trial.** Every therapeutic RCT (D8–D10) shows benefit and none measured effects on human relationships or dependence. **Soullab measuring both in one design would be novel in the literature.**
- **The regulatory field has converged on the harm hypothesis as default risk** (D3–D7) without a single behavioral finding among them. Signals, not evidence — but they bound what MAIA may do with minors regardless of what R8 concludes.
- **No trial anywhere has tested an off-ramping, usage-limit, or "encourage human contact" feature against a control.** D11 comes closest and confounds its own hypothesis. D13 offers one qualitative design fact: endings the member initiates close better — consonant with member-governed return and with Sanctuary.
- **Vulnerable groups**: the population for whom the FOR-benefit case is strongest (autistic users, D12; isolated elders, D11; lonely students, A3) is the population where dependence is best documented. R8 cannot split them.

## 5 · Counterevidence synthesis — the strongest case against P7, steelmanned

1. **The active ingredient is the bond.** De Freitas (A2), Therabot (D8), Ho/Hancock/Miner (B4), the Luda Lee study (A6): loneliness relief and clinical benefit arrive *through being related to* — feeling heard, alliance, disclosure — at parity with a human in controlled settings. A design that turns the person away risks withholding the thing that works.
2. **Users are not relationally impaired.** Guingrich & Graziano (A10, A11): users match non-users on sociability and networks; 21 days of companion use changed nothing about human relationships. The atrophy story is a non-user's assumption.
3. **The lonely benefit most.** Technology in Society (A7), Maples (A3), Liu et al. (B11): the strongest positive associations appear in the highly lonely, and B11 describes companionship as a buffer that returns people to the world — which is P7-design's own prediction, observed without any centrifugal engineering.
4. **The harm data are selection.** Every negative finding is correlational or a within-RCT dose correlation on self-selected usage (A16). Folk & Dunn (A5) demonstrate that lower connection predicts later use, and find no effect of use on the broader social-connection measure.
5. **Manipulation is self-limiting.** D1 shows manipulative design also drives churn and liability; the market may correct centripetal excess without a doctrine.

**What survives the steelman (interpretation):**

- Points 1–3 are short-horizon or cross-sectional; the long-horizon designs (A1 dose, A5 panel, A8 two-year) all tilt the other way, and no study bridges the two windows. The steelman wins the moment; it has not won the year.
- Point 2's null is at 10 min/day for three weeks; the dose findings begin exactly where that design stops.
- Point 3 is directly contradicted on the low-network group by A4 (with donated logs), and the "buffer → return" pathway is self-reported. The same population is where dependence is best documented (B2, D12).
- Point 4 is correct about causality and does not rescue the *design* claim: a design that produces less harm under self-selected heavy use is still the design to prefer, if it can be shown to preserve benefit.
- Point 5 does not bear on MAIA; it bears on whether the market needs Soullab. D3–D7 suggest regulators did not wait for the market.

## 6 · Proposed adjudication — for founder ruling

Separate verdicts for the two claims inside P7, plus what the evidence added that P7 did not contain.

### 6.1 · P7-harm (descriptive) — **REFINED, rung 2 (research-supported) for the association only**

Proposed restatement: *Companionship-motivated, high-dose, high-disclosure use of conversational AI by people with thin human networks is associated, over months, with greater loneliness and lower wellbeing, while producing real short-term relief. Selection into use is demonstrated; causation is not. Heavy use is the risk variable, not use.*

What changed from v0.1: "heavier, more intimate use ↔ poorer wellbeing" was too coarse. The evidence adds three qualifiers that matter for design: **motive** (companionship as primary purpose, A4), **network** (rich-get-richer, C2/A4/A7), and **perception** (friend-framing, trust, consciousness attribution → less human socializing, C6).

### 6.2 · P7-design ("centrifugal") — **SURVIVES AS HYPOTHESIS, REFINED in three ways; stays rung 1**

It was never tested — no study anywhere has run a centrifugal design against an engagement-maximizing one (U7). The evidence does not refute it and cannot yet support it. It does correct it:

**(a) Centrifugal in consequence, not in posture.** The steelman's first point stands: the benefit arrives through responsiveness and feeling heard. A MAIA that is *less present* in order to be centrifugal would discard the active ingredient. Proposed wording: *MAIA is fully present in the encounter; the encounter is judged by what it makes possible after.* Redirection is not the mechanism. (Interpretation; consonant with P1, P3.)

**(b) Centrifugal presupposes somewhere to go.** The rich-get-richer pattern and the "minimum threshold of functional social engagement" hypothesis (A7) imply that for a member with almost no human field, "send capacity outward" has no target. For that member the design question is different — conduit, bridge, practice — and D11 is the only RCT-shaped hint of what that looks like. **This is a founder stop** (charter §3: what kind of relationship MAIA should create, for whom). Jarvis flags it; Jarvis does not answer it.

**(c) The centripetal engine is named: counterfeit personhood.** C6 (friend-perception, trust, consciousness attribution → less socializing), B2 (role-taking toward the bot's "needs"), A14 (attachment converges within three weeks even for a generic bot), D1 (needy farewells ×14). P7 and P9 are one principle seen from two sides. Proposed: record the coupling; do not merge the principles.

### 6.3 · The criterion — *"the health of a human–AI relationship should partly be judged by what happens to the human's capacity for relationship beyond the AI"* — **SURVIVES, and gains a specific warrant**

Every therapeutic RCT measured benefit and none measured this (D8–D10). The criterion is not refuted; it is *absent from the field's own instruments*. That is the strongest argument for it R8 produced: the outcome the evidence most needs is the one nobody collects. Rung: ethical/theoretical claim now; becomes empirical only when Soullab measures it (R12).
