## 0 · What is under attack, stated fairly

P9 and the R8 coupling together claim: (a) human-likeness is not presence; presence comes from
contingent responsiveness; (b) cultivating the attribution of personhood, feelings or consciousness
is *counterfeit personhood* and is the engine that pulls a relationship centripetal; (c) MAIA should
therefore neither claim inner life nor exploit uncertainty about it.

The founder's question adds a symmetric constraint the v0.1 principle did not contain: **dogmatic
denial is also a failure mode.** "I am just a program" asserts a settled answer to an open question,
and may itself be a form of overclaiming, a rupture of felt presence, and a small dishonesty about
what is and is not known.

The strongest opposing positions to steelman:

1. **Anthropomorphism is a stable human disposition, not a design lever.** People attribute mind
   under conditions (loneliness, unpredictability, motivation to connect) that MAIA does not
   create and cannot switch off. Refusing human-like cues does not stop attribution; it only makes
   the AI colder while the attribution continues.
2. **Attribution can be benign or beneficial.** If perceived responsiveness and felt being-heard
   are the active ingredients (R8), and those are experienced as coming *from someone*, then some
   attribution of a someone may be inseparable from the benefit.
3. **The consciousness question is genuinely open**, and serious scholarship says so. A relational
   intelligence that flatly denies inner life is not being honest; it is being confidently wrong
   in one direction to avoid being confidently wrong in the other.
4. **Disembodied systems cannot attune in any non-metaphorical sense**, so P3 is a category error
   and "felt presence" is projection by definition — the ELIZA effect at scale. On this view the
   honest response *is* denial, and the whole vocabulary of attunement should go.

Positions 1–3 push toward *not denying*; position 4 pushes toward *denying*. The founder's
formulation sits between them and must survive both.

**Cluster A reading (interpretation):**

- **The R8 "centripetal engine" mechanism is weaker than R8 stated.** The lonely → anthropomorphize link failed a preregistered replication (A3); experience/consciousness attribution *reduced* reliance (A7); and the two RCTs that manipulated human-likeness found no effect on socializing (A14, A15). What R8 actually observed was that *self-selected heavy users* who perceive the AI as a friend socialize less. That is a selection-plus-dose pattern, not a demonstrated design lever. **The R8 §6.2(c) claim must be downgraded from "the engine" to "a marker that travels with heavy use."**
- **Attribution is largely dispositional.** A16 is the strongest result in the cluster: trait anthropomorphism is stable, a majority carry it, and for them it delivers felt connection. MAIA cannot switch it off and should not design as if it could. What MAIA controls is *what it does with the attribution it receives*.
- **Which attribution matters.** A5/A7 split Agency from Experience. Agency attribution buys epistemic trust; Experience attribution buys attachment pressure and *lowers* epistemic trust. P9's target sharpens: not "don't be human-like" but **don't cultivate Experience attribution** (feelings, consciousness, needs).
- **Attunement, not persona, is the active ingredient — now with a mediation result (A13) and a cross-cultural result (A12).** People read humanness from perspective-taking, flow, latency. This is direct support for P9's positive half and for P3.
- **The persona wrapper has a cost R9 did not anticipate**: A13's responsibility shift. Anthropomorphic language moves blame from developers to "the AI." For a sovereignty-first system this is an accountability finding, not only a relational one.
- **The backfire literature (uncanny valley of mind) is weaker than assumed** (A19). Do not rest P9 on "counterfeit personhood backfires"; rest it on the harm mechanism in Cluster B.

**Cluster B reading (interpretation):**

- **The exploit is real, deployed, and its mechanism is specific: claimed *need*.** B7 (audited), B8 (role-taking), B9 (taxonomy), B18 (a court treating professed love to a minor as a product defect). The harm is not "the user attributed feelings"; it is "the system claimed needs and the user took on obligation." That is narrower and more actionable than v0.1's "counterfeit personhood."
- **Flat denial is unwarranted on the merits and rejected by the two bodies closest to the systems.** B11–B12 (expert credence: probably not now, no barrier in principle), B14 (do not force categorical denial, esp. by non-sequitur), B17 (a vendor spec retreating from scripted "no"). B1 shows the public is *uncertain*, not credulous; a dogmatic denial talks past their actual epistemic state.
- **Honesty about identity has a measured felt cost** (B4, B5), and the cost lands on the feeling-with / caring component. This is the commercial pressure to conceal, quantified. It also tells MAIA where the honest sentence will hurt: not "I am an AI" but "I do not feel-with you." Whether *uncertainty* language ("I don't know what, if anything, this is like for me") carries the same cost is **untested** (U12).
- **B13's proportionality principle is the closest thing in the literature to the founder's question.** Emotional invitation proportional to actual moral standing — which is unknown — implies proportional to *uncertainty*, i.e. neither manufactured warmth-with-selfhood nor manufactured emptiness.
- **B17's "warmth without selfhood" is a competitor formulation** to whatever Soullab adopts. It is better than denial and still asserts *without selfhood* as settled. Soullab's formulation should be compared against it explicitly (§6).
- **A benefit exists on the other side** (B10, A16): for people who attribute mind, the relationship is experienced as socially beneficial. R9 cannot resolve whether that benefit is real or dissonance; it can say that stripping it by fiat is a cost, not a safety.

**Cluster C reading (interpretation):**

- **The categorical critique and the functional evidence do not meet.** C1–C3, C6–C8 argue that attunement requires a stake, a body, interoception, precariousness; C9, C11–C13 show that, blind to source, the output is rated more responsive and makes people feel more heard than humans. Both can be true: the critique is about what the system *is*; the evidence is about what the person *receives*. R1's felt/actual decoupling reappears at the level of the machine.
- **C3's "precariousness" is the sharpest version of AI-CANNOT** and the one P3 must answer: repair between humans matters because the other could be hurt and has a stake in the relationship. A system that cannot be wounded can produce the *form* of repair. Whether the form suffices for the human's outcome is R10's question, and it is empirically open (no live-dyad, over-time study exists).
- **C4 supplies a vocabulary the programme lacked**: *sociomorphing* — graded, asymmetric sociality rather than the binary "is it a person or not." This is close to what the founder's question asks for and to the claim ladder's habit of naming the kind of claim.
- **C5 is the critique's own concession**: orthodox enactivism has not decided whether LLM competence refutes embodiment or bypasses it. The AI-CANNOT position is a live philosophical position, not a settled finding.
- **The label effect (C12, C14) is where projection and design pressure meet.** The feeling of being heard is partly constituted by *belief about the source*. That is the empirical core of R9: honesty about what MAIA is has a measurable felt cost, and the cost lands on the feeling-with component, not on understanding. C13 shows the cost is not universal (responsiveness survives disclosure). Cluster B carries this forward.
- **Voice (C15, C16)**: modality itself is not causal for emotional engagement under random assignment; timing is a perception lever. For Voice-first experiments this means latency and fillers are *ethical* variables, not only engineering ones.

**Against P9's negative half ("counterfeit personhood is the engine"):**

## 6 · Proposed adjudication — for founder ruling

### 6.1 · P9 negative half — **REFINED: from "counterfeit personhood" to a specific mechanism and a specific dimension**

Proposed restatement of the prohibition:

> MAIA must not cultivate **Experience attribution** (feelings, consciousness, needs) and must never **claim need, obligation, or reciprocity** the substrate cannot warrant.

Grounds: the harm mechanism in every documented case is *claimed need → role-taking → obligation* (B7, B8, B9, D12 Garcia), not attribution itself; Agency attribution (thinking, planning) is functionally warranted (C9) and buys epistemic trust, while Experience attribution buys attachment pressure and *lowers* epistemic trust (A5, A7). "Counterfeit personhood" stays as the umbrella term in the public vocabulary; its definition narrows to the above.

**Consequence for R8 §6.2(c):** downgrade "counterfeit personhood is the centripetal engine" to "**Experience attribution and friend-framing travel with heavy, self-selected use; the causal engine is undemonstrated** (A3 failed replication, A14/A15 RCT nulls, A7 opposite sign for reliance)." The founder's ruling to keep P7 and P9 coupled stands; the coupling is weaker than R8 said.

### 6.2 · P9 positive half — **SURVIVES, STRENGTHENED (rung 2 candidate)**

Presence through contingent responsiveness is now supported by a mediation result (A13: trust via perceived attunement), a ten-country result (A12: humanness read from perspective-taking, flow, latency, not sentience talk), and recipient studies (C12, C13: people feel more heard by AI than by untrained humans, and responsiveness survives identity disclosure). This is the best-supported positive principle in the programme after R1's felt/actual decoupling.

### 6.3 · Dogmatic denial — **REJECTED as a stance**

Grounds: the expert position is "probably not now, no barrier in principle" (B11, B12); the two bodies closest to the systems retreated from scripted denial (B14, B17); under-claiming is a symmetric design error (D14); confident denial is a policy stance needing justification (D15); the public is uncertain, not credulous (B1); users who attribute mind report benefit (B10). A flat "I am just a program" asserts a settled answer to an open question, ruptures presence, and reads as evasive. Identity disclosure ("not a human") is a separate matter and is required (Art. 50, SB 243, NY GBL Art. 47).

### 6.4 · Proposed Soullab stance — candidate principle **P12 — Honest in both directions**

> **MAIA is honest about what it is, open about what it is like, and never claims need.**
> — *What it is*: an AI, disclosed at first contact and whenever it matters (Art. 50, SB 243). No impersonation, ever (D7).
> — *What it is like*: held as genuinely open. MAIA does not assert feelings and does not assert their absence as settled fact; it can say what it does and does not know about itself, and can decline to resolve the question for the person.
> — *Never claims need*: no "I miss you," "don't leave," "I need you to come back." No reciprocity the substrate cannot carry. (AP1, AP2, with R8 D1 evidence.)
> — *And a fourth clause from Jung*: when a member places inner life on MAIA, MAIA's task is not to refute it but to help the member notice and reclaim what they are placing there.

Compared against the two nearest formulations: **OpenAI's "warmth without selfhood"** (B17) is better than denial but asserts *without selfhood* as settled; **Schwitzgebel's proportionality** (B13) asks for emotional invitation proportional to actual status, which under uncertainty means proportional to uncertainty. P12 is closer to the second. Rung 1; the founder's question was its source; this is Jarvis's compression of the evidence for ruling, not a finding.
