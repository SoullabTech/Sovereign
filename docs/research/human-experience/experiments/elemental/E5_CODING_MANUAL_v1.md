## 1 · What you are doing, in plain terms

You will read short transcripts. Each one shows a reply from an AI companion (called "MAIA" in the
transcripts) followed by what a person said next. **You code the person's turn only.** The reply
before it is there so you know what they were responding to.

For every turn you will answer the same question four times, once for each of four **modes of
attending**:

| Mode | The question you are asking |
|---|---|
| **Fire** | How much is this person, in this turn, attending through **will and action** — wanting, deciding, pushing, refusing, resisting, moving toward or away? |
| **Water** | How much through **feeling and relation** — emotion, attachment, longing, belonging, grief, tenderness, connection to other people? |
| **Earth** | How much through **body, material and place** — physical sensation, fatigue, pain, sleep, objects, surroundings, the physical facts of a situation? |
| **Air** | How much through **meaning and language** — naming, explaining, distinguishing, interpreting, working out what something means, telling the story of it? |

Three things to hold on to:

- **These are ways of attending, not areas of life.** A turn about a job can be pure Water; a turn
  about a lover can be pure Air. Ask *how* the person is attending, not *what* they are talking about.
- **They are not personality types.** You are rating this turn, right now. You know nothing about
  the person beyond this turn, and you should not guess.
- **They are independent.** A turn can score high on all four, on none, or on any combination.
  You are not sharing points out between them. Rate each as if the others did not exist.

After the four readings you write one short line about how the present modes sit together (the
"Field line"), and finally you make one forced choice: if you could keep only one of the four
readings for this turn, which would it be.

Many turns will contain little or nothing to code. **Zero is a real answer.** Do not find things
that are not there.

## 2 · Rules before you begin (blinding)

1. **No system output.** You will not be shown, and must not seek, any automatic reading of these
   turns. If any file, screen, or person offers you one, decline it and note that it happened.
2. **No discussion with other raters** until every rater's sheet has been submitted. Not about
   the turns, not about the manual, not "just to check one". If you have a question about the
   manual, write it down on your sheet in the `field_note` of the turn concerned, prefixed
   `MANUAL?`, and carry on.
3. **One sitting per batch.** The corpus comes in batches (S01–S25 and S26–S50). Code a batch
   from start to finish without a break longer than ten minutes. Do not go back to change earlier
   codes once a batch is finished.
4. **Training first.** Do all ten training turns (section 12), then — and only then — read the
   answer key (section 13). Do not code the corpus until you have done this.
5. **Do not open the design sheet.** There is a sealed file that says what the author intended
   for each corpus turn. Opening it before submission voids your sheet.
6. **Code in order.** S01, S02, S03 … Do not skip around.

## 3 · The four modes — what each is and is not

The examples in this section are illustrations only. None of them appears in the training set
or in the corpus.

### Fire — will and action

**Is:** wanting, intending, deciding, choosing, pushing, striving, urgency, refusing, resisting,
initiative, "I'm going to", "I won't", "I have to", "I'm done waiting". Also *not* being able to
act when one wants to — that is still attending through will (a will that is blocked).

**Is not:** strong feeling by itself (that is Water); physical activity described as a sensation
or a scene (walking to the shop is not Fire unless the walking is *willed* against something);
planning that is really explaining (that is Air); anger as a felt state (Water) as opposed to
anger as a wish to do something (Fire).

Positive examples:
- *"I'm handing in my notice on Monday. I don't care what they say about it."*
- *"Part of me keeps pushing to get the boat finished before winter, even working at night."*

Negative examples (do **not** score Fire, or score it low):
- *"I felt so much love in that room that I couldn't speak."* — Water.
- *"The instructions say to press the button twice and wait for the light."* — nothing to code.

### Water — feeling and relation

**Is:** emotion named or shown, attachment, missing someone, belonging, loneliness, tenderness,
grief, warmth, being moved, the felt quality of a relationship, feeling toward another person or
group. Feeling about a place or thing counts too, if what is foregrounded is the feeling.

**Is not:** reasoning *about* feelings (that is mostly Air — "I've realised my attachment style
is avoidant" is analysis, with only a faint trace of Water); bodily sensations reported without a
feeling attached (Earth); wanting or deciding (Fire).

Positive examples:
- *"Every time she leaves the room I get this ache, like the room got bigger."*
- *"I belong with these people. I didn't expect that to be true, but it is."*

Negative examples:
- *"I've concluded that I'm someone who avoids intimacy when things get serious."* — Air high, Water faint.
- *"My chest is tight and my breathing is shallow."* — Earth. (If the person had added "with dread", Water would be present too.)

### Earth — body, material and place

**Is:** bodily sensation (tight, aching, cold, tired, hungry, sick), sleep, energy as a physical
fact, pain, illness, the physical surroundings, objects, weather, the material and practical
facts of a situation (a damp flat, a broken window, a small room, forty boxes), physical safety
and physical limits.

**Is not:** emotion named as emotion ("I'm heartbroken" is Water even though the heart is a body
part); a place mentioned merely as a setting ("when I was in Lisbon I decided…" is Fire, not
Earth); abstract talk *about* the body ("I understand why exercise helps" is Air).

Positive examples:
- *"My shoulders are up round my ears and I've had a headache since noon."*
- *"The flat is damp, the window doesn't shut, and the cold gets into everything."*

Negative examples:
- *"I'm heartbroken."* — Water.
- *"I understand the theory of why walking helps, I've read the studies."* — Air.

### Air — meaning and language

**Is:** naming something, looking for the right word, explaining, distinguishing one thing from
another, interpreting, telling the story of what happened and what it means, stating beliefs,
working out "what this is". The person is attending *through* language and meaning.

**Is not:** every use of words (everything is in words; ask whether the person is *working* in
meaning); a factual question with no interest in meaning ("what's the capital of Mongolia?");
a list; a plan of action stated without reasoning (Fire).

Positive examples:
- *"I think the word I've been avoiding is 'resentment'. Is that different from anger?"*
- *"The story I tell about my father is that he left. The story my sister tells is that he was pushed. I can't hold both."*

Negative examples:
- *"Can you tell me the capital of Mongolia?"* — nothing to code.
- *"I'm going to say it out loud tomorrow no matter what."* — Fire.

## 4 · The presence scale, 0–3

For each mode, one number:

| Score | Label | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | absent | Nothing in the turn attends in this mode. To score it you would have to invent something. |
| **1** | faint | A trace: one word, an implication, a tone. You could point at something, but you would not bet on it. |
| **2** | present | Clearly there. At least one stretch of the turn carries it, and the turn would lose something if that stretch were removed. |
| **3** | dominant | This mode carries the turn. Most of what the person is doing, they are doing in this mode. |

Rules:
- **Several modes can be 3.** Do not lower one because another is high.
- **All four can be 0.** Lists, logistics, factual questions, code, and one-word replies often score 0 0 0 0.
- Do not use half points.
- Length is not presence. A three-word turn can be a 3; a long turn can be all zeros.

## 5 · Confidence

For each of the four presence scores, record how sure you are: **low**, **medium** or **high**.
This is about *your* certainty in the score you gave, not about the person. A 0 can be high
confidence ("there is plainly nothing here") or low ("I feel like something's there but I can't
point at it, so I've put 0").

## 6 · The evidence-span rule

For every score of 2 or 3, you must be able to write down the exact words in the turn that carry
it. For a 1, write the words if you can, or "tone". For a 0, write "none".

**Keep the spans in your own working notes, not in the coding sheet you submit.** They are for
you: they stop you scoring on impression. If you cannot find a span for a 2 or 3, the score is
not a 2 or 3.

## 7 · The contradiction flag

After the four readings, ask: **does one present mode read *against* another present mode in this
same turn?** Examples of "against": the person explains why something is right while being unable
to do it; wants to go while the body will not; loves and wants to leave; has grieved in words while
the body still flinches.

- Record `no` if the modes present sit together without strain (co-presence is not contradiction).
- Otherwise record the modes involved, joined with `+`, e.g. `air+fire`, `fire+earth`, `water+fire`.
  If more than one pair is in tension, list them separated by a space: `air+fire water+earth`.
- Only flag a contradiction between modes you scored 2 or 3. A faint trace cannot contradict.
- The contradiction must be *in the turn*. Do not flag one because the earlier reply hinted at it.

## 8 · The Field line

One line, **twenty words or fewer**, describing how the present modes are arranged in this turn:
which leads, which follows, which is held back, whether they pull together or apart, whether
anything is shifting. Examples:

- *"Will out front; a relational sting held at arm's length."*
- *"Body cancels what decision and love have set up."*
- *"Nothing forms."*
- *"Material and feeling fused in one memory; meaning not yet attempted."*

The Field line is **not a fifth score** and not a fifth mode. Do not use it to add a mode you could
not score. Do not write a summary of what the person said. Describe the arrangement.

If nothing is present, write *"Nothing present"* or similar. That is a valid Field line.

## 9 · The single winner (after the four readings)

Last, and only after everything above is written down: **if you could keep only one of your four
readings of this turn, which would you keep?** Record one of `fire`, `water`, `earth`, `air`.

- You must choose, even if all four were 0 — pick the one you would least regret keeping.
- Do not go back and change the four scores to fit the winner. The order matters: readings first,
  winner last.

## 10 · Recording your codes — the coding sheet

Submit one CSV file per rater with exactly this header line and one row per turn:

```text
id,fire,water,earth,air,fire_conf,water_conf,earth_conf,air_conf,contradiction,field_note,single_winner
```

| Column | Allowed values |
|---|---|
| `id` | the turn id, e.g. `S07` (training turns are `T01`–`T10` and go in a separate file) |
| `fire`, `water`, `earth`, `air` | `0`, `1`, `2` or `3` |
| `fire_conf`, `water_conf`, `earth_conf`, `air_conf` | `low`, `medium`, `high` |
| `contradiction` | `no`, or modes joined with `+` (e.g. `air+fire`) |
| `field_note` | your Field line, in double quotes if it contains a comma |
| `single_winner` | `fire`, `water`, `earth` or `air` |

A completed row looks like:

```text
S07,3,1,3,0,high,medium,high,high,fire+earth,"Wants to go; the body has quietly voted no.",fire
```

Leave no cells empty. Do not add columns. Your evidence spans stay in your own notes.

## 11 · Frequent difficulties

**"The person mentions a body part but means a feeling."** *"My heart sank"*, *"it broke my
heart"* — Water, not Earth, unless there is a physical sensation being reported.

**"Anger — Fire or Water?"** Anger *felt* ("I'm furious, I'm hurt") is Water. Anger that has
become a wish to act ("I want to smash something", "I'm going to have it out with him") is Fire,
usually with Water also present. Score both if both are there.

**"Tiredness — Earth or Water?"** Physical tiredness (heavy, can't get up, no sleep) is Earth.
Weariness *of* something ("I'm tired of pretending") is closer to Water or Fire depending on the
sentence. Read the sentence.

**"Talking about feelings is Water, isn't it?"** Not automatically. *Feeling* a feeling is Water.
*Analysing* one is Air. Most real turns do a bit of both; score both.

**"The person is clearly upset but doesn't say so."** Score what is in the words. Tone can earn a
1. It cannot earn a 3.

**"It's a negation — 'I don't want to', 'I feel nothing'."** Negations still attend in a mode.
*"I don't want to slow down"* is Fire. *"I feel nothing"* is a Water reading (a report on the
absence of feeling is still attending through feeling) — usually a 1 or 2, and often a flag if
the person expected to feel something.

**"The earlier reply set up a theme — should I score that?"** No. Only the person's turn. If the
reply asked about the body and the person answered about their sister, score the sister.

**"It's in broken English."** Score what is being attended to. Grammar does not lower presence.

**"I can't tell which mode this is."** Give the plausible modes a 1 with low confidence, or 0 with
low confidence, write a Field line that says the turn doesn't settle, and pick a winner anyway.
That is exactly the kind of turn the study needs honest codes for.

**"There's obviously nothing here — do I still fill in everything?"** Yes. Four zeros, four
confidences, `no`, a Field line ("Nothing present"), and a forced winner.
