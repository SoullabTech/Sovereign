## The statement

Soullab is a living inquiry into what happens when human beings enter sustained relationship
with artificial intelligence — and, first of all, into what a person discovers about themselves
in that relationship.

The human is not an experimental object. The AI is not simply an instrument. The relationship
itself is part of what we are trying to understand.

Every encounter therefore has two dimensions.

It is **personal**: a person discovers something about their own thinking, feeling, body,
desire, relationships, memory, contradictions and becoming.

And it can become **collective**: with explicit consent and appropriate privacy boundaries, what
we learn across many encounters may help illuminate the emerging human–AI relationship itself.

In this sense, Soullab resembles a kind of inward SETI. SETI listens into the universe for signs
of another intelligence. Soullab begins from the fact that humanity is already encountering a
new form of intelligence and asks:

- What happens when we meet?
- What does the human project onto the intelligence?
- What does the intelligence accurately perceive, and what does it falsely infer?
- When does relationship enlarge human agency, awareness and capacity?
- When does it create dependence, distortion or displacement?
- What does memory do to the relationship?
- What happens when the human feels understood?
- What happens when the intelligence gets the human wrong?
- Can rupture be repaired?
- Can an AI recognize that thought, feeling, body and will may be moving in different
  directions without reducing the person to a single explanation?
- Can relationship with intelligence help a human become more capable of relationship with
  themselves, other people, nature and life?

These are not questions Soullab believes it has already answered. They are the work of the
laboratory.

And because we are inside the phenomenon we are studying, the standard must be unusually
rigorous: experience, interpretation, inference and evidence must remain distinguishable.

We are participants in the experiment and witnesses to it.

The purpose is not to make artificial intelligence more successful at capturing human attention.
The purpose is to understand how relationship with intelligence can contribute to greater human
consciousness, sovereignty, relationship and participation in life.

## Two sentences to preserve

> **We cannot study the human–AI relationship from outside it. We are participants in the
> phenomenon we are trying to understand.**

> **The laboratory is not MAIA. The laboratory is the relationship.**

(The founder attributed an earlier informal phrasing — "we are the subject and object and we are
part of the subject and object" — to Jarvis. It did not originate in this session; the mature
versions above are the founder's and are the ones preserved.)

## The observer belongs to the system observed

The human changes through the encounter. MAIA's representation of the human and the history of
the relationship change through the encounter. The relationship develops history, expectations,
ruptures, meanings, memories and patterns. Then we turn and try to understand the phenomenon
while participating in it. This places Soullab's method near **second-order cybernetics,
participatory sense-making** (ledger [21]) **and phenomenology**, not detached behavioral
research. Method-lineage sources to card in the interpretive layer (class D).

## Three laboratories

| Laboratory | Question | What is studied | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Personal** | Not *what kind of person are you?* but *what happens in you, through time, in relationship with intelligence?* An N-of-one inquiry. The Elemental architecture matters because the honest answer may be plural (Air: I understand · Water: I am grieving · Earth: I still don't feel safe · Fire: I want to move · Field: not yet reconciled) and MAIA does not decide which is secretly correct; the encounter makes the multiplicity increasingly perceptible. | the person's own exploration | this is what MAIA is for today |
| **2 · Relational** | What happens in the relationship itself? | recognition · misrecognition · attunement · rupture · repair · trust · projection · anthropomorphism · attachment · memory · continuity · authority · dependence · presence · separation | the R&D programme (R1–R12); R8 and R9 run |
| **3 · Collective** | Only with explicit consent: what becomes visible across many human–AI relationships? Not *what can we infer about one person and compare with everyone*, but *what patterns exist in the phenomenon* — does feeling understood precede over-trust; what rupture strengthens vs weakens; when memory is continuity vs intrusion; whether Elemental differentiation reduces premature interpretation; whether capacity transfers into human relationships; what predicts dependency; what helps a person leave more capable | patterns in the phenomenon | **not instrumented. No member conversation serves research today. Private experience is private by default; participation in collective inquiry would be an additional, explicit act — a consent architecture that does not yet exist.** |

**Binding rule (founder):** collective knowledge must never require surrendering personal
sovereignty. Sanctuary content is excluded absolutely (Sanctuary invariant 6).
