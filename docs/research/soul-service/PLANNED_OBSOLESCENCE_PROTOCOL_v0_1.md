# Planned Obsolescence Protocol v0.1
## Designing AI scaffolds to transfer capacity back to the human

**Programme:** Human–AI Soul-Service Architecture
**Status:** Design / research protocol only
**Date:** 29 September 2026

---

# 0. Principle

> **The ultimate measure of developmental AI is not how indispensable the AI becomes, but how much of its scaffolding can disappear while the human capacity remains.**

Planned obsolescence does not mean ending the relationship with MAIA.

It means allowing particular scaffolds to become unnecessary as the capacities they support are internalized.

The relationship may evolve from:

> instructor / scaffold

toward:

> collaborator / witness / creative partner.

Dependence decreases.

Partnership can deepen.

---

# 1. What may fade

Potentially fadeable scaffolds include:

- repeated frame prompts;
- reminders to inspect counterevidence;
- explanation of source versus hypothesis;
- suggested perspective shifts;
- guided synthesis sequences;
- “what changed?” prompts;
- active recall prompts;
- mapping tutorials;
- action-translation prompts.

Not everything should fade.

Persistent constitutional protections remain:

- provenance;
- authorship;
- privacy;
- consent;
- uncertainty;
- source custody;
- distinction between MAIA hypothesis and member meaning.

We fade **scaffolding**, not safeguards.

---

# 2. Fading triggers

A scaffold becomes a candidate for fading when there is repeated evidence that the member:

- initiates the capacity without prompting;
- uses it correctly enough in novel contexts;
- transfers it across domains;
- corrects MAIA appropriately;
- demonstrates delayed retention;
- prefers less support.

No single behavior is sufficient.

No hidden developmental rank is created.

---

# 3. Fading sequence

> **Full → Guided → Light → Optional → Member-initiated**

The system should not jump from full support to absence.

At every stage:

- member can restore support;
- difficult contexts may legitimately require more support;
- use of support is not regression.

---

# 4. Solo Mode

Soul-Service architecture should support a deliberate **Solo Mode**.

Possible Solo Mode surfaces:

- sources;
- Living Field;
- blank writing space;
- member-authored map;
- minimal structural tools.

Absent by default:

- generated interpretation;
- unsolicited pattern detection;
- automatic perspective suggestions.

Member can explicitly choose:

> **Invite MAIA**

Solo Mode provides direct evidence about independent capacity.

It also creates a product culture in which not invoking AI is legitimate.

---

# 5. Non-engagement law

## SOUL-SERVICE-NONENGAGEMENT-METRIC-LAW-01

> **Engagement duration and frequency must not be treated as developmental success. The system should prefer appropriate participation and real-world transfer over maximizing time spent with MAIA.**

A successful interaction may be:

> notice → discern → leave → live → return later.

---

# 6. Anti-dependence review

Every developmental feature should answer:

1. What human capacity is this intended to strengthen?
2. What evidence would show the capacity is transferring?
3. How can the scaffold become lighter?
4. Can the member perform this without MAIA?
5. Is the product rewarded when the member needs less help?
6. Could this feature instead train dependence?
7. What remains as permanent protection even after scaffolding fades?

A feature without answers to these questions is not yet a Soul-Service developmental feature.

---

# 7. Falsifier

## PLANNED-OBSOLESCENCE-FALSIFIER-01

> **If prolonged use of Soullab increases reliance on MAIA for capacities the system claims to strengthen, without corresponding gains in independent performance, the developmental architecture has failed.**
