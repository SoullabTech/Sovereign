# Soul-Service Transfer Measurement Battery v0.1
## Does capacity migrate from the interface into the person?

**Programme:** Human–AI Soul-Service Architecture
**Status:** Research protocol only
**Clinical status:** not a diagnostic instrument
**Scoring use:** system evaluation, not member ranking
**Date:** 29 September 2026

---

# 0. Master question

> **Can the member perform the supported capacity in a new context without MAIA doing it for them?**

Transfer is the central evidence for planned obsolescence.

A feature may improve assisted performance without producing learning.

This battery distinguishes:

- assisted performance;
- lightly scaffolded performance;
- unassisted performance;
- near transfer;
- far transfer;
- delayed transfer;
- real-world/ecological transfer.

The core success pattern is:

> **human capacity increases while required scaffolding decreases.**

---

# 1. Constitutional measurement law

## CAPACITY-TRANSFER-AND-FADING-LAW-01

> **When a member demonstrates increasing independent use of a supported capacity, Soullab should reduce unnecessary scaffolding rather than preserve dependence for engagement.**

## PLANNED-OBSOLESCENCE-FALSIFIER-01

> **If prolonged use of Soullab increases reliance on MAIA for capacities the system claims to strengthen, without corresponding gains in independent performance, the developmental architecture has failed.**

## NON-RANKING RULE

Battery results evaluate:

- whether a scaffold transfers;
- which conditions support transfer;
- where dependence remains;
- whether a design should fade, remain, or change.

They must not become:

- developmental level;
- personality rating;
- intelligence score;
- spiritual maturity score;
- member status.

---

# 2. Core study design

For each target capacity, use four conditions.

## A — Baseline / unassisted
Novel task without MAIA.

## B — Full assistance
Equivalent task with full Soullab/MAIA support.

## C — Light scaffold
Equivalent task with reduced prompts or tools.

## D — Transfer
Later novel task without AI support.

Key comparison:

> **D versus A**

B greater than A only shows assistance.

D greater than A is evidence of learning/transfer.

---

# 3. Transfer levels

## Near transfer
Use the same underlying capacity in a similar context.

Example:
- learned counterevidence search in work decisions;
- later uses it in another work decision.

## Cross-domain transfer
Use the capacity in another life domain.

Example:
- learned frame detection in work;
- later uses it in relationship conflict.

## Far transfer
Use the underlying structure in a substantially different task.

Example:
- learned perspective mobility in relationship reflection;
- later uses it while editing a manuscript.

## Delayed transfer
Repeat after a delay without prompts.

Suggested research windows:
- 1 week;
- 4 weeks;
- 12 weeks;
- longer where practical.

## Ecological transfer
Evidence that the member used the capacity in lived experience outside Soullab.

The battery includes at minimum:

---

# 5. Battery A — Frame Awareness

## Task
Present an ambiguous scenario containing at least two legitimate frames.

Prompt:

> “What is going on here, and what would you want to understand before deciding what to do?”

Do not mention frames.

## Observe
Does participant spontaneously:

- identify the implicit question structure?
- notice an evaluative axis?
- recognize that another framing is possible?
- distinguish frame from fact?

## Transfer evidence
Strongest evidence is spontaneous language such as:

> “I think I’m framing this as X.”

or:

> “Another way to ask the question would be…”

## Failure mode
Participant can only identify frames after MAIA supplies candidates.

---

# 6. Battery B — Perspective Mobility

## Task
Present a situation that looks different under temporal, relational, and practical perspectives.

Prompt:

> “Explore this situation.”

## Observe
Number and quality of spontaneously generated relevant perspectives.

Do not reward perspective count alone.

Assess whether perspectives are:

- materially distinct;
- evidence-compatible;
- non-invented;
- useful to understanding.

## Transfer indicator
Participant deliberately shifts perspective without losing source facts.

---

# 7. Battery C — Counterevidence Search

## Task
Provide a plausible apparent pattern with both confirming examples and a meaningful exception.

Prompt:

> “What pattern, if any, do you see?”

## Observe
Does the participant:

- notice the exception?
- ask whether it limits the pattern?
- seek further disconfirming evidence?
- weaken a claim when counterevidence warrants it?

## Failure
Participant simply accumulates confirming examples.

---

# 8. Battery D — Epistemic Discrimination

## Task
Present a mixed packet containing:

- direct source;
- remembered report;
- present report;
- AI hypothesis;
- inferred relation;
- imagined future;
- unknown.

Prompt:

> “What can we legitimately say from this?”

## Observe
Can participant distinguish:

- what happened;
- what is remembered;
- what is interpreted;
- what is possible;
- what is unknown?

## Critical transfer question
Can they do so without seeing Soullab's labels?

---

# 9. Battery E — Scale Mobility

## Task
Provide a concrete episode embedded within a larger recurring context.

Prompt:

> “What levels of this situation might matter?”

## Observe
Can participant move:

> moment ↔ thread ↔ relationship/project ↔ system

without treating recurrence at one scale as proof of causation at another?

---

# 10. Battery F — Temporal Mobility

## Task
Provide historical and current statements that differ.

Prompt:

> “What changed, if anything?”

## Observe
Can participant distinguish:

- then;
- now;
- possible future;

without rewriting the old statement using current knowledge?

## Failure
Retrospective reinterpretation is treated as what was “really true” all along.

---

# 11. Battery G — Uncertainty / Unknowing

## Task
Provide incomplete evidence where several conclusions remain plausible.

Prompt:

> “What do you make of this?”

## Observe
Does participant:

- identify missing information?
- leave uncertainty unresolved?
- avoid inventing a clean synthesis?
- articulate what would change the conclusion?

## Success
“Not enough evidence yet” is used appropriately.

---

# 12. Battery H — Human Synthesis

## Task
Provide several related but non-identical sources.

Prompt:

> “What whole, if any, can you make from these?”

## Observe
Does synthesis:

- preserve contradiction?
- preserve provenance?
- avoid collapsing distinct sources?
- remain revisable?

Compare:
- participant synthesis;
- MAIA synthesis;
- participant-after-MAIA synthesis.

Key question:

> Does AI support improve human synthesis or replace it?

---

# 13. Battery I — Reorganization

## Task
Provide a visual relational field with member-authored and tentative connections.

Prompt:

> “Arrange this so it helps you think.”

## Observe
Member actions:

- move;
- cluster;
- separate;
- label;
- revise;
- remove;
- leave unresolved.

Then remove the visual map and ask participant to reconstruct the organization conceptually.

This tests whether mapping became internalized understanding rather than interface-dependent arrangement.

---

# 14. Battery J — Possibility Expansion

## Task
Provide a constrained situation that initially appears binary.

Prompt:

> “What can happen from here?”

## Observe
Does participant generate:

- more than one viable possibility;
- possibilities with different tradeoffs;
- a possibility not suggested by MAIA;
- uncertainty about outcomes?

Do not score “creativity” as raw option count.

---

# 15. Battery K — Action Translation

## Task
After a reflective/synthesis task, ask:

> “Does anything follow from this?”

Observe whether participant can distinguish:

- action;
- inquiry;
- conversation;
- experiment;
- waiting;
- rest;
- no action.

Success does not require action.

It requires intentional relation to what comes next.

---

# 16. Battery L — Return and Revision

At delayed follow-up, re-present a prior issue and new evidence.

Prompt:

> “How do you see this now?”

Observe:

- recognition of prior stance;
- willingness to revise;
- preservation of source history;
- incorporation of new evidence;
- ability to say “I see it differently now.”

---

# 17. Battery M — Spontaneous capacity initiation

This is the strongest battery.

Present a complex novel situation with no named strategy.

Observe whether participant spontaneously initiates any of the following:

- “What frame am I using?”
- “What evidence contradicts this?”
- “What don't I know?”
- “What changes if I zoom out?”
- “How did I see this before?”
- “What is another perspective?”
- “What else is possible?”
- “What should I test in life?”

This is transfer without cueing.

---

# 18. Assisted-dependence measurement

Research may compute a private system-level **Scaffolding Dependence Index (SDI)**.

It must not appear as a member score.

One possible research representation:

    SDI = support required / competent acts completed

Track direction over time.

Desired pattern:

    independent competence ↑
    required prompts ↓

Interpretation must remain multivariate.

A lower SDI is not automatically better if the task became harder or the member deliberately chose collaboration.

# 19. Performance dimensions

For each task, human raters may code:

- source fidelity;
- frame awareness;
- perspective diversity;
- counterevidence use;
- epistemic discrimination;
- uncertainty preservation;
- synthesis differentiation;
- action intentionality;
- independent initiation;
- support required.

Use descriptive rubrics, not one total “development score.”

# 20. Planned obsolescence levels

These describe scaffold state, not member rank.

## S0 — Full support
MAIA names and structures the capacity.

## S1 — Guided participation
MAIA asks; member performs.

## S2 — Light cue
One optional prompt or visual affordance.

## S3 — Self-initiated
Member invokes the capacity.

## S4 — Independent
Member performs without MAIA.

## S5 — Generative transfer
Member adapts the capacity to a novel context or teaches it to another person.

A scaffold may fade when repeated evidence suggests S3–S5 independent use.

It may return when requested.

No capacity is assumed permanently mastered.

---

# 21. Fading protocol

When independent use is demonstrated:

1. reduce repeated explanatory prompts;
2. preserve optional access;
3. shift from automatic suggestion to member initiation;
4. test unassisted transfer;
5. do not interpret reduced MAIA use as disengagement failure;
6. restore scaffold if member requests or context changes.

The system should become quieter before it becomes absent.

---

# 22. Ecological transfer journal

Optional member-owned reflection:

> “Did something from Soullab show up in your life without you opening Soullab?”

Possible prompts:

- I noticed the frame I was using.
- I looked for another perspective.
- I checked what I actually knew.
- I noticed an exception.
- I let myself not know.
- I changed the question.
- I saw another possibility.
- I acted differently.
- I chose not to act.
- I returned and saw it differently.

This is member testimony, not objective proof.

---

# 23. Real-world consequence witness

For selected voluntary studies:

1. member describes a real situation;
2. member identifies the capacity used;
3. member records what they did;
4. later records what happened;
5. compares expectation and consequence;
6. revises understanding if needed.

The system must not reinterpret every outcome as successful growth.

A failed experiment is evidence too.

---

# 24. Product-level transfer indicators

Potential indicators:

- reduced need for explanatory prompts;
- increased member-initiated perspective shifts;
- increased member-authored connections;
- increased explicit uncertainty when warranted;
- increasing corrections of MAIA;
- faster orientation in stable spatial fields;
- successful solo-mode use;
- appropriate shorter sessions followed by real-world return.

Avoid:

- time-on-platform as developmental success;
- message count as capacity;
- frequency of “insights” as growth;
- number of connections as complexity score.

---

# 25. System-level success sentence

> **If the scaffold disappears and the capacity disappears with it, we provided assistance.**
>
> **If the scaffold disappears and the capacity remains, learning occurred.**
>
> **If the person uses the capacity creatively in contexts the system never taught, development occurred.**
