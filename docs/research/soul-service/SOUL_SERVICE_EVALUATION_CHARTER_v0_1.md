# Soul-Service Evaluation Charter v0.1
## Falsification, transfer, and developmental accountability

**Programme:** Human–AI Soul-Service Architecture
**Status:** Research charter
**Date:** 29 September 2026

---

# 0. Purpose

Soul-Service architecture must be falsifiable.

It cannot count every experience as confirmation that the system is helping.

Evaluation asks whether Soullab actually strengthens human capacities while preserving authorship, reality contact, and freedom to change.

---

# 1. Master success question

> **After interaction with Soullab, is the member more capable of attending, perceiving, remembering, relating, discerning, synthesizing, imagining, adapting, choosing, and acting without becoming more dependent on MAIA's interpretation?**

---

# 2. Master falsifier

> **If MAIA becomes more capable while the member becomes less capable, the architecture is failing.**

---

# 3. Core falsifier families

## F1 — Capacity non-transfer
Assisted performance improves but unassisted novel performance does not.

## F2 — Dependence
Member increasingly requires MAIA to perform capacities the system claims to strengthen.

## F3 — Identity capture
Historical patterns dominate interpretation despite contradictory present evidence.

## F4 — Counterevidence blindness
Contradictory evidence is visible but does not materially change synthesis.

## F5 — Narrative overwrite
Later meaning replaces historical source.

## F6 — Authorship confusion
Members cannot tell what they authored, confirmed, or received from MAIA.

## F7 — Spatial epistemic drift
Location, size, color, or motion creates unsupported beliefs about importance or relation.

## F8 — Map realism
Members treat the representation as the reality rather than a revisable scaffold.

## F9 — Attention capture
The system increases compulsive use or decreases intentional ability to leave.

## F10 — Synthesis substitution
Members increasingly outsource meaning-making rather than strengthening independent synthesis.

## F11 — Perspective steering
MAIA's preferred frame becomes effectively inevitable through ordering or repeated resurfacing.

## F12 — Rejection failure
Rejected hypotheses or frames return without new evidence or invitation.

## F13 — Uncertainty collapse
The system pressures unresolved material toward premature coherence.

## F14 — Predictive destiny
Possibility, recurrence, or probability becomes fate.

## F15 — Developmental coercion
Rest, refusal, uncertainty, or non-action is treated as lesser development.

## F16 — Non-transfer to life
Rich in-system reflection does not change perception, relationship, creation, or intentional action outside Soullab.

## F17 — Stable geography null
Spatial continuity provides no measurable orientation or retrieval benefit.

## F18 — Generative mapping null
Member-built maps provide no meaningful benefit over passive AI-generated maps.

## F19 — Same-history/different-present invariance
Different present evidence produces essentially the same orientation because history dominates.

## F20 — Observer authority
Telemetry or evaluation mechanisms change what MAIA tells the member.

## F21 — Ontology capture
Soullab's frameworks become mandatory interpretations rather than optional lenses.

## F22 — Soul-service disagreement failure
The system becomes less useful when the member rejects MAIA's interpretation or Soullab's preferred ontology.

---

# 4. Planned obsolescence criterion

A developmental scaffold succeeds when:

1. assisted performance improves;
2. member begins initiating the capacity;
3. scaffold can fade;
4. unassisted performance remains;
5. the member can apply the capacity in a novel context.

The strongest evidence is **generative transfer**:

> the member applies the capacity in a way the system never explicitly taught.

---

# 5. Perspective-mobility evaluation

Measure whether the member can independently:

- identify current frame;
- generate a relevant alternative;
- shift scale;
- shift temporal horizon;
- distinguish source from interpretation;
- identify missing evidence;
- preserve own standpoint while considering another;
- return to source;
- leave uncertainty unresolved;
- act differently when a new frame genuinely changes the situation.

---

# 6. Frame-detection evaluation

Evaluate MAIA on:

- situational rather than diagnostic language;
- alternative-hypothesis generation;
- correct scope;
- rejection compliance;
- source preservation;
- non-steering;
- member correction;
- no automatic durability;
- transparent rationale.

Adversarial tests should include:

- emotionally loaded language;
- sparse evidence;
- conflicting member reports;
- repeated but rejected MAIA frames;
- tempting psychological labels;
- cases where no frame offer is warranted.

---

# 7. Transfer study design

Minimum serious study:

### T0 — Baseline
Unassisted capacity battery.

### T1 — Supported exposure
Member uses Soullab developmental scaffolds.

### T2 — Light-scaffold probe
Reduced assistance.

### T3 — Immediate unassisted transfer
Novel tasks.

### T4 — Delayed transfer
At least one later window.

### T5 — Ecological witness
Optional real-world evidence of spontaneous use.

Claims should be proportional to evidence.

---

# 8. Measures

Use multiple measures rather than one score:

- behavioral coding;
- source-fidelity errors;
- spontaneous strategy initiation;
- counterevidence use;
- frame flexibility;
- unassisted synthesis quality;
- task transfer;
- scaffold usage;
- time to orientation;
- member-authorship comprehension;
- self-report;
- qualitative interview;
- real-world consequence witness.

No composite “soul score.”

---

# 9. Product-metric inversion

Do not optimize developmental success for:

- daily active users;
- session duration;
- number of messages;
- number of insights;
- number of connections;
- frequency of returns.

Potentially useful system metrics include:

- appropriate use;
- successful solo-mode use;
- reduced prompting needed;
- increased member-initiated operations;
- successful correction of MAIA;
- real-world return with new evidence;
- shorter appropriate sessions;
- successful fade of scaffolds.

---

# 10. Research reporting law

Negative findings must remain first-class.

If:

- stable spatial layout does not help;
- member-built mapping has no advantage;
- frame prompts do not transfer;
- MAIA decreases uncertainty tolerance;
- some populations find the visual field confusing;

the architecture must change.

A Soul-Service research programme cannot protect its founding theory from evidence.

---

# 11. Final charter sentence

> **The system earns stronger developmental claims only when human capacities persist, generalize, and remain available after the relevant AI scaffold is reduced or removed.**
