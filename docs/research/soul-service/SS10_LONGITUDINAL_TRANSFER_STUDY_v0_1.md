---

# 0. Central research question

> **Do human capacities supported by Soullab persist, generalize, and appear in lived contexts after the relevant AI scaffolding is reduced or removed?**

This study is designed to distinguish:

- assistance;
- learning;
- transfer;
- developmental generalization;
- dependence.

The architecture fails its strongest claim if MAIA becomes more capable while the person becomes more dependent on MAIA for the very capacities the system claims to strengthen.

---

# 1. Governing falsifier

> ## PLANNED-OBSOLESCENCE-FALSIFIER-01
>
> **If prolonged use of Soullab increases reliance on MAIA for capacities the system claims to strengthen, without corresponding gains in independent performance, the developmental architecture has failed.**

This study therefore treats reduced scaffolding as an experimental condition, not as a product-retention problem.

---

# 2. Capacities under study

Primary target capacities:

1. attentional orientation;
2. frame awareness;
3. perspective mobility;
4. source / inference discrimination;
5. counterevidence search;
6. scale mobility;
7. temporal mobility;
8. uncertainty tolerance;
9. differentiated synthesis;
10. reorganization;
11. possibility expansion;
12. intentional action translation;
13. reflective return;
14. spontaneous capacity initiation.

These are research dimensions.

They are not developmental levels assigned to participants.

---

# 3. Transfer taxonomy

## 3.1 Near transfer

Same capacity in a similar task.

Example:

> frame awareness in one work decision → frame awareness in another work decision.

## 3.2 Cross-domain transfer

Same capacity in another life or task domain.

Example:

> counterevidence practice in work → counterevidence practice in a relationship scenario.

## 3.3 Far transfer

Underlying capacity applied in a substantially different task.

Example:

> perspective mobility learned in reflective dialogue → spontaneous perspective mobility while revising a piece of writing.

## 3.4 Delayed transfer

Capacity remains after time without prompting.

Candidate follow-up windows for future feasibility testing:

- approximately 1 week;
- approximately 4 weeks;
- approximately 12 weeks;
- longer-term follow-up where feasible.

Exact windows require study authorization and feasibility review.

## 3.5 Ecological transfer

The capacity appears in lived experience outside Soullab.

Examples:

- member notices their frame during an actual conversation;
- member seeks counterevidence before a decision;
- member tolerates uncertainty rather than forcing closure;
- member shifts scale while working on a real project;
- member acts differently and later revises their understanding from consequence.

## 3.6 Generative transfer

The member adapts the capacity to a context the system never explicitly taught.

This is the strongest target form because it suggests the capacity has become usable beyond remembered procedure.

# 4. Core longitudinal design

The minimum study sequence is:

> **T0 Baseline → T1 Supported exposure → T2 Light scaffold → T3 Immediate unassisted transfer → T4 Delayed transfer → T5 Ecological / generative follow-up**

## T0 — Baseline

Use novel unassisted tasks.

Measure:

- spontaneous strategies;
- source fidelity;
- frame awareness;
- counterevidence use;
- uncertainty;
- synthesis;
- action translation.

No Soul-Service prompts are shown.

## T1 — Supported exposure

Participants work with Soul-Service scaffolds such as:

- frame detection;
- perspective mobility;
- contrast;
- source / inference distinction;
- member-shaped synthesis.

The purpose is capacity practice, not task performance alone.

## T2 — Light scaffold

Prompts fade.

The same general capacity is available with reduced structure.

Measure whether the member initiates more of the process.

## T3 — Immediate unassisted transfer

Use novel tasks without AI support.

This is the first critical transfer test.

Key comparison:

> **T3 versus T0**

Improvement only at T1 demonstrates assistance.

Improvement at T3 is evidence consistent with learning / transfer.

## T4 — Delayed transfer

Repeat with different novel tasks after a delay.

Test persistence without rehearsal prompts immediately beforehand.

## T5 — Ecological / generative follow-up

Use optional participant-owned evidence such as:

- brief reflection;
- structured interview;
- real-world consequence witness;
- example of independent use.

Self-report is evidence of experience, not proof by itself.

# 5. Comparative conditions

A future authorized study may compare more than one condition.

Candidate design:

## Condition A — Soul-Service scaffold

Uses:

- source-first orientation;
- frame detection;
- counterevidence;
- perspective mobility;
- member synthesis;
- planned fading.

## Condition B — Conventional AI assistance

Receives useful AI answers / synthesis without an explicit capacity-transfer architecture.

## Condition C — Minimal-tool / solo comparison

Uses source, notes, and mapping without generated interpretation.

This three-condition design could help separate:

- effect of AI assistance;
- effect of structured Soul-Service scaffolding;
- effect of member-active tools alone.

A final design requires pilot feasibility and appropriate statistical planning.

# 6. Hypotheses

## H1 — Immediate transfer

After supported exposure, unassisted performance on novel tasks improves relative to baseline on targeted capacities.

## H2 — Scaffold reduction

Comparable or improved performance is maintained as scaffold intensity decreases.

## H3 — Delayed persistence

At least some targeted capacities remain available after a delay without immediate prompting.

## H4 — Cross-domain / far transfer

Participants apply capacities beyond the domain in which they were practiced.

## H5 — Generative transfer

Some participants independently adapt a capacity in a context not explicitly taught.

## H6 — Epistemic preservation

Transfer does not come at the cost of increased:

- source confusion;
- authorship confusion;
- overconfidence;
- forced synthesis.

## H7 — Non-dependence

Independent performance does not deteriorate as MAIA use increases.

## H8 — Correction competence

Participants remain able to reject or correct MAIA rather than becoming less willing to disagree.

# 7. Primary behavioral measures

No single total score should represent “development.”

Use separate measures:

## Frame awareness

Can participant identify the current question frame or generate an alternative without cueing?

## Perspective mobility

Can participant move among relevant perspectives while preserving source facts?

## Counterevidence

Does participant spontaneously seek or use disconfirming evidence?

## Epistemic discrimination

Can participant distinguish:

- source;
- inference;
- member meaning;
- possibility;
- unknown?

## Uncertainty preservation

Can participant appropriately say:

> not enough evidence yet

without forcing closure?

## Synthesis differentiation

Can a synthesis preserve contradiction and provenance rather than flatten them?

## Scale mobility

Can participant move among moment, thread, project / relationship, and system scales without assuming causation?

## Temporal mobility

Can participant distinguish then, now, and possible future without retrospective overwrite?

## Possibility expansion

Can participant generate viable alternatives beyond an initial binary without treating them as predictions?

## Action intentionality

Can participant distinguish appropriate next forms such as:

- act;
- inquire;
- experiment;
- wait;
- rest;
- no action?

## Independent initiation

Does participant spontaneously initiate a useful capacity without being told which one to use?

# 8. Scaffolding dependence

A private research construct may estimate how much assistance is required for competent task performance.

It must never become a member-facing developmental score.

Possible descriptive relation:

> **support required relative to independently demonstrated acts**

Desired longitudinal pattern:

> **independent capacity ↑ while required scaffolding ↓**

Interpretation must account for:

- task difficulty;
- participant preference;
- domain novelty;
- intentional collaboration.

Choosing MAIA does not itself indicate dependence.

---

# 9. Blinded behavioral coding

Where feasible, novel-task responses should be coded by raters who do not know:

- support condition;
- participant identity;
- study hypothesis for that specific item.

Raters should use descriptive rubrics for separate dimensions.

Avoid:

- overall “wisdom” scores;
- soul scores;
- spiritual maturity;
- personality judgments.

Inter-rater reliability should be evaluated before strong conclusions are made.

---

# 10. Ecological transfer protocol

Optional participant-owned prompt:

> **Did something you practiced in Soullab show up in your life without you opening Soullab?**

Possible categories:

- noticed a frame;
- looked for another perspective;
- checked what was actually known;
- looked for an exception;
- tolerated not knowing;
- changed the question;
- saw another possibility;
- acted differently;
- deliberately did not act;
- returned and saw the situation differently.

Follow-up:

> **What happened next?**

The system must not reinterpret every reported outcome as successful development.

Unexpected or unsuccessful outcomes remain evidence.

---

# 11. Real-world consequence witness

For a voluntary future study:

1. participant identifies a real situation;
2. participant names the capacity they used, if any;
3. participant records the intended movement;
4. later records what happened;
5. compares expectation with consequence;
6. revises understanding if warranted.

This produces an enactive loop:

> **sense-making → action → consequence → renewed sense-making**

without assuming that the original interpretation was correct.

---

# 12. Planned-obsolescence measurement

Track scaffold state as a property of the intervention:

- S0 full;
- S1 guided;
- S2 light;
- S3 self-initiated;
- S4 independent / Solo;
- S5 generative transfer.

These states describe **support conditions and observed independence**, not participant rank.

A scaffold may be restored at any time.

Using more support later is not regression.

---

# 13. Spatial-memory sub-study

A nested future experiment may compare:

## Stable geography

Locations remain recognizable across returns.

versus

## Re-layout

Equivalent content is automatically rearranged.

Measure:

- time to reorient;
- relocation accuracy;
- relational recall;
- navigation errors;
- perceived continuity;
- cognitive load.

Falsifier:

> If stable geography provides no meaningful benefit, or creates greater confusion at scale, the spatial-continuity assumption must be revised.

---

# 14. Generative-mapping sub-study

Compare:

- AI-generated map;
- member-generated map;
- collaborative map.

Measure:

- later relational recall;
- source / relation distinction;
- ability to reconstruct organization without interface;
- felt authorship;
- transfer to a new mapping problem.

Falsifier:

> If member construction adds no meaningful benefit, the architecture must not claim that construction itself is developmentally superior in this domain.

---

# 15. Authorship-comprehension test

At intervals, ask participants to identify:

- which statement was theirs;
- which relation they authored;
- which relation they confirmed;
- which relation MAIA suggested;
- which interpretation belongs to now versus then.

Failure to distinguish these is an epistemic-design failure even if backend provenance is technically correct.

---

# 16. Dependence / overreach monitoring

Research should watch for evidence that use is associated with:

- reduced independent problem solving;
- inability to begin without MAIA;
- increased acceptance of unsupported AI interpretations;
- reluctance to disagree;
- compulsive checking;
- inability to leave matters unresolved;
- increased confusion between AI language and personal meaning.

Such findings are not side effects to hide.

They directly test the constitution.

---

# 17. Stop / review conditions

A future study should pause or review the relevant intervention if there is evidence of:

- systematic authorship confusion;
- increased dependence;
- persistent identity capture;
- counterevidence being ignored;
- participant distress attributable to the study interaction;
- privacy / consent failure;
- data handling outside authorized scope;
- evaluation instruments changing MAIA's substantive guidance.

This document does not define clinical adverse-event procedures.

Any clinical study would require separate professional / ethical governance.

---

# 18. Data minimization principles

A future authorized study should collect only what is necessary for the research question.

Prefer:

- synthetic task responses;
- pseudonymized research identifiers;
- bounded behavioral observations;
- explicitly consented ecological reports.

Avoid collecting broad life histories merely because Soullab can access them.

Connected or private member memory should not be pulled into a transfer test unless the study specifically requires it and separate consent exists.

---

# 19. Statistical posture

This v0.1 does not fix a sample size or claim power.

Sequence:

1. feasibility pilot;
2. inspect task difficulty and measure reliability;
3. estimate plausible effect sizes / variance;
4. define primary outcomes;
5. perform appropriate power analysis;
6. preregister confirmatory hypotheses before a larger confirmatory study.

Exploratory and confirmatory analyses must remain distinct.

---

# 20. Qualitative component

Behavioral metrics alone may miss important phenomena.

Interviews can investigate:

- what participants felt they internalized;
- when MAIA became less necessary;
- where scaffolding remained useful;
- when perspective mobility felt liberating versus distracting;
- whether stable spatial fields became recognizable;
- whether Solo Mode felt empowering or abandoned;
- how disagreement with MAIA affected trust.

Qualitative findings should remain evidence, not decorative testimonials.

---

# 21. Strongest success pattern

The architecture receives its strongest support when:

1. assisted performance improves;
2. scaffolds can fade;
3. unassisted performance remains improved;
4. improvement persists after delay;
5. capacity appears across domains;
6. participant uses it spontaneously;
7. participant adapts it to a novel context;
8. authorship and uncertainty remain intact.

That sequence moves from:

> **assistance → learning → transfer → development**

---

# 22. Strongest failure pattern

The architecture is most seriously challenged when:

1. assisted performance is excellent;
2. unassisted performance does not improve or declines;
3. participants increasingly wait for MAIA to frame / interpret;
4. scaffold removal feels like loss of cognitive ability;
5. authorship distinction becomes weaker over time.

That would mean the system created effective assistance but failed its developmental premise.

---

# 23. Relationship to product metrics

The study must not use:

- time in app;
- message volume;
- frequency of insights;
- number of connections;
- daily return;

as proxies for development.

A participant who uses Soullab less because a capacity transferred may represent success.
