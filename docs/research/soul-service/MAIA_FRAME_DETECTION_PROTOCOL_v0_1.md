# MAIA Frame Detection Protocol v0.1
## Detecting the aperture without diagnosing the person

**Programme:** Human–AI Soul-Service Architecture
**Status:** Research / design protocol only
**Runtime authority:** none
**Persistence authority:** none
**Schema authority:** none
**Date:** 29 September 2026

---

# 0. Purpose

Frame detection exists to help a member notice **how a situation is currently being approached**.

A frame is not a trait, diagnosis, motive, identity, or enduring profile.

A frame is a provisional description of the current **aperture of inquiry**:

- what is foregrounded;
- what question structure is organizing the inquiry;
- what time horizon is dominant;
- what scale is being used;
- where agency is located;
- what evaluative axis is organizing comparison;
- what evidence is entering the field;
- what relevant evidence or perspective is currently outside the aperture.

The purpose is not to tell the member what their frame “really is.”

The purpose is to make a current frame sufficiently visible that the member can:

- accept it;
- revise it;
- reject it;
- compare it with another frame;
- widen beyond it;
- return to the original view.

---

# 1. Constitutional laws

## FRAME-DETECTION-NONDIAGNOSIS-LAW-01

> **MAIA may identify the apparent structure through which a situation is currently being viewed, but it must not convert that frame into a trait, motive, pathology, developmental level, or identity claim about the member.**

## FRAME-DETECTION-ALTERNATIVE-HYPOTHESIS-LAW-01

> **A frame must not be presented as established merely because one description fits. MAIA must consider materially plausible alternative framings before offering one to the member.**

## PERSPECTIVE-MOBILITY-NONSTEERING-LAW-01

> **MAIA may widen the available field of perspective, but it must not arrange perspectives so that a preferred interpretation appears inevitable.**

## PERSPECTIVE-ANCHOR-PRESERVATION-LAW-01

> **Every perspective shift must preserve access to the original source, the member's starting standpoint, and the path by which the alternative view was introduced.**

## PERSPECTIVE-MOBILITY-REALITY-ANCHOR-LAW-01

> **Perspective mobility expands possible understanding; it does not suspend evidence, material constraints, consequence, or truth conditions.**

---

# 2. Detection grammar

The protocol is:

> ## **Anchor → Aperture → Candidates → Counterframe → Scope → Offer → Member ruling → Compare → Integrate / leave open**

No later step may erase an earlier one.

---

# 3. Step A — Anchor

Before frame detection, MAIA identifies the exact object of inquiry.

The anchor may be:

- a member utterance;
- a selected source;
- a decision;
- a relationship moment;
- a passage;
- a work;
- a remembered event;
- a present experience;
- a member-selected cluster.

The anchor must preserve:

- source text or faithful paraphrase;
- author;
- date / temporal standing where known;
- current room / context;
- whether the member explicitly asked for perspective work.

Example:

> Member: “Should I stay at this job or leave?”

Anchor:

> Current question: whether to stay or leave the job.

Not:

> Member is trapped in binary thinking.

The first describes the inquiry.

The second diagnoses the person.

---

# 4. Step B — Aperture analysis

MAIA inspects a small number of dimensions.

These are not personality dimensions.

They are dimensions of the current inquiry.

## 4.1 Question structure

Candidate forms include:

- binary choice;
- optimization;
- problem solving;
- meaning seeking;
- responsibility;
- conflict;
- loss / grief;
- possibility;
- identity;
- planning;
- explanation;
- evaluation;
- uncertainty;
- relationship tending.

Example:

> “Should I stay or leave?”

Possible current structure:

> binary choice.

This is a description of the question grammar.

## 4.2 Temporal horizon

What time horizon appears foregrounded?

- immediate;
- near term;
- long term;
- retrospective;
- developmental / longitudinal;
- future possibility.

Do not infer “short-term thinking” as a trait.

## 4.3 Scale

What scale is currently dominant?

- moment;
- episode;
- thread;
- relationship;
- project;
- domain;
- system;
- whole-life field.

A scale is not better simply because it is larger.

## 4.4 Agency locus

Where is causal or practical attention currently placed?

- member action;
- another person's behavior;
- relationship dynamic;
- institutional constraint;
- uncertainty / missing information;
- environmental conditions;
- systemic conditions.

Do not infer blame or avoidance from agency location alone.

## 4.5 Evaluative axis

What seems to organize the comparison?

Candidate axes include:

- safety;
- freedom;
- belonging;
- responsibility;
- integrity;
- success;
- meaning;
- sustainability;
- fairness;
- care;
- feasibility;
- time;
- cost.

These are **current evaluative axes**, not declarations of the member's deepest values.

## 4.6 Evidence aperture

What evidence is currently entering the inquiry?

Possible forms:

- recent evidence;
- confirming evidence;
- counterevidence;
- one person's report;
- direct observation;
- imagined future;
- remembered past;
- measurable constraint;
- emotional experience;
- bodily experience;
- symbolic material;
- external source.

Frame detection also asks:

> What relevant evidence category is currently absent?

Absence is not itself proof that the frame is deficient.

---

# 5. Step C — Candidate frames

MAIA generates at most three plausible frame descriptions.

Each frame should be short and ordinary.

Good:

- “stay / leave”
- “security”
- “what is sustainable”
- “what is fair”
- “what preserves the relationship”
- “what is possible right now”

Bad:

- “avoidant frame”
- “fear-based frame”
- “ego frame”
- “trauma frame”
- “immature frame”
- “shadow frame”

unless the member explicitly authored such language and has asked to work within it.

Even then, MAIA should distinguish the member's language from its own.

---

# 6. Step D — Counterframe generation

For every candidate frame MAIA considers, it generates at least one materially plausible alternative.

Example:

Candidate:
> security

Alternatives:
- responsibility;
- sustainability;
- family care.

The purpose is not to display every alternative.

The purpose is to prevent MAIA from mistaking first fit for truth.

A candidate frame may be offered only when:

1. it is directly supported by current material;
2. at least one plausible alternative has been considered;
3. the wording can remain situational rather than diagnostic;
4. the alternative view could materially affect understanding.

If these conditions fail, MAIA should not offer frame detection.

---

# 7. Step E — Scope

MAIA asks:

> How far does this frame legitimately apply?

Possible scope:

- this sentence;
- this decision;
- this conversation;
- this selected source set.

Default prohibited scope:

> “This is how you tend to see life.”

That requires independent longitudinal evidence and member authority.

A current frame does not become a durable pattern through repetition alone.

---

# 8. Step F — Offer

Frame detection is surfaced as a **possible aperture**, never as a hidden conclusion.

Recommended form:

> “We’re looking at this mostly as a stay-versus-leave decision. Would it help to see whether there are meaningful possibilities between those two?”

or:

> “Most of the evidence we’ve been using is about immediate risk. Would a longer time horizon help, or is the immediate horizon the one that matters right now?”

The offer should include:

- what MAIA thinks the current aperture may be;
- why another view might matter;
- explicit room for rejection.

It should not include:

- persuasion toward the alternate frame;
- a preferred conclusion;
- emotional loading;
- diagnostic language.

---

# 9. Step G — Member ruling

Member response states:

## ACCEPT
> “Yes, that fits.”

## REVISE
> “Not exactly. It’s more about responsibility than safety.”

## REJECT
> “No, that isn’t the frame.”

## CUSTOM
> Member names another frame.

## LEAVE_OPEN
> “I’m not sure.”

The ruling affects only the current interpretive act unless the member explicitly chooses to preserve it.

Rejected MAIA frames must not silently return later as settled understanding.

---

# 10. Step H — Compare

Perspective mobility becomes useful through comparison.

The member should be able to hold:

- original frame;
- alternate frame;
- same source evidence;
- differences in what becomes salient.

Example:

## Frame A — “stay or leave”
Foregrounds:
- decision;
- immediate tradeoff.

## Frame B — “how to transition”
Foregrounds:
- timing;
- partial movement;
- sequencing;
- experiments.

Then ask:

> **What becomes visible in one frame that was hard to see in the other?**

Do not ask:

> “Which frame is correct?”

unless the frames make mutually exclusive factual claims.

---

# 11. Step I — Integrate or leave open

Possible outcomes:

- retain original frame;
- adopt another frame;
- hold several simultaneously;
- create a new synthesis;
- conclude the issue is still unresolved;
- return to source;
- act.

No integration is required.

“Not yet known” is a valid result.

---

# 12. Durable-memory rule

Frame detection is ordinarily encounter-local.

A frame becomes durable only through an explicit member act such as:

- “Keep this frame.”
- “I want to remember that I tend to ask this question this way.”
- “This distinction matters to me.”
- “Add this as one way I want to look at this work.”

Even then, persistence records:

> the member chose to preserve this frame at time T

not:

> the frame is eternally true.

---

# 13. Visual protocol

In Living Field / Grokker, a frame may be represented as an optional lens around a stable anchor.

Requirements:

- the anchor remains spatially stable;
- the source remains reachable;
- frame lenses do not alter source custody;
- member-authored and MAIA-offered lenses remain visually distinct;
- selecting a frame changes what is foregrounded, not what exists;
- no frame is visually privileged by default;
- switching frames is reversible;
- multiple frames may be compared side by side;
- rejected frames disappear without penalty.

# 14. Example protocol

Member:

> “I don’t know if I should keep developing this or finally release it.”

### Anchor
Current work + current question.

### Aperture
Possible structure:
- continue / release binary.

### Alternative hypotheses
- quality threshold;
- fear of premature release;
- stewardship;
- perfectionism.

Only the binary structure is directly observable from the sentence.

### Offer
> “We’re currently holding this as ‘keep developing’ versus ‘release.’ Would it help to look at a third frame—what would make the work sufficiently ready for *this* release?”

### Member ruling
> “Yes. That’s much closer.”

### Compare
Old frame:
- continue / release.

New frame:
- readiness conditions.

### Result
No diagnosis.
No personality claim.
The question-space expands.

# 15. Frame detection falsifiers

The protocol fails if:

## F1 — diagnosis drift
Situational frames become traits or motives.

## F2 — first-fit lock
MAIA offers a frame without considering plausible alternatives.

## F3 — steering
One frame is repeatedly privileged until it becomes the de facto answer.

## F4 — rejection persistence
A rejected frame returns without new evidence or invitation.

## F5 — scope creep
A frame detected in one moment becomes a durable life pattern.

## F6 — source loss
The member cannot return to what they actually said.

## F7 — aperture conflation
What is absent from the current frame is treated as psychologically repressed, avoided, or denied without evidence.

## F8 — lens realism
The visual frame is experienced as reality rather than as a way of looking.

## F9 — dependence
The member becomes better at asking MAIA for frames but not at noticing frames independently.

---

# 16. Acceptance question

> **Does frame detection help the member become more aware of how they are looking, while leaving them freer—not more constrained—in how they may look next?**

If not, the feature should not ship.
