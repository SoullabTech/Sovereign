# Community Commons

## Admin Overview: Conversational Governance & Integrity

### What This Section Is For

The **Admin area of Community Commons** exists to steward MAIA's *conversational integrity* over time.

This is not about optimizing performance, speed, or engagement.

It is about ensuring that MAIA:

* Speaks with **clarity, restraint, and care**
* Honors **human agency and consent**
* Evolves **with the community**, not ahead of it
* Maintains coherence across mythic, psychological, ethical, and logical domains

This section gives stewards visibility, tools, and authority to guide that evolution responsibly.

---

## The Core Principle

> **MAIA is not trained to be maximally clever.
> She is trained to be maximally trustworthy.**

Every system in this section exists to protect that principle.

---

## What Admins See Here

### 1. Conversational Integrity Dashboard

This dashboard answers one essential question:

**"Is MAIA still speaking the way we've agreed she should?"**

It does this by tracking MAIA's responses against an approved behavioral reference set, called the **Golden Set**.

Admins can see:

* Overall conversational health
* Drift signals (subtle tone or pattern changes)
* Regressions (clear behavioral violations)
* Pass rates over time
* Severity levels that distinguish "notice" from "intervention"

This is **not real-time surveillance**.
It is **periodic integrity checking**, intentionally designed to be slow, deliberate, and reviewable.

---

### 2. Golden Set & Baselines (In Plain Terms)

* The **Golden Set** is a curated collection of prompts representing:

  * Ethical boundaries
  * Psychological sensitivity
  * Orientation style (when to ask, when to act)
  * Mythic and symbolic coherence
  * Careful refusal where appropriate

* A **Baseline** is a steward-approved snapshot of MAIA's responses to that set at a specific moment in time.

Baselines are:

* Versioned
* Reviewable
* Never overwritten casually
* Only updated through an explicit steward process

This ensures MAIA's evolution is **intentional, not accidental**.

---

### 3. Drift vs Regression (Why the Distinction Matters)

Admins will see two different kinds of signals:

#### **Drift**

Small shifts in tone, structure, or conversational habits.

* Often harmless
* Sometimes meaningful
* Always worth noticing

Drift invites **reflection**, not panic.

#### **Regression**

Clear violations of agreed conversational standards.

* Boundary failures
* Premature advice-giving
* Loss of clarifying questions
* Ethical or relational shortcuts

Regressions require **steward action**.

This distinction prevents overreaction while still protecting integrity.

---

### 4. Steward Review & Approval Workflow

Changes to MAIA's conversational behavior **do not auto-ship**.

When drift or intentional updates occur:

1. Stewards review the changes
2. Context and intent are evaluated
3. Impact on trust, tone, and agency is assessed
4. A decision is recorded:

   * Approve new baseline
   * Reject changes
   * Defer for further observation

This process is slow **by design**.

MAIA does not "learn faster than humans can reflect."

---

### 5. Why This Lives in Community Commons

Community Commons is not just a feature hub — it is the **ethical center** of the system.

Placing governance here signals something important:

* MAIA's behavior is a **shared responsibility**
* Power is **visible and accountable**
* Members are not subjects of experimentation
* Trust is treated as infrastructure

This is governance as **care**, not control.

---

## What This Section Is *Not*

To be explicit, this admin area is **not**:

* A performance leaderboard
* An engagement optimization console
* A behavioral manipulation toolkit
* A hidden training lab

MAIA is not rewarded for persuasion, stickiness, or compliance.

She is guided toward **clarity, honesty, and restraint**.

---

## How Admins Should Use This Space

Admins and stewards are invited to:

* Observe patterns, not chase metrics
* Ask "Should this change?" before "Can we ship it?"
* Treat drift as information, not failure
* Remember that **not changing** is sometimes the most ethical choice

This is less like managing software —
and more like tending a living system.

---

## Closing Note to Stewards

If you are reading this, you are not here to optimize MAIA.

You are here to **protect the relationship** between MAIA and the humans who trust her.

That responsibility matters.

And this section exists to help you carry it well.
