## 0 · Method constraint — ⛔ ratified, and it generalizes

Chapter's Terms of Use explicitly prohibit using its service to **benchmark competing AI systems**
or to reverse-engineer its models or prompts. [6]

**JWS-01 is therefore limited, for Chapter and as a standing lane default for every product in the
§9 survey scope, to:**

> public product claims · published documentation · policies and terms · publicly observable
> workflows · legitimately available artifacts.

⛔ **No adversarial instrumentation of any censused product.** It is neither needed nor warranted —
the arrow test (charter §3) runs on a product's *own description of what it does*, which is exactly
the material a vendor publishes most willingly. ⭐ A census that needed to probe a competitor's
internals to reach a verdict would be asking the wrong question.

⚠️ **Per-product check, not a blanket assumption:** each record states the terms position it read.
Chapter's prohibition is Chapter's; the next product's must be read, not inherited.

**Core product shape:** *an AI book-production pipeline, not an authorship-development environment.*

The product page states that Chapter researches a topic and audience, structures the book using
predefined frameworks modeled on successful nonfiction, performs multiple writing and editing
passes, voice-matches, and generates a complete 80–250 page manuscript described as sounding like
the user wrote it. [1] Its own comparison material is more explicit still: **Chapter produces the
book**, while tools such as Claude are better for **interrogating an existing manuscript**. [2]

⭐ **The central arrow-test conclusion survives E-01 strongly, and now on first-party evidence
rather than on characterization.**

### 2a · The research correction

Chapter advertises topic/customer research and AI research agents. [1] But its own current materials
say something much narrower and more revealing: **Chapter does not browse the web**, Gemini beats it
for research, sourcing and fact-checking, and Chapter's own research guide tells authors to use
external research tools and **verify every publishable claim against primary sources.** [3]

```text
CORRECT TO:
  CHAPTER: topic / audience / market research
           within a production pipeline

NOT ESTABLISHED:
  primary-source research engine
  citation-grounded claim verification
  research provenance graph
```

⭐ **This makes A4.8 more clearly ours.** The epistemic substrate — Authority × Time plus typed,
digest-verified `EvidenceRef` — is not something we would be borrowing from Chapter. Chapter's own
documentation hands verification *back to the author*.

### 2b · ⭐⭐ The Whole Work correction — the most interesting E-01 finding

We originally attributed something close to **Whole Work Integrity** to Chapter. But Chapter's own
recent material says **Claude** is substantially better when an existing manuscript needs
manuscript-wide interrogation, structural criticism, continuity analysis, voice-shift detection, or
developmental editing — and that Chapter's strength is generating a book with continuity **enforced
by its production pipeline.** [4]

**Those are not the same capability.**

```text
CHAPTER                          WRITER'S STUDIO  (A4.6 / A4.2 direction)

STRUCTURE                        LIVING WORK
    ↓                                ↓
GENERATE                         MAIA PERCEIVES WHOLE
    ↓                                ↓
MAINTAIN PIPELINE CONTINUITY     OBSERVATION + EVIDENCE
    ↓                                ↓
FINISHED BOOK                    WRITER RECOGNIZES
                                     ↓
                                 WRITER DECIDES
```

Chapter prevents contradiction **by controlling generation**. Writer's Studio would perceive
contradiction **in a work it did not write**. One is a manufacturing tolerance; the other is a
reading.

⛔ **Whole Work Intelligence may NOT be recorded as "copy / adapt Chapter DoubleCheck"** unless the
missing primary source turns up. Corrected provenance statement:

> **JWS's own capability.** Chapter provides evidence that **book-scale continuity is commercially
> valuable** — it is **not** evidence for our proposed perception/diagnostic architecture.

### 2c · Authorial Voice Field — survives, for a different reason

Chapter's product language promises advanced voice matching and prose that sounds like the customer
wrote it. [1] That makes it an **excellent arrow-test exemplar**:

⭐ **E-01 strengthens both halves at once:** the reason to investigate Authorial Voice Field (voice
perception is a real and commercially proven problem) **and** the prohibition against letting it
become a voice generator. It remains **UNROWED — INVESTIGATE**.

## 3 · Sovereignty comparison — confirmed, with precise wording

Chapter's Privacy Policy states that it collects book topics, outlines and content, prompts,
generated manuscripts and usage data; processes material through AI systems; may use anonymized
aggregated data to improve AI models; and may share information with AI infrastructure
providers. [5] Its Terms state that users retain their User Content and own books created through
the service, while granting Chapter permission to process supplied content through its AI systems;
the Terms also warn that AI-generated content may be inaccurate or unoriginal and make the user
responsible for human review and publication correctness. [6]

⛔ **Chapter does not claim ownership of the author's book. Any Soullab material saying otherwise is
false and must not be written.**

The real distinction is **processing architecture, model and data boundaries, provenance, and
sovereignty** — not ownership.

⭐ **That is the stronger comparison because it does not need a straw man.** Binding under
`docs/canon/MARKETING_CLAIM_DISCIPLINE.md`: a competitor comparison that misstates the competitor
fails the Failure Test regardless of how favourable it sounds, and this lane's output may reach
decks and case studies.
