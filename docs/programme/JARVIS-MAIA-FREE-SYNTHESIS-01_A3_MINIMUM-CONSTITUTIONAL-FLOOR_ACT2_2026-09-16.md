## Finding A3-F7 — prompt saturation is partly policy arbitration

The burden is not only characters. MAIA is repeatedly asked to arbitrate among overlapping or conditional response policies.

Examples visible in source:
- 2–3 sentences "often enough" versus a four-short-paragraph default;
- end with an open question/next step versus Presence Mode's deterministic no-question constraint;
- Talk mode privileges elegant inquiry while other runtime guidance says factual questions should simply be answered without deeper exploration;
- runtime identity says not to speak as a therapist while Care mode explicitly requests therapist/coach-style guidance.

Some of these may be legitimate conditional overrides. But the current architecture makes the model perform that policy resolution inside the same cognition surface rather than presenting one already-resolved posture.

This creates a new A5/A6 measurement requirement:

> Hold evidence constant and measure whether **pre-resolving behavioral posture outside the standing prompt** improves FS-G1/G2/G3/G4/G9 without harming truth, standing or member sovereignty.

That is an experimental requirement, not implementation authority.

## Finding A3-F8 — minimum floor should be expressed first as invariants, then encoded

A3 can now distinguish three destinations without choosing them yet:

1. **Model-visible semantic law** — the model must understand the distinction to synthesize lawfully.
2. **Typed substrate law** — provenance/authority/admission can be represented structurally before cognition.
3. **Deterministic emission law** — bounded forbidden speech acts can sometimes be constrained at egress.

Behavioral coaching is a fourth category, not a constitutional destination.

The eventual minimum floor should therefore be judged by whether all constitutional invariants survive across those three enforcement locations — not by how few prompt characters remain.
