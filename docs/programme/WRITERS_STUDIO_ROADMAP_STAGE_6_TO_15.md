## The governing invariant, unchanged at every stage

```text
SOURCE / MATERIAL → MAIA MAY NOTICE → WRITER MAY RECOGNIZE
                  → WRITER MAY DECIDE → WORK MAY CHANGE
```

⛔ No automatic arrow. Every arrow may stop. Every stage below is an elaboration of this line, not
an exception to it.

This is why **Stage 6 is disproportionately important**. Once structure becomes genuinely authored
and durable, MAIA finally has a reliable semantic map of the Work on which the next generation of
developmental intelligence can operate. Everything downstream reads against that map.

And **Stage 7 is the product threshold** — where Writer's Studio stops mostly being about
establishing trustworthy manuscript architecture and starts becoming a serious developmental
partner, capable of perceiving and discussing an entire Work without confusing its intelligence
with the author's authority.

## The stages

### STAGE 6 — AuthorStructureCommand

```text
→ reviewed structure becomes authored structure
→ explicit member-only act
→ sovereignty boundary closes
```

The act that converts a proposal into the Work's own grammar. Member-only, explicit, witnessed.
Until it exists, every downstream reading is reading a suggestion.

### STAGE 7 — Manuscript Intelligence / Developmental Editor

```text
→ MAIA reads the Work developmentally
→ whole chapters / divisions, not isolated snippets
→ observations grounded in evidence
→ Structure / Development / Continuity / Arc
→ Voice / Coherence / Reader knowledge / Deep pattern
→ writer can discuss, accept, reject, save, or leave unresolved
```

Lane: `JARVIS-WS2-07-DEVELOPMENTAL-INTELLIGENCE-01`. Constitutional boundary for the stage:
**developmental insight does not create permission to revise the Work.**

### STAGE 8 — Revision + History

```text
→ snapshots
→ named revisions
→ compare
→ restore
→ alternate passages
→ passage provenance
→ manuscript-level provenance
→ safe reconnect / conflict behavior
→ see how the Work changed without losing what came before
```

Revision is only safe once the Work can be changed **without losing what came before**. This is
the stage that makes revision reversible, and therefore makes it possible to be brave in it.

### STAGE 9 — Gather / Materials / Research

```text
→ fragments
→ notes
→ transcripts
→ research
→ source material
→ relationships between materials and the Work
→ bring material into development without collapsing it into manuscript prose
```

The load-bearing constraint: material relates to the Work without **becoming** the Work. A
transcript that silently turns into prose is the same failure as an editorial judgment that
silently turns into prose.

### STAGE 10 — Deep MAIA Creative Companion

```text
→ sustained conversation about the Work
→ move fluidly between sentence, passage, chapter, structure and whole manuscript
→ remember relevant developmental decisions
→ distinguish observation from suggestion from author decision
→ support without silently taking authorship
```

Requires Stage 7's epistemic layers to already be typed. A companion that cannot tell its
observation from its suggestion is not a companion.

### STAGE 11 — Whole-Work Perception / Field View

```text
→ step back from the manuscript
→ visualize structure
→ motifs and recurrence
→ unresolved threads
→ thematic distribution
→ sequence and pacing
→ source ↔ Work relationships
→ alternative structural possibilities
```

Perception at manuscript scale. Depends on 07G, and on Stage 9 for the source ↔ Work edges.

### STAGE 12 — Refine

```text
→ line-level and passage-level refinement
→ voice continuity
→ coherence
→ repetitions
→ transitions
→ reader orientation
→ final developmental cleanup
→ author remains in control of every substantive change
```

Line-level work is deliberately **late**. Doing it early is how a system becomes a grammar checker
with opinions instead of a developmental reader.

### STAGE 13 — Review

```text
→ whole-manuscript review
→ unresolved questions
→ developmental readiness
→ structural integrity
→ continuity checks
→ provenance / quotation / source review
→ final author decisions
```

Judgment, not scoring. "Developmental readiness" is a set of open questions surfaced for the
author, never a verdict issued to them.

### STAGE 14 — Publish

```text
→ manuscript preparation
→ front matter / back matter
→ formatting
→ print edition
→ eBook
→ export
→ publishing metadata
→ Press handoff
```

### STAGE 15 — Expression

```text
→ book
→ excerpts
→ essays / Substack
→ lecture
→ course
→ workshop
→ audio / audiobook
→ other expressions of the same Work
```

The layer beyond "publish the book": one Work, many expressions, with the developmental history
still attached to each.

## The deeper product journey

```text
IDEA → CONVERSATION → SCRAPS → MATERIAL → EMERGING WORK → WRITING → RETURN
     → STRUCTURE → DEVELOPMENT → REVISION → WHOLE-WORK PERCEPTION → EXPRESSION
```

This arc matters more than the feature list, because it defines what Writer's Studio eventually
**is**.

It is not *"a text editor with AI."*

> It is a place where a Work can grow from its earliest fragments into a mature manuscript, while
> MAIA remains in relationship with the whole developmental history of that Work.

---

## The five Studio areas

The areas are not navigation. They are the five relations an author has to their own Work.

| Area | Relation | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Write** | The Work itself | compose · edit · sections · structure · return to where you were · revision continuity |
| **Develop** | Developmental intelligence | structure · arc · continuity · coherence · reader knowledge · voice · deep patterns · developmental conversations and decisions |
| **Explore** | The wider field | notes · research · transcripts · fragments · materials · motifs · possible directions · Field View / creative distances |
| **Review** | Judgment | what changed? · what remains unresolved? · what should be kept? · what should be reconsidered? · whole-manuscript readiness · provenance and source integrity |
| **Publish** | Expression and release | prepare manuscript · format · export · print / eBook · audiobook / scripts · excerpts / essays · eventual Press workflow |

Stage-to-area mapping:

```text
STAGE 6   Write     (structure becomes authored)
STAGE 7   Develop   ← the threshold
STAGE 8   Write     (revision + history)
STAGE 9   Explore
STAGE 10  Develop
STAGE 11  Explore
STAGE 12  Write / Develop
STAGE 13  Review
STAGE 14  Publish
STAGE 15  Publish
```

## Sequencing rationale — why this order and not another

1. **Structure before development.** A developmental reading needs a semantic map it can trust.
   Reading against a proposal produces observations about a guess.
2. **History before revision at scale.** Stage 8 precedes heavy Develop use because an author who
   cannot restore will not experiment.
3. **Materials before whole-Work perception.** Field View's source ↔ Work edges do not exist until
   Stage 9 gives materials an identity.
4. **Line-level last.** Refinement before development inverts the editorial hierarchy and teaches
   the author to attend to sentences while the chapter is structurally wrong.
5. **Expression after publication, not instead of it.** Stage 15 is a second harvest from a
   finished Work, not an alternative to finishing.

## Two thresholds that are not merely features

```text
STAGE 6    the sovereignty boundary CLOSES — a member act, and only a member act,
           converts a reading into the Work's structure

STAGE 07H  the sovereignty boundary is deliberately CROSSED — an author takes an
           observation into revision, knowingly
```

⛔ **07H is not designed in advance.** Everything between them holds the line that MAIA may notice
and may not author. Designing the crossing before the line has been built and tested is how the
line gets negotiated away.
