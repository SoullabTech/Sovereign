## 2 · FR-14 · GRANT ≠ TRIGGER ≠ CADENCE

The distinction FR-13 exposed, made structural **before** anyone implements the
supportive half:

> ### Permission to support is not permission to interrupt.

Selecting `encourage` must not quietly authorize MAIA to start nagging because
progress changed or a deadline approached.

```text
GRANT     What kind of relationship the writer permits around this goal.
TRIGGER   The circumstance in which support may actually appear.
CADENCE   How often it may recur.

FR-13 grants RELATIONSHIP.
It does not authorize push, interruption, reminders, or a cadence.
```

### v1 triggers, deliberately conservative

```text
track_only
→ numbers / state only
→ MAIA silent

encourage
→ encouragement may appear when the writer INTENTIONALLY ENTERS or ENGAGES
  the Goal surface
→ no unsolicited interruption elsewhere in Studio

help_me_work_with_it
→ offers a DOOR into an active MAIA conversation about the goal
→ the writer initiates the conversation
```

### Specifically NOT yet — each needs a further grant

```text
notifications
"You haven't written today"
deadline reminders
automatic check-ins
encouragement injected while the writer is composing
MAIA initiating because a metric changed
any recurring cadence
```

## 3 · `maiaMaySupport()` ANSWERS **MAY**, NEVER **SHOULD**

> **A stored `encourage` value cannot itself become a command to produce
> motivational prose.**

The eventual MAIA path still has to decide whether support is appropriate *in
the present interaction*. The gate removes a prohibition; it does not supply a
reason.

⚠️ **Correction to the FR-13 implementation.** The docstring shipped saying the
gate answers whether MAIA may speak about a goal **"unbidden"**. Under FR-14
that is precisely what a grant does NOT authorize — the word smuggled a trigger
into a permission. Repaired in place, and a falsifier now asserts the function
takes **no timing, occasion or cadence input at all**, so it cannot become one.
