> **`SQ-5` — What makes a rule binding here, and what resolves a conflict between two texts that
> both bind?**

## RULING

⭐⭐ **A rule is binding because an authorized authority-bearing act made it binding.**

⛔ Binding force does **not** arise from:

```text
constitutional tone · a document calling itself Canon · placement in docs/canon/
age · recency · repeated citation · implementation in code · operational usefulness
being named in CLAUDE.md · being treated as though it were binding
```

⭐ Those may be **evidence about** a rule. ⛔ They are not the **act** that gives it authority.

---

## 1 · How binding force is created

An authority-bearing act must establish, explicitly or by an already-valid delegation:

```text
1  WHO    has authority to rule
2  WHAT   operative language is being made binding
3  WHERE  its jurisdiction lies
4  WHEN   it takes effect
5  HOW    it relates to existing binding material —
          adds · amends · supersedes · narrows · delegates · or leaves untouched
```

```text
⛔ A document cannot ratify itself.
⛔ A descriptive document does not become normative because implementation follows it.
⛔ Code cannot confer constitutional authority on the text it implements.
⛔ Citation is not ratification.
```

## 2 · ⭐⭐ Jurisdiction precedes hierarchy

The constitutional organism is **jurisdictional before it is hierarchical.**

⛔ Two binding texts do not conflict merely because both speak broadly. The first question is:

> **Do they govern the same subject, act, relationship, or authority?**

⭐ Where their jurisdictions are genuinely distinct, **both remain binding in their own territory.**

⛔ **No universal stack is imposed** — `Founder > Canon > ADR > operational instruction > code` is
**not** hereby made the rule across every subject. ⭐ Different binding instruments may govern
different constitutional territories simultaneously.

## 3 · ⭐ Delegated authority cannot enlarge itself

A delegated instrument is binding **only within the authority actually delegated to it.** An ADR,
operational rule, programme instruction, session anchor, gate or similar subordinate instrument may
govern its authorized territory. ⛔ It may not, merely by assertion:

```text
⛔ enlarge its jurisdiction
⛔ amend the authority that delegated it
⛔ convert an operational rule into constitutional law
⛔ override a binding rule outside its delegated scope
```

⭐ **The fact that an instrument is encountered first OPERATIONALLY does not make it
constitutionally prior.**

## 4 · True same-territory conflicts

A true conflict exists **only** where two binding requirements concern:

```text
the same subject · the same relevant act or relationship · the same operative conditions
AND cannot both be obeyed
```

When that occurs:

**⭐ Explicit amendment or override controls.** A later authority-bearing act controls **only where
it explicitly amends, supersedes, narrows or overrides** the earlier rule — **within authority it
possesses.**

```text
⛔ DATE ALONE DOES NOT CONTROL          "later" does not mean "higher"
⛔ SPECIFICITY ALONE DOES NOT CONTROL   a narrower text does not silently override a broader
                                        binding text merely by being more specific;
                                        ⭐ specificity may CLARIFY JURISDICTION —
                                        ⛔ it does not MANUFACTURE AMENDMENT AUTHORITY
⛔ USAGE DOES NOT CONTROL               a rule does not prevail because code, sessions or
                                        operators happen to follow it
```

**⭐⭐ If no valid precedence act exists: `HOLD`.** Where two binding texts truly conflict and no
authorized amendment, override or controlling delegation resolves them —

> ⛔ **Neither silently defeats the other.** The disputed territory enters **`HOLD`** until an
> authority-bearing adjudication resolves it.

⭐ **The conflict itself is preserved.**

## 5 · How far a binding rule extends

A binding rule governs:

```text
1  what it explicitly rules
2  what it explicitly delegates
3  what is NECESSARILY ENTAILED by the rule
```

⛔ A rule does **not** become binding over adjacent territory merely because that territory is:

```text
analogous · thematically related · morally similar · operationally nearby
implemented by the same mechanism · plausibly within the spirit of the rule
```

### ⭐⭐ The necessary-entailment test — all three limbs, or none

```text
A  SAME PROTECTED OBJECT      the application concerns the same thing the rule actually
                              protects or governs

B  NECESSITY                  allowing the adjacent act would make compliance with the
                              explicit rule IMPOSSIBLE, INTERNALLY CONTRADICTORY,
                              or SELF-DEFEATING

C  NO NEW SUBSTANTIVE CHOICE  extending the rule requires NO additional value judgement,
                              policy preference, or strategic decision
```

⛔ **If any limb fails, the adjacent territory is `UNRULED` by that text.** ⭐ Analogy may support a
**future ruling**; ⛔ it does not itself create one.

## 6 · ⛔ The adjacency rule is not chosen for `SQ-4`

This doctrine is adopted **independently of any outcome it may later produce** for `SQ-4` or any
other founder question, and applies equally wherever a binding rule is invoked over adjacent
territory.

> ⛔⛔ **No later adjudication may argue that a rule reaches an adjacent subject merely because
> extending it produces the preferred substantive result.**

⭐ **`SQ-4` remains undecided.**

## 7 · Existing corpus — ⭐ non-retroactivity

This ruling establishes the regime for authority-bearing acts **going forward.** ⛔ It does **not**
retroactively declare that documents previously treated as governing were never governing. Their
recorded standing remains part of the **sealed evidence**.

⚠️ Where the new regime exposes ambiguity in an existing instrument's standing, jurisdiction,
delegation or relationship to another text, that ambiguity requires a **separate reconciliation
act.** ⛔ It is not corrected by rewriting history.

> ⭐⭐ A future ruling may rule **against** an inherited finding or standing. ⛔ It may not **alter**
> the finding to make the ruling easier.

## 8 · Standing of Founder Adjudication rulings

A ruling expressly taken in `FOUNDER-ADJUDICATION-01` under its authorized scope **is an
authority-bearing founder act.** ⭐ Its force comes from that explicit act and recorded authority —
⛔ not from the prose sounding constitutional.

⚠️ Each later ruling must still **identify its subject** and ⛔ may not silently amend `ACT 1` or
another founder ruling.

> **Authority comes from an authorized act, operates within a jurisdiction, and does not expand by
