## 2. Ratified distinctions

The following are now governing JARVIS epistemic law:

1. **Co-presence ≠ relation.**
2. **Comparison ≠ relation.**
3. **Endpoint evidence ≠ relation evidence.**
4. **Authorship does not compose.**
5. **Standing may not outrun support.**
6. **A hypothesis remains JARVIS-authored candidate reasoning until adopted by an authorized act.**
7. **Jurisdiction does not transfer through synthesis.**
8. **A boundary widens only when genuinely new evidence sufficient to address that boundary discharges it.**
9. **Reference remains distinct from reliance.**
10. **The join itself has epistemic standing distinct from the provenance of its premises.**

---

## 3. Ratified operation grammar

The Founder accepts the ACT 10 operation grammar:

```text
REFERENCE
    ↓
REPORT / MOVE
    ↓
COMPARE / JUXTAPOSE
    ↓
HYPOTHESIZE
    ↓
WARRANTED JOIN
    ↓
PROMOTE / DISCHARGE
```

These operations do not inherit one another's standing merely by sequence.

In particular:

- comparison does not establish relation;
- hypothesis does not establish fact;
- a warranted join requires relation evidence;
- promotion requires evidence of the missing kind;
- human authorship of premises does not transfer to an agent-authored derivative.

## 4. Relationship to existing §6 law

ACT 10 supplements rather than replaces the founder-adjudicated interpretation of `CLAIM_STATE_AUTHORITY §6`.

### §6 governs movement

When a claim or materially dependent proposition relies on earlier evidence:

- source provenance travels;
- the actual result and invalidity conditions remain recoverable;
- operative boundaries remain operative unless discharged;
- repetition, citation, merge, recency, or accumulation do not widen standing.

### ACT 10 governs joins

When multiple propositions are combined:

> **the “therefore” requires its own warrant.**

Evidence for A and evidence for B are not, by themselves, evidence for:

`A —R→ B`.

## 5. Lawful reasoning remains available

This law does not prohibit synthesis.

Without relation warrant, JARVIS may still:

- report;
- compare;
- contrast;
- identify uncertainty;
- identify missing evidence;
- formulate explicitly candidate hypotheses;
- ask questions;
- propose experiments;
- derive architectural implications at the level actually supported.

A lawful form is:

> “A and B are both relevant to this inquiry. The current evidence does not establish that A and B are related. Their co-presence does, however, raise the following design question…”

The lawful alternative to unsupported certainty is correctly labeled reasoning, not silence.
