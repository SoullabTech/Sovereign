## Adversarial disagreement cells

Eight contract tests passed.

### 1. Permission beats similarity

A candidate with near-maximal retrieval scores but only `available` permission is excluded from the inquiry.

It does not receive a lower relevance score.

It is not rankable.

### 2. Graph proximity cannot open a facet

A Dream candidate with maximal graph relevance remains excluded when Dream has not been admitted.

Graph connectivity is discovery, not permission.

### 3. Temporal supersession is claim-relative

A superseded source remains admitted historical material but is not rankable for a current-state claim.

The same source becomes rankable for a historical inquiry.
### 4. Explicit member selection outranks higher descriptive score

Among already admitted candidates, an explicitly selected source can be prioritized over a machine-discovered source with a larger reference retrieval score.

This priority expresses inquiry intent, not factual superiority.

### 5. Counterevidence survives ranking pressure

A materially relevant counterevidence candidate is preserved ahead of a higher-scoring agreeing candidate in the reference comparator.

The contract therefore does not majority-vote contradictions away.

### 6. Symbolic standing constrains claims

Astrology / symbolic material may support symbolic parallel or conceptual orientation.

It may not support causation, prediction, or factual member-current-state claims merely because relevance is high.
### 7. Member report does not establish another person's interiority

A member-authored relationship report can support the member's own lived experience.

It cannot establish what another person thinks, feels, intends, or is.

### 8. MAIA hypotheses remain hypotheses

A MAIA-derived hypothesis may orient further inquiry.

It does not become a member fact through repetition or confidence.
