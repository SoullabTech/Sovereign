## The founder's ruling, as given

**C.** The failing claim is on the wrong side of the developmental boundary. It is mechanically
true, but it is not a developmental phenomenon under the commissioned Development lens. Heading
pattern, code-point length band and an even 3/3 authored division are all re-derivable directly from
the Work and its declared structure without a reader — `UNDERSTAND §1` places that in **MECHANICAL
EVIDENCE**, one layer below developmental observation. The Development lens asks whether ideas are
underdeveloped, sufficiently developed, overexplained, introduced too late, abandoned or repeated
without advancing; the uniformity claim says none of those things.

> **The reader should not have advanced that mechanical regularity into a `ReaderClaimDraft` under
> the Development lens.**

**Why B is rejected.** No `structural-uniformity` ninth phenomenon. That would solve the symptom by
promoting an essentially mechanical fact into the developmental ontology. If a legitimate
developmental observation whose phenomenon genuinely is structural uniformity is found later, it
returns as its own evidence-driven vocabulary question; this fixture does not establish one.

**Why not A.** When the classifier answered `unclassifiable` it followed its contract — its prompt
says to answer unclassifiable when nothing fits and *"Do not stretch a category to fit."* The two
`positional-asymmetry` answers stretched a bare label meaning asymmetry across a claim about
uniformity. Making that stretch more reliable is the wrong repair.

**The refusal rule is left alone.** Once the family is properly defined, a mechanical-uniformity
claim reaching the classifier *should* produce `unclassifiable → refuse freeze`. That is correct
fail-closed behaviour. The repair is upstream, at 07B. Changing 07C to silently discard
unclassifiable claims would hide an epistemic boundary failure and turn a reading into an
undocumented subset of what the reader actually produced.

## The boundary rule that must not be broken by the repair

```text
LENS        = what editorial question is being asked
PHENOMENON  = what kind of observation was seen
```

UNDERSTAND §4 makes these independent, many-to-many vocabularies. **The 07B reader is not taught
the eight phenomena** — that would collapse the distinction. The reader needs the meaning of
*Development*; the classifier needs the meaning and boundaries of the eight, and no manuscript
context.

### The provenance seam this exposed

`UNDERSTAND §4` says plainly: *"The phenomenon list is **not** frozen — the left column above is
illustrative … and **DECIDE decides which phenomena are actually detectable and worth naming**."*
DECIDE never did — it names no phenomenon but one passing crossing reference and defines none. So an
illustrative list passed through the document meant to adjudicate it, was not adjudicated, and the
founder's 07C act — correctly taking UNDERSTAND §4 verbatim, no new taxonomy — froze it as closed.
Eight bare labels reached runtime. Nobody erred; a seam between two documents did.
