## 1 · THE DEFECT, RESTATED PRECISELY

> ⭐⭐ **The bridge's admissibility depends on which synonym the member happens to choose for
> the act of remembering.**

At P1, `remember` is present in the probe and becomes the one-token admission bridge. At W1
the member says `mentioned`; `remember` leaves the probe, the naming turn becomes unreachable,
and `remember` then **re-enters as a carrier** from failed retrospective asks. The same
retrieval vocabulary is load-bearing in **different roles** depending only on phrasing.

⭐ **The defect is therefore named: `HOP 1` LEXICAL-ROLE INSTABILITY.** ⛔ Not a ranking
problem. ⛔ Not an aperture problem.

The decisive relation, and the reason the lost log does not weaken it:

```text
P1 probe ∩ naming turn = {"remember"}
W1 probe ∩ naming turn = ∅
```

⭐ Once W1's intersection is empty, **aperture cannot rescue the naming turn**; and because the
naming turn is persisted in the session, **first-ask is excluded**.

## 4 · ⚠️ ONE MEASURED OBSERVATION, OFFERED AS INPUT — ⛔ NOT AN ANSWER

In **both** runs, `HOP 1` admitted **exactly the retrospective asks present in the prefix, and
nothing else**:

```text
P1  prefix {37,38,39}   admitted {38,39} — both retrospective asks;  37 linked ∅
W1  prefix              admitted the 17:50:39Z and 18:52:08Z asks — both retrospective asks
```

⭐ So retrieval-vocabulary overlap is, in practice, a **reliable detector of retrospective
asks** — and a **useless detector of which one names the object**. At P1 the admitted set
happened to contain the turn that named the target; at W1 it did not, because that turn used a
different retrieval word.

⭐⭐ **And the naming turn at P1 was ITSELF a failed retrospective ask.** The marker was named
*in a question*, never in the original telling — so the bridge never reached the moment the
marker was first shared. ⛔ It reached a later turn in which the member repeated it while
asking. That is `C1-BRIDGE-01 §4`'s *"the bridge exists because the member asked twice,"*
measured from the other side.

### 4.1 ⚠️ A question about the frame, raised once and then deferred

This admits a reading that may not sit inside either branch of §3: that the two rules govern
**different objects** — the opacity rule asks *"does the probe identify the object?"*, while
`HOP 1` asks *"which recent turn is the member reaching toward?"* — so retrieval vocabulary
could be legitimate evidence of **reaching-back** while remaining non-evidence about the
**object**, with no conflict between the rules, and `HOP 1` still wrong because **token overlap
is the wrong instrument for a deixis question.**

⛔ **This is not offered as a third outcome and not as an answer.** It is offered because, if it
holds, the **Yes** branch would resolve by revising `HOP 1`'s *instrument* rather than by
revising either rule's *content* — and the ruling should be able to see that before it chooses.
⭐ **The founder decides whether the frame admits it.** ⛔ Nothing here is acted on.
