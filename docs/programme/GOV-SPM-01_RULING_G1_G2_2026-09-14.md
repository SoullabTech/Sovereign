## G2 — invitation permits **drafting**; **adoption** creates representation

⛔ **Neither G2-b nor G2-c taken verbatim.** G2-c drew the domain boundary too tightly: a member may
legitimately ask MAIA to help draft a first-person letter, memoir passage, difficult message, speech
or application. **The stronger distinction is drafting versus representing.**

> **MAIA may generate first-person candidate language as a member only upon an explicit member
> invitation. The generated language remains MAIA-produced proposal until the member adopts it. MAIA
> may not independently convert that proposal into a member-authored fact, consent, commitment,
> identity claim, relational act, or external representation.**

```text
member asks "help me say this"
        ↓
MAIA drafts in first person                      PERMITTED
        ↓
candidate remains MODEL-ORIGINATED
        ↓
member edits / accepts / releases
        ↓
member-authored act                              AUTHORIZED

MAIA drafts → system silently treats it as       ⛔ REFUSED
"Kelly said…"
```

**A separate member act is required** before sending, publishing, sharing, persisting as
member-authored meaning, or otherwise representing the member to another party.

⛔ **Attribution alone is insufficient** — G2-d rejected. ⛔ **An absolute ban is unnecessary** —
explicit invitation plus member adoption protects the member without destroying Writer's Studio.

⭐ This gives the member an identity firewall **symmetrical in spirit, not identical in mechanism**,
to MAIA's own.

## G1 — a **consumer × purpose** grant

Governing principle unchanged and already canon: **competence does not create permission**; legibility
is a gift the member grants and can revoke.

| | Ruling |
| --- | --- |
| **G1-1 Consumer** | **Named consumer required.** A grant identifies who may consume the claim. A capability may implement that later; ⛔ a generic capability cannot erase consumer identity. |
| **G1-2 Purpose** | **Authorized at grant; declared and checked at use.** ⛔ Use-time declaration never *creates* permission — it proves the requested use fits an existing grant. |
| **G1-3 Purpose travel** | **No by default.** Permission for A grants nothing for B. ⛔ **No system-inferred "adjacent purpose."** A materially new purpose requires another grant. |
| **G1-4 Duration** | **Until revoked unless the member explicitly bounds it.** ⛔ No silent expiration, no renewal-by-silence. More sensitive formation rules may still require per-instance invitation. |
| **G1-5 Revocation** | **Consumption authority stops; truth is not rewritten.** A lawfully formed claim may remain historically held if independently permitted, but may no longer be consumed under the revoked grant. |

### ⭐ G1-5's three axes — none may silently edit another

```text
WHAT IS TRUE?        assertion / supersession
MAY IT BE HELD?      formation / retention / deletion
WHO MAY USE IT WHY?  consumption grant
```

⛔ **Do not implement revocation as** *permission withdrawn → assertion becomes historically false* —
that conflates **authority to use** with **epistemic standing**.
⛔ **Nor as automatic deletion** — `UNPOSSESSED` explicitly distinguishes non-formation discipline
from deletion-on-request. **Deletion is a separate member act; correction/supersession is a separate
truth act.**
