### D-18 · Group-size and group-process research — **SUBSTANTIALLY SATISFIED**; remainder transferred to empirical observation

- **No universal numerical Circle-size threshold is supported.** Partial evidence tends
  toward smaller groups for process-oriented work; nothing establishes an optimum.
- **Psychological safety is not monotonic with headcount.** A 2026 multicentre ICU study
  associated smaller nursing teams with higher psychological safety, but it is
  cross-sectional, 22 units, and measures large clinical teams rather than intimate groups.
  Counter-effect: experiments with 3,652 participants found *larger* groups can reduce
  subjective fear under external threat. **Distinguish safety in numbers from psychological
  safety for interpersonal risk-taking. They are not the same phenomenon.**
- **Rupture and repair is more important than first assumed.** Rupture occurs
  member↔member, member↔facilitator, and member↔whole-group, and members who are *not*
  parties to a conflict must still metabolize it. Successful rupture resolution has a
  moderate association with positive outcome, though largely from dyadic rather than group
  evidence. As groups grew and formed informal subgroups, subgroup↔subgroup and
  subgroup↔whole-group conflict increased.
- **Candidate Circle principle (better than "avoid conflict"):**
  > *A living Circle must be capable of rupture and repair without requiring rupture to
  > become moderation.*
  Circle will eventually need a **repair geometry**, not merely report / remove / block.
  Constellations will need it too, because conflict can occur Circle↔Circle.
- **Fission research:** pre-existing subgroup boundaries become fault lines along which
  groups split after conflict. **Do not implement automatic headcount-triggered fission**
  (strengthens D-09).

### D-06 · **CORRECTED** — contribute-before-see does not extend to the shared feed

The prior recommendation to extend contribute-before-you-see to the shared feed is
**withdrawn**. The supporting evidence protects *independent judgment* where independent
judgment is the thing at stake: in the 5,180-person experiment, individuals answered first,
then deliberated in small groups, and preserving pre-deliberation independence improved
collective accuracy. That does not mean a person must speak before being allowed to witness.
Research on online support communities repeatedly finds people who read without posting
derive meaningful benefit, with lurkers and posters differing little across most empowerment
outcomes. This matches legitimate peripheral participation far better than compulsory
contribution.

```text
INQUIRY        Feel → Contribute independently → Encounter others → Deliberate
LIVING FIELD   Arrive → Witness / Offer / Respond / Rest
```

> **A quiet member must be allowed to be present without performing presence.**

## 2 · Additions to the register

### A · Circle lifecycle — distinct phenomena, not one event

```text
formation / coalescence → active life → maturation → then one of:
    continuation · rest · completion · generative birth · rupture-driven separation
```

Communities-of-practice work gives the richer arc (potential → coalescing → active →
dispersed → memorable) and observes that large communities naturally spawn smaller subgroups
as intense mutual engagement becomes difficult. **Rest, completion and birth are not
engagement failures.** And an emerging subgroup means two very different things:

- **Generative differentiation** — "five of us found another inquiry that deserves its own field"
- **Relational schism** — "we can no longer tolerate those people, so we are leaving"

These are not the same lifecycle event and must not share a mechanism. The system may notice
differentiation and **invite inquiry**. It may not declare that a human field has divided.

### B · AI-mediated deliberation — two MAIA roles, carried forward for adjudication

Evidence now shows AI *can* mediate explicit common-ground processes: the 2024 Habermas
Machine study (5,734 people) found AI-generated group statements preferred to human-mediated
ones, groups less divided, and successful statements incorporating minority critique while
respecting majority position. **So synthesis by AI is not inherently corrupting** and should
not be constitutionally forbidden as such.

But purpose decides. That study sought a group statement people could endorse. A Circle may
instead be perceiving unresolved difference, accompanying grief, exploring shadow, holding
incompatible truths, or letting something remain unanswered. Cautions: a Nature review warns
LLMs can reshape how collective information is aggregated and transmitted; two **preprints**
(lower confidence) report persistent minority underrepresentation and input-order bias in
LLM-generated deliberation summaries, and that AI-mediated minority support increased
minority participation while **reducing psychological safety**.

```text
FIELD WITNESS  — DEFAULT. Preserves plurality, difference, unresolved tension and movement.
                 "Three different experiences seem present."  "This tension has not resolved."
                 "Something changed here."  Never declares consensus.

COMMON-GROUND MEDIATOR — INVOKED by the Circle, explicitly. Iterative and member-critiqued:
                 members critique MAIA's proposed synthesis; MAIA never announces what the
                 group believes. May seek synthesis without erasing dissent.
```

**Do not collapse these roles.** Both are candidates for constitutional adjudication, not
ratified.

### C · Ratified: **no numerical size threshold** from this research

No 12, 18, 24 or other number becomes constitutional doctrine. Size remains an **operational
variable observed in living Circles**.

## 3 · Proposed scope — `CIRCLE-02 · Product Constitution` (proposal, not authorization)

**Premise.** Desk research has reached its useful edge. What remains cannot be answered by
another hundred papers; it can only be learned by creating the thing. CIRCLE-02 should
therefore open as an **experimental constitution**, explicitly holding numerical limits and
some MAIA behaviour open to observation.

**In scope**
1. The Circle as a bounded sovereign relational field — its constitutive object, distinct
   from member connection, from Commons, and from decision process.
2. Participation postures: arrive · witness · offer · respond · rest. Silence as a legitimate
   posture, not a deficit. D-06 as corrected.
3. Repair geometry: rupture types (member↔member, member↔facilitator, member↔group,
   subgroup↔group, Circle↔Circle) and what repair may look like short of moderation.
4. Lifecycle: formation → active → maturation → continuation / rest / completion /
   generative birth / rupture-driven separation, with birth and schism structurally distinct.
5. MAIA's two roles: Field Witness as default, Common-Ground Mediator as invoked, iterative
   and member-critiqued.
6. What Circle refuses: ranking, popularity-weighted visibility, activity-based trust tiers,
   engagement scoring, automatic headcount-triggered fission, and system-declared division of
   a human field.

**Held open, by ruling, for empirical observation**
- Any numerical size limit.
- The precise behaviour of Field Witness in a live multi-member field.
- Whether recognizability, reciprocity, differentiation, repair, field legibility, voluntary
  silence and sovereignty survive at 6, 10, 14 and 18 members over several weeks.

**Out of scope**
Constellations design beyond noting the federation analogue; Commons architecture; any
outward claim resting on the unverified citations; any implementation.

**Exit gate (proposed).** An experimental constitution the founder ratifies, with its held-open
items named as held-open, plus a stated design for the first living Circles — since the next
serious research is Soullab's own primary research with living human fields.
