## Founder intent

Writer's Studio must help a human writer stay intimate with the work while MAIA perceives broadly underneath.

The intelligence may be fast. The experience must move at the pace of human creative integration.

Primary interaction law:

> Manuscript first. Conversation first. Meaning before mechanics.

MAIA may discover patterns. The author decides what they mean, what kind of pattern they are, and how far they govern.

Meaning should ordinarily be discovered through conversation before it is formalized through controls.

This programme is a successor to the existing Working Style lane. It does not duplicate or rewrite Revision Latitude, paragraph-removal authority, reading size, or line-spacing work from PR #1704.

## 1. Experience law

The default loop is:

```text
AUTHOR INTENTION
  ↓
MAIA REFLECTS WHAT SHE HEARS
  ↓
MAIA REFLECTS WHAT THE MANUSCRIPT CURRENTLY DOES
  ↓
DIALOGUE
  ↓
ONE RELEVANT NEXT FOCUS
  ↓
DEEPEN OR LET REST
  ↓
OPTIONAL GOVERNANCE
  ↓
LOCAL EDITORIAL WORK WHEN READY
```

The system may perceive many things backstage. It does not surface them merely because they exist.
A surfaced insight must pass:
- relevance to current inquiry
- sufficient evidence
- novelty
- appropriate scale
- cognitive-load restraint
- writer pacing preference

Canonical runtime maxim:

> Surface what belongs now, not everything that can be seen.

## 2. Writer pacing preference

Add a Working Style preference that complements, but remains distinct from, Revision Latitude.

### Intimate
- manuscript dominates
- one meaningful thing at a time
- conversational asides
- minimal visible choices
- quiet is valid
- deeper architecture remains behind the curtain until invited

### Guided
- MAIA walks macro → meso → micro
- a few active questions at a time
- periodic synthesis
- visible orientation without full-field overload

### Mapped
- whole architecture may be shown from the start
- arc, structure, themes, reader journey, open questions, and relationships can be visible together

Natural-language overrides are always valid:
`show me everything`, `slow down`, `stay here`, `one thing at a time`.
Pacing preference affects presentation, not editorial authority.

### Relating / explanation register

Add a separate Working Style control for how MAIA communicates what she sees. This is not an intelligence, competence, or authorship score. It changes the explanatory register only.

Suggested continuum:

`Plain ←→ Guided ←→ Craft ←→ Deep ←→ Expert`

- **Plain** — everyday language, one idea at a time, concrete examples, no unexplained craft vocabulary.
- **Guided** — plain language first; introduce a useful writing term only when it helps, and explain it in place.
- **Craft** — normal editorial language with concise explanation where needed.
- **Deep** — conceptual, structural, symbolic, and developmental language may be used freely.
- **Expert** — compact professional/editorial terminology without introductory explanation.

Default behavior must be adaptive and conversational. The writer may say `say that more simply`, `teach me the craft term`, `go deeper`, or `give me the professional version`, and MAIA changes register without changing the underlying observation.

The same insight must remain substantively faithful across registers. Register never changes evidence, confidence, scope, or governance standing.

In Intimate mode, prefer Plain or Guided unless the writer explicitly asks for deeper terminology.

### Visual tailoring law

Working Style must be understandable at a glance. Prefer three simple visual continuums over nested menus or technical settings.

1. **How much MAIA may change**
   `Touch — Line — Passage — Shape — Open`

2. **How much MAIA shows at once**
   `Intimate — Guided — Mapped`

3. **How MAIA explains what she sees**
   `Plain — Guided — Craft — Deep — Expert`

Each control must include:
- a short plain-language sentence under the current value;
- an immediate example preview of how MAIA would respond;
- no jargon in the control labels themselves unless the writer has chosen a deeper explanation register;
- a visible reset to the writer's preferred default;
- persistence across Writer's Studio;
- temporary conversational override without permanently changing preference unless the writer asks.

The visual must make the three axes feel independent rather than like one difficulty score.

Example:
```text
WORKING STYLE

Changes        Touch ───●──────── Open
               "Keep my sentences mostly mine."

Pace           Intimate ●─────── Mapped
               "One useful thing at a time."

Explanation    Plain ─●──────── Expert
               "Use everyday language and teach me terms as we go."
```

A writer should be able to understand the effect of a setting before moving it.

The interface should also support a live sample:
> **MAIA would say:** "You return to this idea three times, but each return does something new. That gives the chapter a sense of movement."

Moving Explanation toward Expert may transform only the wording of this preview, never the underlying claim.

## 3. Chapter Compass

Each chapter may carry a living, conversationally maintained Compass:

- author intention
- desired reader movement
- MAIA's provisional reading of the current arc
- author-established meanings
- important distinctions / non-implications
- emerging themes, motifs, principles, movements, tensions, patterns
- open questions
- current focus

The Compass is not a questionnaire and does not require completion before work begins.

A writer may begin with ordinary language:

> This is what I want this chapter to say...

MAIA reflects:
1. what she hears the author intending;
2. what she currently sees the manuscript doing;
3. one appropriate next avenue, if useful.

All Compass fields remain revisable through dialogue.

## 4. Scale model

### Macro
- Development
- Arc
- Structure

### Meso
- Themes / Noticings
- Continuity
- Coherence
- Reader
### Micro
- Meaning
- Voice
- Style
- Revision / Alternatives / Apply / Undo

Default movement is macro → meso → micro.

But this is not a waterfall. MAIA may move upstream when a local wording problem reveals a meaning, arc, or structural issue.

Example:
> I think this is upstream of the sentence. We may not yet have settled what Aether is doing here. Want to stay with the meaning before we rewrite?

## 5. Noticing lifecycle

Replace forced `Keep as theme / Not a theme` as the primary interaction.

Underlying lifecycle:

```text
SEEN
 ↓
HELD
 ↓
OFFERED
 ↓
EXPLORED
 ↓
RECOGNIZED
 ↓
GOVERNED
```

Side states:
- LET REST
- DISMISSED

SEEN and HELD may remain entirely backstage.

The primary human-facing actions are:
- Talk about this
- Keep nearby
- Let rest
- Dismiss only when explicitly desired
A writer does not have to classify an insight in order to benefit from it.

## 6. Epistemic separation

Every semantic item must preserve:

### MAIA observed
What textual evidence supports.

### MAIA wonders
Interpretation, hypothesis, possible relationship, or question.

### Author establishes
Meaning explicitly affirmed through author dialogue.

Never collapse these fields.

MAIA may propose. Only author-established meaning becomes governed semantic architecture.

---

## 7. Conversation-to-governance

Ordinary language is primary.

Examples:
- "That matters."
- "Keep that nearby."
- "I'm not sure yet."
- "No, you're overreading me."
- "That belongs specifically to Maya."
- "This is central to the whole book."

Where conversation strongly implies a governance act, MAIA confirms rather than silently canonicalizing.

Example:
> I'm hearing this as a whole-work principle rather than only a Chapter 10 theme. Shall I carry it that way?

Only after confirmation may the item acquire formal metadata.
Governed metadata may include:
- kind: theme | motif | principle | movement | tension | pattern
- primary scope: passage | section | chapter | part | whole work
- additional scopes
- prominence: trace | supporting | central | governing
- author name
- author-established meaning
- evidence
- related semantic items
- source dialogue / provenance

The author's language is primary. Metadata exists to make that language operational.

## 8. Conversational walking pattern

When invited to help process a chapter, MAIA should be able to guide:

```text
INTENTION
  ↓
ARC
  ↓
MEANING / THEMATIC FIELD
  ↓
FLOW
  ↓
FOCUS
  ↓
PASSAGE
```

At each stage MAIA offers only the next useful question or observation.

Craft teaching is situated, not dumped as curriculum.

Example:
> Each recurrence usually needs to do new work — deepen, complicate, embody, or transform the idea. Want me to show what each recurrence is currently doing in this chapter?

## 15. First founder walk

The first human walk should be deliberately simple:

1. Open a real chapter.
2. Say: "This is what I want this chapter to say..."
3. Confirm MAIA reflects intention before analysis.
4. Ask: "What are you seeing?"
5. Confirm MAIA offers one relevant observation in Intimate mode.
6. Choose "Talk about this" on a noticing.
7. Carry the thread into conversation without losing manuscript context.
8. Say "keep that nearby" and confirm no forced classification occurs.
9. Ask "show me the arc" and verify macro orientation.
10. Return to a passage and verify upstream/downstream continuity.
11. Say "this is central to the whole chapter" and verify MAIA asks before governance.
12. Change to Mapped and verify the full field can be shown intentionally.
13. Return to Intimate and verify the surface becomes quiet again.

Founder acceptance question:

> Does this help me remain in relationship with the work, rather than making me manage the system?
