## Question

Can the Layered Admission candidate be presented for explicit founder ratification without
silently borrowing authority from adjacent candidate material, widening existing constitutional
law, or turning a rollout heuristic into a general feature-flag doctrine?

## Finding

**Yes, after one status-precision amendment.**

The candidate is semantically consistent with the ratified authority laws that actually bear on
its subject. One dependency description was too broad: the document grouped ratified law,
governing design disciplines, and the still-unratified `THE_MEMBERS_WORLD_IS_PRIMARY.md`

No governing status was changed by that amendment.
## Reconciliation against standing law

### Representation Authority — compatible

`REPRESENTATION_AUTHORITY_LAW.md` is ratified canon. It says that presentation,
computation, classification, or table membership do not by themselves create authority.

Layered Admission follows that boundary: a UI layer may be visible, but admission authority
must come from a governed act. The candidate does not create a second authority taxonomy.

### Declared Membership — compatible

`DECLARED_MEMBERSHIP_RATIFICATION_2026-09-16.md` is ratified constitutional law.
Its invariant is that standing comes from explicit admission under a rule and may not be inherited
from location, naming, container membership, or access mode.

Layered Admission is a direct rollout application of that law: experimental standing is explicit,
narrow, fail-closed, and independent from adjacent memberships.

### Constitutional Direction of Authority — compatible

`CONSTITUTIONAL_DIRECTION_OF_AUTHORITY.md` is ratified canon. It forbids the system from
manufacturing higher-order meaning or standing above what the member has authored or accepted.

Layered Admission removes authority rather than adding it: the experimental layer is withheld
unless admitted, and consent after admission remains a separate member act.
### Inhabitable Architecture — compatible, distinct standing

`INHABITABLE_ARCHITECTURE_STANDARD.md` is a governing design principle rather than a
separately ratified constitutional article. Layered Admission uses it as a design constraint:
the base room must remain legible and inhabitable when the experimental layer is absent.

The candidate now names that standing instead of flattening it into “ratified canon.”

### Interface Humility — compatible, distinct standing

`INTERFACE_HUMILITY.md` names an operational discipline already present across MAIA canon.
Layered Admission uses the same relational posture: an experimental interpretation or AI-mediated
act arrives as invitation, not possession.

Again, the candidate now names the source as operational discipline rather than claiming a
ratification status the source does not claim for itself.

### The Member's World is Primary — compatible, but still candidate

`THE_MEMBERS_WORLD_IS_PRIMARY.md` explicitly says it is a founder-authored **canon candidate**
that becomes binding only through an explicit act.

Layered Admission is consistent with it, but no longer says it is subordinate to that document
as though the document were already ratified. Ratifying Layered Admission would **not**
ratify `THE_MEMBERS_WORLD_IS_PRIMARY.md`.
## Scope test

The candidate does **not** say:

- every experimental feature must be decomposed into a smaller gate;
- every feature flag is an admission authority;
- every cohort should share membership;
- presentation is sufficient security;
- admission is consent;
- a member's use of one experiment admits them to another;
- elapsed time or low complaint volume authorizes widening.

Its operative question is narrower:

> **When a new governed layer enters an existing member habitat, where should the admission
> membrane sit?**

The answer remains:

> **At the smallest semantically complete boundary that contains the experimental authority
> while preserving the member's legitimate underlying world.**

That is a constraint on rollout boundary placement, not a mandate to add flags.
