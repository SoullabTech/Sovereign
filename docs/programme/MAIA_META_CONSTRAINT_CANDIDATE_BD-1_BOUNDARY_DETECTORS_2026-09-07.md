# Candidate BD-1 — Boundary Detectors

**Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Status: CANDIDATE — NOT RATIFIED**
**Register: META-CONSTRAINT** · **BINDS: any mechanism or institution created to detect a boundary**

Filed separately from any single Field because its reach is general.

## The rule

> **A boundary detector may not exceed the constitution of the thing it bounds.**

Precise form:

> A boundary detector may not acquire **data, durability, interpretive authority, intervention
> power, or relational authority** that the constitution denies to the system or field it is
> intended to protect, **merely because** those powers would make the boundary easier to detect.

**"Merely because" is load-bearing.** A separately constituted activity may lawfully hold
capabilities the protected field does not — it is not a secretly enlarged detector, but a distinct
activity with its own subject, consent, custody, purpose, duration and withdrawal.

```text
ILLEGAL ESCALATION
  Shadow cannot retain longitudinal turns
  → a "safety monitor" retains them anyway

LAWFUL SEPARATION
  Shadow retains nothing
  + the member separately opts into a constituted study
    with explicit custody, purpose, duration, and withdrawal
```

## The discriminator — refusability without loss

Consent alone does not distinguish the two, because an escalation can be consented to. The test:

> **Declining the separate activity must leave the protected thing intact.**

If a member who refuses the study loses access to the Field, or receives a degraded version of it,
the study was never separately constituted — it is an enlarged detector wearing consent.
**Consent that is priced is not consent.** This is checkable when the "separate" system is
proposed, not only in hindsight.

## Reach

Dependency detection · crisis and safety mechanisms · abuse monitoring · memory safeguards ·
manipulation detection · practitioner oversight · collective-field governance · eventually
alignment mechanisms inside MAIA.

Safety architecture recurrently says *"we need to see more in order to protect you."* BD-1 forces
the prior question:

> **Are we permitted to see that?**

That is the right order.
