## 3 · TEN REPLACEMENTS

### I-1 — PASS

The law is now explicitly bounded to a **member-initiated sovereign destructive act**. A delegated
or administrative act is not prohibited; it must carry a separately governed authority chain.
Caller assertion never substitutes for either self-identity or delegated authority.

This preserves F5-B's subject-binding finding without universalizing actor = subject across all
possible destructive operations.

### I-3 — PASS

The law now distinguishes:

- accidental lexical / structural adjacency — insufficient; from
- a deliberately declared namespace policy whose scope intentionally includes descendants — valid.

The `/api/sovereign` → `/api/sovereignty` coincidence remains condemned without outlawing ordinary
hierarchical access policy.

### I-4 — PASS

The law now protects **trusted system provenance**, which is what D9-B B9 actually tested. It no
longer forbids preserving caller-declared source or author metadata as declared metadata.

The critical invariant remains: caller assertion cannot mint trusted member provenance for
MAIA-authored material.

The B9 fragility note remains attached. R2 does not promote negative space into evidence of a guard.

### I-7 — PASS

The law now protects exactly what the D9/F5 examples protect: existence, ownership and material the
caller lacks authority to know. It simultaneously preserves I-29's requirement that a member
receive the truthful governed reason and change-state for their own act.

Non-disclosure is no longer generic opacity.

### I-10 — PASS

Participation authority must now carry an **explicit executable scope**. Room-, purpose-, consumer-
and deliberately governed wildcard scopes are all representable.

This preserves D9's purpose-travel prohibition without converting "one boolean" into a forbidden
storage type. A compact control passes if its governed semantics actually resolve the relevant
scope.

### I-16 — PASS

The law now distinguishes object/version-bound authorizations from class-, relationship- and
audience-scoped warrants.

Exact wording cannot silently travel under an exact-object warrant. At the same time, the D9-C
Circles warrant is no longer contradicted merely because it lives on member × audience and can
govern multiple in-scope objects.

Strengthening epistemic belief still never enlarges a warrant.

### I-20 — PASS

The protected interest is now the **other sovereign's independently governed record or stake**, not
a hard-coded choice between refusal and tombstone.

F5-A's observed mechanisms remain valid positive specimens. Other explicit dispositions can pass
only if they preserve the second sovereign's governed interest and state truthfully what changed.

### I-25 — PASS

The law now requires storage-sufficient evidence of erasure completion rather than a literal
filesystem-style re-read everywhere.

`destroyVaultBytes` remains the positive specimen. Transactional evidence, governed verification,
or a durable owed-erasure state can satisfy the same constitutional obligation where appropriate.
A successful invocation alone cannot.

### I-26 — PASS

Lineage is now required when the derivative remains **source-dependent for governance**. Explicit
anonymization or de-linking is permitted as a governed disposition, but the resulting derivative
may not later be represented as source-traceable.

This preserves F5-C's lineage finding without contradicting Standing Constraint 1.2's recognition
of anonymized and transformed dispositions.

### I-32 — PASS

Manifestability now consists of governed plan + recorded execution + governed verification.
Verification reads are allowed when they are part of the governed mechanism.

What remains forbidden is exactly what F5 earned: reconstructing an unaccounted act after the fact
from incidental FK or final-state behavior and calling that reconstruction governance.
