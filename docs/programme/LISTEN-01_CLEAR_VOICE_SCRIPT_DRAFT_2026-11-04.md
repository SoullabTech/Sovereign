## Conventions

`[pause Ns]` is silence with the bed continuing. Speak slowly; about 110 words a minute, less than ordinary speech. Roughly 900 spoken words across 23 minutes; most of the time is silence.

---

## Arrive (0:00–3:00)

Welcome. This is a short practice for the minutes before a conversation that matters to you. It is not therapy and it is not advice. You can stop at any point, and you can pause it whenever you like.

[pause 6s]

Find a way of sitting or standing that you can stay in. Let your feet, or your back, be held by what is under them.

[pause 8s]

Notice the room. Notice one sound you can hear, near or far. You don't have to do anything about it.

[pause 10s]

Notice your breathing without changing it. If it wants to change by itself, let it.

[pause 15s]

There is nothing to get right. For now, only arriving.

[pause 45s]

If your mind has already gone to the conversation, that's fine. It will be welcome here shortly. For now, let it rest where it is.

[pause 60s]

## Natural voice (3:00–7:00)

In a moment you may speak out loud, quietly, if that is possible where you are. If it isn't, speak inside, at the same pace.

[pause 6s]

Say your own name, slowly enough to hear it.

[pause 15s]

Say one ordinary sentence about where you are right now. For example: "I'm sitting by the window, and it's late afternoon."

[pause 20s]

Hear how your voice sounds. Not how it should sound; how it does.

[pause 25s]

Say it again, a little slower. Let the sentence finish before the next breath.

[pause 30s]

You are not performing. This is simply your voice, in the room, at its own speed.

[pause 90s]

## Retrieval (7:00–15:30)

Now bring to mind the conversation. Not the whole of it. Only the reason it matters to you.

[pause 20s]

Think of one time you did something like this well. It doesn't need to be large. A time you explained something, or listened, or told the truth about something difficult.

[pause 40s]

Where were you? Who was there? What did you do, in plain terms?

[pause 60s]

Notice what you already know how to do. It has been there all along; you aren't inventing it now.

[pause 45s]

Now let one thing you want the other person to understand come to you. One thing. If more than one arrives, let them wait.

[pause 60s]

Say that one thing in your own words, in a single sentence. If it takes two sentences, that is fine too.

[pause 45s]

Is there something you don't know the answer to? You can allow yourself to say so. "I'd want to think about that" is a complete sentence.

[pause 60s]

Let the sentence come back to you, as it is. Nothing needs to be polished.

[pause 90s]

## Interview rhythm (15:30–21:00)

This part is a rhythm to practise: hear, pause, find, speak, stop. I will say each word, and you take the time you need. Nothing here has a right pace except yours.

[pause 8s]

Hear. Imagine the other person asking a question. Let the whole question arrive before you reply.

[pause 20s]

Pause. One breath, before answering. The pause belongs to you; it is not an error.

[pause 15s]

Find. Look for the thing you actually know, or the true thing you can say. It might be small.

[pause 25s]

Speak. Say it, plainly, in the voice you found earlier.

[pause 20s]

Stop. When you have said it, stop. You don't need to fill the silence. Let the other person take it.

[pause 25s]

Again. Hear.

[pause 12s]

Pause.

[pause 10s]

Find.

[pause 18s]

Speak.

[pause 18s]

Stop.

[pause 30s]

Once more, in your own time, without my words. I'll be quiet.

[pause 90s]

## Return (21:00–23:00)

You can let that go now. Let your attention come back to the room, to the sounds you noticed at the start.

[pause 15s]

Feel the weight of your body where it is held.

[pause 20s]

Whatever the conversation brings, you have your own voice, your own pace, and the right to pause. These were available before this practice, and they remain with you after it.

[pause 20s]

When you're ready, you can stop. If you'd like to continue sitting for a while, the music will go on.

[pause 25s]

Thank you for taking the time.

## Review notes

- **Not claimed:** that this reduces anxiety, improves interview results or builds confidence. It invites a practice; effect is not evidenced.
- **Safety framing:** includes stop/pause permission, "not therapy", and no instruction to recall distressing events (the retrieval prompt asks for a time something went *well*). A clinician or qualified reviewer should read it before any member sees it. No crisis path is built in; the listener is not asked about their wellbeing.
- **Cultural/language care (Invariant 14):** uses plain words ("voice", "pace"), no framework vocabulary and no assumption about what "confidence" means; the examples are neutral.
- **Length:** the arc is timed to the spec; real timing depends on the speaker and needs a read-through with a stopwatch.
- **Unknown:** how it sounds spoken, whether the silences feel right, whether 90-second silences are too long for a first-time listener. Needs a human read.
