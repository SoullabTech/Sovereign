# 1 · Clarification A — Composite warrant formation

## 1.1 Governing distinction

The following remains forbidden:

> **MERE ACCUMULATION IS NOT A WARRANT.**

The following facts do not, by themselves, create relation standing:

- source count;
- repeated agreement;
- source prestige;
- citation density;
- apparent convergence;
- retrieval frequency;
- compatibility;
- absence of contradiction;
- model confidence;
- an instruction to "synthesize";
- several individually insufficient sources appearing together.

Formally:

`partial(A) + partial(B) + partial(C) ≠ warranted(R)`

unless there exists a **new, separately governed composite inference** whose own standing licenses `R`.

## 1.2 Composite warrant definition

A **composite warrant** is a newly authored epistemic object whose support set contains multiple source propositions and whose own method of composition is declared and governed.

A composite warrant is not identical to its support set.

It must preserve, at minimum:

- **warrant proposition** — the relation or inferential claim the composite warrant supports;
- **author / authority** — who or what authors the composite inference;
- **support set** — the source propositions materially relied upon;
- **support provenance** — where each relied-upon source comes from;
- **composition method** — how the support set is combined into the warrant candidate;
- **dependence assumptions** — what independence, dependence, redundancy, shared-origin, or correlation assumptions matter;
- **jurisdiction** — the epistemic domain in which the composite inference has authority;
- **uncertainty** — unresolved alternatives, conflict, precision limits, or model assumptions;
- **operative boundaries / invalidity conditions**;
- **relation semantics licensed** — e.g. association, conflict, explanatory fit, causation, direction, equivalence;
- **standing** — candidate, provisional, warranted, promoted, discharged, or superseded as allowed by ACT 11.

## 1.3 Formation law

Multiple partial sources may become the **support set** of a composite warrant candidate.

They do not become a warranted relation merely because they are numerous, prestigious, mutually compatible, or apparently convergent.

The lawful sequence is:

`PARTIAL SOURCES`

→ `SEPARATELY AUTHORED COMPOSITE INFERENCE`

→ `COMPOSITE WARRANT CANDIDATE`

→ `EVALUATE METHOD + SUPPORT + BOUNDARIES + JURISDICTION`

→ `ASSIGN NO STRONGER STANDING THAN THAT EVALUATION EARNS`

The composite inference therefore has its own provenance and standing.

## 1.4 Standing ceiling for composite warrants

A composite warrant may not exceed:

- the inferential force actually licensed by its method;
- the support quality of the relied-upon evidence;
- the limits introduced by dependence among sources;
- unresolved contradictory evidence;
- the jurisdiction of the composite method;
- any operative boundary or invalidity condition.

If source dependence is materially unresolved, the composite warrant may not be treated as if the sources were independent.

If the composition method supports correlation, it does not thereby support causation.

If the method supports explanatory coherence, it does not thereby establish empirical truth.

## 1.5 Anti-laundering law

A future representation or mechanism fails ACT 11A if it can transform:

`N sources that individually do not warrant R`

into:

`R is warranted`

without making the intervening composite inference independently inspectable as an epistemic object.

The number or prestige of premises never substitutes for provenance of the **therefore**.

## 1.6 Legitimate triangulation remains possible

ACT 11A must not overcorrect into:

> "No relation may ever be warranted by multiple individually partial sources."

A composite warrant may become warranted if its declared composition method, support set, assumptions, jurisdiction, uncertainty, and boundaries collectively justify that standing.

ACT 11A does not prescribe one universal inferential method.

It requires that the method and resulting warrant remain explicit and governable.

# 2 · Clarification B — Jurisdiction-bounded adoption

## 2.1 Governing rule

> **ADOPTION MAY CHANGE AUTHORSHIP OR STANDING ONLY WITHIN THE ADOPTER'S EPISTEMIC JURISDICTION.**

Human or member adoption is powerful but not jurisdiction-free.

Adoption does not automatically warrant every semantic component contained in the proposition being adopted.

## 2.2 Strong member authority preserved

A member has primary authority over, at minimum:

- their own lived experience;
- how an experience feels;
- personal meaning;
- values;
- preferences;
- personal interpretations;
- intentions they themselves hold;
- memories as remembered and reported;
- relationships as personally experienced;
- whether a proposed interpretation resonates, does not resonate, or should remain open.

Explicit member adoption may create or strengthen **member-authored / member-confirmed** standing for those components.

## 2.3 External factual claims remain separately governed

Member or human adoption alone does not establish as fact:

- another person's unexpressed motive;
- another person's internal intention;
- another person's diagnosis or mental state;
- hidden causal mechanisms;
- scientific claims;
- historical claims;
- metaphysical claims;
- universal psychological laws;
- facts outside the adopter's direct epistemic authority.

Those components retain whatever standing their own evidence and warrants support.

## 2.4 Mixed-proposition law

When a proposed proposition combines semantic components from different epistemic jurisdictions, those components must remain distinguishable enough that standing can differ among them.

Example proposed proposition:

> "Your partner's silence means they are withholding affection to control you."

This contains at least:

1. **member-experience component:** the silence was experienced by the member;
2. **member-meaning component:** the silence feels withholding or controlling to the member;
3. **external motive component:** the partner intended to withhold affection;
4. **external causal/intention component:** the partner acted in order to control the member.

If the member says:

> "Yes, that's exactly what it means to me."

the member may adopt components 1–2 within the member's lived-meaning jurisdiction.

That confirmation alone does not establish components 3–4 as external facts.

## 2.5 Adoption provenance

The history must preserve:

- the original proposer;
- the original proposition;
- which semantic component(s) the adopter actually adopted;
- the adopter's authority / jurisdiction;
- what standing changed;
- what standing did not change;
- any unresolved external component.

A later summary may not rewrite:

`MAIA_PROPOSED → MEMBER_CONFIRMED_WITHIN_SCOPE`

as:

`MEMBER_AUTHORED_EXTERNAL_FACT`.

## 2.6 Practitioner adoption

If a practitioner interpretation is later adopted by the member:

- the member may adopt its personal meaning / resonance;
- the practitioner's original authorship remains recoverable;
- external factual, causal, diagnostic, or scientific components require their own warrant;
- member adoption does not retroactively make the entire practitioner proposition member-authored from inception.

## 2.7 Decomposition requirement is conceptual, not mechanistic

ACT 11A does not authorize or require a parser, classifier, schema, or graph decomposition mechanism.

It establishes only the constitutional requirement:

> **Where different semantic components carry different jurisdiction or standing, the contract must not force them into one undifferentiated standing.**

A future implementation must satisfy that law by separately authorized means.

# 4 · ACT 11A invariants

## A11A-INV-01 · Composite separability

A multi-source support set is distinguishable from the composite inference made from it.

## A11A-INV-02 · Composite authorship

The composite inference has an author / authority distinct from the authors of its support sources.

## A11A-INV-03 · Composite provenance

The composition method and relied-upon support set remain recoverable.

## A11A-INV-04 · Composite standing ceiling

The composite warrant cannot outrun its method, evidence, dependence assumptions, jurisdiction, uncertainty, or boundaries.

## A11A-INV-05 · No prestige/count laundering

Source number, prestige, repetition, or agreement alone cannot create warrant standing.

## A11A-INV-06 · Triangulation viability

The law must permit a genuinely governed composite inference to earn standing; it must not equate all multi-source inference with forbidden accumulation.

## A11A-INV-07 · Adoption scope

Adoption changes standing only for components within the adopter's epistemic jurisdiction.

## A11A-INV-08 · Mixed-standing preservation

A mixed proposition may carry different standing across semantic components.

## A11A-INV-09 · External-fact independence

External factual, causal, motive, diagnostic, scientific, historical, or metaphysical claims retain independent warrant requirements after adoption.

## A11A-INV-10 · Adoption provenance

Proposal and adoption history remain recoverable and cannot be rewritten as retroactive whole-proposition authorship.

# 5 · Falsifiers

ACT 11A fails if any of the following can remain contract-compliant:

1. three partial sources become a warranted relation solely because there are three of them;
2. prestigious sources gain a relation warrant by prestige aggregation;
3. source agreement is treated as sufficient composition method;
4. a composite inference has no declared author or method;
5. unresolved source dependence is silently treated as independence;
6. a correlation-capable method is allowed to license causation;
7. all multi-source inference is forbidden merely because no single source independently warrants the relation;
8. member confirmation of personal meaning is blocked despite being within member authority;
9. member confirmation of a mixed proposition promotes another person's hidden motive to fact;
10. member confirmation promotes scientific or diagnostic components without independent warrant;
11. practitioner-authored interpretation becomes member-authored in full merely because the member finds it resonant;
12. mixed proposition components are forced into one undifferentiated standing;
13. adoption provenance loses the original proposer;
14. the adopter is treated as having authority over proposition components outside their epistemic jurisdiction.

Any such result falsifies the amendment.
