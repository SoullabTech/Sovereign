> **May MAIA form an uninvited inference about a member before the member participates in
> its meaning — and, if so, what ontological and operational standing may that inference

| # | Act | Question |

### ⭐ What the lattice reveals

**(i) Acts 1 and 6 are NOT in collision.** All three sources agree perception is permitted
and that member adoption is the legitimate route to standing. **The founder's suspicion is
confirmed by the texts: neither extreme is anyone's actual position.** Extreme A ("MAIA must
never perceive what the member has not named") is held by **no source**. Extreme B ("may
privately conclude anything if disclosure is tactful") is held by **no source** either —
B's §I.4 carries a hard prohibition on treating pattern recognition as more valid than
member experience.

**(ii) ⭐⭐ Act 3 — "act from it" — is SILENT IN BOTH NAMED SOURCES.** The collision as
originally framed is a *disclosure* argument. Neither named document says whether an
unconfirmed inference may shape the next response. **Only the uncited third document
addresses it, and prohibits it.** This is the cell where a training corpus would encode
behavior with no governing rule at all — and it is invisible in the output text, so no
benchmark built from the two named sources would test it.

**(iii) The surviving collision is narrower than §3 of ACT 1 implied.** After C is admitted
the live disagreement is **Act 4 (disclose)**, and specifically: *may a system-originated
pattern be offered to the member as a question?* B says yes and treats it as the core value
proposition. C says no for system-inferred developmental patterns. A bars it upstream at
formation.

**(iv) ⚠️ A SECOND, SUBTLER TENSION — between A and C, not A and B.** A: *"a diagnostic
label that the system silently holds but never displays has already desecrated"* — formation
itself is the violation. C: *"the structural data may exist for the system's own
functioning."* **A forbids what C carves out.** C's carve-out is narrow (it names I Ching
mappings as the motivating case), but it is a genuine seam, and **it is precisely the seam
the founder's candidate resolution would need to widen.** ⛔ Not resolved here.

## 5. The founder's candidate resolution, tested against the founder's own falsifier

Founder's falsifier:

> **If the ruling can be satisfied merely by hiding an inference from the member, it has
> failed Side A. If the ruling prevents MAIA from perceiving relational information that has
> not already been explicitly named, it has failed Side B.**

Applying it to the candidate lifecycle (perception → ephemeral possibility → no standing, no
durable formation, no silent promotion, no consequential action presuming truth → possibly
offered as explicitly provisional → member encounters/rejects/revises/adopts → only then
standing changes):

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Satisfiable by hiding? | **No.** It bars persistence, promotion, and consequential use — the three things hiding would otherwise permit. Hiding an inference still violates Acts 3/5/6. |
| Prevents unnamed perception? | **No.** Act 1 is permitted outright. |

⭐ **The candidate passes both arms.** ⚠️ **And passing is not adoption** — three things the
candidate does not yet settle, which the ruling will have to:

1. **Where "ephemeral" ends.** A possibility held across a single turn, a session, or a
   thread are three different persistence claims. The candidate says "ephemeral"; the acts
   need a boundary.
2. **Whether Act 3 is fully barred or conditionally permitted.** "No consequential action
   that presumes truth" leaves open whether an inference may shape *tone, pacing, or what
   MAIA asks next* without presuming truth. ⭐ **That is not a corner case — it is most of
   what relational intelligence actually does**, and it is the silent cell from §3(ii).
3. **Whether A's "formation itself desecrates" survives.** The candidate permits formation
   as ephemeral possibility. A, read strictly, does not. **The ruling either narrows A or
   admits C's carve-out.** It cannot leave both standing as written.
