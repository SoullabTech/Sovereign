## 2. Adjudicated meaning

The founder ratifies the following as a **canonical corollary of the Representation Authority Law**:

> **GRANT–EFFECT BOUNDARY**
>
> **Authority existence ≠ authority consumption ≠ authority sufficiency.**
>
> A governed effect may exercise only authority it actually consumes from a governed grant sufficient for that effect, and it may not exceed the scope of that grant.

The corollary sharpens the existing constitutional sequence:

`SUBSTITUTION → GRANT → ATTESTATION`

by making explicit what must remain true after a grant exists:

1. **existence** — a governed grant can actually be named;
2. **consumption** — the specific governed effect actually consumes that authority rather than merely coexisting with it;
3. **sufficiency** — the consumed grant and its required attestation are sufficient for that exact effect;
4. **scope** — the effect does not silently widen the grant into a stronger or different authority.

---

## 3. Standing acquired

The Grant–Effect Boundary now has constitutional standing beneath the existing Representation Authority Law.

It may therefore be used to test future authority-bearing effects by asking:

- does the required authority exist?
- does this effect actually consume it?
- is that authority sufficient for this exact effect?
- does the effect remain inside scope?

The corollary does **not** replace `SUBSTITUTION → GRANT → ATTESTATION`; it makes the post-GRANT boundary explicit.

A shared constitutional invariant does not imply a shared implementation.

## 5. Retained defeater

The semantic-drift defeater remains live:

> if a future seam fits only by changing what **grant**, **effect**, **consumption**, **sufficiency**, or **scope** mean, the corollary must narrow or fail.

The existing Representation Authority Law defeaters also remain live.

This ruling therefore promotes the invariant without making it unfalsifiable.
