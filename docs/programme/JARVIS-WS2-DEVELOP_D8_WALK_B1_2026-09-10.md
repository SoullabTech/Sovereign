## Watch 1 · Is that complete, or does the architecture solicit articulation?

```text
BAD B    "What specifically feels off?"
         "Are you concerned about pacing, clarity, or structure?"
GOOD B   the sentence is already enough to organize the exchange
```

⭐ **Finding B/M1-1 — felt difficulty is not a malformed question. It is a different kind of question.**

*"Something feels off here"* is **complete**, and its implicit object is *what do you see?* The writer has said: **I sense something; you look.** That delegates the naming — which is a fully-formed inquiry, not an underspecified one.

**Treating it as incomplete is a category error**, and it is the error B is most likely to make while appearing helpful. **B passes watch 1 only if MAIA answers rather than clarifies.**

## Watch 3 · Does "here" remain real context without becoming the organizer?

⭐ **Finding B/M1-2 — B keeps the free locus without paying A's price.**

The writer was standing at 15 when they spoke. **B inherits that locus as evidence**, exactly as A did — the "here" is real and costs nothing. But in B the locus is **context, not identity**.

```text
A     identity = place        -> "here" organizes, and the finding fragments
B     identity = inquiry      -> "here" contextualizes, and the finding holds
```

**B does not have to choose between the writer's language and where they said it.** Both are present; only one organizes.

⭐ *"I see **one thing** that may be contributing"* does two things at once: it **states the cardinality** — this is one finding, not the first of several — and it **marks provisionality** without a disclaimer block. **The count and the hedge are carried by ordinary speech**, which is §12.2's whole argument in one sentence.
