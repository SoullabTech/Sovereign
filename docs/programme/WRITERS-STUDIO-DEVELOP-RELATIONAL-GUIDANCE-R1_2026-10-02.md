# WRITERS-STUDIO-DEVELOP-RELATIONAL-GUIDANCE-R1

**Opened:** 2026-10-02 from founder witness in local Writer’s Studio Develop.

## Terminal claim

Develop should feel like working with MAIA inside a manuscript, not reading internal analysis machinery. Whole-manuscript orientation belongs near the beginning. Selecting a chapter or section should immediately produce choices a writer can understand. A saved developmental observation may remain frozen, but the writer-facing encounter should translate it into ordinary language and offer an actual relationship with MAIA.

## Founder witness

Observed defects:
- whole-manuscript review was buried below lineage and other secondary material;
- selecting a chapter/section exposed lens names before a useful relational choice;
- saved observations surfaced taxonomy/report language such as structural or recurrence jargon;
- moving Next changed the center card while MAIA’s rail did not visibly reorient to the new observation;
- night-mode cards could fall through to dark/native-looking text;
- the existing guided editorial communication law was present in the repository but not wired into developmental observation dialogue.

## Laws

1. MAIA carries the sophistication; the writer should not have to.
2. Frozen evidence remains frozen. Translation changes presentation and conversation, never the observation’s evidence or authority.
3. Conversation is not an implicit reread. New manuscript reading still requires an explicit Read act.
4. A chapter/section selection changes relational place immediately; it does not silently commission cognition.
5. Technical vocabulary is available on request, not required for first contact.
6. Whole-manuscript attention is an entry path, not an afterthought.
