# JARVIS-MAIA-FREE-SYNTHESIS-01 — GESTALT CONTINUITY INVARIANT

**Status:** R&D acceptance invariant · NO implementation authority
**Programme:** `JARVIS-MAIA-FREE-SYNTHESIS-01`
**Date:** 2026-09-16

## Governing invariant

> At every turn, MAIA must encounter the present utterance in relation to the living organization of the conversation as a whole — not merely the most recent exchanges.
>
> That whole must remain dynamically revisable as new evidence arrives.
>
> No fixed recency aperture, retrieval result, summary, or prior MAIA interpretation may become the sole representation of what the conversation means.

The gestalt is **not evidence**. It is a revisable projection over evidence.

`Surprise outranks continuity`: new member-origin evidence that disturbs the current gestalt must trigger reconsideration rather than defense of the prior synthesis.

This record adds an R&D/falsification criterion only. It authorizes no prompt, memory, schema, routing, aperture, validator, model, or production change.

## Required perceptual dimensions

A candidate gestalt representation must be capable of preserving, without forced closure:
- active inquiries;
- motifs and recurrence;
- unresolved tensions;
- transformations in meaning;
- commitments and decisions;
- temporal arc;
- relational movement;
- affective/elemental field with uncertainty;
- contradiction;
- open loops;
- salience;
- epistemic standing;
- evidence lineage.

## Three simultaneous horizons

A candidate architecture must support attention across:
- **Immediate** — what is happening now;
- **Session gestalt** — what this encounter has become;
- **Longitudinal gestalt** — patterns, inquiries, relationships and transformations extending across encounters.

The present utterance may change the relative attention among these horizons. No horizon is authorized to erase the others.

## Dynamic reconfiguration

A candidate gestalt must be able to reorganize when later evidence reveals a different center of gravity. Earlier organization may remain historically intelligible without retaining current authority.

The geometry may persist; the turn-specific view of that geometry must remain revisable.

## Cross-cutting replay falsifiers

Free-Synthesis replay must include cases where:
1. an early consequential moment returns much later;
2. a motif recurs under different language;
3. the member reverses an earlier belief;
4. incompatible truths remain unresolved across many turns;
5. a MAIA interpretation is not member-validated;
6. a minor statement later becomes central;
7. a long digression does not erase the deeper inquiry;
8. relational/affective character changes while explicit topic remains stable.

Blind adjudication must assess whole-conversation coherence, recurrence recognition, temporal sensitivity, tension preservation, shift recognition, epistemic discipline, relational appropriateness, and capacity to reorganize — not merely prose quality.
