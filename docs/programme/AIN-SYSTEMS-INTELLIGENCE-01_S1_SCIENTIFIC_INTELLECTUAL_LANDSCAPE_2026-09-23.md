# 0 · Executive finding

S1 finds that **"systems thinking" as a popular category is not one tradition but at least fourteen, which share a small vocabulary and disagree about what most of it means.** The faculty S0 constituted cannot be grounded in the popular category; it can be grounded in the specific traditions, provided the disagreements are carried rather than smoothed.

The strongest defensible statement is:

> Several mature traditions independently treat organization, interaction and change in wholes through feedback, self-organization, nested levels, attractors, thresholds, boundaries and the observer's participation. They make systems perception a plausible object of inquiry. They do not converge on one meaning of "system," "boundary," "feedback," "emergence," "self-organization," "observer" or "information," and the two traditions closest to S0's anti-control law (second-order cybernetics, autopoiesis/enactivism) are in direct disagreement with the two traditions that supply the most operational vocabulary (first-order cybernetics, system dynamics) about whether a system is something one regulates.

Four findings shape everything downstream:

1. **The disagreement is not lexical noise; it is the substance.** The traditions split on control versus autonomy, observer-independent versus observer-inclusive description, reduction versus relational causality, and description versus intervention (§4). S0's anti-control law is not an external ethic imposed on the science; it is one side of the field's own oldest argument (Bateson S07, von Foerster S05, Maturana S19 against Wiener S02, Ashby S03, Beer S39).

2. **The popular category is intervention-inclusive by its own consolidated definition** (Arnold & Wade S52: "devising modifications to produce desired effects"). S0 §III's claim that SI is not the popular category is therefore confirmed by the category's own literature, not merely asserted.

3. **Evidentiary standing is highly uneven across the eight primitive acts** (§5). TRANSFORM has the best-characterized evidence base and the best-characterized limits (S27–S29). EMERGE has a usable operational definition (Bedau S45) and a serious philosophical objection to any causal reading (Kim S47). BOUND has a well-developed normative literature (S36–S38) and no empirical one. REFLECT has an intellectual lineage and, as S0 anticipated, no mechanical evidence base at all.

4. **The one empirical literature on machines perceiving systems structure measures positive cases only.** Language-model extraction of causal loop diagrams from text reaches roughly 60% of links and loops (S49) and 77–89% pass rates on a purpose-built leaderboard (S51), but as far as S1 read, no reported evaluation includes a text with no warranted feedback structure. The human literature does include the null case, and humans fail it (S33, S35). F-SI8's mandatory null fixture therefore has no precedent in the machine literature and a strong precedent in the human one.

S1 establishes none of the following: that the faculty exists, that it is distinct from RGR in practice, that any tradition is "correct," or anything about Spiralogic.

# 2 · Lineage map by tradition

Each tradition is recorded under the same four heads: what exists; what it contributes to systems perception; its constraint; its standing for SI. "Standing for SI" is a judgement about *usefulness to a faculty constituted under S0*, not about the tradition's truth.

## 2.1 General systems theory

### What exists
Bertalanffy's programme (S01): systems as wholes of interacting parts; open systems that maintain themselves by exchange; equifinality (the same end state from different initial conditions); and the claim that general principles hold across kinds of system.

### Contribution
"System" as a first-class object of inquiry. Open-system maintenance is the ancestor of every later account of a whole that persists by exchange rather than by isolation.

### Constraint
The isomorphism claim is programmatic. The tradition offers few results and many examples; its vocabulary is inherited by everyone and defined precisely by almost no one.

### SI standing
**High historical relevance; low operational yield.** The tradition names the object; it does not tell a faculty how to perceive it.

Sources: S01.

---

## 2.2 First-order cybernetics and information

### What exists
Wiener (S02): feedback, negative feedback, oscillation, stability, control and communication as one science. Ashby (S03): variety, regulation, requisite variety, and a formal treatment of what a regulator must be relative to what it regulates. Shannon (S04): information as a measure over probabilities, explicitly indifferent to meaning.

### Contribution
The precise, original meaning of FEEDBACK (a loop in which output re-enters as input and alters subsequent output), of REGULATION, and of one meaning of INFORMATION. Requisite variety is the first formal constraint linking a perceiver's internal variety to what it can track.

### Constraint
The frame is regulation. The observer stands outside the system with a goal; the system is what is regulated. Applied to a person, this is exactly the chain S0 §VII.1 prohibits. The concepts survive the prohibition; the posture does not.

### SI standing
**Highest operational yield for TRACE; the posture is a named hazard.** S0's inheritance of the concept while refusing the stance is the correct relation to this tradition.

Sources: S02, S03, S04.

---

## 2.3 Second-order cybernetics and the ecology of mind

### What exists
Von Foerster (S05, S06): the cybernetics of observing systems as distinct from observed systems; the observer's description as the observer's responsibility. Bateson (S07, S08): information as a difference that makes a difference; mind as a property of circuits that may extend beyond the skin; the double bind; and, in "Conscious Purpose versus Nature," the argument that purposive intervention in living systems, ignoring the circuits it is part of, is itself pathological.

### Contribution
The observer inside the system; REFLECT's lineage. The second meaning of INFORMATION, which is relational and meaning-bearing. And the earliest explicit statement in the lineage that the control posture is a systemic error rather than merely an ethical one.

### Constraint
Programmatic and essayistic. The tradition operationalizes almost nothing and never intended to; its value is a stance and a set of distinctions, not a method. It also has a constructivist wing that, taken strongly, makes every systems claim observer-relative to the point of unfalsifiability, which is in tension with S0 §X.

### SI standing
**Constitutionally central; operationally thin.** This tradition is where S0 §VII.3 and REFLECT come from, and it is the reason S0 could say that MAIA is a participant inside the field. It supplies no falsifier.

Sources: S05, S06, S07, S08.

---

## 2.4 Dynamical systems

### What exists
The mathematics of change over time (S09): state space, trajectories, fixed points, limit cycles, attractors, basins, bifurcations, sensitivity to initial conditions.

### Contribution
The only precise meanings in the lineage of ATTRACTOR (a set toward which trajectories converge under the dynamics) and of a THRESHOLD (a bifurcation, where a small change in a parameter changes the qualitative structure of the dynamics). Everything S0 §IX says about PATTERN and TRANSFORM presupposes these meanings.

### Constraint
Requires identified state variables and either equations or adequate time series. It says nothing about how to identify state variables in a manuscript, a team or a life, and it is silent on meaning. A "recurring organization" in a text is not an attractor unless something like a state and a dynamics have been defined.

### SI standing
**The evidentiary backbone for PATTERN and TRANSFORM; a hard constraint on when those words may be used at all.**

Sources: S09.

---

## 2.5 Self-organization far from equilibrium

### What exists
Nicolis & Prigogine (S10): dissipative structures; order through fluctuations in open systems driven far from equilibrium. Prigogine & Stengers (S11): the philosophical extension. Haken (S12): synergetics; order parameters, control parameters, the slaving principle, circular causality between macro-pattern and micro-components.

### Contribution
The physical meaning of SELF-ORGANIZATION and, in Haken, the most formal account of how a whole-level variable can constrain its parts while remaining generated by them. Circular causality is the physicist's version of a whole that is both product and constraint of its components.

### Constraint
Both accounts are regime-specific (near instabilities, with definable thermodynamic or order-parameter quantities). The popular extension (S11) is where "self-organization" became a word applied to anything that seems to organize itself; that usage carries no evidence.

### SI standing
**High for the meaning of SELF-ORGANIZATION and for SCALE-EMERGE coupling; low direct applicability outside physical systems without an explicit analogy declared as such.**

Sources: S10, S11, S12.

---

## 2.6 Complexity science

### What exists
Kauffman (S13, S14): self-organization in Boolean networks; order at the edge of chaos; self-organization as a source of biological order alongside selection. Holland (S15): complex adaptive systems and their properties and mechanisms, including internal models. Bak (S16): self-organized criticality and power-law statistics. Simon (S17): hierarchy and near-decomposability as the architecture of complex systems. Mitchell (S18): a survey candid that "complexity" has no agreed definition or measure.

### Contribution
A second, computational meaning of SELF-ORGANIZATION (S13), a third (criticality, S16), and the most precise account of LEVELS in the lineage (S17): levels are real where interactions within subsystems are much stronger than between them, and this is measurable. Holland's "internal models" is a system's own anticipation, which connects to Rosen (§2.8) and to REFLECT.

### Constraint
Model results with contested biological interpretation (S13); universality claims that have not held generally (S16); and a field whose central term is unsettled (S18). Power laws and "edge of chaos" are the two most over-read patterns in the entire lineage.

### SI standing
**High for SCALE (Simon) and for the meaning of a discrete attractor (Kauffman); a standing caution against pattern over-reading.**

Sources: S13, S14, S15, S16, S17, S18.

---

## 2.7 Autopoiesis and its transpositions

### What exists
Maturana & Varela (S19, S20): a living system is a network of processes that produces the components that produce the network, and thereby its own boundary; organization versus structure; operational closure; structural coupling; the observer as a living system describing living systems. Luhmann (S21): the same words transposed to social systems as closed systems of communication, with persons as environment, not components. Mingers (S22): a systematic argument that the transposition fails.

### Contribution
A definition of "living" by organization rather than by substance; a boundary produced by the system rather than drawn by the observer; and the clearest demonstration in the lineage that a single term (autopoiesis, closure, boundary) can carry two incompatible meanings in two traditions that cite each other.

### Constraint
Definitional theory with contested empirical content; excludes representation by design, which sits awkwardly with any faculty that models. The Luhmann transposition is a live disagreement (S22), not a settled extension, and it carries a consequence S0 should notice: on Luhmann's account the person is *never* inside the social system, which is one available reading of why SC-2 and SC-4 are different subject classes.

### SI standing
**High for the meaning of BOUNDARY (as self-produced) and for the observer-inside stance; medium as a source of testable structure; a first-class example for the disagreement map.**

Sources: S19, S20, S21, S22.

---

## 2.8 Relational biology and anticipation

### What exists
Rosen (S23): an anticipatory system contains a model of itself and/or its environment and changes state in accord with that model's predictions of a later instant. Rosen (S24): relational biology; complexity as non-fragmentability (no largest model); organisms as closed to efficient causation; a formal argument that organisms are not mechanisms.

### Contribution
The strongest formal statement in the lineage of "a living relational system" as an object distinct from a machine, and a precise notion of a system that includes its own model. Anticipation is the one concept in the lineage that makes a system's relation to its own future part of its present organization.

### Constraint
The central impossibility claims are disputed, and the category-theoretic machinery has been challenged. Rosen is the tradition most likely to be cited for its conclusions rather than its arguments.

### SI standing
**High conceptual relevance to the programme's motivating phrase; contested formal standing; ⛔ never to be cited as established.**

Sources: S23, S24.

---

## 2.9 Ecological systems and critical transitions

### What exists
Holling (S25): resilience as the capacity to absorb disturbance and persist, distinct from stability as return to equilibrium; multiple stable states. Gunderson & Holling (S26): the adaptive cycle and nested cycles across scales. Scheffer et al. (S27): generic early-warning signals before critical transitions. Dakos et al. (S28): a methods toolbox validated on simulated series with known ground truth. Boettiger & Hastings (S29): proposed indicators rarely characterize error rates; false alarms and missed warnings must be quantified.

### Contribution
The best-developed evidentiary basis in the lineage for TRANSFORM: threshold approach can be detected from time series, the methods have been validated on synthetic fixtures with known transitions, and the limits of detection have been quantified by the same community. This is the closest existing precedent to the S0 §X discipline (synthetic fixture with known ground truth; falsifier characterizes its own error rate).

### Constraint
Requires long, well-sampled time series; the signals are generic and therefore ambiguous; S29 is explicit that a detection without a characterized error rate is not evidence. The adaptive cycle (S26) is a heuristic schema, and it is the schema most likely to be mapped prematurely onto a four-phase model; it is held for S6 and not used here.

### SI standing
**Highest evidentiary standing of any tradition for a single primitive act (TRANSFORM), with the limits already stated by the tradition itself.**

Sources: S25, S26, S27, S28, S29.

## 2.10 System dynamics

### What exists
Forrester (S30): stocks, flows, delays and feedback loops as a simulation substrate. Meadows (S31, S32): leverage points; the primer that made the vocabulary public. Sterman (S33, S34): experimental evidence of systematic misperception of feedback and delay; the reference text on modelling and validation. Cronin, Gonzalez & Sterman (S35): stock–flow failure persists in highly educated subjects.

### Contribution
The operational meanings of STOCK, FLOW, DELAY and LOOP that the causal-loop diagram literature (§2.18) inherits; and, critically, the only rigorous human baseline in the lineage. People who are fluent and educated fail simple dynamics tasks. This is the human analogue of F-SI8: fluency is not perception.

### Constraint
The frame is modelling for intervention (S31 is literally a ranking of places to intervene). Model validity depends on the modeller's boundary choices, which Sterman treats seriously and the popular literature does not.

### SI standing
**High for TRACE and DELAY; the human-baseline results are load-bearing for the falsification programme; the intervention frame is a named hazard.**

Sources: S30, S31, S32, S33, S34, S35.

## 2.11 Soft, critical and management systems

### What exists
Checkland (S36): the hard/soft distinction; "system" as an epistemological device of the inquirer. Churchman (S37): the systems approach begins when one sees through another's eyes; the ethics of the boundary. Ulrich (S38): boundary critique; boundary judgements are value judgements that must be explicit and contestable. Beer (S39): the Viable System Model; recursive regulation in organizations.

### Contribution
The normative account of BOUND. S0 §VIII.2 ("a boundary is a decision, not a discovery") is not an invention of this programme; it is the settled position of this tradition, and Ulrich's boundary critique gives it teeth: whose purposes, whose values, who is affected but not consulted. Beer supplies the mature organizational regulator architecture relevant to SC-2.

### Constraint
Soft-systems work has no empirical falsification tradition; its validity criterion is defensibility to stakeholders. Beer is regulator-centred and, transferred to persons, is the prohibited shape.

### SI standing
**Authoritative for BOUND and for the ethics of REFLECT; no falsifier; Beer is a hazard for SC-3/SC-4 and a resource for SC-2.**

Sources: S36, S37, S38, S39.

## 2.12 Process philosophy

### What exists
Whitehead (S40): an ontology in which entities are constituted by their relations and by process.

### Contribution
The philosophical position most congenial to "living relational system" and to the idea that dynamics have an interior. Admitted, per S0 §XIII, only where genuinely relevant.

### Constraint
No formal or empirical bridge. This is the tradition most at risk of decorative citation, and S1 records that it appears in the lineage only as inspiration.

### SI standing
**SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION only.** Relevant to the S6 question, and to nothing before it.

Sources: S40.

## 2.13 Enactivism and embodied cognition

### What exists
Varela, Thompson & Rosch (S41): enaction; cognition as embodied action; dialogue with first-person practice. Thompson (S42): the deep continuity of life and mind; autonomy, sense-making, phenomenology and dynamical systems in one frame. Di Paolo (S43): autopoiesis alone does not yield sense-making; adaptivity, self-regulation with respect to conditions of viability, is required.

### Contribution
The only tradition that tries to hold systemic organization and lived experience together without reducing either, and a precise argument (S43) that organization plus regulation, not organization alone, is what makes a system care about its own trajectory. This is the tradition that will matter most at S6, and the one most likely to be laundered into a Spiralogic correspondence before S6 opens.

### Constraint
Philosophical and programmatic; empirical commitments are stated rather than tested. Its relation to representation is contested and bears directly on whether a modelling faculty can be enactive at all.

### SI standing
**High for the S6 question; medium for the meaning of AUTONOMY and BOUNDARY; ⛔ not to be used to pre-answer S6.**

Sources: S41, S42, S43.

## 2.14 Emergence in philosophy of science

### What exists
Anderson (S44): the whole obeys new laws not derivable in practice from the parts; broken symmetry. Bedau (S45): weak emergence as derivability only by simulation. Chalmers (S46): strong (not deducible even in principle) versus weak (unexpected but deducible). Kim (S47): the causal exclusion argument against emergent downward causation.

### Contribution
The distinctions S0 §VIII.4 relies on. Bedau's definition is the most usable by a benchmark, because it is relative to a derivation procedure and therefore testable: a whole-level property is weakly emergent if it cannot be derived except by simulating the parts. Kim supplies the standing objection to any EMERGE claim that asserts the whole *causes* anything.

### Constraint
Weak emergence is relative to a derivation procedure and a choice of level; strong emergence has no accepted empirical cases; and the causal-exclusion argument means an EMERGE claim with causal content is philosophically expensive.

### SI standing
**Authoritative for EMERGE; supplies both the operational definition and the objection.**

Sources: S44, S45, S46, S47.

## 2.15 Unifying claimants

### What exists
Friston (S48): the free-energy principle; action, perception and learning as minimization of variational free energy; organisms as self-evidencing systems.

### Contribution
The most influential contemporary attempt to unify self-organization, regulation and cognition under one principle, and therefore the most likely vocabulary a competent implementer would reach for.

### Constraint
Two, and they are different in kind. Scientifically, the principle's status (empirical theory, mathematical framework, or tautology) is contested. Constitutionally, the principle is stated as an optimization: a person modelled under it is, by construction, an optimizer of a single quantity. That is lawful as a scientific hypothesis about organisms. As a stance toward a member it is the prohibited chain, arriving with the best credentials in the field.

### SI standing
**ENGINEERING_ANALOGY / HYPOTHESIS; a named constitutional hazard for S7 and S8.**

Sources: S48.

## 2.16 Machine detection of systems structure

### What exists
Hosseinichimeh et al. (S49): a language-model pipeline extracting causal loop diagrams from text, identifying roughly 60% of links and loops against expert diagrams. Curated-prompting generation (S50): comparable-to-expert diagrams for simple structures. Leitch (S51): a 53-test extraction leaderboard, cloud models at 77–89%, best local model 77%.

### Contribution
The only empirical measurements S1 found of machines perceiving feedback structure. They establish that extraction of *stated* causal structure from text is partially achievable and improving.

### Constraint
Three, all decisive for this programme. First, every reported evaluation S1 read is on positive fixtures: texts that contain loops, scored against diagrams that contain loops. No null-organization condition is reported. Second, the task is extraction of causal claims already present in the text, not perception of dynamics from evidence; the text asserts the loop and the model transcribes it. Third, the scores are for simple structures.

### SI standing
**INTERNAL_EMPIRICAL_RESULT of other programmes; establishes an extraction ceiling; establishes nothing about refraining (F-SI8), about dynamics from time-indexed evidence (S0 §VIII.1), or about SI's distinction from transcription (F-SI7).**

Sources: S49, S50, S51.

# 3 · The disagreement map

S0 §XIII made seven entries mandatory: `system · boundary · feedback · emergence · self-organization · observer · information`. Three more are added because the traditions split on them in ways that bear on the primitive acts: `attractor · complexity · regulation/adaptivity`. For each: meanings by tradition; agreement; conflict; homonymy; consequence for SI.

## 3.1 System

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| General systems (S01) | a whole of interacting parts, ontologically in the world |
| First-order cybernetics (S02–S03) | what is regulated; defined by state variables and a regulator's interest |
| Soft systems (S36–S38) | an epistemological device of the inquirer; "a system" is a way of looking |
| Autopoiesis (S19) | a unity whose organization produces its own boundary; defined by closure, not by the observer |
| Luhmann (S21) | a closed system of communications; persons are its environment |
| Dynamical systems (S09) | a state space and a rule of evolution |

**Agreement:** a system is a whole whose parts interact. **Conflict:** whether the whole is in the world or in the inquirer (S01 vs S36), and whether the boundary is drawn by the observer or produced by the system (S36 vs S19). **Homonymy:** "system" in S21 excludes the people whom "system" in S01 includes.

**Consequence for SI:** S0 §VIII.2 (boundary as decision) sides with S36–S38 and is in tension with S19 for living systems, which produce their own boundary. S2 must decide whether BOUND admits both an observer-drawn boundary and a system-produced one, and how the two are distinguished in a claim. This is not settled here.

## 3.2 Boundary

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Soft/critical systems (S36–S38) | a value-laden judgement; must be explicit and contestable |
| Autopoiesis (S19) | self-produced by the network of processes; constitutive of the living unity |
| Ecology (S25–S26) | a scale and extent choice for analysis |
| Dynamical systems (S09) | a basin boundary (separatrix) in state space |
| Luhmann (S21) | the system/environment distinction as the system's own operation |

**Agreement:** none beyond the word. **Conflict:** normative choice (S38) versus constitutive self-production (S19) versus geometric object (S09). **Homonymy:** severe; "the boundary of the system" means three unrelated things in three traditions.

**Consequence for SI:** the S0 §VIII.2 law is correct for observer-drawn boundaries and incomplete for self-producing ones. A BOUND claim must say which kind it is making. This is the sharpest S2 constraint S1 records.

## 3.3 Feedback

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| First-order cybernetics (S02) | output re-enters as input; negative feedback stabilizes, positive amplifies |
| System dynamics (S30, S34) | a closed causal loop of stocks and flows with polarity and delay |
| Second-order cybernetics (S07) | a circuit of differences; mind is the circuit; the observer is in it |
| Biology / enactivism (S43) | regulation with respect to viability conditions (adaptivity) |
| Popular usage | any influence that comes back |

**Agreement:** circular causal structure. **Conflict:** whether feedback is a mechanism observed from outside (S02, S30) or a circuit the observer belongs to (S07). **Homonymy:** "feedback" in ordinary language (a response, a comment) is unrelated and contaminates text-extraction tasks.

**Consequence for SI:** TRACE inherits the S02/S30 meaning (loop, polarity, delay) as the only falsifiable one; REFLECT inherits the S07 meaning. F-SI1 (loop is lexical) is directly motivated by the homonymy: a text that says "feedback" is not evidence of a loop.

## 3.4 Emergence

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physics (S44) | new laws at a level, not derivable in practice from below |
| Philosophy, weak (S45, S46) | derivable only by simulation; unexpected but deducible |
| Philosophy, strong (S46) | not deducible even in principle |
| Synergetics (S12) | an order parameter that enslaves the components that generate it |
| Complexity (S15) | aggregate behaviour arising from agent interactions |
| Popular usage | anything surprising in a complex system |

**Agreement:** a whole-level property that is not a property of any part. **Conflict:** whether it is epistemic (S45) or ontological (S46 strong), and whether it can cause anything (S12 says yes via slaving; S47 argues no). **Homonymy:** the popular use has no content.

**Consequence for SI:** EMERGE must declare which meaning it asserts. Bedau's is the only benchmark-usable one. A causal reading must answer Kim. F-SI3 (emergence is aggregation) is well-founded: the tradition itself distinguishes the aggregate from the emergent.

## 3.5 Self-organization

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nonequilibrium thermodynamics (S10) | dissipative structure sustained by throughput far from equilibrium |
| Synergetics (S12) | pattern formation via order parameters near instability |
| Complexity (S13) | spontaneous order in networks; edge of chaos |
| Criticality (S16) | a system driving itself to a critical state without tuning |
| Autopoiesis (S19) | self-production of the components and the boundary |
| Popular usage | order without a designer |

**Agreement:** order not imposed from outside. **Conflict:** four distinct physical or computational mechanisms plus one definitional (S19) that is about self-*production*, not pattern formation. **Homonymy:** severe; the S19 sense and the S10 sense are not the same phenomenon.

**Consequence for SI:** any SI claim of self-organization must name the mechanism class. "Self-organizing" as an adjective of approval is exactly what S0 §VIII.4 forbids for "emergent," and S1 extends the same rule to this term.

## 3.6 Observer

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| First-order cybernetics (S02–S03) | outside the system; the regulator's designer |
| Second-order cybernetics (S05–S06) | inside; a system that observes; responsible for its descriptions |
| Autopoiesis (S19) | a living system whose distinctions bring forth the observed |
| Soft systems (S36–S37) | the inquirer whose purposes define the system |
| Dynamical systems / ecology | absent from the formalism; treated as measurement |

**Agreement:** none. **Conflict:** the deepest in the lineage; whether the observer is outside or inside is the first-order/second-order split. **Homonymy:** mild; the disagreement is substantive rather than lexical.

**Consequence for SI:** S0 §VII.3 takes the second-order side as constitution. S1 records that this is a choice against a large and operationally richer tradition, and that the price is REFLECT's lack of a mechanical falsifier, which S0 §IX already anticipated.

## 3.7 Information

| Tradition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shannon (S04) | a measure over a distribution; indifferent to meaning |
| Bateson (S07) | a difference which makes a difference; relational and meaning-bearing |

**Consequence for SI:** any use of "information" in an SI claim must say which. TRACE follows flows of Shannon-type quantities where measurable; REFLECT concerns Bateson-type differences. Conflating them is the single easiest laundering move in the lineage.

# 4 · Substantive conflicts across traditions

The disagreement map shows lexical divergence. Beneath it are five substantive conflicts that any formal vocabulary will have to take a side on or explicitly straddle.

## 4.1 Control versus autonomy
First-order cybernetics, system dynamics and management cybernetics (S02, S03, S30, S31, S39) treat a system as something regulated by a designer, modeller or manager. Second-order cybernetics, autopoiesis and enactivism (S05, S07, S19, S43) treat a living system as self-regulating and the observer as a participant. S0 §VII is on the second side. S1's finding is that the second side supplies the constitution and the first side supplies most of the operational vocabulary; S2 must import concepts across this line without importing the posture, and must be able to show it did.

## 4.2 Observer-independent versus observer-inclusive description
Dynamical systems, ecology and physics describe systems without an observer in the formalism. Second-order cybernetics and soft systems place the observer inside the description. S0 sides with the latter for the faculty and with the former for evidence (S0 §X). S1 records this as a deliberate two-sidedness rather than an inconsistency: the claim is observer-inclusive; the falsifier is not.

## 4.3 Reduction versus relational causality
Anderson (S44), Haken (S12) and Rosen (S24) hold that organization has causal or nomological standing of its own. Kim (S47) holds that emergent causal powers are excluded by the causal closure of the base. This is unresolved in philosophy and S1 does not resolve it. Consequence: EMERGE claims that stop at description (a whole-level property exists and is not derivable except by simulation) are on firmer ground than EMERGE claims that assert the whole does something.

## 4.4 Equilibrium versus far-from-equilibrium
Classical control and much early systems theory assumes return to equilibrium. Prigogine, Haken, Holling and Kauffman treat the interesting organization as far from equilibrium, multi-stable and sometimes critical. Consequence: PATTERN should not assume a single attractor; TRANSFORM should expect multiple basins.

## 4.5 Description versus intervention
Meadows (S31) and Arnold & Wade (S52) fold intervention into the definition of systems thinking. Bateson (S07) argues that purposive intervention by a part that ignores the whole is the characteristic pathology of conscious purpose. This is the oldest form of the disagreement S0 §VII settles, and S1 records that S0's position has a lineage as long as the position it refuses.

# 6 · What is genuinely useful for SI

Recorded per primitive act as *scopes of usable content*, not as vocabulary.

- **BOUND** — Checkland's hard/soft distinction (S36) and Ulrich's boundary critique (S38) give a procedure for making a boundary explicit and contestable, which is what S0 §VIII.2 needs to be more than a slogan. Maturana (S19) gives the case that must be distinguished from it.
- **RELATE** — Forrester's stock/flow/link discipline (S30) and Kauffman's network formalisms (S13) give relation types with defined semantics; RGR's `Relation` primitive remains the substrate (S0 §V.1).
- **SCALE** — Simon's near-decomposability (S17) is the one account in the lineage under which a level is measurable rather than asserted; Haken's order/control-parameter split (S12) is the regime-specific complement; Holling's panarchy (S26) is a heuristic to be held, not used.
- **TRACE** — the cybernetic/system-dynamics loop with polarity and delay (S02, S30, S34) is the only falsifiable content; Sterman's misperception results (S33, S35) define what a competent-looking failure looks like.
- **PATTERN** — attractor and basin from S09 and S13; resilience from S25; the null comparison against a process with matched statistics is standard practice in the ecological EWS literature (S28) and transfers directly.
- **EMERGE** — Bedau's simulation-relative definition (S45) is usable; Chalmers' distinction (S46) fixes the vocabulary; Kim (S47) fixes what may not be claimed cheaply.
- **TRANSFORM** — the EWS toolbox (S28) and its limits (S29) are a near-complete template for an S4 fixture: known transition, generic indicators, characterized false-alarm rate.
- **REFLECT** — von Foerster's responsibility of the observer (S06) and Churchman's "through the eyes of another" (S37) give the content; Rosen's anticipation (S23) and Holland's internal models (S15) give a formal shape for "a system that includes its own model," which is the nearest thing to a mechanizable REFLECT and is recorded here as a scope for S2, not a proposal.
- **For F-SI8 specifically** — the human literature (S33, S35) is the precedent that fluent reasoners fail null and simple-dynamics cases; the machine literature (S49–S51) has no null case. The mandatory null fixture is therefore not conservative; it is the missing experiment.
- **For F-SI7 specifically** — the CLD extraction results (S49–S51) are a warning: transcribing a loop the text asserts is achievable and is *not* systems perception. An SI claim indistinguishable from extraction of stated causal claims has added nothing over RGR's `Relation` + `Trajectory`.

# 7 · WHAT THE CURRENT LITERATURE DOES NOT ESTABLISH

- That "systems thinking" is a single faculty, tradition or skill. It is a family with a shared lexicon and unshared meanings.
- That any tradition's definition of system, boundary, feedback, emergence, self-organization, observer or information is the correct one.
- That feedback loops, attractors or emergent properties can be perceived from prose without time-indexed evidence. The machine literature shows extraction of *stated* causal structure only.
- That a language model, or any system, can refrain from asserting organization where none is warranted. No null-fixture evaluation was found.
- That emergent properties have causal powers. This is contested (S44, S12 versus S47).
- That early-warning signals detect transitions reliably outside well-sampled series with characterized error rates (S29).
- That autopoiesis transposes to social systems (S21 versus S22).
- That Rosen's relational biology is formally sound (S24, contested).
- That the free-energy principle is an empirical theory rather than a framework (S48, contested).
- Anything about Spiralogic, Elemental Alchemy, or the qualitative interior of dynamics. No source in this corpus addresses them, and S1 did not look for correspondences, which is S6's act.
- That Systems Intelligence exists, is acquirable, or is distinct from RGR in practice.

# 8 · Hazards specific to this programme

Named so they are not absorbed.

1. **The optimizer with credentials.** The free-energy principle (S48) is the most sophisticated frame available and it models the organism as minimizing one quantity. Adopting it as SI's account of a person would satisfy every scientific instinct and violate S0 §VII.1 by construction. It may be studied; it may not be the stance.
2. **Intervention folded into definition.** Meadows' leverage points (S31), Beer's VSM (S39) and the consolidated definition (S52) make intervention part of what systems thinking *is*. Any S2 vocabulary that imports "leverage," "intervention point" or "regulator" imports the posture with the word.
3. **Homonym laundering.** "Information" (S04 vs S07), "boundary" (S38 vs S19), "self-organization" (S10 vs S19), "attractor" (S09 vs popular). Each pair allows a claim to be made in the weak sense and heard in the strong one.
4. **The four-phase schema.** Holling's adaptive cycle (S26) has four phases and nested scales. It will look like Spiralogic to anyone who wants it to. S1 records the resemblance as a hazard for S6, not as a finding.
5. **Conclusions without arguments.** Rosen (S24) and Bateson (S07) are cited far more often for their conclusions than for their arguments. A programme that motivates itself with "living relational system" will be tempted to cite them as established. They are not.
6. **Extraction wearing perception's clothes.** The CLD literature (S49–S51) shows that a model can produce a causal loop diagram from a text. That is transcription of the author's causal claims. If SI's outputs cannot be distinguished from this, F-SI7 fails and the faculty does not exist.
