# CIRCLE_SCALE_FALSIFICATION

**Lane:** `JARVIS-CIRCLES-01 · SHARED-INTEREST FIELDS` · DISCOVER
**Mandated by:** founder Amendment B, 2026-09-06
**Posture:** *falsify the thresholds, do not justify them.*
**Retrieval:** web retrieval pass run in-session 2026-09-06. Sources are named per claim. Sources
were read as search-result syntheses, **not** as full-text papers — every claim below is
**RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (secondary)**, never OBSERVED, and none is ratified.

> **⛔ Headline result: the provisional thresholds are NOT confirmed. The strongest single finding
> in this pass contradicts them.** A 2025 systematic review of group size in group psychotherapy
> (combined N = 21,425, 17 studies) found significant group-size associations in **only 7 of 17
> studies**, and reports *"little or no evidence that group sizes under or over five members fared
> worse."* Size is a **weak** predictor in the literature closest to Soullab's use case.
>
> The provisional bands survive this pass as **design clues, not as thresholds.**

## 1. Evidence supporting small bounded Circles

| Finding | Source | Bears on |
|---|---|---|
| Groups of **5–9** favoured over both smaller and larger; ideal therapeutic group **6–9** | Group-psychotherapy review literature (Tandfonline 2025 systematic review; ResearchGate, *Maximizing the therapeutic effectiveness of small psychotherapy groups*) | 6–15 band — **partially supports a tighter band than 6–15** |
| Groups **>5** develop cohesion and group identity more easily; group identity is *"perhaps the most important single factor in therapeutic effectiveness"* | same | **the lower bound, and the dyad question** |
| Sharp drops in verbal interrelation at **9** and again at **17** members; 5–8 optimal for participation | 1962 inpatient-group verbal-interrelation study, cited in the same review | **two discontinuities, both below 24** |
| Larger groups show **more inequality of participation**; ideal meeting size 3–8; dominance↔speaking-time relation **strengthens as group size increases** | Rose, Diamond & Powers 2020, *Inequality in talk and group size effects*; Karpowitz et al. 2012 | **quieter-member participation** |
| **Ringelmann effect**: individual contribution falls as group grows; productivity loss rises *in direct proportion to group size*; coordination loss dominates at larger n | Ringelmann; Steiner process-loss literature | **facilitation burden, participation** |
| As online communities grow, activity **centralizes in a smaller core** | *Super-Linear Growth and Rising Inequality in Online Social Communities: Insights from Reddit* (arXiv 2503.02661) | **the fission law directly** |
| Growth without matching structure reaches a **fragmentation point where more members stops meaning more value** | Discourse, *Community Fragmentation: When Growth Becomes Your Obstacle* | **the fission law directly** |

**Verdict on §1:** the *direction* is well supported — participation equality, cohesion, and
per-member contribution all degrade monotonically with size. **The specific numbers are not.**

## 2. Evidence CONTRADICTING the provisional thresholds

1. **The systematic review is the strongest contradiction.** Only 7/17 studies found a significant
   size association; *"little or no evidence that group sizes under or over five members fared
   worse."* Its own conclusion is that group-size decisions currently rest on *"clinical opinion,
   conventional wisdom, and recommendations made by influential commentators"* rather than
   evidence. **This is precisely the failure mode Soullab must avoid: ratifying a number because
   it sounds right.**
2. **Dunbar's number will not carry architectural weight.** Re-analysis (Lindenfors et al.,
   *'Dunbar's number' deconstructed*, Biology Letters 2021) yields group-size estimates of 69–109
   and 16–42 by different methods, with **95% CIs of 4–520 and 2–336** — the authors conclude
   *"specifying any one number is futile."* The 5/15/50/150 layer series is a description of
   observed network structure, **not a cognitive law**, and cannot license 24 as a boundary.
3. **The observed discontinuities are at 9 and 17, not at 15 and 24.** If any threshold is
   evidence-backed in this pass, the candidates are **~9** and **~17** — *both inside the
   provisional bands, neither at their edges.*
4. **Communities absorb growth better than expected under the right conditions.** Stanford HCI,
   *Better When It Was Smaller?* / *Surviving an "Eternal September"*: communities can remain
   high-quality after a newcomer influx, and **larger communities were less disrupted than smaller
   ones.** Three factors carried them: consistent leader enforcement, member moderation,
   technological norm-maintenance — i.e. **structure, not size.**

## 3. Cases where groups LARGER than 24 retain genuine relational integrity

- **⭐ Deliberative minipublics / citizens' assemblies.** Routinely **~100+ members** by design
  (sortition, for representativeness), and they sustain high-quality deliberation. **How they do it
  is the finding that matters most to this lane:** the assembly runs as **facilitated small
  groups**, with a learning phase explicitly devoted to *relationship building*, trained
  moderators, and time/resource provision — *"quality of participation over quantity."*
  → **This is the fractal architecture already working in the field.** A 100-person assembly is
  not one relational field; it is a **Constellation of small facilitated fields with a shared
  inquiry.** It corroborates Amendment B's structure while falsifying any claim that >24 is
  inherently incapable of collective intelligence.
- **Ostrom's 8th design principle — nested enterprises.** Large commons are governed as *"multiple
  layers of nested enterprises, with small local CPRs at the base level,"* resembling a federated
  system; nesting is *"a way of scaling up community-based management much beyond the local
  level."* Paired with **subsidiarity** (govern at the lowest capable layer by default).
  → **Amendment B's morphology is a re-derivation of a validated commons-governance principle.**
  This is the strongest external support the lane has, and it supports the *architecture*, not
  the *numbers*.
- **Large subreddits** persist for years at enormous scale — but at the documented cost of
  centralization into a small active core (§1). They retain *community*; they do not retain
  *mutual relational field*. **This distinction is the load-bearing one.**

## 4. Cases where substantially SMALLER groups nevertheless fail

- **Dyads are structurally fragile.** Simmel: in a dyad, if one person withdraws **the group
  ceases to exist**; a triad survives the same withdrawal. Dyads carry high intensity and intimacy
  but *"arbitrary and unpredictable properties."*
- **Below ~5, cohesion forms less easily** (§1) — the therapy literature's lower bound is a real
  finding, not a convention.
- Small online communities were **more** disrupted by newcomer influx than large ones (§2.4) —
  smallness is not protective on its own.
- The Ringelmann coordination/motivation split implies a small group with **no structure** still
  loses; effort protection comes from identifiability and accountability, not from size.

**Verdict:** size is neither necessary nor sufficient. **A small Circle with no facilitation,
no purpose, and no consent structure will fail. This falsifies "small ⇒ good."**

## 5. Do dyads belong inside the Circle ontology?

**Finding: probably not — and the evidence gives a clean reason.**

Simmel's dyad/triad distinction is **qualitative, not quantitative**. The triad is *"the smallest
social configuration capable of generating emergent properties not present in pairs"*: indirect
influence, alliance, majority, and — critically — **survival of one member's withdrawal**.

Mapped onto Soullab's own doctrine, a dyad cannot instantiate the Circle primitives:

| Circle primitive | In a dyad |
|---|---|
| Contribute-before-you-see | degenerate — one other response, trivially attributable |
| Anonymity threshold (doctrine: 2-member minimum for theme surfacing) | **impossible** — every signal is attributable |
| Plurality / "three different experiences are present here" | **structurally unavailable** |
| Leaving cleanly | **ends the field**, rather than the field continuing without you |
| MAIA as field witness | collapses into MAIA-as-third-party-to-a-relationship — a *different* ethical object |

**Recommended founder distinction (Docket D-04):** a two-person field is a **Dyadic Field**, a
first-class object with its own rules, **not a small Circle**. Amendment B's `~3–24` band is
therefore **evidence-supported at its lower bound** — the one place in this pass where a
provisional number survives falsification.

## 6. Does viability depend more on size, duration, facilitation, frequency, or purpose?

**Finding: size ranks LOW. This is the pass's most consequential result.**

Ranked by strength of support in the retrieved literature:

| Rank | Variable | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Facilitation / structure** | Minipublics achieve integrity at 100+ **through facilitation**; Eternal-September survival came from enforcement + member moderation + technical norm-support; Ringelmann's losses are coordination losses |
| **2** | **Purpose / shared inquiry** | Minipublics: a defined question plus a learning phase; the review's tentative trend of *greater size-influence in **process-oriented** groups* — i.e. size matters **conditionally on purpose** |
| **3** | **Interaction structure (order of contribution)** | See §7 — the strongest effect measured in the whole pass |
| **4** | **Duration / cohesion-building time** | Minipublic learning phase for relationship building; cohesion as the primary therapeutic factor |
| **5** | **Size** | Significant in only 7/17 studies; *"little or no evidence"* below/above 5 |

> **⭐⭐⭐ Design consequence: Soullab should govern Circles primarily by facilitation, purpose, and
> interaction structure — and treat size as a *derived* constraint, an early-warning signal that
> those three are about to be overloaded.** Ratifying a size number while leaving facilitation
> unspecified would optimize the weakest variable.

**But note the asymmetry that saves the fission law:** facilitation burden itself scales with size
(Ringelmann coordination losses rise in direct proportion to n). Size is not the cause of failure;
**size is what makes the real causes unaffordable.** Fission remains the correct response — for a
better-stated reason.

## 7. Implications for MAIA as field witness

1. **⭐ Contribute-before-you-see is independently validated — and it is the strongest empirical
   result in this pass.** Aggregating independent judgments yields accuracy; *"this positive
   effect turns negative if group members do not first contribute an independent vote"* and
   observe each other sequentially (Management Science 2021, *Social Influence Undermines the
   Wisdom of the Crowd*; PNAS 2017). Observing others *"narrows the range of opinions, raises
   collective error, and breeds overconfidence"* **without a corresponding accuracy gain.**
   → Soullab's `getInquiryWithResponses` returning `responses: []` before contribution is not
   merely an ethical choice. **It is the mechanism that makes a Circle's collective intelligence
   real rather than performed.** It should be treated as **load-bearing**, and the census finding
   that the *shared feed does not enforce it* (Gap Register G-07) is now a substantive defect, not
   a stylistic inconsistency.
2. **The failure mode MAIA must not have is named by the same literature: convergence.** A field
   witness that summarizes, ranks, or names consensus **is an information cascade with an
   authoritative voice.** "No scoring, no ranking, no declaring a winner" is empirically, not just
   ethically, correct.
3. **MAIA's legitimate function is the inverse: preserving divergence.** *"Three different
   experiences are present here"* protects the independence that produces accuracy. Reflecting
   **plurality** is pro-accuracy; reflecting **majority** is anti-accuracy.
4. **Perceiving a field vs. summarizing a crowd is a real threshold, and it tracks facilitation
   load, not headcount.** At the point where MAIA can no longer represent each contribution as
   distinguishable without aggregating, it has begun crowd-summarization. **Candidate falsifiable
   test: MAIA may only reflect a field in which every contribution remains individually
   attributable to its author within the reflection.** That is a *representability* criterion, and
   it will bind at different n for different Circles — which is the correct shape for the
   variable that actually matters.
5. **At Constellation scale MAIA's role must change kind, not degree.** A Constellation's unit is
   Circles. MAIA there may only witness **what Circles have explicitly released** — never their
   interiors. This is CIRCLE-09's hardest constraint and should not be designed before CIRCLE-05
   produces observation.

## 8. Recommended distinctions among Circle, Constellation, Commons, Co-Lab

Proposed as **CANDIDATE** definitions for the Product Constitution (Docket D-03). Each is
distinguished by its **unit** and its **membrane**, never by headcount.

| Field | Unit | Constituted by | Membrane rule | Size as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dyadic Field** *(proposed new object, §5)* | 2 persons | mutual relationship | strictly bilateral; withdrawal ends it | definitional (=2) |
| **Circle** | persons | a bounded, facilitated, mutual relational field with a shared inquiry | nothing enters or leaves without an explicit member act | **derived signal**, not a rule |
| **Constellation** | **Circles** | a shared inquiry exceeding any one Circle | receives **only** what each Circle explicitly releases; no ambient federation; no Circle interior is visible to another | **not defined by headcount** |
| **Commons** | Circles + persons | shared interest; discovery and wider culture | receives only explicitly consented contributions; **discovery may not read Personal-field material** | dozens → thousands |
| **Co-Lab** | persons in roles | an intentional shared **work** commitment | governed by `studio_teams` scoping and the existing Co-Lab gate | determined by the work |

**Two category errors to retire explicitly:**
- **"Commons Circle" (61–200)** — a Commons is not a large Circle. Founder-flagged; this pass
  finds no evidence for the category and clear structural evidence against it (§3).
- **"Circle that scales to 200"** — the minipublic evidence shows the successful form at that
  scale is *always* nested facilitated small groups. **Enlargement is not the mechanism that works
  at scale; nesting is.**

**Candidate design law — survives this pass, with an amended rationale:**

> **Circles scale by multiplication, not enlargement.**
> Not because 24 is a law of human cognition — the evidence does not support any such number —
> but because **facilitation, purpose, and independent contribution are what make a Circle work,
> and enlargement makes all three unaffordable at once.**

Status: **CANDIDATE. Not ratified.** Ratification is a founder act (Docket D-02).
