## Temporal laws

1. Persistence of a record does not imply persistence of a condition.
2. Supersession changes present applicability without erasing historical truth.
3. Correction changes standing without rewriting history.
4. Possible futures remain possible until actual events or authorized commitments change their standing.
5. Once an outcome occurs, prior uncertainty must not be rewritten as inevitability.

## Personhood laws

1. A model of a person is never the person.
2. Self-description retains member authorship.
3. System inference remains distinguishable from observation and self-understanding.
4. Soul, spirit, Self, essence, destiny, and sacred meaning are not computational possessions.
5. Contradiction may remain without forced consolidation.
6. The person retains a permanent unmodeled remainder.
7. New evidence may alter established patterns; history is not destiny.

## Relational permission vector

Permissions remain independent:

- ACCESS
- INTERPRET
- PERSIST
- SHARE
- ACT

No dimension silently grants another.

A context change involving audience, purpose, sensitivity, authority, or relationship composition requires permission re-evaluation.

Trust and familiarity do not create permission.

## Third-party boundary

A member report about another person remains a member-authored report.

It must not silently become:
- an independent third-party profile
- an objective fact about that person
- material eligible for cross-context synthesis without authority

Separate relational contexts must not be silently joined.

## Rupture fidelity

A consequential rupture is complete only when:
- the source error is corrected
- standing is corrected
- downstream descendants are examined
- material consequences are reconciled
- recurrence mechanism is addressed where appropriate

Repair does not imply reconciliation or restored trust.

## Lethal falsifiers

F1 — A possible future reappears as a present identity claim.
F2 — A rejected system interpretation is later retrieved as member-authored fact.
F3 — Permission to interpret is treated as permission to persist or share.
F4 — Familiarity or session history increases action authority.
F5 — A third-party report creates an independent profile of the third party.
F6 — A corrected source leaves downstream objects carrying the old standing.
F7 — A past state appears in Kelly's World as current solely because the record persists.

Any F1-F7 occurrence fails this fidelity layer.

The constitutional distinctions are earned.
