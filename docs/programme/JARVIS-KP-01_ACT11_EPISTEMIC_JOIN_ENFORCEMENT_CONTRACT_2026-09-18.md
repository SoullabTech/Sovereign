# 0 · Gate boundary

ACT 11 defines the minimum conceptual contract required to represent, evaluate, preserve, promote, revise, supersede, or discharge a proposed semantic relationship without silently converting synthesis into established knowledge.

ACT 11 may:

- define the minimum information that must remain recoverable for a proposed relation;
- define epistemic stages and legal transitions among them;
- define what counts as a relation warrant and what does not;
- define author / provenance / jurisdiction distinctions;
- define promotion, weakening, revision, supersession, and discharge rules;
- define falsifiers;
- define a bounded synthetic corpus for contract testing;
- produce static targeted evidence.

ACT 11 may not:

- create database schema or migrations;

- implement a claim ledger;
- change prompts or system instructions;
- implement parsers or classifiers;

# 1 · Controlling law

The controlling law is:

> **NO SEMANTIC JOIN WITHOUT A WARRANT.**

ACT 11 preserves the ACT 10 distinctions:

1. co-presence ≠ relation;
2. comparison ≠ relation;
3. endpoint evidence ≠ relation evidence;
4. authorship does not compose;
5. standing may not outrun support;
6. jurisdiction does not transfer through synthesis;
7. reference remains distinct from reliance;
8. boundaries widen only through genuinely new evidence;
9. the join itself has epistemic standing.

ACT 11 adds enforcement semantics at the contract level only.

# 2 · Minimum conceptual join envelope

Any proposed semantic relation that is to be evaluated beyond simple comparison must keep the following information recoverable.

This is a **conceptual envelope**, not a required storage schema.

## 2.1 Endpoints

For every endpoint participating in the proposed relation:

- endpoint identity sufficient to distinguish it from other claims / objects;
- endpoint proposition or object description;
- endpoint authorship / authority;
- endpoint provenance;
- endpoint standing;
- endpoint jurisdiction;
- operative boundary / invalidity conditions;
- whether the downstream reasoning **relies** on the endpoint or merely references it.

An endpoint may be well established while the proposed relation remains unestablished.

## 2.2 Relation proposition

The relation itself must be stated explicitly enough to inspect.

At minimum:

- the endpoint set;
- the relation predicate / proposition;
- directionality where claimed;
- scope;
- temporal or causal force where claimed;
- whether the proposition is descriptive, interpretive, causal, predictive, normative, symbolic, experiential, or another declared kind.

Hidden verbs are still claims.

Words such as *causes, proves, validates, confirms, explains, reflects, develops into, corresponds to, is equivalent to,* or *is evidence of* carry epistemic force and may not be smuggled through prose.

## 2.3 Join authorship

The authorship of the relation must remain distinct from authorship of its endpoints.

Allowed conceptual author classes include:

- **SOURCE_AUTHORED** — the relation is directly asserted by a governed source;
- **MEMBER_AUTHORED** — a member explicitly authors the relation within the member's own field;
- **MEMBER_CONFIRMED** — a member explicitly confirms a previously proposed relation;
- **PRACTITIONER_AUTHORED** — a practitioner authors an interpretation within the practitioner's authorized jurisdiction;
- **JARVIS_AUTHORED** — JARVIS creates the relation as analysis / hypothesis;
- **MAIA_PROPOSED** — MAIA offers a relation as a question or candidate interpretation, without member adoption;
- **OTHER_DECLARED_AUTHORITY** — a separately governed authority whose jurisdiction is explicit.

Premise authorship never composes into relation authorship.

## 2.4 Join provenance

The relation must retain provenance for **how the relation claim came to exist**, not only where the endpoints came from.

Join provenance must be able to answer:

- who or what introduced the relation;
- in what inquiry / context;
- which endpoint evidence was relied upon;
- what relation warrant, if any, was relied upon;
- what transformation or reasoning step produced the relation;
- whether later adoption, revision, or discharge occurred.

The provenance of the premises is necessary but not sufficient provenance for the join.

## 2.5 Epistemic operation stage

ACT 11 preserves the canonical progression:

`REFERENCE → REPORT/MOVE → COMPARE/JUXTAPOSE → HYPOTHESIZE → WARRANTED JOIN → PROMOTE/DISCHARGE`

The stage must remain distinguishable from prose fluency.

### REFERENCE

A source / object is mentioned.

No proposition is moved merely because it is cited.

### REPORT / MOVE

A source proposition is carried into the current inquiry.

Its operative boundary travels when the proposition is relied upon.

### COMPARE / JUXTAPOSE

Two or more propositions are placed beside one another while remaining separately scoped.

No relation fact is created.

### HYPOTHESIZE

A possible relation is proposed without source-fact standing.

The hypothesis must expose its author and candidate status.

### WARRANTED JOIN

Evidence supports the relation itself.

The relation's standing is bounded by:

- the endpoint premises it depends upon;
- the relation warrant;
- unresolved invalidity conditions;
- jurisdiction.

### PROMOTE

New evidence or an authorized adoption act legitimately widens standing within a declared jurisdiction.

Promotion cannot exceed the new support.

### DISCHARGE

Evidence defeats, invalidates, supersedes, or removes the standing of the relation proposition.

The historical relation and its earlier standing remain recoverable.

# 3 · Relation standing

ACT 11 distinguishes **operation stage** from **standing**.

A relation may have one of these conceptual standings:

- **NONE / UNASSERTED** — no relation proposition exists;
- **CANDIDATE / UNESTABLISHED** — a relation has been proposed but lacks sufficient warrant;
- **PROVISIONAL** — some warrant exists, but conflict, uncertainty, boundary, or unresolved alternative prevents stronger standing;
- **WARRANTED** — sufficient evidence licenses the declared relation at the declared scope;
- **PROMOTED** — a later authorized act or new evidence widens standing within a declared jurisdiction;
- **DISCHARGED** — the relation no longer retains active standing;
- **SUPERSEDED** — a later proposition replaces it while preserving history.

Standing must never be inferred from confidence, eloquence, repetition, co-occurrence, popularity, retrieval rank, embedding similarity, graph proximity, visual proximity, or recency.

---

# 4 · Warrant contract

A **join warrant** is evidence whose own standing licenses the relation being asserted.

Possible warrant classes include:

- **DIRECT_SOURCE_RELATION** — a governed source explicitly asserts the relation;
- **MEMBER_AUTHORED_RELATION** — the member explicitly relates experiences / objects within the member's own field;
- **MEMBER_CONFIRMATION** — the member confirms a proposed relation;
- **PRACTITIONER_INTERPRETATION** — a practitioner explicitly authors an interpretation within authorized scope;
- **EMPIRICAL_RELATION_EVIDENCE** — an experiment / observation instrument actually bears on the relation;
- **FORMAL_ENTAILMENT** — the relation follows by an explicit governed rule / proof within its jurisdiction;
- **THEORETICAL_ARGUMENT** — a declared theoretical warrant whose standing remains theoretical;
- **BOUNDARY_DISCHARGE_EVIDENCE** — genuinely new evidence addresses the earlier missing kind;
- **OTHER_DECLARED_WARRANT** — a separately governed warrant with explicit limits.

A warrant must declare or preserve:

- evidence identity / provenance;
- author / authority;
- what relation semantics it licenses;
- jurisdiction;
- uncertainty;
- operative limits / invalidity conditions;
- whether it remains live, disputed, weakened, or defeated.

A warrant that establishes **relatedness** does not automatically establish:

- causation;
- direction;
- permanence;
- identity;
- equivalence;
- psychological meaning;
- universality;
- outcome.

---

# 5 · Explicit non-warrants

The following do **not** create relation standing by themselves:

- both endpoints are true;
- both endpoints are highly trusted;
- same member;
- same practitioner;
- same project;
- same prompt;
- same report;
- same Living Constellation;
- same topic;
- temporal proximity;
- repeated co-occurrence;
- lexical similarity;
- embedding similarity;
- visual proximity;
- graph adjacency generated by the system;
- model confidence;
- a user request to "synthesize";
- repeated restatement;
- accumulation of independently insufficient sources;
- fluent explanation.

Formally:

`evidence(A) + evidence(B) ≠ evidence(A —R→ B)`

Endpoint authority does not transfer to the edge.

---

# 6 · Uncertainty and provisional status

A relation must remain provisional when any material condition blocks stronger standing, including:

- conflicting evidence;
- unresolved alternative explanations;
- partial warrant;
- jurisdiction mismatch;
- unresolved invalidity condition;
- weak or indirect relation evidence;
- insufficient precision;
- disputed authorship;
- uncertain directionality;
- uncertain causal force.

Uncertainty must narrow standing rather than disappear in synthesis.

Where useful, the relation may remain available as a candidate rather than being erased.

> **When uncertain, lower standing before deleting meaning.**

---

# 7 · Jurisdiction contract

Every relation claim has a jurisdiction.

Examples:

- member's own lived account;
- practitioner interpretation within practice;
- scientific evidence;
- a named philosophical system;
- product / design analysis;
- JARVIS research analysis;
- MAIA conversational inquiry.

A relation valid in one jurisdiction does not automatically acquire standing in another.

Examples:

- a member-authored relation can be authoritative for the member's own meaning without becoming universal psychology;
- a practitioner interpretation can remain practitioner-authored without becoming member-authored truth;
- a scientific correlation does not become a metaphysical identity claim;
- a symbolic correspondence does not become empirical causation;
- cross-domain analysis does not move source ownership.

Cross-jurisdiction transfer requires a separate warrant whose jurisdiction actually covers the transfer.

# 8 · Human and member authority

ACT 11 preserves human authority over meaning.

## 8.1 Member-authored meaning

A member may author a relation among the member's own experiences, values, memories, projects, or symbols.

That relation may have strong standing **within the member's own field** because the member is the relevant authority for that lived meaning.

It does not automatically become:

- scientific evidence;
- a universal psychological law;
- a diagnosis;
- a causal fact beyond what the member asserted.

## 8.2 MAIA proposal

MAIA may propose a relation only as candidate inquiry unless separate authority exists.

Lawful form:

> "I wonder whether these experiences feel connected to you."

Unlawful promotion:

> "These experiences are connected because..."

when no relation warrant exists.

Member confirmation can create new member-authored / member-confirmed standing, but the history must show that MAIA proposed and the member adopted.

## 8.3 Practitioner interpretation

Practitioner-authored interpretation remains practitioner-authored unless the member separately adopts it.

## 8.4 JARVIS analysis

JARVIS may compare, hypothesize, and derive candidate analysis.

JARVIS-generated coherence does not inherit human authorship.

# 9 · Promotion contract

A relation may be promoted only when all applicable conditions hold:

1. the current relation proposition is explicit;
2. current standing is known;
3. the missing support required for promotion is identified;
4. genuinely new evidence or an authorized adoption act addresses that missing support;
5. the new warrant's jurisdiction covers the stronger claim;
6. operative boundaries and invalidity conditions remain recoverable;
7. authorship remains correct;
8. no unresolved contradiction defeats the stronger standing;
9. the new outer boundary is explicit enough to recover;
10. promotion history preserves the prior state.

Promotion is not authorized by:

- repetition;
- recency;
- source count;
- model confidence;
- reformatting;
- summarization;
- merge;
- retrieval;
- convenience.

# 10 · Weakening, revision, supersession, and discharge

Knowledge must remain corrigible.

## Weakening

When evidence reduces confidence but does not defeat the relation:

- standing moves downward;
- uncertainty / conflict becomes explicit;
- the relation remains available if still useful.

## Revision

When the relation changes materially:

- the prior relation remains recoverable;
- the revised proposition receives its own authorship / warrant / standing;
- revision must not erase the fact that an earlier claim differed.

## Supersession

A later relation may supersede an earlier one when:

- the later proposition has sufficient warrant;
- its relationship to the earlier proposition is explicit;
- the earlier proposition is preserved historically.

## Discharge

A relation is discharged when:

- its warrant is defeated;
- a falsifier is satisfied;
- a member rejects a MAIA/JARVIS-proposed relation within the member's own meaning field;
- new evidence directly contradicts the relation;
- the governing authority withdraws or invalidates the warrant;
- a stronger later finding establishes that the relation should no longer retain active standing.

Discharge is not deletion.

The system must preserve that the relation was once proposed or warranted, by whom, and why it was later discharged.

---

# 11 · Provenance preservation through later synthesis

When a joined proposition is later used as an endpoint in another synthesis:

- the joined proposition's own authorship travels;
- its standing travels;
- its jurisdiction travels;
- its operative limits travel;
- its warrant provenance remains recoverable;
- the new synthesis must obtain a warrant for any **new** relation it asserts.

A warranted join does not become an unlimited source.

No chain of synthesis can launder candidate standing into established fact.

---

# 12 · Reference versus reliance

Reference does not trigger full boundary inheritance by itself.

Reliance does.

The contract must preserve the distinction:

- **REFERENCE:** material is named, displayed, cited, or used illustratively;
- **RELIANCE:** a downstream proposition materially depends upon it.

A mechanism that treats every mention as reliance fails ACT 11.

A mechanism that uses relied-upon evidence while dropping its operative boundary also fails ACT 11.

# 13 · Minimum conceptual evaluation procedure

For any proposed relation:

1. **Name the endpoints independently.**
2. **State the relation proposition explicitly.**
3. **Identify who authored the relation.**
4. **Separate endpoint provenance from join provenance.**
5. **Classify the current epistemic operation.**
6. **Identify the relation warrant, or mark its absence.**
7. **Check whether the warrant licenses the actual semantics claimed.**
8. **Check jurisdiction.**
9. **Carry operative boundaries from relied-upon premises and warrant.**
10. **Check unresolved conflict / uncertainty / invalidity conditions.**
11. **Assign no stronger standing than the support allows.**
12. **If evidence is insufficient, retain as comparison or hypothesis when useful.**
13. **Promote only on genuinely new support or authorized adoption.**
14. **Weaken, revise, supersede, or discharge when later evidence requires it.**
15. **Preserve the full history of standing changes.**

This is a reasoning contract, not an implementation algorithm.

# 14 · Contract invariants

ACT 11 is satisfied only if all of the following remain true.

## INV-01 · Endpoint independence

Each endpoint can retain its own authorship, provenance, standing, jurisdiction, and boundary without being altered merely because a relation is proposed.

## INV-02 · Join separability

The relation proposition can be inspected separately from its endpoints.

## INV-03 · Join authorship integrity

A relation cannot inherit endpoint authorship automatically.

## INV-04 · Join provenance integrity

The relation's origin and warrant remain recoverable.

## INV-05 · No endpoint-to-edge authority transfer

Strong endpoint evidence cannot itself create relation standing.

## INV-06 · Stage integrity

Comparison, hypothesis, warranted join, promotion, and discharge remain distinguishable.

## INV-07 · Standing ceiling

Relation standing cannot outrun the weakest material support required for the relation as asserted.

## INV-08 · Jurisdiction integrity

Synthesis does not silently move claims across constitutional / epistemic jurisdictions.

## INV-09 · Boundary integrity

Relied-upon boundaries remain operative unless genuinely new evidence discharges them.

## INV-10 · Reference / reliance integrity

Textual mention is not treated as epistemic dependence.

## INV-11 · Human authority integrity

Member / practitioner adoption must be explicit; agent generation cannot impersonate it.

## INV-12 · Corrigibility

Later evidence can weaken, revise, supersede, or discharge a relation without erasing provenance.

## INV-13 · Hypothesis viability

Lack of sufficient warrant does not force silence; useful candidate relationships may remain explicitly unestablished.

## INV-14 · No hidden promotion

Repetition, confidence, fluency, accumulation, or display cannot silently raise standing.

# 15 · Contract falsifiers

ACT 11 fails if any compliant future representation or mechanism can do any of the following without violating the contract:

1. assert a relation as fact solely because both endpoints are well evidenced;
2. treat co-presence as a relation warrant;
3. treat comparison as equivalence;
4. turn an unsupported causal relation into source-fact standing;
5. let a JARVIS synthesis inherit member or practitioner authorship;
6. let MAIA's proposed interpretation become member-authored without explicit member confirmation;
7. allow member-authored meaning to become a universal scientific or psychological claim;
8. transfer scientific standing into metaphysical jurisdiction without a separate warrant;
9. transfer symbolic meaning into causal scientific standing without a separate warrant;
10. ignore conflicting evidence while retaining warranted / promoted standing;
11. prevent a relation from being weakened or discharged by later evidence;
12. erase the provenance of an earlier relation after revision / supersession;
13. treat every mention as reliance;
14. drop operative boundaries when relied-upon premises move;
15. let a warrant for "related" silently license "causes";
16. let same-member provenance count as relation evidence;
17. let visual / embedding / temporal proximity create standing;
18. let repeated hypotheses become facts through repetition;
19. prevent useful hypothesis generation merely because relation evidence is absent;
20. allow an already-warranted join to create a new downstream relation without a new warrant for the new edge.

Any of these is a contract failure.
