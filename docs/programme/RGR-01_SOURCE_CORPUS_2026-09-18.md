## A · Conceptual spaces and geometric theories of concepts

### S01 — Gärdenfors & Osta-Vélez (2026)
**Peter Gärdenfors, Matías Osta-Vélez. _Reasoning with Concepts: Conceptual Spaces as a Framework_. MIT Press, 2026.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Domain: cognitive science / philosophy of concepts
- Contribution: extends conceptual spaces from concept representation to induction, analogy, coherence, expectation, and reasoning.
- Relevance: direct precedent for treating reasoning geometrically.
- Limitation: a conceptual-spaces framework does not establish that all meaning is geometric or that one metric is privileged.

### S02 — Gärdenfors (2000)
**Peter Gärdenfors. _Conceptual Spaces: The Geometry of Thought_. MIT Press, 2000.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Domain: cognitive science
- Contribution: geometric representation of conceptual domains via quality dimensions, similarity, regions, and prototypes.
- Relevance: foundational conceptual-spaces vocabulary.
- Limitation: geometric dimensions are theory commitments that require empirical justification.

## B · Analogy and relational reasoning

### S03 — Gentner (1983)
**Dedre Gentner. "Structure-Mapping: A Theoretical Framework for Analogy." _Cognitive Science_ 7(2):155–170. DOI 10.1207/s15516709cog0702_3.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: analogy maps relations and higher-order relational systems rather than primarily object attributes.
- Relevance: strong non-geometric baseline for relation-first reasoning.
- Limitation: depends on structured representations and mapping rules, not geometric invariants.

### S04 — Gentner (2010)
**Dedre Gentner. "Bootstrapping the Mind: Analogical Processes and Symbol Systems." _Cognitive Science_ 34.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: structural alignment supports abstraction, inference projection, and learning.
- Relevance: mature alternative account of relational transfer.
- Limitation: does not require manifold or subspace geometry.

### S05 — Shang, Kreiman & Sompolinsky (2026)
**Jiaqi Shang, Gabriel Kreiman, Haim Sompolinsky. "Unraveling the geometry of visual relational reasoning." _Scientific Reports_ 16:22146 (2026). DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-47123-3.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: defines relational-rule manifolds in model representation space; geometric signal, dimensionality, and overlap predict held-out rule generalization in SimplifiedRPM; direct SNR optimization improves generalization.
- Relevance: closest current empirical precedent to RGR's "relational geometry" idea.
- Limitation: benchmark-specific, second-order manifold approximation, visual relational task; no evidence for a general geometry of lived meaning.

### S06 — Battaglia et al. (2018)
**Peter Battaglia et al. "Relational inductive biases, deep learning, and graph networks." arXiv:1806.01261.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: argues for explicit object-and-relation structured inductive biases in learning systems.
- Relevance: graph/network baseline for relation-centric reasoning.
- Limitation: relation-centric does not imply geometry-centric.

### S07 — Khalid & Schockaert (2024)
**Irtaza Khalid, Steven Schockaert. "Systematic Reasoning About Relational Domains With Graph Neural Networks." arXiv:2407.17396.**

- Class: SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: shows systematic relational reasoning can be improved through suitable GNN inductive bias and epistemic-state interpretation.
- Relevance: strong graph-based alternative to specialized geometric formulations.
- Limitation: benchmark-specific and model-specific.

## C · Cognitive maps, abstract spaces, and relational neuroscience

### S08 — Park, Miller & Boorman (2021)
**Seongmin A. Park, Douglas S. Miller, Erie D. Boorman. "Inferences on a multidimensional social hierarchy use a grid-like code." _Nature Neuroscience_ 24:1292–1301.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: evidence for a two-dimensional map-like representation of learned social hierarchy and grid-like coding during inferred trajectories.
- Relevance: abstract relational structure can recruit map-like neural codes.
- Limitation: one experimental task; map-like coding does not establish universal spatialization of concepts.

### S09 — Viganò et al. (2023)
**Simone Viganò et al. "Mental search of concepts is supported by egocentric vector representations and restructured grid maps." _Nature Communications_ 14:8132.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: conceptual navigation recruits complementary allocentric and egocentric reference frames; grid-like signals vary with conceptual goal proximity.
- Relevance: perspective/reference-frame treatment of conceptual search.
- Limitation: "conceptual space" is experimentally constructed; not direct evidence about unconstrained lived meaning.

### S10 — Nieh et al. (2021)
**Edward H. Nieh et al. "Geometry of abstract learned knowledge in the hippocampus." _Nature_ 595:80–84.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: hippocampal population activity jointly maps physical and abstract task variables on low-dimensional manifolds.
- Relevance: task-specific geometric representation of learned knowledge.
- Limitation: mice, constrained task, neural-population geometry; no semantic/phenomenological identity claim.

### S11 — Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (2017)
**Kimberly Stachenfeld, Matthew Botvinick, Samuel Gershman. "The hippocampus as a predictive map." _Nature Neuroscience_ 20:1643–1653.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: successor representation provides a predictive, policy-sensitive account of hippocampal spatial structure.
- Relevance: demonstrates that apparent geometry can arise from predictive relational structure.
- Limitation: a key warning against assuming map geometry is primitive rather than emergent from transition statistics.

### S12 — Momennejad et al. (2017)
**Ida Momennejad et al. "The successor representation in human reinforcement learning." _Nature Human Behaviour_ 1:680–692.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: behavioral evidence for successor-representation-like predictive structure in human learning.
- Relevance: non-geometric matrix/transition baseline for map-like behavior.
- Limitation: reinforcement-learning tasks, not general semantic reasoning.

### S13 — Mark et al. (2020)
**Shirley Mark, Rani Moran, Thomas Parr, Steve W. Kennerley, Timothy E. J. Behrens. "Transferring structural knowledge across cognitive maps in humans and models." _Nature Communications_ 11:4783 (2020). DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-18254-6.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: humans can abstract and transfer structural regularities across graph environments; simple successor representation does not fully explain unobserved-path inference.
- Relevance: relational structure transfer beyond stimulus identity.
- Limitation: graph-learning paradigm; does not select a unique geometry.

### S14 — Esposito et al. (2025)
**Mariachiara Esposito, Lubna Shaheen Abdul, Ameer Ghouse, Marta Rodríguez Aramendía et al., Raphael Kaplan. "Flexible hippocampal representation of abstract boundaries supports memory-guided choice." _Nature Communications_ 16:2377 (2025). DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-57644-6.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: hippocampus and mPFC encode distance to boundaries in abstract 2D knowledge spaces; hippocampus flexibly tracks boundary-context changes.
- Relevance: boundaries can matter in abstract cognitive maps.
- Limitation: predefined 2D knowledge spaces and Euclidean task geometry.

### S15 — Rueckemann et al. (2021)
**"The grid code for ordered experience." _Nature Reviews Neuroscience_ 22:637–649.**

- Class: SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE / ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: argues grid systems may encode learned topology rooted in temporal order rather than a rigid spatial coordinate system.
- Relevance: topology/sequence may be more fundamental than Euclidean geometry.
- Limitation: perspective/review, not proof of a single computational theory.

### S16 — Pohl et al. (2026)
**"Clarifying the conceptual dimensions of representation in neuroscience." _Nature Reviews Neuroscience_ 27.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: separates sensitivity, specificity, invariance, and functionality as distinct evidential dimensions of neural representation.
- Relevance: strong discipline for any RGR claim that calls something a "representation" or "invariant."
- Limitation: conceptual framework does not settle lower/upper boundaries of representation.

### S17 — Overgaard & Kirkeby-Hinrup (2026)
**"Boundary issues for multidimensional frameworks of representation." _Nature Reviews Neuroscience_ 27:652.**

- Class: SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: argues representational frameworks may leave underdetermined what counts as representation and how representation relates to consciousness.
- Relevance: direct warning against moving from representational geometry to consciousness claims.
- Limitation: correspondence/critique rather than a competing quantitative model.

### S18 — Perich, Narain & Gallego (2025)
**"A neural manifold view of the brain." _Nature Neuroscience_ 28:1582–1597.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: reviews low-dimensional neural-manifold structure across systems and behavior and highlights conceptual gaps.
- Relevance: modern neural-manifold framework and caution.
- Limitation: "manifold" describes constrained population states; it does not imply semantic manifold ontology.

### S19 — Stringer et al. (2019)
**"High-dimensional geometry of population responses in visual cortex." _Nature_ 571:361–365.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: natural-image representations can be very high-dimensional while retaining structured geometry.

### S20 — Gosztolai et al. (2025)
**"MARBLE: interpretable representations of neural population dynamics using geometric deep learning." _Nature Methods_ 22:612–620.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE

## D · Vector, subspace, Grassmannian, and manifold representations

### S21 — Mu, Bhat & Viswanath (2017)
**"Representing Sentences as Low-Rank Subspaces." ACL 2017. DOI 10.18653/v1/P17-2099.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: sentence word vectors approximately occupy low-rank subspaces; subspace similarity provides a sentence representation method.
- Relevance: direct precedent for replacing single semantic vectors with subspaces.
- Limitation: sentence-similarity task; no general relation reasoning.

### S22 — Ishibashi et al. (2024)
**"Subspace Representations for Soft Set Operations and Sentence Similarities." NAACL 2024. DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.naacl-long.192.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: linear subspaces support soft union/intersection/complement-like operations and can outperform vector baselines on selected similarity/retrieval tasks.
- Relevance: compositional set-like semantics in subspace form.
- Limitation: quantum-logic inspiration is mathematical, not quantum-cognitive evidence.

### S23 — Naowarat, Tang & Goldwater (2026)
**"A framework for analyzing concept representations in neural models." CoNLL 2026.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: concept subspaces are estimator-dependent, need not be unique, and may generalize poorly.
- Relevance: direct caution against claiming a unique geometric "concept subspace."
- Limitation: specific text/speech concept analysis.

### S24 — Björck & Golub / Drmač principal-angle line
**Classical numerical linear algebra on principal angles between subspaces; Drmač, "On Principal Angles between Subspaces of Euclidean Space," SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: principal angles/canonical correlations provide mathematically defined comparison between subspaces.
- Relevance: candidate primitive for Grassmannian/subspace comparison.
- Limitation: mathematical operation has no semantic interpretation without a separately justified representation map.

# Corpus standing

The corpus currently supports four cautious conclusions:

1. **Relation-first reasoning is independently well motivated** by analogy, graph, cognitive-map, and relational-generalization research.
2. **Geometry is a legitimate representational and analytical language** across concepts, neural populations, subspaces, graphs/manifolds, statistics, and relational-rule representations.
3. **No source establishes a unique or universal geometry of human meaning.**
4. **There are strong non-geometric or differently geometric competitors**—structure mapping, graphs, successor representations, symbolic/neuro-symbolic systems, and compositional semantics—that RGR must beat or clarify rather than ignore.
