## 2 · DEFECTS FOUND IN THE INITIAL 15-LAW DRAFT

The initial contract at `4096f915…` was **not frozen**. Adjudication found three substantive contract defects and two wording loopholes.

### A1 · Missing F5 identity distinction — corrected

F5 required the combined contract to preserve **Identity — who holds deletion authority**.

The initial conceptual table omitted it, and no C1–C15 law directly tested binding of the erasure actor to the sovereign subject.

The amended contract therefore adds:

- **Identity / subject authority** to the conceptual distinctions; and
- **C16 — ERASURE AUTHORITY MUST BE BOUND TO THE CORRECT SOVEREIGN SUBJECT**, with N16/P16 controls.

This does not require a new identity table, token, route or schema. It requires observable subject-binding.

### A2 · Missing refusal / uncertainty semantics — corrected

D9-A established refusal, withdrawal and the unprovable middle as first-class distinctions. In particular, `attempted` does not mean “nothing crossed,” and refusal of one proposition does not evidence its opposite.

The initial contract governed rejection mainly as a present-standing transition under C5 but did not prevent semantic laundering of:

- rejection → opposite member claim;
- silence → consent/adoption/rejection;
- attempted/unknown → confident positive or negative crossing fact.

The amended contract adds:

- **C17 — REFUSAL, SILENCE AND ATTEMPT MUST NOT BE CONVERTED INTO CLAIMS THEY DO NOT EVIDENCE**, with N17/P17 controls.

This preserves epistemic neutrality; it does not add a new runtime state model.

### A3 · Declared-scope escape — corrected

The initial §19 allowed a future implementation slice to claim conformance for “the laws in its declared scope.” Standing alone, that language allowed a slice to exclude an already-known F5 STOP and obtain apparent conformance by narrowing the test boundary.

That would contradict the frozen programme standing:

**F5 ERASURE CONFORMANCE — FAIL / STOP**

and would let scope declaration do work that evidence had not earned.

The amended PASS standard now states:

- implementation scope may narrow what is built;
- scope may not exclude a contract law materially implicated by state the slice creates, reads, writes, crosses, retains, withdraws or erases;
- scope may not convert a programme-wide STOP into PASS or NOT APPLICABLE;
- any slice persisting member-linked SPM state must declare/test the erasure disposition and erasure-authority binding for that state;
- no full SPM conformance/release may be claimed while the frozen F5 STOP remains unresolved.

This is a gate rule, not an implementation prescription.

### A4 · C2 member-standing loophole — tightened

The initial C2 PASS wording allowed member standing to arise through “a lawful member act or other independently governed basis.” That phrase was broader than the frozen evidence and could silently re-introduce machine-manufactured member standing through a renamed basis.

The amended C2 now permits recurrence to change machine salience or machine epistemic confidence while requiring member standing to remain grounded in a **member-originating or member-ratifying act**.

### A5 · C4 synthesis-standing loophole — tightened

The initial C4 said a synthesis remained synthesis “unless a separate lawful act changes its standing.” That did not distinguish system epistemic standing from **member** standing.

The amended C4 now says system-side epistemic standing may change under governed system rules, but member authorship/confirmation/adoption requires a lawful member act.

## 3 · INTERNAL CONTRADICTION REVIEW

After the amendments, no contract contradiction remains that forces mutually incompatible outcomes.

### C1 / C5

Provenance may remain historically true while present standing changes. No contradiction: one governs origin; the other governs currentness.

### C2 / C4

Machine recurrence may affect salience and a synthesis may gain system confidence, while neither becomes member-standing without a member act. The laws reinforce rather than duplicate each other: C2 varies recurrence; C4 varies derivation.

### C5 / C11

Historical retention is permitted; resurrection as current influence is not. A predecessor can remain evidence without regaining current effect.

### C6 / C14

C6 asks whether an **independently governed seam** actually preserves crossing authority separately from material standing. C14 asks whether any authority—independent, scoped or otherwise—silently travels into a different context. Neither implies that independent warrant is universal.

### C7 / C8

C7 governs revocation while the sovereign actor exists. C8 governs account erasure so deletion cannot strand a formerly revocable authority after the holder disappears.

### C8 / C9 / C10 / C12 / C15 / C16

These form a coherent erasure set:

- C16 — correct subject/actor;
- C9 — complete governed disposition;
- C10 — retained lineage;
- C8 — no orphan operative authority;
- C12 — member-visible truth;
- C15 — governable explanation.

None dictates that all retained material must be deleted.

### C13

C13 remains intentionally meta-level: every protection must preserve supported legitimate member acts. It is not redundant with any specific law because it carries the false-refusal failure class across the whole contract.

### C17

C17 governs the semantic meaning of refusal/non-action/uncertainty. C5 governs present standing after rejection/withdrawal. A rejection may end current standing under C5 while still refusing to manufacture the opposite claim under C17.

**Ruling: no internal contradiction requiring removal or merger of a law.**
