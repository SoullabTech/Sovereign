## 2 · Formal source — continuous-time dynamical systems

**Source:** Encyclopedia of Mathematics, “Flow (continuous-time dynamical system)”
**URL:** https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Flow_%28continuous-time_dynamical_system%29

Relevant formal point:

A flow is an action of the additive real group (or non-negative semigroup) on a phase space. Each time corresponds to a transformation, with identity and composition laws. For a fixed starting point, the time-dependent motion is its trajectory/orbit.

**RGR standing:** ESTABLISHED_SOURCE for the mathematical definition.

**RGR consequence:** strong evidence that at least one major formal meaning of Flow is constructible from STATE + TRANSFORMATION + TRAJECTORY.

No claim about human meaning follows from this.

## 3 · Formal source — network flow

**Sources:**

- Stanford CME 305 Network Flow notes: https://stanford.edu/~rezab/classes/cme305/W17/Notes/3.pdf
- Cornell Networks and Markets, Chapter 6: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rafael/networks-html/chapter6.html
- Stanford CS261 notes: https://cs.stanford.edu/people/trevisan/cs261/all-notes-2010.pdf

Relevant formal point:

A network flow is represented as a function assigning values to edges of a directed/capacitated graph, with capacity and conservation constraints.

**RGR standing:** ESTABLISHED_SOURCE for the bounded mathematical definition.

**RGR consequence:** supports representing flow amount as a valued relation/state with BOUNDARY/constraint and COMPATIBILITY/invariance conditions.

## 4 · Formal source — temporal networks

**Source:** Petter Holme and Jari Saramäki, “Temporal Networks,” arXiv:1108.1780
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1780

Relevant formal point:

Network edges may be active at different times; temporal ordering materially changes dynamical behavior. The review explicitly notes that temporal information may be moved from a dynamical system on a network into the network representation itself.

**RGR standing:** ESTABLISHED_SOURCE for the reviewed temporal-network framework.

**RGR consequence:** evidence that dynamic information has multiple legitimate representational placements. This counts against declaring one universal Flow primitive merely from intuitive necessity.

## 5 · Formal source — compositional open dynamical systems

**Source:** John C. Baez and Blake S. Pollard, “A Compositional Framework for Reaction Networks,” arXiv:1704.02051
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02051

Relevant formal point:

Open reaction networks allow entities to flow through designated inputs/outputs, compose by connecting interfaces, and map functorially to open dynamical systems.

**RGR standing:** ESTABLISHED_SOURCE for the paper’s formal construction.

**RGR consequence:** supports treating Flow/process as compositionally first-class without forcing a new universal primitive into RGR’s common scaffold.

## 6 · Formal source — signal-flow and compositional network languages

**Source:** Brendan Fong and David I. Spivak, An Invitation to Applied Category Theory, especially “Signal Flow Graphs” and “Electric Circuits.”
**Publisher:** Cambridge University Press
**Book URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/an-invitation-to-applied-category-theory/D4C5E5C2B019B2F9B8CE9A4E9E84D6BC

Relevant formal point:

Signal-flow graphs, circuits, and other network languages can be treated compositionally, with systems as morphisms and composition expressing how interfaces are connected.

**RGR standing:** ESTABLISHED_SOURCE for the mathematical framework.

**RGR consequence:** further supports a process/flow-centered architecture whose formal substrate remains compositional relations and transformations.
