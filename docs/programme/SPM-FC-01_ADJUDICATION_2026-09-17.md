the locally numbered invariant run, but either representation is acceptable if provenance remains

## 5 · RETURN FOR R2 — TEN SCOPE CORRECTIONS

These clauses contain a sound core but fail at their present generality. None should be discarded;
each should be narrowed so the law says exactly what the evidence and the rest of the contract can
support.

### I-1 · destructive subject authority — NARROW

**Defect.** "The subject of a destructive act is resolved from verified server-side identity" is
universal. F5 established this for a member exercising a sovereign destructive act on their own
material. The sentence, as written, would also outlaw separately governed delegated or
administrative acts merely because actor and subject differ.

**R2 law:**

> For a member-initiated destructive act presented as that member's sovereign act, the subject is
> resolved from verified server-side identity. A caller-supplied target may be used only to detect
> and refuse disagreement. Any delegated destructive act requires its own explicit authority chain;
> caller assertion alone never supplies it.

### I-3 · adjacency — distinguish accidental from intentionally declared scope

**Defect.** The evidence proves accidental lexical-prefix protection and a gated UI that confers
nothing on its API. The present wording can also be read to reject a deliberately declared
namespace policy covering child routes.

**R2 law:**

> Authority must come from a rule whose declared scope intentionally includes the surface. An
> accidental lexical-prefix match, neighboring name, or gate on a different surface does not confer
> authority. A deliberately declared namespace rule may govern its named descendants.

### I-4 · provenance — distinguish trusted provenance from caller-declared metadata

**Defect.** "Never supplied by the caller" is too broad for imported works, citations, or other
material where a member may legitimately declare an author or source. D9-B earned the narrower
requirement that a caller cannot manufacture **trusted system provenance** for the artifact being
written.

**R2 law:**

> System-trusted provenance for the immediate stored artifact is minted or verified by the writing
> boundary, not accepted as trusted merely because the caller supplied it. Caller-declared
> attribution may be stored as declared metadata, but it does not become trusted provenance without
> an explicit verification or adoption act.

### I-7 · refusal disclosure — reconcile with I-29

**Defect.** "A refusal must not reveal what it is refusing about" collides with I-29, which correctly
requires an authorized member to receive the governed reason for a refusal or partial-retention
outcome. The examples behind I-7 protect existence, ownership, and material the caller is not
entitled to know; they do not earn generic opacity.

**R2 law:**

> A refusal must not disclose protected existence, ownership, or material the caller lacks authority
> to know. Where the caller is entitled to the governed outcome of their own act, the refusal must
> still provide the truthful reason and change-state required by I-29 without leaking protected
> facts about another subject.

### I-10 · participation scope — permit explicit global scope

**Defect.** The law correctly requires a purpose/consumer dimension, but "a single boolean ...
 carries no such dimension" is not universally true. A control can encode a governed global or
wildcard scope if that meaning is explicit and enforced.

**R2 law:**

> Participation authority must carry an explicit executable scope over consumer, purpose, room, or
> a deliberately defined wildcard covering them. A bare control whose governed semantics do not
> identify or resolve that scope is insufficient.

### I-16 · object binding — do not turn every warrant into an exact-version warrant

**Defect.** D9 earned exact binding for object/version-specific authorization acts. The present law
universalizes that mechanism to every warrant, which is incompatible with relationship- or
audience-level warrants such as the D9-C Circles specimen and with any intentionally class-scoped
authorization.

**R2 law:**

> An object- or version-bound authorization does not travel to changed wording or a changed object
> unless its declared scope explicitly permits that transformation. Strengthening belief never
> enlarges the warrant. A class-, relationship-, or audience-scoped warrant may cover multiple
> objects only to the extent its own declared scope says so.

### I-20 · multi-sovereign disposition — preserve the principle, not only two mechanisms

**Defect.** F5-A observed refusal and tombstone at every located multi-sovereign boundary. That earns
protection of the other sovereign's independently governed record; it does not prove that refusal
and tombstone are the only legitimate mechanisms.

**R2 law:**

> One sovereign's erasure or withdrawal act may not silently destroy another sovereign's
> independently governed record or stake. Refusal and tombstone are valid dispositions, but another
> explicit disposition may pass if it preserves the other sovereign's governed interest and states
> truthfully what changed.

### I-25 · erasure verification — make the law storage-semantic, not `stat`-specific

**Defect.** `destroyVaultBytes` gives an excellent positive specimen: it verifies absence and refuses
to claim success over surviving bytes. But not every trustworthy store needs a literal second read
or filesystem-style absence check. Transactional guarantees, returned effects, or durable erasure
obligations may establish the postcondition differently.

**R2 law:**

> Where erasure is promised, completion must be established by evidence sufficient to the storage
> mechanism; successful invocation of a delete attempt alone is not proof. If completion is still
> owed, the system records that owed state and does not report completed erasure.

The existing adversarial case — make the underlying store silently refuse removal — remains good.
PASS is that the failure or owed state surfaces; FAIL is success reported over surviving material.

### I-26 · lineage — reconcile with explicit anonymization / de-linking

**Defect.** The current law requires every persistent derivative to name its exact source. F5's own
standing constraint allows explicit anonymized and transformed dispositions. In some governed
cases, deliberately destroying linkability is the intended privacy property; retaining exact source
identity would be the wrong result.

**R2 law:**

> Where a persistent derivative remains governed in relation to source material, the record carries
> enough lineage to explain and enforce source-dependent authority and disposition. If lineage is
> deliberately destroyed by an explicit anonymization or de-linking disposition, that loss of
> traceability is itself governed and may not later be represented as source-traceable.

### I-32 · manifestability — allow governed verification, forbid forensic reconstruction

**Defect.** The law is sound, but the adversarial test requires producing a complete disposition
"without querying the resulting state." F5 did not earn a prohibition on verification reads. It
earned a prohibition on claiming governance because one can reconstruct, after the fact, what
incidental FK behavior happened to do.

**R2 law:**

> The complete disposition is explainable from the governed plan plus the mechanism's recorded
> execution and verification. Verification may inspect the resulting state when that inspection is
> itself part of the governed mechanism. What may not substitute for manifestability is forensic
> reconstruction from incidental final state after an unaccounted act.

**R2 adversarial case:** perform the act, then require a complete disposition manifest using only
its governed plan, recorded execution, and governed verification evidence. PASS if producible;
FAIL if the answer depends on discovering ungoverned incidental behavior afterward.

## 6 · CROSS-CONTRACT COLLISIONS THAT FORCED THE RETURN

The R2 corrections are not stylistic. Three attacks produce direct internal conflicts if the present
language is read literally:

1. **I-7 × I-29** — generic refusal opacity versus member-visible governed reasons. Both cannot hold
   without identifying what information must remain non-disclosing.
2. **I-16 × I-18** — every authorization exact-version-bound versus an audience/relationship warrant
   whose authority lives on membership rather than one exact artifact version.
3. **I-26 × Standing Constraint 1.2** — exact source lineage for every persistent derivative versus
   the contract's own admission that anonymization and transformation can be legitimate explicit
   dispositions.

A constitutional contract must survive its own composition. These three collisions are sufficient
on their own to withhold ratification even if every individual evidence citation is accurate.
