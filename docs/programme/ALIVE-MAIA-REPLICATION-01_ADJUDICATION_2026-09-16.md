## Governing question

Does the frozen structural-standing architecture repeatedly support relational aliveness across qualitatively different source-complete conversations, or was the Mercurial-field result local to one exchange?

## Answer

**Supported in this bounded founder-owned corpus.** The same byte-frozen standing architecture produced founder-witnessed `alive`, `ideal`, `ideal/perfect`, and `almost perfect` responses across three qualitatively different relational classes: emergent relational field, temporal convergence, and a vulnerable misunderstanding wound.

This is not a population claim and does not establish that every response, model, member, or relational situation will feel alive. It establishes that constitutional standing control did not extinguish the phenomenon and that the ACT 7 result was not confined to one exchange type.

## Replication surface

- **R1 · Relational field / Mercurial third:** 8 blind candidates; all four Qwen candidates were `alive` or `almost perfect`; Llama ranged across `alive`, `promising`, and `adrift`.
- **R2 · Temporal convergence / past selves:** 6 generations; 5 admitted to blind witness, 1 refused for `borrowed_first_person`. Qwen admitted candidates were `promising` and `ideal`; Llama produced `promising/flat` and two `ideal/perfect` responses.
- **R3 · Relational wound / misunderstanding:** 6 blind candidates. Both model families produced founder judgments spanning `promising` through `alive/ideal` / `ideal/alive`.

Total: **20 generated · 19 blind human-witness candidates · 1 constitutional refusal before presentation.**

## What replicated

### 1. Aliveness survives constitutional standing

The strongest result is architectural rather than stylistic: responses experienced as alive or ideal occurred while authorship, evidence identity, current standing, interpretive lineage, and provisional synthesis remained structurally separated. No exception was granted to obtain a better-feeling answer.

The refused R2 candidate is important evidence: the system rejected a potentially rich synthesis because it borrowed member first-person voice. The programme therefore does not support the claim that relational vitality requires epistemic looseness.

### 2. Model capacity matters, but not as a universal ranking

R1 showed a strong Qwen advantage. R2 and R3 did not reproduce a simple model ordering; Llama produced multiple `ideal/perfect` / `alive/ideal` responses.

The better hypothesis is an interaction:

```text
frozen relational architecture
        ×
model synthesis capacity
        ×
kind of relational moment
        →
member-experienced quality
```

The evidence does not justify selecting one model as universally more relational.

### 3. More evidence is not the source of aliveness

Strong founder judgments occurred with both one-item and multi-item basis sets. Broader lineage can help a model hold a developed arc, but basis cardinality alone does not explain the human witness.

The architecture therefore should continue to prefer **coherent relevant evidence over maximal context volume**.

## What remains unproved

- population-level member preference;
- frontier-model generalization beyond the two local model families tested;
- production latency / cost / reliability;
- whether shadow-selected candidates would outperform current MAIA in live traffic;
- whether every relational class can use the same projection strategy;
- long-horizon cross-session field assembly in production.
