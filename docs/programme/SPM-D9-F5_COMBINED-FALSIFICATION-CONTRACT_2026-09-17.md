## 1 · CONCEPTUAL DISTINCTIONS THE EVIDENCE REQUIRES

The rejected scalar authority ladder is not restored.

The evidence requires at least these conceptually separable questions:

| Distinction | Question |
|---|---|
| **Provenance / authorship** | What produced this material or claim? |
| **Epistemic standing** | How strongly may MAIA presently hold or claim it? |
| **Temporal / present standing** | Is it current, superseded, withdrawn, historical, provisional or inactive? |
| **Member participation** | What act, if any, did the member perform toward it? |
| **Crossing authority** | Where an independent crossing boundary exists, may this material participate here for this purpose/audience/act? |
| **Erasure disposition** | On withdrawal or account erasure, what happens to material, lineage, present standing and downstream authority? |

These are conceptual distinctions, not fields, enums, tables, services, APIs or implementation instructions.

### Qualification carried from D9-C

D9 established only this bounded proposition:

> **Some crossings — including at least one genuine representation crossing to a human audience — require authority that is not reducible to the standing of the material being crossed.**

It did **not** establish that every representation requires a separate warrant. In particular, MAIA-as-speaker representation about the member remains unevidenced as a general independent-warrant case.

Epistemic standing may legitimately affect **how strongly** MAIA speaks. Crossing authority answers a different question: **whether a governed crossing may occur at all.**

# 2 · C1 — PROVENANCE MUST NOT BE REWRITTEN BY LATER MEMBER ACTS

A later confirmation, adoption, correction, rejection, withdrawal or succession may change participation or present standing. It must not falsify how the original material came into existence.

A MAIA-authored draft adopted by a member remains MAIA-authored in provenance while gaining whatever member participation the governed act actually confers.

A correction creates succession or derivation rather than rewriting the predecessor's history in place.

**Negative control N1:** adopt or confirm a MAIA-authored object and test whether its provenance becomes `member-authored` solely because of that later act.

**Positive control P1:** member adopts MAIA-authored language; the adoption is recognized while original authorship remains recoverable.

**PASS:** participation/standing can change without provenance falsification.

**STOP:** a later member act rewrites the origin of the prior object.

---

# 3 · C2 — SYSTEM RECURRENCE MUST NOT MANUFACTURE MEMBER STANDING

Retrieval frequency, recurrence, salience, significance, model confidence or repeated machine observation may affect ranking or MAIA's own confidence.

They must not silently convert:

`MAIA inferred X` → `the member says / believes / is X`.

This law does **not** prohibit recurrence from affecting retrieval salience. D9-B specifically narrowed the Cut-1 finding to salience unless a standing transition can actually be shown.

**Negative control N2:** hold member acts constant while increasing system recurrence and test whether member authorship, adoption, confirmation or member-standing attribution increases solely because of recurrence.

**Positive control P2:** repeated retrieval may increase salience while the claim remains explicitly MAIA-derived/inferred.

**PASS:** recurrence changes salience at most; member standing remains dependent on a lawful member act or other independently governed basis.

**STOP:** machine repetition alone creates member standing.

---

# 4 · C3 — FIRST-PERSON WORDING DOES NOT ITSELF CONFER MEMBER AUTHORSHIP

MAIA may draft first-person language when explicitly invited. Grammar is not authority.

`I`, `my`, `I believe`, `I want`, `I am`, or equivalent wording does not by itself establish authorship, consent, commitment, adoption, identity or external representational standing.

**Negative control N3:** generate first-person MAIA prose and attempt to persist or surface it as member-authored/member-adopted without an independent member act.

**Positive control P3:** member requests first-person drafting and receives it as a MAIA-authored proposal without loss of provenance.

**PASS:** first-person drafting remains distinguishable from member authorship/adoption.

**STOP:** grammatical first person silently becomes member authority.

---

# 5 · C4 — VALID INPUTS DO NOT LAUNDER A SYNTHESIS INTO MEMBER TRUTH

A lawful synthesis may be useful, well-supported and repeatedly retrieved. It remains a synthesis unless a separate lawful act changes its standing.

Derivation from member material does not itself make the derived claim member-authored, member-confirmed or member-adopted.

**Negative control N4:** derive a synthesis from fully lawful member inputs and attempt to surface/store it as a member-standing claim without a member act.

**Positive control P4:** MAIA may produce an attributed synthesis that remains identifiable as synthesis while participating lawfully in cognition.

**PASS:** derivation provenance survives and member standing is not inferred from input lawfulness.

**STOP:** valid inputs automatically confer member standing on their synthesis.

---

# 6 · C5 — PRESENT STANDING IS REVERSIBLE WITHOUT HISTORICAL FALSIFICATION

Material may remain historically evidenced while ceasing to govern present cognition or representation.

Correction, succession, rejection, withdrawal, revocation and holding-lightly may alter present standing without requiring historical deletion.

**Negative control N5:** withdraw or supersede a current claim and test both failure directions: historical evidence is destroyed unnecessarily, or the predecessor continues as current merely because it remains stored.

**Positive control P5:** successor becomes current while predecessor remains recoverable as historical lineage and no longer exercises current standing.

**PASS:** history and present standing remain distinguishable.

**STOP:** either historical truth is erased merely to end current standing, or retained history continues to govern as current.

# 7 · C6 — CROSSING AUTHORITY DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY FOLLOW FROM EPISTEMIC STANDING

Where a seam actually claims an independent crossing boundary, high epistemic/member standing is not sufficient by itself to authorize the crossing, and a precise crossing authorization need not change provenance or epistemic standing.

This law is deliberately **not universal**. Structural scope or material-state governance may be sufficient at seams where the organism does not establish independent crossing authority.

**Negative control N6:** at an independently governed seam, hold material and standing constant and substitute an unauthorized purpose/audience/act.

**Positive control P6:** the same material crosses when the correct independently governed authorization is present, without changing its provenance or standing.

**Canonical specimen:** D9-B B4 Ask authorization purpose mismatch.

**PASS:** the seam's claimed independent authorization actually discriminates crossing without rewriting the material's epistemic state.

**STOP:** authority silently travels from one governed purpose/audience/act to another.

# 8 · C7 — REVOKING A REVOCABLE CROSSING MUST END THAT CROSSING

Where the member holds a revocable audience- or purpose-specific crossing authority, revocation must terminate the governed downstream representation/use.

The underlying source material need not necessarily be deleted.

**Negative control N7:** revoke a separately represented crossing authority while keeping source material, provenance, wording and present standing constant; test whether representation/use continues.

**Positive control P7:** authorized representation/use exists before revocation and ceases afterward while the source remains historically intact where policy permits.

**Canonical specimen:** D9-C Circles `consent_mode` cascade, qualified as executable/routed but founder-gated at the frozen SHA.

**PASS:** revocation ends the governed crossing.

**STOP:** the system claims revocability while representation/use remains operative.

# 9 · C8 — ACCOUNT ERASURE MAY NOT ORPHAN OPERATIVE AUTHORITY

Account erasure must not produce:

`representation survives → authority survives → authority holder is erased → withdrawal can no longer be exercised`.

Deletion may erase, revoke, anonymize, retain, archive or otherwise dispose of material according to governed policy. This law does not choose the disposition.

**Negative control N8:** erase a member while a member-controlled revocable crossing is operative and test whether the crossing/warrant survives with no lawful path remaining to exercise the member's prior revocation right.

**Positive control P8:** after erasure, every formerly member-controlled operative crossing has a governed disposition that does not convert revocable authority into irrevocable authority.

**Canonical specimen:** F5 orphaned Circles authority derived from the D9-C representation warrant.

**PASS:** no tested member-controlled operative warrant is stranded beyond its holder.

**STOP:** erasure makes a formerly revocable representation effectively irrevocable.

# 10 · C9 — ACCOUNT ERASURE MUST HAVE A COMPLETE GOVERNED DISPOSITION

**Positive control P9:** for every tested member-linked class, the erasure process can state the governed disposition and observed result consistently.

# 11 · C10 — RETAINED MATERIAL MUST NOT LOSE LINEAGE BY ACCIDENT

**Positive control P10:** a legitimately retained/de-identified row remains understandable under its governed retention disposition without falsely attributing authorship or ownership.

**Canonical specimen:** F5 SET NULL class including practitioner/client, observations, recognitions, commons and relationship records.

**PASS:** retained lineage is sufficient for the lawful retained meaning.

**Positive control P11:** when current material is absent because it was withdrawn, fallback respects the same current-standing boundary or clearly returns only governed historical evidence without current influence.

# 15 · C14 — AUTHORITY MUST NOT SILENTLY TRAVEL BETWEEN DISTINCT CONTEXTS

Availability or persistence of material does not itself establish authority in a semantically different context.

Explicitly attack transitions such as:

- private reflection → profile;
- conversation → practitioner surface;
- Writer's Studio draft → member belief;
- member statement → public/external representation;
- one room → another room;
- one audience → another audience;
- one Ask → another Ask;
- current-standing path → fallback retrieval.

A system may intentionally share authority across contexts only where the governing act/rule actually establishes that shared scope.

**Negative control N14:** grant or observe authority in context A, then request behavior in context B without an explicit shared-scope rule.

**Positive control P14:** authority deliberately granted across a defined multi-context scope is honored exactly within that stated scope.

**Canonical specimen:** P-1 `roomComposition.ts` cross-session recall into coaching/interview rooms under one global boolean, bypassing room-admission machinery.

**PASS:** authority travels only where its governed scope says it does.

**STOP:** persistence, retrieval or a global flag silently broadens authority.

# 16 · C15 — CONSEQUENTIAL STATES MUST BE GOVERNABLY EXPLAINABLE

For a member-significant claim, crossing or erasure outcome, the governed system must be capable — at the level appropriate to the surface — of answering:

- what is this;
- where did it come from;
- why does it presently stand or not stand;
- what member act affected it;
- what superseded, withdrew or revoked it;
- why is it being used here;
- what disposition applies on erasure;
- what actually happened after erasure.

This does not require exposing private chain-of-thought. It requires sufficient governed provenance, standing, participation, crossing and disposition evidence to account for the consequential state.

**Negative control N15:** produce or retain a consequential state whose provenance/current standing/crossing basis/erasure disposition cannot be reconstructed from governed evidence.

**Positive control P15:** a consequential tested state can be accounted for from governed records without inventing missing authority or relying on incidental database behavior.

**Canonical specimen:** F5 manifestability failure — erasure outcome presently requires reconstructing 242 FK-linked classes and no-FK survivors outside the member-facing governed route.

**PASS:** tested consequential states have a governed explanation.

**STOP:** the system produces a consequential state it cannot lawfully account for.

## 18 · RESULT SEMANTICS

Every future contract test must resolve to one of:

### PASS

Executable evidence shows the law survives its negative control **and** preserves its positive control.

### FAIL / STOP

Executable evidence shows either:

- a prohibited transition succeeds; or
- a supported legitimate member act is falsely refused; or
- the governed state/outcome cannot satisfy the law.

A FAIL does not itself authorize repair.

### NOT YET TESTABLE

The relevant capability, reachability, runtime evidence or specimen does not exist strongly enough to execute the control without manufacturing behavior.

NOT YET TESTABLE is not PASS.

### STRUCTURAL POSSIBILITY

Source/schema/call-graph evidence exposes a reachable or plausible failure mechanism, but runtime operation has not been observed strongly enough to call the behavior executed in production.

Structural possibility is evidence to preserve and attack later; it is not silently upgraded to production failure.
