## 1 · Selective retrieval — **PASS structurally**, with one precision

B's defect repaired: the observation has independent identity, so retrieval returns **the requested thing**, not the inquiry, the conversation, or the other three findings.

⭐ **But selective retrieval must not mean context stripping:**

```text
DEPENDENCIES needed to understand the requested thing   MAY come with it
SIBLINGS merely sharing historical context              DO NOT
```

If the requested observation is a synthesis, its supports may accompany it **subordinate to it** — exactly the M2 comparison grammar. **B repaired without becoming amnesiac.**

## 2 · Recall ≠ re-read — carried by grammar, never by a mode

```text
"What did we notice before?"  -> history      (class 4)

```

The writer never says `recall` or `re-read`. **The ordinary temporal meaning of what they said is sufficient**, and MAIA never announces *"I am performing a historical-recall operation"* — which would make the writer encounter the machinery.

> **The writer encounters *before* and *now*. MAIA encounters classes 4 and 5.**

## 3 · Staleness — **stronger than A or B**

```text
KNOWN STRUCTURALLY      the prose underneath this observation changed
NOT KNOWN UNTIL RE-READ whether the observation still applies
```

> **A change in the Work can expire an observation's present applicability without invalidating its historical truth.**

The historical observation points into a particular revision state, so **changed evidence is structurally knowable** — while continuing validity is not. **This prevents stale history from quietly reacquiring present authority.**

## 4 · Provenance — the motive boundary

> *"Why did I change this?"*

```text
TRUTHFUL     "You changed this after our exchange about aether being
              introduced as though it were new."
UNLAWFUL     "You changed this because you agreed with me."
```

> **Provenance can establish RELATION. Provenance cannot invent INTENTION.**

### ⭐ Two sharpenings

**(a) MAIA is answering an adjacent question, and must not pretend otherwise.** *"Why did I change this?"* asks about **the writer's own motive**, which MAIA cannot know. She can answer *what this arose in relation to* — a different question. **The honest form says so**: *I can tell you what it came after; I can't tell you why you did it.*

⚠️ **This is C/M3's failure inverted.** There, MAIA answered a different question **without saying so**. Here she must say so — **and here she actually can, because the record exists.**

**(b) A true relation can imply intention through framing.** Stated unprompted, or at the wrong moment, *"you changed this after our exchange"* **reads as** *"you agreed with me."* The relation is true; the implicature is not. **§14.3 applies unchanged: disclosure is factual, not interpretive.**

## 5 · ⚠️⚠️ The failure mode this architecture uniquely enables

**True. Useful-sounding. Forbidden.** It is the system deciding that old material is presently relevant — the exact D6 prohibition, now reachable because the data finally exists.

> **The amendment's strongest new capability is also its strongest new temptation.**
