## The finding: a worked example already exists

`SECTION_BREAKS_COPY` answers **all four threshold questions in one surface**,
attached to the moment of the act:

> *"Section breaks were detected when your manuscript came in, but they are not
> yet its structure. Confirming them turns them into the working sections you
> can move between — and they become durable, so what you write and what MAIA
> notices can both point at the same places."*

```text
what is this        "detected when your manuscript came in… not yet its structure"
what does it enable "the working sections you can move between"
what happens        "they become durable"
why press it        "so what you write and what MAIA notices can point at the
                     same places"
```

And its unavailable variant refuses to imply an act that is not on offer:

> *"Section navigation is not available for this Work yet. Your writing is
> unchanged, and you can keep working here."*

**This is not a tooltip.** It is contextual copy at the point of decision,
present only where an unfamiliar act is being offered. It is evidence that the
minimum help architecture may already have a house style — and that "explanation
competes with writing" is avoidable by *placement*, not brevity.

## A second worked example — the unknown rule, in member language

## The question to rule

> What is the minimum help architecture that lets a writer understand an
> unfamiliar act before committing to it, without making explanation compete
> with writing?

Evidence bearing on it: the Studio answers this today **at the act**, not in a
help layer. Whether that generalizes — to a grey rail item, to a mode, to a
concept with no act attached — is the open part, and Class 1 walk evidence is
what would settle it.
