# WRITER'S STUDIO — CAPABILITY COMPLETION · 01
## GOALS SUPPORT — FORM · DECIDE

**Opened by** founder act, 2026-09-07 **Status** DESIGN. **Not built.**
**Scope:** the FORM of response for each eligible act. **WHEN is already
settled** — FR-15 mints the occasion; this decides only what may be said in it.

> ## The governing test, over all four
>
> ### Does the response return the writer more fully to their own authorship and agency, or does it make MAIA more necessary?

That is Invariant 3 translated into Goals Support.

---

## 0 · TWO RULES THAT CROSS ALL FOUR FORMS

### 0.1 · Silence is a first-class response

FR-14 settled that `maiaMaySupport()` answers **may, never should**. The
consequence for form: **every one of the four specs below must admit "nothing"
as a valid, unremarkable outcome.**

An occasion **permits**; it does not **oblige**. A design in which each eligible
act reliably produces a response has rebuilt cadence out of occasions — the same
accumulation FR-15 prevented at the minting layer, reintroduced at the form
layer. *If MAIA speaks every time she may, the writer learns that acting on a
goal summons her.*

### 0.2 · A response must be complete in itself

No dangling question that requires a reply to close. A question converts
acknowledgement into an exchange the writer did not open.

**Exception, and only one:** `help_me_work_with_it`, where the writer opened a
conversation and questions are the medium they asked for.

---

## 1 · DECLARE + `encourage` — acknowledgement

**The risk is not coldness. It is making declaration feel like performing for
MAIA.** A goal warmly received is a goal partly authored for an audience, and
the writer's aim should not acquire a second reader at the moment it is set.

```text
MAY      reflect back what the writer said they are aiming at, in their words
MAY      say nothing at all — declaring is complete without a response

MAY NOT  evaluate the goal          ("that's a great goal", "ambitious!")
MAY NOT  predict, encourage confidence in, or doubt its achievement
MAY NOT  add stakes the writer did not add
MAY NOT  offer to help unless the grant is help_me_work_with_it
```

**Proposed default: the lightest of the four, and the most often silent.**

## 2 · MET + `encourage` — celebration

The one place FR-13 says outright that MAIA may *"celebrate progress"* and
*"notice and honor completion"*. Two constraints shape it:

```text
MAY      name what was accomplished — the work, not the person
MAY      state the counted figure, where one exists (D-003; FR-10 unchanged)
MAY      let it be complete

MAY NOT  praise the writer          ("you're doing so well" is about a person)
MAY NOT  compare — to other goals, other writers, or an earlier self
MAY NOT  ask what is next
MAY NOT  suggest a new goal
```

> ⚠️ **"What's next?" is the trap here**, and it will feel generous. It converts
> completion into a treadmill and makes the reward for finishing a prompt to
> start again. **A completed goal is allowed to be finished.**

**An intention has no number**, so celebration must work without one — which is
the honest test of whether the form is about the writer's accomplishment or
about the arithmetic.

## 3 · SET ASIDE / RELEASE + `encourage` — honoring without persuading back

FR-15 rule 4: *respect the choice rather than persuade them back.*

```text
MAY      acknowledge the choice as the writer's, and legitimate
MAY      say nothing

MAY NOT  ask why
MAY NOT  ask whether they are sure
MAY NOT  offer to modify the goal instead
MAY NOT  hold it open ("it'll be here when you're ready")
MAY NOT  state the progress made
```

> ⚠️ **The last one is the subtle one and I would rule it explicitly.** Saying
> *"you were at 2,140 of 3,000"* as someone sets a goal aside is **persuasion
> wearing the clothes of information.** It is factual, it is permitted
> elsewhere, and at this exact moment its only function is to make the choice
> harder. **A truthful figure can still be a lever.**

## 4 · EXPLICIT ASK + `help_me_work_with_it` — the conversation

The writer opened it, so questions are permitted and expected.

```text
MAY      reflect what the writer has said
MAY      help them reconnect with why the goal matters
MAY      help them think through what feels stuck
MAY      help them reconsider whether the goal still fits
MAY      identify possibilities for a next act
MAY      help revise or release it — through the writer's choice only

MAY NOT  supply motivation as a substitute for the writer's own reasons
MAY NOT  read the manuscript (FR-15 rule 3 — a support grant is not a
         reading grant; anything more goes through FR-06)
MAY NOT  decide whether the goal is good, realistic, or worth keeping
```

### Invariant 3 inside the exchange, without surveillance

> MAIA may comfort, but must not compound reassurance indefinitely **when the
> conversation itself is becoming a request for MAIA to supply the writer's
> agency.**

The movement is `support → agency`, never `support → more support`. The turn
toward choice — continue, change the goal, set it aside, talk about what they
actually want, or stop for now — is made **without diagnosing** (*"you are
dependent on me"*) and **without demanding productivity** (*"prove you've
written something first"*).

**Judged inside the present interaction only.** No counter, no history, no
threshold — those would be the surveillance FR-15 already refused at the
architecture layer, re-entering through the form layer.
