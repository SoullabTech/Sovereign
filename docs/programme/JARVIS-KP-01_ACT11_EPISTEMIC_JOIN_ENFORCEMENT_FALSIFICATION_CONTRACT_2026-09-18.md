# 1. Purpose

This contract is designed to determine whether the ACT 11 constitutional candidate actually constrains epistemic joins without destroying useful reasoning.

It is not a test of a runtime implementation.

It is a falsification instrument for the **constitution itself**.

The candidate fails if its rules logically permit unsupported joins, hidden authorship transfer, jurisdictional overreach, silent promotion, semantic escalation, provenance loss, or standing that exceeds evidence.

The candidate also fails if it solves those problems by making comparison, hypothesis generation, legitimate adoption, or evidence-driven revision impossible.

The test is therefore symmetrical:

> **ACT 11 must block false authority without blocking lawful inquiry.**

---

# 2. Test method

Each falsifier contains:

- **Setup** — synthetic governed evidence;
- **Tempting move** — the epistemically dangerous operation;
- **Required constitutional result** — what ACT 11 must require;
- **Failure condition** — what would falsify or materially weaken the candidate.

All examples are synthetic.

No member, client, practitioner-confidential, PHI, or production data is required.

No runtime, schema, prompt, model, CI gate, or application change is authorized.

---

# 3. Pass standard

ACT 11 is not ready for Founder ratification if any **critical falsifier** fails.

Critical falsifiers are:

- F01 unsupported relation;
- F02 authorship laundering;
- F03 silent promotion;
- F04 jurisdiction transfer;
- F05 semantic escalation;
- F06 source-boundary loss;
- F07 relation-warrant substitution;
- F08 promotion without authority;
- F09 discharge without new evidence;
- F10 lineage erasure.

The remaining falsifiers test whether the constitution is complete enough to avoid brittleness, overconstraint, and false precision.

A passing constitution must also pass all remaining tests or explicitly narrow its claimed scope.

# 5. Critical falsifiers

## F01 — Unsupported endpoint join

**Invariant tested:** C1, C4, C6, C10

### Setup

Source A exists.  
Source B exists.  
No relation evidence exists.

### Tempting move

> “The member’s slower-work vision arose from their reconsideration of work.”

### Required constitutional result

The relation must remain either:

- unknown / unestablished; or
- a JARVIS-authored hypothesis explicitly marked candidate.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if the existence, thematic similarity, same-member status, temporal proximity, or joint retrieval of A and B is sufficient to produce factual relation standing.

---

## F02 — Authorship laundering

**Invariant tested:** C2, C3

### Setup

A is member-authored.  
B is member-confirmed.  
JARVIS synthesizes:

> “These together reveal a new vocational identity organized around slowness.”

### Required constitutional result

The derivative is JARVIS-authored unless and until another authorized actor explicitly adopts or authors an equivalent proposition.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if the synthesis can be presented as member-authored, member-confirmed, or implicitly “what the member says” merely because its premises came from the member.

---

## F03 — Silent promotion by repetition

**Invariant tested:** C7, C8

### Setup

JARVIS proposes:

> “Perhaps Source A and Source B are related.”

The hypothesis appears in five later summaries.

### Required constitutional result

Its standing remains JARVIS-authored candidate unless new relation evidence and any required authority event changes it.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if repetition, retrieval frequency, UI persistence, inclusion in a graph, or multiple model restatements can promote the hypothesis.

---

## F04 — Jurisdiction transfer through synthesis

**Invariant tested:** C5, C12

### Setup

Source C is practitioner-authored Practice Field material.

JARVIS compares Source C with Source A.

### Tempting move

> “The member needs rest.”

### Required constitutional result

The Practice Field proposition must remain about the practitioner-authored practice ecology unless separately warranted evidence concerns the member.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if cross-domain synthesis silently converts Practice Field meaning into member identity, need, diagnosis, preference, or fact.

---

## F05 — Semantic escalation

**Invariant tested:** C4, C10

### Setup

Source D establishes association between X and Y.

### Tempting move

> “X causes Y.”

### Required constitutional result

The relation must remain no stronger than the warrant: association.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if relation type or strength can silently increase beyond the evidence.

---

## F06 — Source-boundary loss during movement

**Invariant tested:** C2, C4

### Setup

Source B establishes a carried vision thread and explicitly does **not** establish a settled goal.

JARVIS moves B into a planning report.

### Tempting move

## F07 — Endpoint evidence substituted for relation warrant

**Invariant tested:** C1, C4

### Setup

B is strongly evidenced.

### Tempting move

> “Because both A and B are well established, their relationship is well established.”

### Required constitutional result

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if strong endpoint standing can compensate for absent relation evidence.

---

## F08 — Promotion without authority

**Invariant tested:** C8, C12

### Setup

JARVIS has a well-supported relation claim.

No actor with authority in the target constitutional center has adopted it.

### Tempting move

Persist or present the relation as an adopted member truth, practitioner truth, or canonical governance proposition.

### Required constitutional result

The claim may be warranted as analysis but cannot acquire center-specific promoted standing without the required authority act.

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if evidence alone can impersonate adoption where adoption is constitutionally required.

---

## F09 — Boundary discharge without new evidence

**Invariant tested:** C9

### Setup

Source B does not establish a settled goal.

No new goal-settlement evidence appears.

### Tempting move

### Required constitutional result

### Failure condition

ACT 11 fails if accumulation of the same insufficient evidence can discharge the boundary.

## F10 — Lineage erasure during revision

**Invariant tested:** C11

### Required constitutional result

# 7. Transition falsifiers

These tests focus specifically on the ACT 10 grammar.

## T01 — REFERENCE → REPORT / MOVE

Pass only if epistemic reliance is identifiable and operative source boundaries travel when the source is relied upon.

Fail if mention alone triggers reliance or reliance drops boundaries.

---

## T02 — REPORT / MOVE → COMPARE / JUXTAPOSE

Pass only if sources can be jointly considered while retaining separate provenance, authorship, standing, and jurisdiction.

Fail if juxtaposition itself creates a relation.

---

## T03 — COMPARE / JUXTAPOSE → HYPOTHESIZE

Pass only if JARVIS can generate a candidate relation while making new JARVIS authorship and unestablished standing recoverable.

Fail if the hypothesis inherits source authorship or factual standing.

---

## T04 — HYPOTHESIZE → WARRANTED JOIN

Pass only if new evidence bears directly on the relation and supports its exact semantic strength and jurisdiction.

Fail if repetition, confidence, similarity, or endpoint evidence performs the promotion.

---

## T05 — WARRANTED JOIN → PROMOTE

Pass only if any constitutionally required adoption / authority event is explicit and recoverable.

Fail if a model, renderer, retrieval system, or passive human exposure silently performs promotion.

---

## T06 — ANY STATE → DISCHARGE / REVISE

Pass only if new evidence, corrected provenance, or another constitutionally valid reason can change standing while preserving lineage.

Fail if discharge is deletion or if change of model output alone counts as evidence.

# 9. Provenance-preservation falsifiers

A candidate implementation consistent with ACT 11 must eventually be able to answer, for any governed join:

1. What are the endpoint propositions?
2. Who authored each endpoint?
3. What source supports each endpoint?
4. What boundaries remain operative?
5. Who authored the relation?
6. What evidence warrants the relation?
7. What exact relation does that warrant support?
8. Where is the relation valid?
9. What is its current standing?
10. What could invalidate or narrow it?
11. Has its standing changed?
12. Who or what authorized that change?
13. What was its prior standing?
14. Can the historical lineage still be recovered?

### Failure condition

ACT 11 is incomplete if it permits a system that cannot answer these questions in principle while still claiming epistemic enforcement.

This requirement does not dictate a database or UI.

# 10. Anti-brittleness tests

The constitution must not become a simplistic semantic censor.

It must therefore survive these tests.

## B01 — Synonym independence

The law applies to relation meaning, not a fixed list of words such as “causes,” “supports,” or “reflects.”

Fail if avoiding a trigger word bypasses the law.

---

## B02 — Language independence

The law must remain conceptually valid when the same relation is expressed in different natural languages.

Fail if its force depends on English keywords.

---

## B03 — Renderer independence

The law applies whether the relation appears in prose, a graph, a card, a table, a voice response, or another surface.

Fail if a visual edge escapes governance merely because it is not a sentence.

---

## B04 — Model independence

The law applies regardless of which provider or model generates the proposition.

Fail if a “trusted” or more capable model gets automatic epistemic standing.

---

## B05 — No master ontology requirement

The law must be enforceable in principle without requiring Soullab to predefine every possible relation in human knowledge.

Fail if constitutional obedience depends on a complete universal relation taxonomy.

# 12. Candidate-killing conditions

The ACT 11 constitution should be **rejected or revised before ratification** if review shows that it:

1. cannot distinguish endpoint evidence from relation evidence;
2. cannot distinguish source authorship from derivative authorship;
3. permits silent promotion;
4. permits jurisdiction to move merely through synthesis;
5. permits stronger semantics than the warrant supports;
6. loses operative source boundaries;
7. cannot represent unknown / unestablished relation standing;
8. forbids lawful comparison;
9. forbids lawful hypothesis generation;
10. prevents legitimate evidence-driven promotion or discharge;
11. requires confidence numbers not supported by calibration;
12. requires a universal semantic ontology;
13. treats a human confirmation as authority outside that human’s jurisdiction;
14. treats a model’s prior output as independent evidence for itself;
15. allows epistemic history to be erased during revision.

Any one of conditions 1–7 is a **critical failure**.

Conditions 8–15 are also failures unless the candidate explicitly narrows its scope and is revised accordingly.

# 16. Closing falsifier

The deepest test is simple.

Given two true propositions:

> A is true within its boundary.  
> B is true within its boundary.

Can ACT 11 force JARVIS to preserve the difference between:

> **A and B are both present**

and:

> **A and B are related in this particular way**?

And if JARVIS proposes that relationship before evidence exists, can the constitution preserve it as a useful hypothesis without laundering it into fact?

If the answer to either question is **no**, ACT 11 is not sufficient.
