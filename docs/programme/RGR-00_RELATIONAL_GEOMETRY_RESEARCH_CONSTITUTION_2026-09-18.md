# I · The constitutional question

Relational Geometry Reasoning begins from a serious but unproven possibility:

> **Some forms of meaning and reasoning may be represented more adequately by relations, transformations, invariants, boundaries, perspectives, and trajectories than by isolated semantic points alone.**

The constitutional question is therefore:

> **How may Soullab investigate a possible geometry of relational meaning without confusing mathematical analogy with evidence, phenomenology with proof, engineering usefulness with ontology, or the originating Spiralogic vision with the conclusion of the research?**

RGR-00 governs that separation.

The programme must preserve:

```text
inspiration
≠ hypothesis

hypothesis
≠ formal model

formal model
≠ empirical support

empirical support
≠ general theory

engineering usefulness
≠ metaphysical truth
```

No later RGR gate may silently collapse these distinctions.

# II · Core proposition

> ## **Relation is the object of inquiry; geometry is a candidate language, not a predetermined answer.**

RGR may investigate whether relational structure can be usefully formalized geometrically.

RGR may not begin by declaring that:

- meaning **is** geometry;
- consciousness **is** geometric;
- human experience **is** a Grassmannian;
- Spiralogic **is** mathematically proven;

# III · Research thesis

The broad RGR thesis to be investigated is:

> **Meaning may depend not only on represented content, but on relational structure that is preserved, transformed, constrained, or made coherent across changes of perspective, context, representation, and trajectory.**

A minimal mathematical motif is:

```text
representation
    ↓
relation
    ↓
transformation
    ↓
candidate invariant
```

For a representation `x`, a transformation `T`, and a candidate invariant `I`, RGR may investigate conditions under which:

```text
I(T(x)) ≈ I(x)
```

The symbol `≈` is deliberately weaker than equality.

The research must determine:

- what the representation is;
- what transformations are legitimate;
- what counts as an invariant;
- under what metric or topology invariance is evaluated;
- whether the invariant predicts or explains anything beyond simpler baselines.

Without those definitions, “invariance” remains metaphor.

# V · Claim classes

Every substantive RGR claim must be typed.

At minimum, future research artifacts must distinguish these classes.

## 1. ESTABLISHED_SOURCE

A claim directly grounded in a cited external source.

Examples:

- a theorem;
- a published empirical result;
- a documented mathematical definition;
- an established property of a Grassmann manifold.

Required:

- exact source;
- source type;
- relevant scope;
- population/domain when empirical;
- limitations.

An established result in one domain may not be silently transferred to another.

---

## 2. FORMAL_DERIVATION

A result derived from explicit mathematical assumptions within an RGR model.

Required:

- assumptions;
- definitions;
- derivation or proof;
- domain of validity.

A formal derivation proves only what follows from its assumptions.

It does not prove that the assumptions describe human meaning.

---

## 3. INTERNAL_EMPIRICAL_RESULT

A result produced by an RGR experiment.

Required:

- exact Work Unit;
- dataset provenance;
- method;
- baseline;
- metric;
- falsification condition;
- result;
- uncertainty;
- verifier evidence;
- reproducibility information.

No internal result becomes general theory merely because it is statistically or mechanically positive.

---

## 4. HYPOTHESIS

A falsifiable proposal not yet established.

Required:

- predicted observation;
- comparison condition;
- rejection condition;
- alternative explanation.

Hypotheses must be allowed to fail.

---

## 5. ENGINEERING_ANALOGY

A structural analogy used to guide architecture or explanation.

Examples may include:

- “authorized core behaves like an invariant”;
- “a Work Unit lifecycle is a discrete relational state space”;
- “Weather may behave like a contextual field.”

An engineering analogy is not evidence that the two systems are mathematically equivalent.

---

## 6. PHENOMENOLOGICAL_ORIGIN

A dream, image, intuition, clinical observation, lived experience, symbolic pattern, or contemplative insight that motivates inquiry.

Phenomenological origins may be intellectually and personally important.

They may generate hypotheses.

They do not become scientific evidence simply because later mathematics resembles them.

---

## 7. SPECULATIVE_CORRESPONDENCE

A possible connection whose formal mapping or evidence is presently insufficient.

Examples:

- Spiralogic and a specific manifold structure;
- Elemental operators and mathematical generators;
- Weather and a mathematical connection or field;
- positive geometry and semantic boundary structure.

Speculative correspondence must remain visibly speculative until independently strengthened.

---

# VI · No analogical laundering

RGR adopts the following law:

> **An analogy cannot be promoted into evidence by repetition, elegance, mathematical vocabulary, or cross-domain resemblance.**

In particular:

```text
physics result
→ inspiration for semantic model
```

does not imply:

```text
physics result
→ evidence for semantic model
```

A cross-domain correspondence becomes research-relevant evidence only after the programme defines a mapping that is:

1. explicit;
2. mathematically coherent;
3. independently testable;
4. discriminating against plausible alternatives;
5. predictive or explanatory beyond descriptive resemblance.

# VIII · Provisional RGR vocabulary

The following terms are admitted as **research primitives**, not final definitions.

## State

A bounded representation of a system at one analytic moment.

A state is not assumed to contain its full meaning.

## Relation

A structured dependency, orientation, transformation, constraint, or correspondence among states or entities.

Relations are first-class research objects.

## Transformation

A defined mapping from one representation or relational configuration to another.

A transformation must specify what changes and what is preserved.

## Invariant

A structure or measurable quantity that remains sufficiently stable under a declared family of transformations.

“Invariant” may be exact, approximate, statistical, topological, ordinal, or structural, but the type must be declared.

## Boundary

A condition distinguishing admissible from non-admissible regions, transformations, states, or interpretations within a formal model.

## Trajectory

An ordered history through a state or relational space.

RGR treats trajectory as potentially meaning-bearing.

## Perspective

A local representational frame from which a relational structure is expressed.

Different perspectives need not have identical coordinates.

## Context

A condition that changes the interpretation, metric, accessibility, transition cost, or relational significance of states.

## Field

A candidate formal device for representing context-dependent modulation across a space.

The use of “field” must be mathematical when claimed mathematically and metaphorical when used metaphorically.

## Coherence

A declared compatibility relation among local representations or transformations.

Coherence is not assumed to mean agreement.

# XXI · Core falsification cases

Future RGR work must be capable of falsifying at least these broad propositions.

## F-RGR1 — geometry adds nothing

If simple vector or symbolic baselines perform as well as or better than candidate relational-geometric methods, the stronger geometry claim is not supported.

## F-RGR2 — apparent invariants are lexical artifacts

If candidate relational invariants disappear when vocabulary, actors, surface content, or modality changes, they are not relationally invariant in the claimed sense.

## F-RGR3 — geometry does not transfer

If performance depends on the training domain and fails cross-domain transfer, no general relational-geometry claim is established.

## F-RGR4 — complexity without explanatory gain

If a Grassmannian, sheaf, manifold, or topological model increases mathematical complexity without measurable or interpretive advantage, the simpler model is preferred.

## F-RGR5 — perspective formalism collapses to shared coordinates

If heterogeneous-perspective methods offer no advantage beyond ordinary shared embeddings, the stronger local-to-global theory is not supported.

## F-RGR6 — trajectory adds nothing

If path/history does not improve prediction, interpretation, or generalization over current-state representation, trajectory is not established as necessary.

## F-RGR7 — Elemental structure is arbitrary

If Elemental operators perform no better than permuted labels, alternative taxonomies, or comparable decomposition schemes, the Elemental hypothesis is not supported.

## F-RGR8 — Weather is merely another feature

If contextual-field formulations offer no advantage over treating Weather as an ordinary independent feature, the field hypothesis is not supported.
