### Q5 · Focus → Ask MAIA · "How do I compare my version with yours?"

> What you're asking is practical: you want to see your wording and an alternative wording side by side, so you can judge them against each other rather than having one quietly replace the other.
>
> Here's how that works in the Studio. Nothing I offer ever lands in your manuscript on its own. If you ask for a revision, it comes back as something to look at while your words stay where they are. In Passage Focus, "Show edit options" and "Show revision options" are the places where I offer a version for you to judge, and "Why this?" on a revision mark tells you what I was after. Only "Apply my version" puts anything into the manuscript, and only you press it — with "Undo" sitting right there if you don't like what you see. There's also "Craftsman's Table," which works on the selected words with the changes visible and me beside you as you go. That one is probably closest to what you're describing.
>
> What I can't do is press any of those for you, and I can't put two versions into a split-screen myself. The comparison happens in what I hand you and what you already have on the page.
>
> On this passage itself, before any of that: the three short pieces of it do different jobs. "They could not get along" is flat and almost offhand. "Their differences were not subtle — they were total" tightens the screw. Then "by some accident of fate" opens the door to the story proper, and the easy, unhurried walk through the woods sits against everything you've just told us about these four. That contrast between the flatness and what's coming is doing real work, and it's worth knowing that before anyone suggests smoothing it.
>
> I do have one thing in mind — it's small, a matter of a couple of words around "by some accident of fate," and what it would be for is keeping that phrase from doing the comic work that the storm and the punchline do better later. Do you want to see it, or would you rather tell me first what you were reaching for with that phrase?

### Q4 · Focus → Ask MAIA (fresh) · "What's the best way to work on this chapter here?"

> What you're asking is practical: you want to know how to work on this chapter here, in the Studio, rather than asking me to touch these three sentences yet.
>
> Two controls are built for exactly that. In Develop, on the Overview tab, "Read this chapter" has me read the whole chapter myself and say what it's doing, what's working, and where I'd put attention — nothing changes in your text. If you'd rather go place by place, Develop → More ways to explore → Edit with MAIA → "Start an editorial pass" has me read the current manuscript and bring you the spots where an edit might help, in order, still without applying anything. Either way you choose what, if anything, happens. What you're doing right now — selecting words in Edit and opening Work with passage — is the close-up version, good for one bit of wording at a time.
>
> About this bit of wording, since you've put it in front of me: it's doing two jobs in three sentences. "They could not get along. Their differences were not subtle — they were total." closes the portrait of the four yogis. "And yet, by some accident of fate, the four of them happened to be walking together one afternoon through the woods." opens the story. The dash is doing real work — the short flat statement, then the correction that goes further than you expected. And "by some accident of fate" is quietly the whole joke of the chapter arriving early, since the end reveals it was no accident at all. I'd want to keep that.
>
> The one thing I'd want from you before suggesting any change: is "accident of fate" meant to be read straight here, so the ending overturns it, or do you want the reader to catch the wink on the way past? I have a small wording idea that would depend entirely on which you mean. Which is it?
