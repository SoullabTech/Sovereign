## R1 · No universal Circle-size threshold — the question itself is replaced

The research does **not** support a constitutional maximum: not 12, not 18, not 24, not 50, not 150.
Size remains an **empirical variable**, not a constitutional one.

| Retired question | Current question |
|---|---|
| *How many people may a Circle contain?* | **Does this formation still possess the relational capacities that make it one Circle?** |

**Candidate relational capacities** (research dimensions — **never member scores, never displayed,
never aggregated into an index**):

```text
recognizability                    reciprocal addressability
meaningful participation           preserved difference
field legibility                   capacity for rupture and repair
voluntary silence / peripheral participation
individual sovereignty within belonging
```

**Two findings behind this.** A 2026 multicenter ICU study associated smaller nursing teams with
higher psychological safety — but cross-sectional, 22 units, large clinical teams, not intimate
relational groups: *evidence in the expected direction, not a Circle threshold.* And a
counter-effect from experiments with 3,652 participants: **larger groups can reduce subjective fear
under external threat.**

> **⭐ Distinguish *safety in numbers* from *psychological safety for interpersonal risk-taking*.
> They are not the same phenomenon.** Psychological safety is **not monotonic with headcount.**

## R2 · Structure matters more than number — the law survives, its rationale is restated

**Smallness is instrumentally valuable, not constitutionally essential.** It makes reciprocal
relationship, facilitation, participation, differentiation and repair *affordable*. That is all.

**Candidate law retained:**
> **Circles scale by multiplication, not enlargement.**

**Rationale, as recorded (supersedes the DISCOVER-v1 wording):**
> Enlargement eventually makes the relational conditions of Circle increasingly difficult or
> expensive to preserve. Multiplication and nesting allow larger collective intelligence without
> requiring one field to absorb unlimited relational complexity.

⛔ **No headcount threshold attaches to this law.**

## R4 · ⛔ D-06 CORRECTED — contribute-before-see does not govern the whole field

**The DISCOVER-v1 recommendation to extend contribute-before-you-see to the shared feed is
withdrawn.** It was over-drawn from the independent-judgment literature.

What the evidence actually supports: in the 5,180-person experiment, people answered
**individually first**, then deliberated — preserving independent judgment **before deliberation**
improved collective accuracy. That protects *independent judgment*. It does **not** establish that
people must speak before they may witness one another.

Against compulsory contribution: research on online support communities finds people who read
without posting derive meaningful benefit — one study found lurkers and posters differed little
across most empowerment outcomes. This matches Wenger's **legitimate peripheral participation**
far better than compulsory contribution does.

**The distinction to carry into CONSTITUTE:**

```text
STRUCTURED INQUIRY                      LIVING CIRCLE FIELD
  Feel                                    Arrive
   ↓                                       ↓
  Contribute independently                Witness / Offer / Respond / Rest
   ↓                                       ↓
  Encounter others                        Participate when and how one chooses
   ↓
  Deliberate / witness
   ↓
  Integrate
```

> **A member may legitimately witness without being forced to perform participation.**
> **A quiet member must be allowed to be present without performing presence.**

Consequence for the substrate: the existing implementation — server-side contribute-before-see on
**inquiries**, absent on the **feed** — is now **closer to correct than the doctrine wording is.**
Gap **G-02 is downgraded from 🔴 defect to a doctrine-wording reconciliation** (Contradiction Map
C-02). **No code change.** The exact doctrine wording returns to the founder during CONSTITUTE.

## R5 · Rupture and repair is first-class — and is not moderation

Rupture in groups is relational and multi-directional:

```text
member ↔ member          member ↔ facilitator      member ↔ Circle
subgroup ↔ subgroup      subgroup ↔ Circle         Circle ↔ Circle
```

Critically: **members who are not parties to the original conflict are affected and must
metabolize the rupture.** Broader psychotherapy evidence finds a moderate association between
successful rupture resolution and positive outcome — though largely from *dyadic* therapy.
And the scale link this programme was missing: **as groups grow and develop informal subgroups,
subgroup↔subgroup and subgroup↔whole-group conflict increases.**

**Candidate Circle principle:**
> **A living Circle must be capable of rupture and repair without requiring rupture to become
> moderation.**

Four distinct responses that must not collapse into one another:

| | |
|---|---|
| **RELATIONAL REPAIR** | attempt to restore or transform relationship |
| **BOUNDARY ACTION** | distance, leave, removal, protection |
| **MODERATION** | enforcement of shared rules |
| **SAFETY INTERVENTION** | action where relational repair is inappropriate or unsafe |

> The Constitution must not assume every conflict should be repaired.
> It also must not assume every conflict should be moderated.

Constellations need a repair geometry too — conflict can be **Circle ↔ Circle**.

## R6 · Circle birth ≠ schism — the generic "fission" concept is retired

Experimental work on group fission found **pre-existing subgroup boundaries become fault lines
along which groups split after conflict.** So an emerging subgroup means one of two very different
things:

- **Generative differentiation** — *"Five of us have found another inquiry that deserves its own field."*
- **Relational schism** — *"We can no longer tolerate those people, so we're leaving."*

These are **not the same lifecycle event** and must not share a mechanism.

Wenger-Trayner's communities-of-practice lifecycle (potential → coalescing → active → dispersed →
memorable) also observes that large communities **naturally spawn smaller subgroups** as intense
mutual engagement becomes difficult.

**Candidate lifecycle:**

```text
FORMATION → COALESCENCE → ACTIVE LIFE → MATURATION
                                            ↓
        ┌────────────┬────────────┬────────────┬────────────────┐
     CONTINUE       REST       COMPLETE    DIFFERENTIATE
                                                ↓
                                          CIRCLE BIRTH

and separately:   RUPTURE → REPAIR / TRANSFORMATION / SEPARATION
```

**Rest, completion, and birth are not engagement failures.**

**D-09 strengthened.** The system may **notice recurring differentiation, reflect it, and ask
whether something new is emerging.** The system may **not** split a Circle, and **there is no
headcount-triggered fission.** *The system may notice; it may not declare that a human field has
divided.*

## R7 · MAIA role differentiation — the most consequential new finding

**AI mediation can genuinely improve some group deliberation.** The 2024 *Science* Habermas Machine
study (5,734 participants): AI-generated group statements were **preferred to human-mediated ones**,
groups became **less divided**, and successful statements **incorporated minority critiques while
respecting majority positions.**

> ⛔ **Therefore the Constitution must NOT say that AI synthesis or common-ground seeking is
> inherently corrupting.** That would be a claim the evidence contradicts.

**But purpose is decisive.** The Habermas Machine was producing *a group statement people could
endorse*. A Soullab Circle is often doing something else entirely: perceiving unresolved
difference · accompanying grief · exploring shadow · holding incompatible truths · **letting
something remain unanswered.**

**Cautions, carried at their real confidence:** a Nature review warns LLMs can alter how collective
information is aggregated and transmitted — opportunity and risk. Two **preprints** (lower
confidence, worth watching): persistent **minority underrepresentation and input-order bias** in
LLM-generated deliberation summaries; and AI-mediated minority support that increased minority
participation while **unexpectedly reducing psychological safety.**

**Two candidate MAIA roles, carried forward without collapsing:**

### A · FIELD WITNESS — default candidate
Helps the Circle perceive: plurality · recurring questions · difference · tension · movement ·
unresolved material · emergent connections.
Does **not** automatically: declare consensus · rank positions · identify winners · score members ·
decide what the Circle means · resolve disagreement.

> *"Several different experiences remain present."* — not — *"The group believes…"*

### B · COMMON-GROUND MEDIATOR — invoked candidate
Available **only when the Circle explicitly requests** synthesis or common ground. Iterative and
member-critiqued — members critique MAIA's proposed synthesis rather than MAIA announcing what the
group believes. Any synthesis must remain **contestable · member-reviewable · revisable · explicit
about unresolved dissent.**

> ⛔ **The default Field Witness role must never drift silently into Common-Ground Mediator.**
> Silent drift is the failure mode; the role boundary is the control.
