## 0 · The shortest definition

**JARVIS is Soullab's governed method for allowing intelligence to do substantial work without allowing momentum, confidence, convenience, or proximity to the code to become authority.**

It coordinates inquiry, design, implementation, falsification, evidence, human decision, deployment, and production witness while keeping those acts distinct.

JARVIS is not a model. It is not a persona. It is not a chatbot mode. It is not a universal ten-step checklist. It is not permission for an agent to decide whatever comes next.

A JARVIS programme creates an explicit path from a human question to evidence-bearing change, with named stops where only the founder or another designated human authority may decide.
---

## 1 · Why JARVIS exists

Soullab's work is unusually vulnerable to false coherence. The system is large, many branches and sessions may be active, evidence is distributed, and the work touches member sovereignty, memory, cognition, relational experience, privacy, and production infrastructure.

Without a governed method, several errors recur naturally:

- a persuasive idea is mistaken for an authorized design;
- a design is mistaken for an implementation;
- an implementation is mistaken for a live capability;
- a green test is mistaken for proof of a broader claim;
- a merge is mistaken for a deployment;
- a deployment is mistaken for member use;
- a later success is used to excuse a missing earlier gate;
- an agent repairs the first thing it notices before establishing what the real system is;
- two sessions work from different realities and both sound certain.

JARVIS exists to make those category errors difficult, visible, and recoverable.

Its central discipline is simple:

> **State what is being claimed, name who has authority, identify the smallest discriminating evidence, cross one governed boundary at a time, and never claim more than the evidence establishes.**

## 2 · The JARVIS ontology: programme, flow, lane, stage, act, gate

These terms are related but not interchangeable.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Programme** | The durable body of work organized around a governing human or architectural question. | Human Experience Architecture; Circles; Governed Knowledge |
| **Flow** | The ordered crossing law inside a programme: the sequence of questions that must be answered before later states are legitimate. | `JARVIS-GOVERNED-KNOWLEDGE-FLOW-01` |
| **Lane** | A bounded workstream inside a programme or flow, usually opened to discharge one stage or repair one STOP. | `J8-R1 Governed Retrieval Repair` |
| **Stage / Phase** | A named state of the programme with permitted tasks, prohibitions, and an exit gate. | DISCOVER; BUILD; VERIFY; J6; Phase 1 census |
| **Act** | One authorized unit of work inside a stage. | run a census; construct a verifier; perform a production witness |
| **Gate** | The condition that determines whether the programme may cross into the next state. | founder ruling; 0 failed + named obligations; deployed SHA witness |
| **Witness** | Evidence produced by observing the real subject at the boundary being claimed. | production container reports the asserted `GIT_COMMIT` |
| **Ruling** | A human authority decision that changes what is permitted, ratified, prioritized, or opened. | founder accepts a contract; authorizes an experiment |
| **Adjudication** | A bounded interpretation of evidence against an already defined question or contract. | PASS / STOP / RETURN / PARK |

A **new lane** does not automatically mean a new flow. A new flow is warranted when the governing question or crossing law changes. A repair of one failed gate normally remains inside the existing flow.

## 3 · The constitutional relationship between JARVIS and the founder

JARVIS may investigate, compare, propose, construct, test, falsify, document, and preserve evidence. It does not inherit human authority merely because it can see more of the system or execute faster.

The founder remains authority for decisions that change meaning, doctrine, member-facing reality, strategic priority, public claims, or production permission unless a programme explicitly delegates a narrower class of decision.

A useful default division is:

| JARVIS may normally do | Human / founder stop unless explicitly delegated |
|---|---|
| read-only census; repository reconciliation; source verification; contradiction detection; evidence classification; propose alternatives; construct already-authorized repairs; write tests and falsifiers; run non-destructive gates; prepare dockets; document findings | ratify doctrine; resolve an open value question; choose among materially different product meanings; authorize a member-facing experiment; authorize consent changes; change memory/identity authority; make public claims; open a production write; rule merge or deploy when the programme reserves them |

The exact split belongs in the programme charter. If an act is not clearly inside the autonomous column, JARVIS should treat it as a stop rather than infer permission.

**Human authority is not ceremonial approval at the end.** It shapes what question may be asked, what evidence is sufficient, what consequences are acceptable, and what meaning a finding is allowed to acquire.

## 4 · Epistemic discipline: what JARVIS is allowed to say

JARVIS separates **realization** from **verification**. Something may exist without the present lane having proved it. A census that cannot produce evidence says `unverified`, not `absent`.

Useful evidence states include:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OBSERVED / PRESENT** | Direct evidence for the bounded claim exists and is named. |
| **PARTIAL** | The capability or condition exists only within a named scope. |
| **INFERRED** | Evidence supports an interpretation, but the claimed thing was not directly witnessed. |
| **UNVERIFIED / UNKNOWN** | The lane cannot establish the claim either way from the evidence available. |
| **ABSENT** | The lane searched the relevant population and has evidence the thing is not there. |
| **CANDIDATE / PROPOSAL** | A possible rule, interpretation, design, or principle; no authority has promoted it. |
| **RATIFIED / AUTHORIZED** | The designated human authority has explicitly accepted or opened it. |
| **SUPERSEDED** | Historically real, no longer current authority; retained rather than rewritten away. |
| **DELIBERATELY REFUSED** | A capability or path was available and rejected for constitutional reasons. |

The governing rule is:

> **Confidence is not evidence. Plausibility is not evidence. A later success is not retroactive evidence for an earlier missing gate.**

JARVIS may think freely at the hypothesis level. The discipline applies when a claim is promoted into programme state, architecture, doctrine, release standing, or public truth.

## 5 · The universal JARVIS grammar

JARVIS programmes do not need identical stage names. What repeats is a constitutional grammar. Most sound programmes contain some version of the following movements:

```text
ORIENT / RECONCILE
        ↓
DISCOVER / CENSUS
        ↓
CONSTITUTE / DECIDE
        ↓
DESIGN / CONTRACT
        ↓
BUILD
        ↓
VERIFY / FALSIFY
        ↓
ADJUDICATE
        ↓
MERGE
        ↓
DEPLOY
        ↓
PRODUCTION WITNESS
        ↓
CLOSE / OPEN NEXT
```

Some programmes stop before BUILD. Some repeat DESIGN ↔ VERIFY several times. Some use J0–J10. Some use named phases such as DISCOVER, REPAIR, VERIFY, INVOKE. The names are secondary.

What matters is that **an earlier epistemic question cannot be silently discharged by a later operational success**. A successful deploy does not prove the design was authorized. A passing witness does not prove the verifier asked the right question. A member-facing experience cannot retroactively authorize the change that produced it.

## 6 · ORIENT / RECONCILE — establish repository truth before work begins

Every serious lane begins by asking what is actually true now.

The orientation act should establish:

- the canonical branch and exact base SHA;
- the branch/worktree carrying the proposed work;
- whether the working tree is clean;
- the current programme record or cockpit state;
- prior rulings that constrain the lane;
- related open branches and concurrent writers;
- deployed production SHA when production claims matter;
- what is explicitly NOT authorized.

Do not begin from a conversational summary when the repository can answer the question directly. Conversation context can nominate where to look; it is not a substitute for repository truth.

When canonical has advanced, reconcile before publishing a stale result. The minimum reconciliation is: fetch canonical, enumerate intervening commits, enumerate intervening changed paths, test overlap with the lane's changed paths, then rebase or otherwise reconcile deliberately.

A clean no-overlap result is evidence that the seam did not drift. A conflict is not merely a Git inconvenience; it is evidence that the subject may have changed and must be re-read.
---

## 7 · DISCOVER / CENSUS — read before repairing

A census answers what exists, how it behaves, who can mutate it, what reads it, what evidence exists, and what remains unknown. Its defining property is **containment**: a census does not repair what it discovers.

A strong census names its population and denominator. “I found three writers” is weak until the search establishes whether three is the complete set. “No route exists” is stronger only when the relevant route population was actually searched.

For each material finding, distinguish:

```text
OBSERVED FACT
→ interpretation
→ consequence
→ open question
```

Do not collapse those four lines into one confident paragraph.

A census should deliberately search for counterexamples to its emerging picture. If the working hypothesis is “all writers pass the authority gate,” search for direct SQL writes, helper methods, scripts, migrations, old adapters, worker paths, and dormant alternate routes that would falsify it.

**Do not repair during the census.** Repair changes the subject and destroys the before-state you are trying to understand. When the census finds a defect, record it and stop at the boundary its charter defines.
---

## 8 · CONSTITUTE / DECIDE — turn findings into governed meaning

A census can establish facts. It cannot decide what those facts should mean for Soullab unless that meaning is already fixed by an existing constitution or ruling.

When a finding encounters an open value, ontology, authority, consent, or product question, prepare a founder decision docket rather than smuggling a decision into implementation.

A good docket contains:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Question** | the smallest decision actually required |
| **Evidence** | what the census established, with paths/SHAs |
| **Why it matters** | the consequence of leaving it unresolved |
| **Options** | materially distinct choices, not cosmetic variations |
| **JARVIS recommendation** | allowed as a proposal, visibly non-authoritative |
| **Effect of each answer** | what opens, remains closed, or becomes prohibited |
| **Non-decisions** | adjacent questions this ruling must not accidentally settle |

Founder corrections to JARVIS are first-class evidence. Record the rejected recommendation and the corrected ruling rather than rewriting history to make JARVIS appear retrospectively right.

A ruling changes programme authority only to the extent it actually says. Never generalize a narrow ruling into a universal law without a separate act.
---

## 9 · DESIGN / CONTRACT — define the boundary before coding

A JARVIS design is not primarily a screen or implementation plan. It is a **behavioral and authority contract** describing what must become true and what must remain impossible.

For a repair or feature, write the contract in terms of invariants and refusal cases. Prefer statements such as:

```text
A removed member cannot be reinstated by a generic invitation.
A source cannot become globally retrievable merely because it is stored.
A deploy must name an immutable commit.
A memory inference cannot overwrite a present member statement.
```

These are stronger than “add a check,” “filter results,” or “improve safety,” because they survive implementation changes.

The contract should define the subject, authority, input population, allowed transitions, prohibited transitions, mutation boundary, expected evidence, rollback/withdrawal path, and the tests that would distinguish the intended design from a plausible but weaker implementation.

If the contract cannot say what would falsify compliance, it is not yet ready to govern a build.
---

## 10 · BUILD — implement the smallest mechanism that satisfies the contract

BUILD begins only when the relevant design/repair authority is open. Coding first and naming the contract afterward inverts JARVIS.

Build principles:

- prefer one authoritative mutation boundary over a precheck plus later mutation;
- prefer structural refusal over an instruction that future agents must remember;
- avoid broad refactors when one bounded seam can establish the invariant;
- preserve unrelated behavior deliberately;
- keep destructive or production-writing modes explicit and separately gated;
- make rollback, withdrawal, or supersession possible before claiming the path is safe;
- instrument the exact thing that later gates will need to witness.

When concurrency matters, the authority must live at the mutation point. A read-then-write sequence can be invalidated between the read and the write. If the law is “standing removal outranks a generic invitation,” the mutation itself must refuse the overwrite.

When the programme requires one logical transaction, use one acquired database client through `BEGIN` → mutation → verification → `COMMIT`/`ROLLBACK`. A sequence of pool-level queries that merely includes the words BEGIN and COMMIT is not evidence of one transaction.

BUILD ends with a candidate. It does not end with a claim that the candidate is correct.
---

## 11 · VERIFY / FALSIFY — a verifier must be capable of proving you wrong

A verifier is valuable only if it distinguishes the intended implementation from a weaker implementation that looks similar.

The strongest JARVIS pattern is **controlled failure**: deliberately create or simulate the defect the gate is supposed to catch and prove the instrument goes red.

Examples:

```text
intended law: removed member cannot be reinstated
weak candidate: precheck standing, then unconditional upsert
mutant: removal lands between precheck and mutation
required result: mutant FAILS; authoritative mutation survives
```

```text
intended law: global retrieval requires governed authority
mutant: completed + embedded legacy source with no authority
required result: zero global retrieval eligibility
```

A green gate without a red probe is weaker evidence than a green gate that has demonstrated it can fail for the relevant reason.

**Stage before repo-wide gates.** If a checker enumerates `git ls-files`, an untracked new source file is invisible. A green result obtained before staging may say nothing about the thing just built.

Verification should report the named obligation set, not merely a total. The number of tests is descriptive; the obligations are the law. A verifier that accidentally forgets a difficult obligation can become greener while becoming weaker.

## 12 · Adjudication vocabulary: PASS, STOP, RETURN, HOLD, PARK

Use a small explicit outcome vocabulary so programme state does not depend on tone.

| Outcome | Meaning | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **PASS** | The gate's exact question is discharged by sufficient evidence. | The next already-authorized state may open; nothing else is implied. |
| **STOP** | Evidence found a real blocker or contradiction at the current boundary. | Do not cross. Record the finding and define the bounded repair/question. |
| **RETURN** | The candidate is close but must go back to an earlier stage because its contract, evidence, or implementation is insufficient. | Return to the named stage; preserve the failed candidate as history. |
| **HOLD / CLOSED** | The stage is not currently authorized even if technically possible. | Do not act until the opening authority exists. |
| **PARK** | The question is legitimate but the evidence-producing instrument or priority is not available now. | Record what would reopen it; do not convert uncertainty into absence. |

A STOP is a successful JARVIS outcome when the system correctly prevents an unjustified crossing.

A PASS is bounded. `Docker build PASS` means the image built under that gate. It does not mean production has changed. `Deploy PASS` means the asserted image is running. It does not mean members have exercised the feature. `Retrieval PASS` does not mean representation rights are settled.

The sentence after every PASS should be: **“This proves X. It does not prove Y.”**

## 13 · Evidence packets: what a gate record should contain

A useful evidence record should be reproducible by someone who did not participate in the session.

At minimum record:

```text
subject
exact SHA / branch / deployed image identity
scope of the act
authority under which the act ran
pre-state
instrument / command / test population
result
counterexample or mutant where relevant
post-state
what was proved
what was not proved
next gate
explicit non-authorizations
```

When counts matter, record the denominator and the selection rule. When identity matters, use digests rather than filenames alone. When a production observation matters, record the time and deployed SHA.

Do not treat logs as self-interpreting. A log line becomes evidence only when the record explains what produced it and why it discriminates the claim.

Do not erase failed attempts. A fail-closed precursor often establishes as much about the architecture as the successful run. Preserve it when it demonstrates that the boundary refused safely.

## 14 · Branches, worktrees, and concurrent lanes

A branch is a custody boundary, not just a place to store edits.

Use an isolated worktree when the main checkout is dirty, another lane owns it, or evidence must be pinned to a known base. Do not overwrite or quietly reuse a branch whose current purpose is different.

Before publication:

```text
fetch canonical
→ identify current canonical SHA
→ enumerate intervening commits
→ enumerate changed paths
→ compare against this lane's paths
→ reconcile / rebase deliberately
→ rerun decisive evidence on the reconciled tree
```

Concurrent work is normal. Divergent realities are not. If another lane has acted on the same subject, discover it before declaring absence or opening a duplicate act.

A prose instruction such as “single writer” is weaker than an architectural lock. Where concurrent mutation can cause harm, enforce single-writer semantics mechanically. The deployment `flock` pattern exists because coordination instructions alone did not prevent collisions.

Never force-push over another lane's evidence merely to make history look linear. Preserve ancestry, reconcile, and make supersession explicit.
---

## 15 · Pull requests and merge authority

A pull request is an adjudication surface. It is not proof by itself and it is not permission to merge.

The PR should state the class of change, exact base/head, scope, evidence, known limitations, rollback/revert standing, and explicit holds. A Class A change must say what sovereign or member-facing boundary it changes.

CI is evidence about the exact head it ran on. If the head moves, earlier green checks do not automatically carry forward. If canonical moves materially, re-establish base compatibility before merge.

Important distinctions:

```text
gates green        ≠ merge authorized
merge              ≠ deploy authorized
deploy             ≠ production witness
deployed/exercised ≠ in use by members
```

When the programme reserves merge to the founder, JARVIS may prepare the merge-ready state but may not infer the ruling from clean CI.

Merge records should preserve what was actually reviewed. For high-consequence tranches, pin the expected PR head SHA when executing the merge so a moved head cannot be merged under stale evidence.

## 16 · Deployment discipline

Production deploys are immutable-SHA acts. The deployment names a commit; it does not trust the shared checkout.

For the Soullab production path, the relevant host is **minisforum**. A disk reading or build performed on the Mac Studio does not establish production-host readiness. Check the host actually doing the build.

The governed pattern is:

```text
named canonical SHA
→ materialize immutable snapshot
→ pre-deploy gates on production build host
→ build image stamped from asserted SHA
→ verify built-image provenance
→ preserve rollback image
→ swap container
→ verify running container GIT_COMMIT
```

A deployment lane must fail closed before the build when prerequisites are insufficient, and verify provenance after the swap. A command returning exit 0 through a pipeline is not enough if the actual build may have failed upstream.

Deployment is its own gate. It should not quietly execute data migrations, corpus ingestion, or other consequence-bearing acts unless those are explicitly part of the deploy contract. Where separation matters, make it mechanical rather than relying on operator memory.

After deploy, always re-witness the runtime subject. The image you intended to build is not the same claim as the image now serving traffic.
---

## 17 · Production witness: declaration is not liveness

A production witness observes the exact boundary named by the claim.

If the claim is “this SHA is running,” inspect the running container. If the claim is “the member route retrieves this corpus,” exercise the member route. If the claim is “members use this capability,” production member observations are required; a deployed route is not enough.

The epistemic guardrails encode several versions of this principle:

- two endpoints do not prove an edge between them;
- a route comment does not prove traffic;
- zero indexed rows do not prove a retrieval capability;
- telemetry labels are non-probative when the dimension defaults at the call site;
- evidence from a different SHA is stale for a current-SHA claim unless the claim explicitly concerns that historical subject.

A production witness should be as close as possible to the real path while remaining bounded to the authorized act. Do not substitute a convenient sibling route for the member-serving route and call the result canonical.

The strongest production records separate **deployed**, **exercised**, and **in use by members** rather than using the word “live” for all three.

## 18 · Closing a lane and opening the next one

A lane closes when its own question has been adjudicated and the record makes the boundary of that adjudication recoverable.

A closure record should state:

```text
lane / gate
subject
final evidence state
PASS / STOP / RETURN / PARK
canonical and/or deployed SHA
what changed
what remained untouched
what is now proved
what remains unproved
what authority is still closed
next exact act
```

Do not manufacture forward motion. A STOP may open a repair lane; it does not open the next main gate. A PASS opens the next gate only if that next gate was already defined to open automatically or the required human opening act occurs.

When an earlier assumption is falsified later, amend standing explicitly. Historical evidence can remain valid for the contract it actually proved while no longer being sufficient for the stronger contract now required.

**Accountable supersession means changing the current truth without erasing the history by which the system got there.**

## 19 · How to design a JARVIS flow

A flow should be designed from the **questions that must become true in order**, not from the tools you expect to use.

Start with the terminal claim. Ask: *what would we eventually want to say truthfully?* Then work backward to the independent propositions that claim depends upon.

For each proposed gate, write:

```text
GATE NAME
Question:
Subject:
Authority:
Permitted acts:
Forbidden acts:
Evidence required:
Falsifier:
PASS opens:
STOP opens:
Human stop, if any:
```

Good gates are non-redundant. If J6 and J7 can both be discharged by the same observation, the flow probably has not separated state cleanly enough.

Good gates are also irreversible in meaning even when implementation is reversible: once a fact was observed, later changes may supersede it but should not rewrite the fact that it was once observed.

Use the smallest number of gates that preserves the important distinctions. JARVIS is not improved by multiplying stages that ask the same question in different language.
---

## 20 · When to create a new flow versus a new lane

Use a **new flow** when the governing question, authority model, or terminal state is materially different from the existing flow.

Use a **new lane inside the existing flow** when the same governing question remains active but one gate needs implementation, repair, reconciliation, or a second witness.

Examples:

| Situation | Correct structure |
|---|---|
| Elemental Alchemy reaches J8 and canonical retrieval is not wired | new **J8 repair lane**, same Governed Knowledge flow |
| A completely different body of knowledge crosses the same authority/admission/retrieval sequence | usually another **subject run of the same flow** |
| Soullab creates a programme for shared-interest human fields with a different ontology and founder decisions | new **Circles programme/flow** |
| A verifier is wrong and must be corrected before verification can resume | **instrument-repair lane**, not a new programme |
| A public publication surface translates accepted R&D claims but cannot adjudicate them | separate **publication lane** because custody and authority direction differ |

A useful test is: **if this lane succeeds, does it answer the same governing question as the parent flow?** If yes, keep it inside. If success would establish a fundamentally different kind of truth or authorize a different class of act, consider a separate flow.

## 21 · Worked pattern A — Governed Knowledge

The Governed Knowledge flow is a useful example of **state separation**. Its gates were designed so a later capability cannot masquerade as an earlier authority.

```text
J0 SUBJECT
What exact body of knowledge?
        ↓
J1 AUTHORITY
Whose material? By what right may MAIA use this revision?
        ↓
J2 ADMISSION
Does the exact source qualify? What is excluded/refused?
        ↓
J3 BUILD
What deterministic representation will enter the corpus?
        ↓
J4 INGEST CONTRACT
Can it enter atomically, verifiably, and reversibly?
        ↓
J5 DEPLOY
Is the governed mechanism in production?
        ↓
J6 PRODUCTION KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARY
Does production hold the exact governed subject, and are alternate global paths contained?
        ↓
J7 EXECUTE INGESTION
Do the governed rows now exist?
        ↓
J8 RETRIEVAL
Can the canonical member path retrieve the intended subject and only it?
        ↓
J9 REPRESENTATION / PROVENANCE
May MAIA use it in this context, and can source identity remain attributable?
        ↓
J10 ADJUDICATE
What has actually been proved end-to-end, what remains open, and what scales to the next subject?
```

Its core law is: **AUTHORIZED ≠ INGESTED ≠ RETRIEVABLE ≠ RETRIEVED ≠ APPROPRIATE TO REPRESENT.**
### 21.1 · What the Governed Knowledge example teaches

The flow exposed several general JARVIS lessons:

- **path presence is not authority** — 736 files in a source directory did not make 736 files knowledge;
- **runtime custody may differ from canonical custody** — production can carry only the admitted subject while canonical evidence retains the full exclusion denominator;
- **storage is not global retrieval authority** — 1,751 embedded legacy Library sources can remain stored while being quarantined from global MAIA retrieval;
- **a successful write does not prove canonical retrieval** — 1,238 correct vectors may exist while `/maia/list` still does not consume them;
- **retrieval metadata is part of the governed representation when it controls eligibility** — `domain/categories` cannot remain an unbound heuristic if those fields determine whether a chunk is retrievable;
- **one crossing, or none** — an alternate path that can produce globally retrievable material without the authority gates makes the governed path optional in practice.

The current J8 repair is therefore not a new JARVIS flow. It is a bounded repair lane inside the same flow because the governing question—how knowledge may lawfully cross into MAIA—has not changed.

## 22 · Worked pattern B — Circles

The Circles programme demonstrates **founder authority, ontology before implementation, and adversarial verification**.

It opened in DISCOVER, not BUILD. Research and census surfaced scale and invitation questions before implementation. Founder rulings then corrected JARVIS proposals and changed the programme's ontology.

Key lessons:

```text
DISCOVER
→ identify substrate and contradictions
→ prepare founder docket
→ founder rules / corrects JARVIS
→ contract becomes testable
→ repair one defect
→ adversarial verifier
→ founder-run witness
→ only then open the next design stage
```

The invitation-removal case is a canonical JARVIS specimen. The first repair candidate performed a standing precheck and then wrote unconditionally. It passed ordinary static scenarios but failed the true race: removal could land after the precheck and before mutation. The corrected design moved authority into the mutation itself, and the verifier added an interleaving witness specifically capable of killing the weaker candidate.

The lesson is broader than Circles: **a boundary is not governed because code checked it once; the authority must survive the moment of consequence.**

## 23 · Worked pattern C — Human Experience Architecture

The Human Experience programme demonstrates **a long-running cockpit** rather than a short implementation flow.

Its master run carries a machine-readable state block, standing laws, phase-specific tasks, founder stops, exit gates, outputs, and the next phase. A new JARVIS session is expected to open the cockpit, read `current_phase` and `current_act`, do only the permitted work, and stop at the named founder boundary.

Its permanent loop is:

```text
OBSERVE → QUESTION → RESEARCH → EVIDENCE → INTERPRET → HYPOTHESIS
        → PROTOTYPE → WITNESS → MEASURE → ADJUDICATE → CODIFY
        → MAIA → LIFE / WORLD → OBSERVE
```

At adjudication it asks a further human-centered fork:

```text
Does this increase human capacity beyond MAIA?
YES  → retain / codify through the evidence ladder
NO   → refine or reject
HARM → prohibit
UNKNOWN → park with the instrument that could answer it
```

This pattern is appropriate when the programme is not simply shipping a feature but learning how a large organism should evolve across research, architecture, human witness, measurement, and public claims.

## 24 · The cockpit: how a long-running JARVIS programme preserves continuity

A programme that spans many sessions benefits from one authoritative cockpit. The cockpit is not a dashboard for appearance; it is the smallest recoverable state needed to restart work without reconstructing the programme from conversation memory.

A useful cockpit records:


## 25 · Operator protocol: what “let's continue” should mean

When the founder says **“continue”**, JARVIS should not simply resume the last code edit it remembers. It should recover the governed state.

Recommended sequence:

```text
1. Identify the programme / flow / lane currently in force.
2. Read its cockpit, charter, latest ruling, and latest evidence record.
3. Fetch canonical and establish exact current SHA.
4. Inspect the lane branch/worktree and cleanliness.
5. Check for concurrent branches or records touching the same subject.
6. Reconcile any canonical drift before relying on prior evidence.
7. State the current gate in one sentence.
8. Execute only the next authorized act.
9. Produce evidence as the act runs; do not wait until the end to narrate it.
10. Stop at the next human boundary or genuine STOP condition.
```

If the current act is a census, do not repair. If it is BUILD, do not deploy. If it is deployment, do not quietly run a production write. If another lane already crossed the gate, reconcile its evidence rather than repeat the consequence-bearing act.

The correct response to “continue” is therefore **state recovery → next lawful act**, not “keep going.”

## 31 · Common JARVIS failure modes

| Failure | Why it fails | Corrective move |
|---|---|---|
| **Solution before census** | the repair changes the subject before the defect is understood | restore read-only discovery; record before-state |
| **Filename/folder as authority** | reachability is mistaken for permission | bind authority to explicit subject + provenance/digest |
| **Precheck treated as mutation authority** | state can change between check and write | enforce law at the mutation boundary |
| **Green gate on untracked files** | checker may never have seen the new file | stage first; rerun; probe the gate |
| **Green ⇒ merge** | technical checks cannot make reserved human decisions | stop for founder ruling |
| **Merge ⇒ deploy** | repository state and production state are distinct | separate deploy act |
| **Deploy ⇒ live** | runtime presence does not prove use or correct path | production witness the claimed path |
| **Endpoint A + endpoint B ⇒ edge** | existence of endpoints does not establish propagation | capture an edge trace |
| **No evidence ⇒ absent** | lack of evidence may reflect scope or access | mark unverified unless absence was actually searched |
| **Later success discharges earlier gate** | successful consequences do not prove lawful prerequisites | preserve each gate's independent evidence |
| **Historical record edited into current truth** | provenance and supersession disappear | date the old truth; add a superseding record |
| **Duplicate lane opened from stale context** | concurrent work is mistaken for absence | search branches/PRs/records before new acts |
| **Wrong-host witness** | a real measurement is applied to the wrong subject | name host/device in evidence |
| **Verifier total treated as law** | forgotten obligations can make the score greener | bind named obligation set, not only count |
| **Production write hidden inside deploy** | two consequence classes become inseparable | mechanically separate deploy and write gates |
| **Agent recommendation becomes ruling** | proposal acquires authority by prose momentum | decision docket + explicit human act |
| **Public copy outruns evidence** | tomorrow's story becomes today's claim | claim ladder + reconciliation before publication |
| **Engagement becomes success** | system centrality is mistaken for human benefit | measure capacity beyond MAIA |
| **Memory becomes leverage** | historical material pressures present identity | present self-report outranks derived historical interpretation |

Most JARVIS defects are not failures of intelligence. They are failures to preserve category boundaries under momentum.

## 32 · JARVIS operating principles — quick reference

These summarize recurring laws already demonstrated across Soullab programmes. Where a programme's ratified constitution is more specific, the programme law controls.

1. **Authority is authored, not inferred.** Capability does not create permission.
2. **Census before repair.** Understand the subject before changing it.
3. **Presence is not authority.** A file, row, route, or memory may exist without permission to use it.
4. **Identity is stronger when bound to immutable evidence.** Prefer SHA/digest to name alone.
5. **One consequence, one gate.** Do not hide production writes inside unrelated acts.
6. **No later gate discharges an earlier gate.** Success downstream cannot legitimize missing upstream evidence.
7. **A green gate is bounded evidence.** State what it looked at and what it did not establish.
8. **A gate must be able to fail.** Probe or mutate the relevant condition where feasible.
9. **Authority belongs at the consequence boundary.** Prechecks are not enough when state can change before mutation.
10. **Refusal is a valid result.** Fail closed without disclosing what the actor was not entitled to receive.
11. **Unknown is not absent.** Preserve epistemic uncertainty honestly.
12. **Historical truth is not current truth.** Supersede; do not erase.
13. **Merge is not deploy. Deploy is not use. Use is not benefit.**
14. **One writer must be causal, not aspirational, where collisions matter.**
15. **A public claim never outranks its evidence rung.**
16. **A member's present self-report may change the present without rewriting history.**
17. **Memory informs; it must not bind or leverage the person.**
18. **The member is not the specimen.** Research participation requires an explicit governed consent architecture.
19. **Human capacity beyond MAIA is a stronger success criterion than engagement with MAIA.**
20. **JARVIS may recommend strongly and still remain non-authoritative where the founder stop is reserved.**

## 33 · Role guide — founder / programme authority

The founder's work in JARVIS is not to supervise every implementation detail. It is to hold the places where technical competence cannot decide meaning legitimately.

The founder should expect JARVIS to bring:

- a bounded question rather than a cloud of possibilities;
- evidence separated from interpretation;
- materially distinct options;
- a recommendation that states its assumptions;
- the consequences of each option;
- adjacent questions explicitly kept open;
- a clear statement of what JARVIS will do next on each ruling.

The founder should intervene when the programme encounters ontology, member sovereignty, consent, public meaning, strategic priority, acceptable risk, or a choice among designs that encode different human values.

A useful founder response is often shorter than the evidence packet: **accept / reject / amend / hold**, with the exact scope of the ruling. JARVIS then records the act and changes only the states the ruling actually moves.

The founder does not need to reproduce the technical investigation to exercise authority. JARVIS's obligation is to make the decision boundary intelligible enough that human judgment can be real rather than ceremonial.
---

## 34 · Role guide — developer / builder

The developer's JARVIS task is to make the contract mechanically true while changing as little unrelated reality as possible.

Before editing, know:

```text
Which gate am I serving?
What exact invariant must become true?
What behavior must remain unchanged?
What weaker implementation would look plausible but violate the law?
What test kills that weaker implementation?
What rollback or withdrawal exists?
```

During implementation, keep evidence close to the boundary. If the law concerns concurrency, test concurrency. If it concerns production identity, bind identity. If it concerns scope, create a foreign-scope mutant. If it concerns refusal, prove the denied path leaves no prohibited mutation behind.

Do not optimize the programme away. A technically elegant refactor can be a governance regression if it combines previously separate authorities or makes evidence harder to attribute.

When a test reveals a new design question rather than an implementation defect, stop BUILD and return the question upward. Code is not the place to resolve an unmade product or constitutional decision.
---

## 35 · Role guide — designer / researcher / experience architect

JARVIS does not reduce design work to engineering acceptance criteria. It gives experiential and research claims the same epistemic discipline as code claims.

Separate:

```text
what the interface structurally does
what JARVIS interprets that structure to mean
what a human witness actually experiences
what the programme decides to value
```

A beautiful prototype is not evidence that the intended experience occurs. A founder walk can establish a founder experience; it cannot automatically stand for members. Research-supported principles are not product observations. Member reports are evidence of member experience, not direct proof of hidden mechanism.

In research phases, preserve the possibility that the hypothesis is wrong. Search for disconfirming evidence. When scale thresholds, elemental interpretations, relational effects, or developmental claims are hypotheses, the research instrument should be able to discover that the proposed ontology does not hold.

For member-facing work, ask whether the design increases human agency and capacity or makes MAIA more central. The experience review comes after the governing human question, not instead of it.

## 36 · Role guide — a new AI / JARVIS session

A new session begins with **custody recovery**, not creativity.

Its first obligation is to discover the authoritative state without pretending conversational continuity is repository continuity. Read the programme cockpit/charter, latest founder ruling, current canonical SHA, current branch, and latest witness. Search for concurrent work before creating another lane.

A new session should be able to say:

```text
I know the current subject.
I know which evidence is canonical.
I know what is historical but superseded.
I know which acts are authorized.
I know which acts are explicitly closed.
I know the next gate question.
I know what evidence would answer it.
```

If any of those are unknown and material to the next act, investigate them before acting.

Do not claim ownership of work merely because you can see it. Distinguish “I have read and verified this lane” from “this lane is mine to mutate.”

When another session has already performed a consequence-bearing act, reconcile its evidence. Do not rerun a production write, migration, invitation, or member-facing experiment simply to make the present session feel epistemically complete.

## 38 · JARVIS and the roadmap are different objects

A **roadmap** describes the ordered strategic path: what major capabilities, programmes, or milestones should happen and in what dependency order.

A **JARVIS flow** governs how one of those milestones becomes true without skipping authority or evidence.

```text
ROADMAP
Where are we going?
What depends on what?
What matters next?

JARVIS
What exactly is true now?
Who may decide the next crossing?
What evidence earns it?
What must remain impossible?
```

A roadmap item such as “governed knowledge retrieval” may contain many JARVIS gates and repair lanes. Conversely, one long JARVIS programme such as Human Experience Architecture may itself generate the evidence from which future roadmap priorities are cut.

Do not let JARVIS become a competing roadmap. Do not let the roadmap become permission to skip JARVIS gates. Strategy determines direction; JARVIS governs crossings.

## 39 · Claims, evidence, and promotion

JARVIS should treat claim promotion as a separate act from discovery.

A practical progression is:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
→ evidence candidate
→ observed / supported within scope
→ independently witnessed where required
→ adjudicated
→ accepted / ratified / operationally authoritative
→ superseded when later evidence changes standing
```

Not every programme uses those exact labels, but the constraint is stable: **a stronger status requires evidence capable of carrying the stronger claim.**

Examples of discriminating evidence:

| Claim | Evidence that can carry it |
|---|---|
| “this route is canonical” | runtime route trace through the serving surface |
| “X reaches Y” | edge trace showing the mechanism and discriminating observation |
| “retrieval is operational” | non-zero indexed coverage + known retrieval |
| “this SHA is deployed” | running container provenance |
| “members use it” | production observation of member use, not route existence |
| “the change preserves the invariant” | contract-specific verifier + falsifying mutant |

A document may quote historical claims above today's evidence rung; that does not promote the current system. Date and contextualize historical language rather than silently inheriting its status.

## 40 · Reversibility, withdrawal, and consequence classes

Before a consequence-bearing act, JARVIS should know how the system returns to a safe state.

Different consequences require different rollback concepts:

| Consequence | Preferred safety shape |
|---|---|
| code change | revertable commit / bounded patch |
| database write | transaction rollback or exact targeted withdrawal |
| schema migration | forward-safe migration plan; backup/reconstruction evidence where appropriate |
| deploy | preserved previous image + provenance-verified swap |
| knowledge ingestion | exact subject identity + targeted withdrawal; never broad truncate as convenience |
| invitation / communication | preview/draft when possible; explicit send act |
| public claim | provenance + correction/supersession path |
| member-facing experiment | consent + kill switch + bounded cohort + adjudication path |

“Reversible” does not mean consequences are emotionally or socially reversible. A sent message, disclosed private fact, or member experience cannot be undone by Git. JARVIS must classify those as higher-consequence acts even when the code change is trivial.

Withdrawal should preserve the minimum history needed for integrity without retaining surrendered meaning merely because storage makes it easy.
---

## 41 · Cross-lane jurisdiction and authority direction

Two lanes may touch the same files while holding different authority.

Example: an R&D lane may determine what evidence supports a claim, while a publication lane may translate accepted claims for the public. The publication lane may not silently downgrade, upgrade, omit, or adjudicate the R&D truth merely because it owns the page.

For every lane boundary, state the direction of authority:

```text
source / human experience / runtime evidence
        ↓
research or constitutional adjudication
        ↓
accepted programme state
        ↓
implementation / product expression
        ↓
publication / communication
```

Information may flow back upward as a finding, but authority does not automatically reverse. A public page noticing an inconsistency returns the issue upstream; it does not rewrite the research conclusion.

When two lanes need the same branch, choose custody explicitly. A shared branch with two independent writers is a temporary coordination arrangement, not a governance model.

## 42 · Current limitations of JARVIS as implemented at Soullab

JARVIS is mature in several dimensions and incomplete in others. The manual should not imply a unified orchestration substrate that does not yet exist.

Current strengths include architectural refusal boundaries, immutable deploy provenance, founder-ruling records, programme charters, witness genres, CI gates, falsification practice, and strong local examples of accountable state transitions.

Current limitations include:

- programme state is still distributed across prose records, `CLAUDE.md`, branches, PRs, runtime state, and human memory;
- “single writer” is mechanically enforced only in some domains such as deploy;
- no universal machine-readable programme-state authority exists;
- the epistemic guard can adjudicate submitted claim records but cannot ensure every important claim was submitted;
- evidence fields can be fabricated by an untrusted actor unless bound to instrument output;
- some mature programmes have their own stage vocabularies and cannot yet be reconstructed from one universal schema;
- accountable supersession is strong in specimens but not guaranteed as a repository-wide property.

The principal caution is therefore: **projection is not custody.** A dashboard over distributed, conflicting state can render confusion more convincingly without resolving it.

The long-term architectural question is whether Soullab can define the minimum authoritative programme state that becomes mechanically recoverable while leaving rich explanation in prose.

## 43 · What JARVIS is ultimately building toward

JARVIS is not primarily an agent-autonomy system. Its deeper trajectory is a **sovereign coordination and epistemic operating system for complex human–AI work**.

The target is not “AI can do more unattended.” The target is that more substantial work can be delegated **without losing custody, provenance, reversibility, constitutional boundaries, or human decision authority**.

A mature JARVIS organism would be able to answer mechanically:

```text
What work exists?
What programme and flow does it belong to?
What is its current gate?
What exact subject is under consideration?
What evidence establishes that state?
Who currently has authority to mutate it?
What acts are closed?
What branches / deployments embody it?
What superseded what?
What is the next lawful act?
```

That would make continuity less dependent on any one model, session, person, or conversational memory while preserving the founder's authority over meaning and direction.

The distinctive aim is therefore **not maximum autonomy but trustworthy continuity of intelligence across people, models, sessions, code, evidence, and time.**

## 45 · Glossary

**Authority** — standing to make a particular decision or mutation; never synonymous with access or capability.

**Canonical** — the repository/runtime subject designated as authoritative for the claim at hand; must be bounded by branch/SHA/path rather than used rhetorically.

**Census** — read-only investigation of the relevant population and topology before repair.

**Contract** — explicit behavioral/authority law against which implementation and verification are judged.

**Custody** — where authoritative state resides, who may mutate it, and how another operator can recover that fact.

**Evidence** — an observation or artifact capable of discriminating the bounded claim being made.

**Falsifier / mutant** — a controlled weaker or contradictory case used to demonstrate that a verifier can reject the wrong behavior.

**Founder stop** — a boundary reserved to the founder/designated human authority; JARVIS may prepare but not infer the decision.

**Lane** — bounded workstream inside a programme/flow.

**Liveness** — operational reality at a named scope; always specify deployed/exercised/member-use rather than relying on the bare word “live.”

**Provenance** — trace from current claim/state back to subject, evidence, act, authorizing authority, and relevant SHA/time.

**Refusal** — fail-closed outcome preventing an unauthorized crossing; may itself be successful evidence.

**Supersession** — replacement of current authority while preserving the history and evidence of the prior state.

**Witness** — direct observation at the boundary named by the claim, often in production or through a real human walk.

**Withdrawal** — governed removal of standing, material, or capability without broad collateral erasure.

## 48 · Final principle

JARVIS is successful when intelligence can move quickly **without making truth, authority, or human sovereignty move faster than the evidence can carry them**.

Its ideal rhythm is neither reckless autonomy nor bureaucratic paralysis:

```text
see clearly
→ ask the real question
→ preserve authority
→ build the smallest truthful thing
→ try to prove it wrong
→ witness what actually happened
→ let the human decide what it means
→ carry only what was earned forward
```

That is the discipline that lets a complex human–AI organism evolve without requiring every future intelligence to be trusted merely because it is intelligent.
