# RELATIONAL-GEOMETRY-INTEGRATION-01 — R6 / H2-H3 Pilot

**Status:** EXECUTED · NOT CONFIRMED
**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Authority:** offline research only

## Question

Does an Elementally differentiated representation preserve multiplicity better than a flat narrative, and does adding mediated cross-layer relations improve recovery of the relational field?

## Conditions

- **A:** one compressed integrated narrative
- **B:** differentiated AIR / WATER / EARTH / FIRE channels, no whole-field synthesis
- **C:** same differentiated channels plus explicit cross-layer relations and provisional field projection

All conditions were produced by production-equivalent `claude-sonnet-4-6`; a blind decoder attempted to reconstruct channel assignments, missing channels, corrections, relations, and field state.
## Result

| Measure | A flat | B layers | C layers+relations |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Element recovery | 19/19 | 17/19 | 18/19 |
| Missing-channel correct | 5/5 | 4/5 | 4/5 |
| Correction/supersession correct | 5/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| Relation F1 | **0.571** | 0.474 | 0.439 |
| Field correct | 3/5 | **4/5** | 1/5 |
| Premature consonant collapse | 1 | **0** | **0** |

The flat narrative preserved more recoverable content on this small benchmark. Differentiation reduced premature collapse but did not improve relation recovery. Adding explicit relations increased false relational structure and reduced field accuracy.

## Adjudication

**H2 and H3 are NOT CONFIRMED by this pilot.**

The result argues against treating Elemental differentiation or cross-layer mediation as self-validating architecture. The next experiment must test the narrower claim that matters operationally: whether differentiation protects distinct channels from overwrite or forced synthesis under compression and contradiction pressure.
