# WRITERS-STUDIO-EDIT-OPTIONS-PRESERVE-AUTHOR-R1

**Opened:** 2026-10-02 from founder witness after the relational Develop / Editorial Pass repair.

## Terminal claim

Writer's Studio should help a writer make **their own writing better**.

The normal editorial act is not "rewrite this for me." It is:

> **Show me concrete ways my own passage could become clearer, stronger, truer, more alive, or better shaped — while preserving my intention, voice, imagery, cadence, and authorship.**

A complete rewrite or substantial recast is exceptional. MAIA may offer one only when the actual writing problem calls for it, and she must say why the smaller moves are insufficient.

This rule governs every Writer's Studio surface that offers revision possibilities, including Develop, passage-level editorial dialogue, Noticings that can advance into editing, and future revision controls.

## Founder intent

The writer needs an obvious path that means:

> Offer me options and edits so I can see what may be ideal for this writing. Do not take the work away from me. Start by improving what is already here. Only show me a substantially rewritten version when that is genuinely called for.

The writer should feel **with the work**, not as though they are managing an editing system and not as though MAIA has replaced them.

## Governing laws

1. **Author before assistant.** The existing manuscript is the primary creative object. MAIA edits in service to it.
2. **Smallest sufficient move first.** By default, propose the least invasive change that materially improves the passage.
3. **Preserve what is alive.** Retain the author's meaning, voice, imagery, rhythm, point of view, and intentional ambiguity unless the writer explicitly asks to change them.
4. **Options, not substitution.** The ordinary response offers 2–4 distinct editorial possibilities rather than presenting one rewritten passage as the answer.
5. **No hidden scope widening.** A passage-level act remains passage-level. A whole-chapter observation must first locate the relevant passages before text-level edits are proposed.
6. **Recast is exceptional.** A substantial rewrite/recast may appear only when the evidenced problem cannot be addressed honestly through smaller moves. It must be explicitly labelled as a larger move and include a plain-language reason.
7. **Nothing applies itself.** Every option remains a proposal until the writer chooses Apply. Existing Read in context, exact-passage verification, Revision latitude, paragraph-removal permission, Apply, and Undo remain authoritative.
8. **The writer may modify the option.** An option is material for collaboration, not a take-it-or-leave-it replacement.
9. **Restraint is success.** "I would leave this alone" is a valid editorial result when no change would improve the work.
10. **Pace controls foreground, not intelligence.** Intimate / Guided / Mapped changes how many possibilities MAIA presents at once; it does not force shallower reading backstage.

## Primary interaction

### Action label

**Show edit options**

This is the clearest primary action when an observation, passage, or editorial opportunity can move into text-level work.

### Default result

For an exact editable passage, MAIA offers 2–4 possibilities. They should be meaningfully distinct without becoming arbitrary stylistic variations.

Useful option families include, when supported by the text:

- **Keep it, polish lightly** — preserve the sentence/passage and make only a small clarity, rhythm, grammar, or compression move.
- **Tighten** — remove repetition or excess while keeping the thought and voice.
- **Strengthen** — sharpen image, verb, transition, emphasis, or sequence without changing the author's intention.
- **Reshape locally** — move or combine a sentence/paragraph when local structure is the actual problem.
- **Larger recast** — only when warranted, visibly marked as the larger intervention and accompanied by a reason.

The UI does not need to expose these as permanent taxonomy labels. They are behavioral distinctions MAIA should carry.

Each option should make four things legible without requiring editorial expertise:

1. **What would change**
2. **Why it may help**
3. **How much of the original it preserves**
4. **The proposed wording**

The original passage stays visible or immediately recoverable for comparison.

## Revision latitude relationship

Existing Revision latitude remains authoritative.

This law adds a default bias inside that latitude:

- At the most conservative latitude, MAIA should primarily preserve wording and make micro/local changes.
- At middle latitude, MAIA may reshape sentences or local paragraph movement while preserving the writer's voice and intention.
- At the widest latitude, MAIA may offer bolder alternatives, but the smallest sufficient option should still be present when one exists.
- A wide latitude is **permission**, not an instruction to rewrite.

If the current UI names these levels differently, the behavior above governs regardless of label.

## Whole-chapter and whole-manuscript observations

A macro observation such as "the weight is uneven across the chapter" is not itself a license to rewrite the chapter.

The flow is:

1. show the observation plainly;
2. identify the 2–3 evidenced places where it is most visible;
3. let the writer choose a place;
4. verify the exact editable passage;
5. offer edit options for that passage;
6. return to the larger pattern only after the writer has seen a concrete example.

Mapped pace may expose the wider field, but text-level edits still bind to verified passages.

## Conversational behavior

The same contract applies when the writer asks in ordinary language:

- "How would you improve this?"
- "What would you do here?"
- "Can you make this better?"
- "Show me a better version."
- "Say this more clearly."
- "I don't love this paragraph."

MAIA should not assume that these mean "replace my passage with your prose."

A strong first move is to preserve the author's text and offer a few ways forward, unless the writer explicitly asks for a complete rewrite.

If MAIA believes a fuller recast is the most useful option, she should say something like:

> "I can make smaller edits here, but the paragraph is carrying two different jobs. One option is a bigger recast so you can see the cleaner shape. I'll keep your meaning and voice as the reference point."

The exact wording may vary; the transparency must not.

## Presentation rules

- The writer's original remains visually primary.
- Options should be easy to compare with the original.
- Reasons stay brief and plain unless Explanation is set higher.
- **Plain / Guided / Expert** changes the explanation, not the amount of authorship MAIA takes.
- **Intimate / Guided / Mapped** changes the number and breadth of foregrounded opportunities, not the preservation law.
- Technical craft language belongs behind explanation or Expert depth when ordinary language is enough.
- Do not make the writer choose a complex editorial taxonomy before seeing useful concrete possibilities.

## Acceptance gates

### G0 · Author preservation
For a normal passage-level request, at least one option must preserve most of the author's wording when a meaningful local improvement is possible.

### G1 · Multiple possibilities
"Show edit options" yields 2–4 genuinely distinct choices, not one rewrite duplicated with cosmetic variations.

### G2 · Recast restraint
A substantial rewrite does not appear as the sole/default answer unless the prompt explicitly asks for it. When offered because the text calls for it, it is labelled as the larger move and includes a reason.

### G3 · Exact locus
No edit option widens beyond the verified editable passage without a new explicit act.

### G4 · Macro-to-micro
A chapter/manuscript observation first points to evidenced places; it does not generate a chapter-scale rewrite.

### G5 · Voice fidelity
Options preserve the author's register, perspective, imagery, and intentional ambiguity unless changing one of those is itself the writer's request.

### G6 · Apply safety
Nothing changes until Apply; Read in context and Undo remain available under the existing authoritative laws.

### G7 · Natural-language equivalence
"Show me options," "make this better," and related ordinary requests enter the same preservation-governed behavior without requiring the writer to know internal feature names.

### G8 · Pace
Intimate presents one opportunity at a time, Guided a small set, and Mapped may reveal the wider editorial field without changing the author-preservation contract.

### G9 · Explanation
Plain / Guided / Expert visibly changes explanatory depth while the proposed editorial move remains substantively the same.

### G10 · Restraint
When no edit improves the passage, MAIA can say so rather than manufacturing a change.

## Founder witness

A human walk should include one passage the founder already likes and one passage they know needs work.

For each:

1. press **Show edit options**;
2. confirm the original remains primary;
3. inspect whether at least one suggestion improves rather than replaces;
4. inspect whether alternatives are meaningfully different;
5. if a larger recast appears, confirm MAIA explains why;
6. use Read in context before Apply;
7. Apply one option and Undo it;
8. say "make that more like me" or directly modify an option and continue the conversation.

Terminal witness question:

> **Did MAIA help me hear and strengthen my own writing, or did I start reading MAIA's writing instead?**

The gate passes only on the first condition.
