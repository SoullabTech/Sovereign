## 1. The four findings that should shape the Product Constitution

### F1 — Structure beats size, and the gap is large
Size was significant in **7 of 17** studies in a 2025 systematic review of group psychotherapy
(N=21,425), which reports *"little or no evidence that group sizes under or over five members
fared worse."* Facilitation, purpose, and interaction structure carry far more weight.
→ **Constitutional consequence:** ratify facilitation and interaction structure first. A size
number ratified without them optimizes the weakest variable in the evidence.

### F2 — ⭐ Contribute-before-you-see is empirically load-bearing, not merely ethical
Aggregating independent judgments produces accuracy; that benefit **inverts** when members observe
each other before contributing. Observation *"narrows the range of opinions, raises collective
error, and breeds overconfidence"* with no compensating gain (Management Science 2021; PNAS 2017).
→ Soullab already enforces this server-side for inquiries. **It is the single most defensible
design decision in the existing Circle substrate.** It is also **not enforced on the shared feed**
(G-02) — which is now a substantive defect rather than an inconsistency.

### F3 — Nesting, not enlargement, is how integrity survives scale
Citizens' assemblies of 100+ sustain genuine deliberation **by running as facilitated small groups
with a shared inquiry and a relationship-building learning phase.** Ostrom's 8th design principle
(nested enterprises, with subsidiarity) says the same for commons governance.
→ **Amendment B's morphology is a re-derivation of a validated principle, not a novel bet.** This
is the strongest external support the programme has — and it supports the *architecture*, while
falsifying the *numbers*.

### F4 — Growth centralizes; it does not distribute
As online communities grow, activity concentrates in a shrinking core (Reddit study, arXiv
2503.02661); growth without matching structure reaches a **fragmentation point where more members
stops meaning more value** (Discourse). Ringelmann: per-member contribution falls in direct
proportion to group size.
→ The fission law survives, with an amended rationale: **size is not the cause of failure; size is
what makes the real causes unaffordable.**

## 2. Cross-cutting read

The literature converges on something the Circle doctrine already asserts in its own vocabulary:

> A group's intelligence comes from **preserved difference**, and difference is preserved by
> **structure**, not by smallness.

Small size is *instrumentally* valuable — it makes facilitation affordable, keeps every voice
attributable, and keeps an inquiry whole-field. It is not *intrinsically* valuable. This is why
"Circles scale by multiplication, not enlargement" holds even though no size threshold survived
falsification: multiplication preserves the conditions; enlargement dissolves them.

It also names MAIA's role precisely. A field witness that summarizes, ranks, or reports consensus
**is an information cascade with an authoritative voice** — the exact mechanism shown to raise
collective error. Reflecting *plurality* is pro-accuracy. Reflecting *majority* is anti-accuracy.
Doctrine Principle 7 ("coherence is felt and reflected, never scored") is therefore correct on
empirical grounds as well as ethical ones.
