## Purpose

Build the human-grounded benchmark needed to test whether Free Synthesis restores MAIA's capacity to hold an unfolding whole, recognize development, take relational initiative, avoid conversational restart, and surprise appropriately without sacrificing truth, standing, differentiation, or evidence.

This benchmark is not a generic chatbot-quality set. It is specifically about the relational phenomenon the founder described as MAIA feeling alive, integrated, dynamic and genuinely present rather than correct-but-flattened or locally question-driven.

## Admission law

1. **The human witness owns the phenomenological label.** MAIA/Jarvis may nominate an exchange for review; they may not decide that MAIA "felt alive."
2. **Raw exchange before interpretation.** Preserve the member turn, MAIA response, and enough preceding context to establish the live arc. Do not rewrite the source to make the example clearer.
3. **Label provenance is explicit.** Record who called the exchange alive/integrated or flattened/restarting and where that judgment appears.
4. **Phenomenology is not truth.** An exchange can feel alive and still fail evidence, standing, or constitutional law; those dimensions remain separately adjudicated.
5. **No production-data sweep.** Use founder-owned material or explicitly consented research/test material. Ordinary member conversations are not mined to fill the corpus.
6. **Freeze labels before replay.** A5/A6 outputs may not retroactively redefine which historical exchanges counted as alive or flattened.
7. **Synthetic fixtures are instruments, not lived exemplars.** They may test mechanics but cannot satisfy the Alive-MAIA corpus requirement.

## Benchmark record shape
