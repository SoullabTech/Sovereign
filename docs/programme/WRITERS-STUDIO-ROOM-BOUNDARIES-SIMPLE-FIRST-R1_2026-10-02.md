# WRITERS-STUDIO-ROOM-BOUNDARIES-SIMPLE-FIRST-R1

**Founder ruling:** 2026-10-02

## Governing sentence

> **Develop sees the Work. Write changes the Work. Review tests the Work.**

Writer's Studio should feel like writing with MAIA, not operating an editorial system.

## Simple-first law

The primary surface is humane, direct, and actionable. Technical machinery is secondary and collapsed unless the writer asks for it.

The normal order is:

1. what MAIA understands the writer is trying to do;
2. what is already working and should be protected;
3. what is getting in the way;
4. the few places that deserve attention;
5. a clear next act.

Only after that should the writer encounter evidence coordinates, coverage, taxonomy, craft terminology, diagnostic detail, model/process information, or other advanced material.

Those deeper layers remain available through disclosures such as:
- Why MAIA thinks this
- See the passages
- Craft explanation
- Reading details
- Advanced

They are not prerequisites to using the Studio.

## Develop

Develop is for understanding the Work from macro to micro.

It may address:
- intention and chapter role;
- what is working;
- structure;
- development;
- arc;
- themes;
- coherence;
- continuity;
- repetition and balance;
- reader orientation;
- places where attention may have the most leverage.

Its ordinary output should be short enough to grasp without learning an editorial vocabulary.

Develop does not become the editing desk. Its primary handoff for a text-level issue is:

> **Work on this in Write**

Structure recovery belongs in Develop because it concerns how the Work is organized, not rewriting prose.

## Write

Write is the prose workshop.

MAIA works on the exact passage and normally offers 2–4 concrete possibilities.

The default is the smallest useful change that improves the writing while preserving:
- meaning;
- voice;
- imagery;
- rhythm;
- perspective;
- intentional ambiguity.

A larger recast is exceptional and should explain why the smaller move is insufficient.

The writer may compare, modify, apply, ignore, or undo any option.

## Review

Review reads the changed Work as a reader/editor after revision.

It asks whether:
- the change solved the original problem;
- the chapter still holds together;
- anything else weakened;
- voice remains coherent;
- confusion, redundancy, gaps, or awkwardness remain.

Review is not another drafting surface.

## Presentation

The writer should see authored prose, not ingestion machinery.

Write should present a calm, professionally typeset chapter view. Import artifacts, page numbers, storage sections, evidence coordinates, and technical scaffolding are not primary manuscript content.

## Product test

At every surface ask:

> **Can the writer understand what matters and make the next useful choice without opening the advanced layer?**

If not, the surface is too complicated.

## Audience law — wisdom before writing technique

Writer's Studio is for people who may have deep lived, clinical, spiritual, creative, practical, or cultural knowledge without being trained authors.

A struggle with prose, structure, or book-making must never be treated as evidence that the underlying wisdom is weak.

MAIA's job is to help what is within become clear enough to enter the world, and then help what is already on the page become its strongest form for the reader.

The default editorial posture is therefore:

> **Protect what is alive. Clarify what is meant. Improve the form without replacing the source of the wisdom.**

Research, craft theory, editorial taxonomy, cognitive models, pedagogy, and advanced analysis remain backstage intelligence unless the writer asks to see them.

## Prose View law

Write opens on the Work as writing, not on the storage model.

For chapter-scale work, the normal presentation is a continuous professionally typeset chapter:

- chapter title and subheads read as hierarchy, not database rows;
- epigraphs, lists, and transitions receive appropriate visual treatment;
- print/PDF folios and storage coordinates are suppressed from the prose presentation;
- the canonical source text is not rewritten merely to make the display cleaner;
- exact section identity remains underneath so editing, saving, selection, Apply, and Undo stay precise.

**Prose** is the default reading/writing context. **Edit** is the exact section-working surface.

A Develop handoff to an exact problem may enter Edit directly because the writer has already chosen what they want to work on.

## Chapter-first Develop law

Develop opens with one primary act:

> **Read this chapter**

The writer should not have to choose a lens before MAIA can demonstrate that she understands the chapter.

A completed chapter read begins with:
1. one specific, earned positive observation that shows MAIA understands what the chapter is trying to do;
2. what is already working and should be protected;
3. what may need attention;
4. where MAIA would start.

The tone is conversational, light, clear, and directive. Technical vocabulary is optional.

The existing editorial pass, developmental process models, intent cards, lineage, evidence, saved readings, and other advanced machinery remain available under **More ways to explore** rather than dominating the first encounter.

## Macro-to-micro options

After the chapter read, the writer may choose:
- How does this chapter fit the book’s arc?
- Does it belong here?
- Show me what to strengthen.
- Show me an edited version.
- Chapter scorecard.

These are options, not required workflow.

The chapter scorecard is explicitly optional and transparent. It uses the same frozen chapter readings rather than rereading the Work and reports evidence-bound craft scores for Clarity, Coherence, Reader orientation, Voice, and Momentum. Each score states the smallest change that could improve it. It is not a verdict on the writer or the value of the Work.

## Edit-strength law

Writer-facing language for revision scope is:

**Light → Careful → Moderate → Substantial → Heavy**

This is presentation over the existing five-level enforced editorial latitude law; the enforcement thresholds do not change.

Light stays close to the writer’s wording. Heavy permits a larger recast. The writer’s voice remains the reference. Paragraph removal remains a separate explicit permission and is never implied by Heavy.

When the chapter review offers **Show me an edited version**, it hands the strongest evidenced locus into the existing Write/Focus editorial path. MAIA’s proposed wording remains optional and Apply/Undo remain authoritative.

## Continuous Edit law

Switching from Prose to Edit must not collapse a chapter back to one ingestion/storage section.

Edit uses the proven section-native whole-manuscript surface scoped to the current chapter:
- the chapter remains continuous;
- headings remain visible;
- each section retains its own save identity;
- mounted editors are captured before the surface is replaced;
- existing version, conflict, Apply, Undo, and exact-passage laws remain intact.

## Founder acceptance cases

### Case A — a mature book seeking its strongest final form

**Elemental Alchemy — The Art of Living a Phenomenal Life** is already a book.

The Studio's task is not to manufacture authorship. It is to help the author:
- understand what the chapter and book are already doing;
- protect the voice, images, ideas, stories, and passages that are already alive;
- identify macro and micro friction without drowning the writer in editorial machinery;
- show the smallest useful improvements first;
- offer stronger edits and larger recasts only by explicit choice;
- test how revisions affect the chapter and book as a whole;
- help bring the manuscript to a level the author can confidently share with respected readers, prospective foreword writers, and the public.

**Terminal acceptance question:**

> Does the finished manuscript feel more fully itself — clearer, stronger, more coherent, and more generous to the reader — without ceasing to sound like its author?

### Case B — wisdom-rich, writing-inexperienced healer

A healer and community presence carries decades of stories, ceremonies, healing experience, faith, Spirit, guides, lived medicine, and service across many kinds of people, but does not think of herself as a strong writer and may never have written a book or manual.

The Studio must be able to help her move from spoken/lived knowing into form without mistaking lack of writing technique for lack of wisdom.

It should help her:
- speak or tell stories naturally;
- discover the book or guide latent in those materials;
- distinguish story, teaching, practice, ceremony, reflection, and context;
- find a humane structure without imposing a generic template;
- preserve oral cadence, symbolic language, relational warmth, and distinctive worldview;
- improve clarity and reader orientation without professionalizing the life out of the voice;
- progress from fragments and speech to coherent chapters and eventually a publishable Work.

**Terminal acceptance question:**

> When she reads the finished page, does she say “Yes — that sounds like me,” while a new reader can finally receive what she has carried?
