# AIN-SOURCE-FABRIC-02R10 — Emergence Semantic Fidelity Contract

Date: 2026-09-28
Parent: `990fe23d91d9dec8b4f913cd5f98cd783a54095d`

## Purpose

R10 moves from structural emergence to semantic emergence.

The system must now produce a proposition that says something about the actual source content rather than merely describing the architecture that connected the sources.

## Content-bearing law

A semantic proposition must carry identifiable content from its supporting source claims.

Generic language such as:

> the analytic and associative pathways meet in the Crystal Center

is insufficient.

A valid proposition must contain source-specific semantic anchors that can be traced back to admitted claims.

## Claim registry law

Each semantic claim used in R10 carries:

- claim reference;
- source reference;
- human-readable claim summary;
- semantic anchors;
- contribution role;
- temporal qualifier.

A proposition may not cite an unknown claim or a source it does not declare.

## Retrieval semantic witness

The first R10 proposition binds four source roles:

1. architectural direction — ADR 004;
2. witnessed current substrate state — Source Fabric census;
3. governance/orchestration law — Source Fabric contract;
4. historical qualification — J11 reconciliation.

Together they support a content-bearing conclusion:

> the system is converging on one governed retrieval engine; current semantic and full-text machinery is present but still fallback-orchestrated rather than fused; Source Fabric therefore belongs above the substrate as governance/orchestration rather than as another retrieval engine; historical runtime claims remain time-scoped.

## Semantic-ablation law

For constitutive semantic sources, removal must prevent the full proposition from being regenerated.

R10 distinguishes constitutive sources from qualifying sources.

Removing a historical qualifier may preserve the core proposition while narrowing its provenance and deleting the historical qualification.

This is not treated as equivalent output.

## Human-legible provenance law

Every admitted semantic proposition must produce a provenance receipt that explains:

- what is being proposed;
- which sources support it;
- the source path;
- what each source contributes;
- its temporal qualification;
- why each source mattered;
- what changes if that source is removed;
- contradiction;
- absence;
- uncertainty.

## Human-review law

Automated semantic-fidelity validation is necessary but not sufficient.

Human review remains mandatory for semantic adequacy.

The review asks:

1. Does each source summary accurately represent the source?
2. Does the proposition add a genuine relation rather than concatenate summaries?
3. Are temporal qualifications preserved?
4. Are contradiction and absence visible?
5. Do the claimed counterfactual dependencies hold?
6. Does the proposition avoid unsupported implementation or authority claims?

The human reviewer may reject a proposition that passes structural validation.

## Falsification conditions

R10 fails when:

- the proposition is generic architecture language;
- required source content is absent;
- a claim points to an undeclared source;
- a constitutive source can be removed without semantic consequence;
- historical standing is converted into current fact;
- contradiction is erased;
- absence is silently filled;
- uncertainty is omitted;
- provenance cannot explain why a jewel mattered.

## Authority boundary

Semantic fluency does not widen authority.

R10 grants no authority for:

- whole-person conclusions;
- psychological diagnosis;
- hidden interiority;
- prediction;
- causation beyond source support;
- spiritual revelation;
- persistence.

## Soul-Service law

> **Traceability can establish where a proposition came from. It cannot make the proposition the person's truth.**

The final meaning remains human.

## No-build boundary

R10 remains benchmark-only.

No live MAIA binding.
No member data.
No persistence.
No production deployment.
