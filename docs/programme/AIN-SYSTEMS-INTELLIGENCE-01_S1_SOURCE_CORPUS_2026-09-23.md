## A · General systems theory

### S01 — Bertalanffy (1968)
**Ludwig von Bertalanffy. _General System Theory: Foundations, Development, Applications_. George Braziller, New York, 1968.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: the programme of a general theory of systems as wholes of interacting parts; open systems; equifinality; the claim that models, principles and laws apply across kinds of system.
- Relevance: the origin of "system" as a first-class object of inquiry across disciplines.
- Limitation: the isomorphism claim (that the same laws hold across domains) is a programme, not a result; much of the book is argument by example.

## B · First-order cybernetics and information

### S02 — Wiener (1948)
**Norbert Wiener. _Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine_. Technology Press / John Wiley & Sons, 1948 (2nd ed. MIT Press, 1961).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_HAZARD
- Contribution: feedback as the unifying concept of control and communication in machines and organisms; negative feedback, oscillation, stability.
- Relevance: the original precise meaning of FEEDBACK that later traditions inherit and dilute.
- Hazard: the orientation is regulation and control, described from a standpoint outside the description. The regulation described may itself be endogenous to the organism (see S03, S55); the hazard is the design stance over a person, not the location of regulation. SI inherits the concept while refusing the stance (S0 §VII).

### S03 — Ashby (1956)
**W. Ross Ashby. _An Introduction to Cybernetics_. Chapman & Hall, London, 1956.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_HAZARD
- Contribution: variety; the Law of Requisite Variety ("only variety can destroy variety"); regulation formalized over state sets.
- Relevance: the sharpest early formalization of what a regulator must be relative to what it regulates.
- Hazard: a regulator-centred formalism that Ashby applies to organisms as much as to machines; regulation in Ashby is frequently endogenous and homeostatic (S55). The prohibited chain of S0 §VII.1 arises when the formalism becomes a stance of designing regulation over a person, not from the formalism's description of regulation as such. S1's first draft conflated observer position with regulator location here; S1R1 corrects it.

### S04 — Shannon (1948)
**Claude E. Shannon. "A Mathematical Theory of Communication." _Bell System Technical Journal_ 27: 379–423, 623–656 (1948).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: information as a measure over a probability distribution; channel, noise, entropy.
- Relevance: one of two non-equivalent, non-interchangeable conceptions of INFORMATION in the lineage (the other is Bateson's, S07); they operate at different explanatory levels rather than as logical contradictories.
- Limitation: Shannon information deliberately brackets semantics from the engineering problem.

## C · Second-order cybernetics and the ecology of mind

### S05 — von Foerster (ed.) (1974)
**Heinz von Foerster (ed.). _Cybernetics of Cybernetics_. Biological Computer Laboratory, University of Illinois, 1974.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: the distinction between the cybernetics of observed systems (first-order) and of observing systems (second-order).
- Relevance: the direct intellectual ancestor of S0's REFLECT and of the rule that the observer is inside the system.
- Limitation: a programmatic and partly pedagogical volume; the distinction is stated more than it is operationalized.

### S06 — von Foerster (2003)
**Heinz von Foerster. _Understanding Understanding: Essays on Cybernetics and Cognition_. Springer, New York, 2003.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: collected essays including the constructivist epistemology of the observer ("On Constructing a Reality", 1973) and the ethics of the observer's responsibility.
- Relevance: the claim that a description of a system is a description by an observer with responsibility for it.
- Limitation: philosophical; not a method.

### S07 — Bateson (1972)
**Gregory Bateson. _Steps to an Ecology of Mind_. Chandler, San Francisco, 1972.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: information as "a difference which makes a difference"; mind as a systemic property of circuits; logical types; the double bind; the essay "Conscious Purpose versus Nature" on the pathology of purposive intervention in systems.
- Relevance: the second, system-relative conception of INFORMATION (a difference that makes a difference to a receiving system), non-interchangeable with Shannon's but not its logical contrary; secondary literature cautions against reading the phrase as a complete universal definition. Also the earliest explicit warning that purposive control of living systems is itself a systemic pathology, which is the ancestor of S0 §VII.
- Limitation: essayistic; concepts are not operationalized and were never intended to be.

### S08 — Bateson (1979)
**Gregory Bateson. _Mind and Nature: A Necessary Unity_. E. P. Dutton, New York, 1979.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: criteria of mental process as properties of systems; "the pattern which connects."
- Relevance: a candidate reading of what a "living relational system" is that does not reduce to control.
- Limitation: as above.

## D · Dynamical systems

### S09 — Strogatz (1994)
**Steven H. Strogatz. _Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos: With Applications to Physics, Biology, Chemistry, and Engineering_. Addison-Wesley, Reading MA, 1994 (later Perseus/Westview).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: the standard exposition of phase space, fixed points, limit cycles, bifurcations, attractors, and chaos.
- Relevance: the precise, testable meaning of ATTRACTOR and of a THRESHOLD (bifurcation); the mathematics under S0's PATTERN and TRANSFORM.
- Limitation: requires identified state variables and equations or time series; says nothing about how to find them in a text, a team or a life.

## E · Self-organization far from equilibrium

### S10 — Nicolis & Prigogine (1977)
**Grégoire Nicolis, Ilya Prigogine. _Self-Organization in Nonequilibrium Systems: From Dissipative Structures to Order through Fluctuations_. Wiley, New York, 1977.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: dissipative structures; order arising through fluctuations in open systems far from equilibrium.
- Relevance: the physical meaning of SELF-ORGANIZATION.
- Limitation: physical-chemical systems with defined thermodynamic quantities.

### S11 — Prigogine & Stengers (1984)
**Ilya Prigogine, Isabelle Stengers. _Order Out of Chaos: Man's New Dialogue with Nature_. Bantam, New York, 1984.**

- Class: SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: popular and philosophical extension of dissipative-structure theory to time, irreversibility and the sciences generally.
- Relevance: the most-cited bridge from thermodynamics to general "self-organization" talk.
- Limitation: the extension beyond physics is interpretive; it is the source of much loose usage.

### S12 — Haken (1977)
**Hermann Haken. _Synergetics: An Introduction. Nonequilibrium Phase Transitions and Self-Organization in Physics, Chemistry and Biology_. Springer, Berlin, 1977.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: order parameters, control parameters, the slaving principle, circular causality between macro-pattern and micro-components.
- Relevance: a precise account of how a whole-level variable can constrain parts without violating micro-dynamics; the most formal treatment of what SCALE and EMERGE might mean together.
- Limitation: near-instability regimes; the slaving principle is a regime-specific approximation, not a general law of organization.

## F · Complexity science

### S13 — Kauffman (1993)
**Stuart A. Kauffman. _The Origins of Order: Self-Organization and Selection in Evolution_. Oxford University Press, 1993.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: NK models, Boolean networks, order at the edge of chaos; self-organization as a source of biological order alongside selection.
- Relevance: a concrete computational meaning of SELF-ORGANIZATION and of attractors in discrete networks.
- Limitation: model results; the biological interpretation is contested.

### S14 — Kauffman (1995)
**Stuart A. Kauffman. _At Home in the Universe: The Search for Laws of Self-Organization and Complexity_. Oxford University Press, 1995.**

- Class: SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: popular synthesis of S13; the "adjacent possible."
- Limitation: interpretive; treated as inspiration only.

### S15 — Holland (1995)
**John H. Holland. _Hidden Order: How Adaptation Builds Complexity_. Addison-Wesley, Reading MA, 1995.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: complex adaptive systems; aggregation, tagging, nonlinearity, flows, diversity, internal models, building blocks.
- Relevance: a vocabulary of properties and mechanisms of adaptive wholes; "internal models" as a system's own anticipation.
- Limitation: agent-based; presupposes identifiable agents and rules.

### S16 — Bak (1996)
**Per Bak. _How Nature Works: The Science of Self-Organized Criticality_. Copernicus / Springer, New York, 1996.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: self-organized criticality; power-law statistics of avalanches without a tuned control parameter.
- Relevance: a different meaning of "self-organization" from Prigogine's and Kauffman's; a caution that power-law patterns are often over-read.
- Limitation: universality claims are contested; many power laws have other explanations.

### S17 — Simon (1962)
**Herbert A. Simon. "The Architecture of Complexity." _Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society_ 106(6): 467–482 (1962).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: hierarchy and near-decomposability as the architecture of complex systems; why levels exist.
- Relevance: the most precise account in the lineage of what a LEVEL is and why nested scales are real rather than analytic convenience; the ground of S0's SCALE.
- Limitation: near-decomposability is a structural claim about interaction strengths; it must be measured, not assumed.

### S18 — Mitchell (2009)
**Melanie Mitchell. _Complexity: A Guided Tour_. Oxford University Press, 2009.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: a careful survey of complexity science that is explicit that "complexity" has no agreed definition or measure.
- Relevance: an authoritative statement that the field's central term is unsettled.
- Limitation: survey.

## G · Autopoiesis and its transpositions

### S19 — Maturana & Varela (1980)
**Humberto R. Maturana, Francisco J. Varela. _Autopoiesis and Cognition: The Realization of the Living_. Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science 42. D. Reidel, Dordrecht, 1980.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: autopoiesis; organization versus structure; operational closure; structural coupling; the observer as a living system describing living systems.
- Relevance: a definition of "living system" by organization, and a boundary that the system produces rather than the observer draws; the origin of enactivism.
- Limitation: a definitional theory whose empirical content is debated; deliberately excludes representation.

### S20 — Maturana & Varela (1987)
**Humberto R. Maturana, Francisco J. Varela. _The Tree of Knowledge: The Biological Roots of Human Understanding_. Shambhala, Boston, 1987.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: accessible restatement of S19 extended to language, social systems and ethics.
- Limitation: the social extension is asserted, not demonstrated.

### S21 — Luhmann (1984 / 1995)
**Niklas Luhmann. _Soziale Systeme: Grundriß einer allgemeinen Theorie_. Suhrkamp, Frankfurt, 1984. English: _Social Systems_, trans. J. Bednarz Jr., Stanford University Press, 1995.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: social systems as operationally closed, autopoietic systems of communication; persons are environment to social systems, not their elements.
- Relevance: the clearest case of one word (autopoiesis, boundary, closure) carrying a different meaning in a different tradition; also the strongest claim that the human being is not inside the social system as a component.
- Limitation: whether social systems can be autopoietic is contested (S22).

### S22 — Mingers (2002)
**John Mingers. "Can Social Systems Be Autopoietic? Assessing Luhmann's Social Theory." _The Sociological Review_ 50(2): 278–299 (2002). DOI 10.1111/1467-954X.00367.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: a systematic critique of the transposition of autopoiesis from biology to communication.
- Relevance: documents that the transposition is a live disagreement, not a settled extension.

## H · Relational biology and anticipation

### S23 — Rosen (1985)
**Robert Rosen. _Anticipatory Systems: Philosophical, Mathematical, and Methodological Foundations_. Pergamon, Oxford, 1985 (2nd ed. Springer, 2012).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: an anticipatory system contains a predictive model of itself and/or its environment and changes state in accord with that model's predictions of a later instant.
- Relevance: a precise notion of a system that includes its own model; directly relevant to REFLECT and to the internal-model idea in S15.
- Limitation: formalism (category-theoretic) is dense and contested; anticipation is defined, not measured.

### S24 — Rosen (1991)
**Robert Rosen. _Life Itself: A Comprehensive Inquiry into the Nature, Origin, and Fabrication of Life_. Columbia University Press, New York, 1991.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: relational biology; complexity as non-fragmentability (a complex system is one with no largest model); organisms as closed to efficient causation.
- Relevance: the strongest formal statement in the lineage of "a living relational system" as an object distinct from a mechanism.
- Limitation: the central impossibility claims are disputed; the mathematics has been challenged.

## I · Ecological systems and critical transitions

### S25 — Holling (1973)
**C. S. Holling. "Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems." _Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics_ 4: 1–23 (1973).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: resilience as the capacity to absorb disturbance and persist, distinguished from stability as return to equilibrium; multiple stable states.
- Relevance: a non-equilibrium meaning of "pattern" and "attractor basin" for living systems.

### S26 — Gunderson & Holling (eds.) (2002)
**Lance H. Gunderson, C. S. Holling (eds.). _Panarchy: Understanding Transformations in Human and Natural Systems_. Island Press, Washington DC, 2002.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_INSPIRATION
- Contribution: the adaptive cycle (growth, conservation, release, reorganization) and nested cycles across scales ("panarchy").
- Relevance: a multi-scale, cyclic model of transformation in living systems; the closest thing in the ecological lineage to a qualitative phase model.
- Limitation: heuristic; the adaptive cycle is a descriptive schema rather than a derived result, and it is precisely the kind of schema that invites premature mapping onto Spiralogic (held for S6).

### S27 — Scheffer et al. (2009)
**Marten Scheffer, Jordi Bascompte, William A. Brock, Victor Brovkin, Stephen R. Carpenter, Vasilis Dakos, Hermann Held, Egbert H. van Nes, Max Rietkerk, George Sugihara. "Early-warning signals for critical transitions." _Nature_ 461: 53–59 (2009). DOI 10.1038/nature08227.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: generic early-warning signals (critical slowing down, rising variance and autocorrelation) that may precede a critical transition across system classes.
- Relevance: the strongest evidentiary basis for detecting an approaching THRESHOLD from time series.

### S28 — Dakos et al. (2012)
**Vasilis Dakos, Stephen R. Carpenter, William A. Brock, Aaron M. Ellison, Vishwesha Guttal, et al. "Methods for Detecting Early Warnings of Critical Transitions in Time Series Illustrated Using Simulated Ecological Data." _PLoS ONE_ 7(7): e41010 (2012). DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0041010.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: a methods toolbox for early-warning detection, applied to simulated series with known ground truth.
- Relevance: a precedent for exactly the synthetic-fixture discipline S0 §X requires; the fixtures have known transitions.

### S29 — Boettiger & Hastings (2012)
**Carl Boettiger, Alan Hastings. "Quantifying limits to detection of early warning for critical transitions." _Journal of the Royal Society Interface_ 9(75): 2527–2539 (2012). DOI 10.1098/rsif.2012.0125.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_ALTERNATIVE
- Contribution: proposed early-warning methods rarely characterize their error rates; false-alarm and missed-warning rates must be quantified.
- Relevance: the limiting argument for TRANSFORM claims — a signal without a characterized error rate is not evidence.

## J · System dynamics

### S30 — Forrester (1961)
**Jay W. Forrester. _Industrial Dynamics_. MIT Press, Cambridge MA, 1961.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: stocks, flows, delays and feedback loops as the modelling substrate of organizational behaviour; simulation over time.
- Relevance: the origin of the causal-loop and stock-flow vocabulary that later "systems thinking" popularized.

### S31 — Meadows (1999)
**Donella H. Meadows. _Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System_. The Sustainability Institute, Hartland VT, 1999.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_HAZARD
- Contribution: twelve ranked places to intervene, from parameters to paradigms.
- Relevance: the most-cited account of intervention in systems; its ranking is explicitly heuristic.
- Hazard: the essay's frame is intervention; applied to a person it is the prohibited chain.

### S32 — Meadows (2008)
**Donella H. Meadows (ed. Diana Wright). _Thinking in Systems: A Primer_. Chelsea Green, White River Junction VT, 2008.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: accessible account of stocks, flows, feedback, delays, resilience, self-organization and hierarchy, with explicit "system traps."
- Relevance: the standard reference behind the popular category; careful about boundaries being choices.

### S33 — Sterman (1989)
**John D. Sterman. "Modeling Managerial Behavior: Misperceptions of Feedback in a Dynamic Decision Making Experiment." _Management Science_ 35(3): 321–339 (1989). DOI 10.1287/mnsc.35.3.321.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: experimental evidence that people systematically misperceive feedback, delays and accumulation in dynamic tasks.
- Relevance: a human baseline for systems perception failure; the empirical reason a null fixture is needed for machines and humans alike.

### S34 — Sterman (2000)
**John D. Sterman. _Business Dynamics: Systems Thinking and Modeling for a Complex World_. Irwin/McGraw-Hill, Boston, 2000.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: the reference text for system dynamics modelling; explicit treatment of delays, nonlinearity, and model validation.
- Relevance: the operational meaning of DELAY and of a validated dynamic model.

### S35 — Cronin, Gonzalez & Sterman (2009)
**Matthew A. Cronin, Cleotilde Gonzalez, John D. Sterman. "Why don't well-educated adults understand accumulation? A challenge to researchers, educators, and citizens." _Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes_ 108(1): 116–130 (2009).**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: stock–flow failure persists in highly educated subjects across presentation formats.
- Relevance: the human dynamics-misperception baseline: fluent, educated reasoners fail the simplest dynamics task. Not a null-organization experiment (S1R1 correction; see landscape §11.3, finding 2).

## L · Process philosophy

## M · Enactivism and embodied cognition

### S41 — Varela, Thompson & Rosch (1991)
**Francisco J. Varela, Evan Thompson, Eleanor Rosch. _The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience_. MIT Press, Cambridge MA, 1991.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: enaction; cognition as embodied action; explicit dialogue between cognitive science and first-person contemplative practice.

### S42 — Thompson (2007)
**Evan Thompson. _Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind_. Belknap Press of Harvard University Press, Cambridge MA, 2007.**

### S43 — Di Paolo (2005)

- Contribution: autopoiesis alone does not yield sense-making; adaptivity (self-regulation with respect to conditions of viability) is required.
- Relevance: a precise argument that organization plus regulation, not organization alone, is what makes a system care about its own trajectory.

## O · Unifying claimants

### S48 — Friston (2010)
**Karl Friston. "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?" _Nature Reviews Neuroscience_ 11: 127–138 (2010). DOI 10.1038/nrn2787.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE / SOURCE_OF_HAZARD
- Contribution: a proposal that action, perception and learning minimize variational free energy; a unifying claim over organisms as self-evidencing systems.
- Relevance: the most influential contemporary claim to unify self-organization, regulation and cognition under one principle. Its formal foundations have been technically criticized (S53, S54), which is the support for calling its status contested.
- Hazard: the principle is stated as an optimization; a person modelled under it is, by construction, an optimizer of a single quantity, which is the frame S0 §VII forbids as a stance toward a member even where it is lawful as a scientific hypothesis about organisms.

## P · Machine detection of systems structure

### S49 — Hosseinichimeh, Majumdar, Williams & Ghaffarzadegan (2024)
**Niyousha Hosseinichimeh, Aritra Majumdar, Ross Williams, Navid Ghaffarzadegan. "From text to map: a system dynamics bot for constructing causal loop diagrams." _System Dynamics Review_ 40(3): e1782 (2024). DOI 10.1002/sdr.1782. Preprint arXiv:2402.11400.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE
- Contribution: a language-model pipeline that extracts causal loop diagrams from text; identifies roughly 60% of links and feedback loops across two datasets (in the second, 30 vignette responses, 56% of causal relationships and 83% of loops). In that second dataset the modelers found no feedback loop in 25 of the 30 responses, and the bot reported zero loops in 23 of those 25 (two false-positive loops), which the authors describe as a high true-negative rate.
- Relevance: the first controlled measurement S1 found of a language model detecting feedback structure from prose, and the first machine precedent for a loop-absent negative condition.
- Limitation: the negative condition is at the feedback-loop level (no loop warranted → report no loop); it is not a whole-faculty null-organization fixture of the kind S0's F-SI8 names. S1's original characterization ("reports no null-organization condition") was falsified at independent review and is withdrawn.

### S50 — Liu & Keith (2024 / 2025)
**Ning-Yuan Georgia Liu, David R. Keith. "Leveraging Large Language Models for Automated Causal Loop Diagram Generation: Enhancing System Dynamics Modeling through Curated Prompting Techniques." SSRN (June 2024); arXiv:2503.21798 (2025); earlier version in _Proceedings of the International System Dynamics Conference_ 2024.**

- Class: ESTABLISHED_SOURCE (preprint; authorship completed at S1R1)
- Contribution: for simple model structures, curated prompting yields diagrams of quality comparable to expert-built ones.
- Limitation: "simple structures"; comparison to experts on positive cases only, as far as S1 read.

### S51 — Leitch (2026)
