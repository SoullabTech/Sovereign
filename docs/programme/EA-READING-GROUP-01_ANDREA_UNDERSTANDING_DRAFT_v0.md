## 1. What we are doing

A reading group around *Elemental Alchemy: The Ancient Art of Living a Phenomenal Life*, hosted by
Andrea, running on software Soullab already has. Readers gather around the book; Andrea holds the
container.

It is the first group of its kind on this platform. Nothing is being built for it — we are
deliberately using what exists rather than guessing first and asking people to inhabit the guess.

## 2. Roles

**Andrea is the host.** In the software this is the role called `facilitator`. It means she — and
only she — opens the question that each gathering forms around. Readers respond in their own words.

**Kelly is the author of the book and the steward of the platform. He is not the host.** He has no
host capability in this group. If he takes part in a gathering, he joins as an ordinary member and
his presence is disclosed, like anyone else's.

The line we are holding, in both directions:

> Kelly's authorship does not give him authority over the gathering.
> Andrea's hosting does not give her authority over the book or the platform.

## 3. What Andrea can and cannot see

Stated in both directions, so that neither half is left ambiguous.

**She can see** material a reader intentionally shares into the group, according to the sharing
controls of that surface. That is what hosting shows her.

**She cannot see** a reader's private journal, private MAIA conversations, private reflections,
memory, or any other material merely because she is the host. A reader can also un-share something
later; the original stays theirs and untouched.

Hosting does not change Andrea's existing MAIA, memory, or other personal account settings.

## 4. Andrea's own material

Andrea will bring things that are hers: how she structures a gathering, the questions she asks,
her sequencing, her language, the practices she introduces.

**Proposed default: it stays hers.**

- Her facilitation material is not Soullab's by virtue of being used here.
- If Soullab later wants to generalize any of it into a reusable product feature — a template
  other hosts could use — that is a **separate conversation and a separate agreement, asked for
  before any reuse**, and **"no" is an available answer**.
- Hosting this group is not permission to generalize. Nothing is folded in quietly.
- If we are unsure whether something originated with her or with the platform, it does not get
  generalized until we have worked out which.

**A distinction that will matter later**, so it is written now rather than argued about then:

> **Andrea's feedback about what the platform needs is not the same thing as Andrea's facilitation
> material.**
>
> If she says *"I need to be able to see which chapter we're discussing,"* Soullab can learn a
> product requirement from that observation. If she devises a beautiful three-part inquiry
> sequence for Chapter 4, that sequence does not become part of a reusable template merely because
> it revealed a useful pattern.

**Attribution**: Andrea is named as the host of this group wherever the group is described.
**OPEN** — whether and how she wants to be credited beyond that is hers to say.

### Time, compensation, and how either of us can stop

To be settled together, before readers are invited — not after:

- the time she is being asked to give: preparation, facilitation, administration, and any
  feedback or debrief
- compensation, or its absence, named honestly either way
- the initial duration of the experiment, if it has one
- that she can decline to continue
- what happens if she steps away after the group has begun

> We will agree before launch on the time Andrea is being asked to give, any compensation, and the
> initial duration of the experiment. Either Andrea or Soullab may decide not to continue. If
> Andrea stops hosting, anything she created remains subject to the same attribution and ownership
> understanding described above.

**Ending her hosting does not change ownership of her material and does not retroactively
authorize its reuse.**

## 5. What Soullab learns from this

What Soullab is trying to learn is **the shape of the hosting experience and the group container**:
what a host actually requires, what people expect to keep private, what cadence feels supportive
rather than obligating, whether people read in step at all, where discussion naturally wants to
happen, and what should still be there for someone who disappears for three weeks and comes back.

The boundary, stated as a promise that can actually be kept:

> Soullab will not use this Reading Group to profile individual readers or to produce
> participant-level reports for product discovery. Ordinary platform operations, security, consent,
> and support records remain governed by the platform's existing policies and are **not** converted
> into Reading Group research.

And:

> **MAIA does not host, and does not summarize the group.** It may help an individual member with
> their own reading and their own materials. It does not represent the group, infer a group mind,
> summarize members to one another, or combine what different people said. Andrea is the host —
> not a co-host with software.

## 6. Ending, pausing, leaving

- Andrea can stop hosting at any time, for any reason, without explaining.
- If she stops, the group does not continue under her name, and her material is not reused to run
  it without her.
- Readers can leave without their contributions being deleted or their absence being flagged.
- There is no attendance, no progress tracking, and no "keeping up." Someone who has read the book
  twice and someone who never finishes are both fully in the group.

## 7. What we are deliberately not deciding in advance

> **The group does not exist as research for Soullab.** It exists for Andrea and the readers to
> engage the Work. Its ordinary life may nevertheless teach us what a good reading group needs to
> be.

Left open on purpose:

- whether this is a **cohort** that begins, travels and concludes, or an **ongoing club** that
  persists while people come and go
- how often gatherings happen
- how much of the book each gathering covers, if it tracks the book at all
- whether the practices belong to the book or to the gathering
