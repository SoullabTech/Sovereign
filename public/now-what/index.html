Workshop Companion · live today

Six weeks in one executive's life

Michael, 52, CEO. He left your three-day program with one sentence: "I know something has changed. I don't know how to live it."

Workshop

Something cracks open. You say: "Between now and our next gathering, continue working inside Now What?"

First arrival

The room asks one question: Where's your attention right now? His answer becomes the first turn of the conversation.

A practice

One commitment emerges, in his own words: "Three quiet minutes before my first executive meeting: what kind of leader do I want to become today?"

Weeks 2–4

The budget review. The COO conflict. An unexpected recognition: achievement no longer defines identity — it expresses it. Some days he forgets. That's information, not failure.

Return

The room opens with: "Last time you chose this practice. What happened?" — and your next gathering starts six weeks deeper.

Your side: a field view showing only what Michael chose to share — questions alive, his practice, recognitions, shared reflections. His private reflections never appear. The full walkthrough: the overview deck. Walk it live: the room (sign-in required).

Workshop cohorts and private clients need different rhythms. The platform underneath is exactly the same — only the context changes.

My Studio · the vision — designed around your practice

A place to become the practitioner you're becoming

You already have a lifetime of experience. The studio helps that experience become a coherent practice.

Three decades of leadership. A Harvard certification in positive psychology. A coaching practice in formation. You don't start empty — you start with raw material. Dropbox stores files; Notion stores notes; a studio develops a body of work.

The studio does not ask practitioners to perform certainty. It gives uncertainty, experimentation, and emerging insight a place to mature into authentic practice — "I'm exploring… this is still emerging… this has become one of my core principles" are all legitimate entries.

States of practice — not rankings

Emerging → Developing → Established → Distinctive. The studio knows which season a practice is in — not to judge it, but to ask the right questions. An emerging practitioner needs help integrating learning with experience. An established one needs help articulating what they already know. Same environment, different questions.

Build my next workshop

"I'm teaching flourishing for healthcare leaders next month." The studio gathers what already exists — relevant slides, stories, exercises, book passages, consented participant insights, questions alive in recent cohorts. You assemble rather than start over.
