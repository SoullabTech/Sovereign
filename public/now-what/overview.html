Now What? × MAIA

The workshop creates transformation.
The companion helps people live it.

A walkthrough of the participant companion, the facilitator's view, and the environment that stewards your body of work. Fifteen minutes, one complete journey.

The central question

What happens between transformational moments?

Every transformational program faces the same gap. People leave inspired — and then life resumes. The insight starts fading by Monday.

Now What? creates the catalytic experiences. MAIA becomes the developmental companion that helps participants actually live what they discovered — until they return to the next human encounter.

Act one · A participant

Meet Michael

52. CEO of a regional manufacturing company. Successful, respected, financially secure.

And carrying a growing sense that success no longer answers the deeper question: now what?

A trusted colleague recommends your program. He attends the three-day executive workshop. Something shifts. He leaves with one sentence:

"I know something has changed. I don't know how to live it."

The problem every program knows

Monday arrives.

Meetings begin. The inbox fills. The old patterns reach for him.

Ordinarily, Michael would wait three months for the next gathering — running on remembered inspiration. Instead, you say: "Between now and our next gathering, I'd like you to continue working inside Now What?"

He opens MAIA.

Act two · First arrival — live today

Now What?

How are you entering this room today?

In your own words…
Begin

What you carry stays private in your own field. Sharing with your practitioner is a separate, explicit choice — off by default.

The interface is intentionally quiet. Nothing competes for attention. Michael writes: "I keep slipping back into the person I was before the workshop." His own words become the first turn of the conversation — he is received, not interrogated.

Act three · One practice — live today

Now What?

Now what will you actually live?

One practice. One experiment. One commitment. Not ten.

Three quiet minutes before my first executive meeting: what kind of leader do I want to become today?

Carry this practice

When you return, the room will begin from this practice — not from the beginning.

The conversation doesn't end in notes. It ends in one committed practice — in Michael's own words, carried into his field by his own gesture.

What actually happened…

This is the differentiator. The room never begins again — it begins from what happened. And by design, not living the practice is information about the practice or the season — never a failure of the person. No adherence scores. No compliance praise. Continuity, not conversation history.

Notice what's missing: technology. The person's development is the story — the companion only holds the thread. The same spine carries every relationship you hold: workshop cohorts between gatherings, executive clients between sessions, and — in time — leadership teams between off-sites. Only the rhythm changes. The architecture doesn't.

The second field · Your own — the vision

Vision Studio: a place to become the practitioner you're becoming

You already have a lifetime of experience. Vision Studio helps that experience become a coherent practice.

Everything so far serves your participants. This one serves you. Not an admin dashboard — a developmental environment. Three decades of leadership, a Harvard certification in positive psychology, a coaching practice finding its own voice: you don't start empty, you start with raw material. The studio does not ask you to perform certainty — it gives uncertainty, experimentation, and emerging insight a place to mature into authentic practice.
