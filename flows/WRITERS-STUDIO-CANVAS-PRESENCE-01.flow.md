**A, B and C are answered independently. An answer to one may not license a
change under another.** Specifically:


# A · CANVAS IDENTITY

### Question

> What are the actual acts available on the Canvas, and where — if anywhere —
> must they become perceptible for WRITE to read as a **place** rather than
> merely the current **mode**?

### Method

Census **actual acts only**.

⛔ **Do not invent a Canvas toolbar because the founder said "I don't see Canvas
options."** The report establishes that identity is weak. It does not establish
that controls are missing, and treating it as a feature request would answer a
question nobody asked.

Classify every act found:

```text
ACT EXISTS + HIDDEN / REMOTE
ACT EXISTS + ALREADY LEGIBLE
ACT EXISTS + NEEDS LOCAL PRESENCE
ACT DOES NOT EXIST
```

### What the output must determine

Which of these the finding actually is:

```text
missing controls        · poor composition
naming                  · merely insufficient identity
```

These have different repairs, and only the census can tell them apart. An
"insufficient identity" answer may require no new control at all.

## ⭐ The discipline that makes the lock workable

> **A finding may span questions; a remedy may not.**

So an observation made during B — *"the writing area feels lost partly because
the room has no identity"* — is legitimately recorded as relevant to **both A and
B**. But B still may not fix it by naming Canvas, **because A already owns that
remedy.**

This is what keeps the lock from suppressing real evidence. Findings travel
freely. Remedies stay where they were ruled.

## When a boundary must be crossed

⛔ If anything encountered requires crossing these boundaries, **stop and name the
category change** rather than quietly solving it inside the current question.

That is the drift protection: the failure mode is not a wrong answer, it is a
right answer given under the wrong question's authority, where nobody later can
see which ruling licensed it.

```text
A       RULED   · R1 approved · R2 hold · R3 open separately
```
