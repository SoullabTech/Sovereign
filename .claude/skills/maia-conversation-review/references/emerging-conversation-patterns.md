# EMERGING CONVERSATION PATTERNS

A research and discernment pass within the MAIA Conversation Stewardship Loop.
**Founder-authored 2026-08-13.** It does **not** authorize implementation.

Its purpose is to identify genuinely new conversational interaction capabilities that could deepen
MAIA's relational experience while protecting MAIA from trend-chasing, imitation, unnecessary
stimulation, and interface accumulation.

## Governing question

> **Is conversational AI learning a fundamentally better way for a human and an intelligence to be
> together — and, if so, what principle belongs in MAIA?**

Do not ask: *What new AI feature should MAIA add?*
Ask: **What human conversational limitation is becoming technically unnecessary?**

---

## ⛔ CONSENT RIDER — governs domains 1, 2, 6 and 10 (JARVIS-added, needs founder ratification)

Continuous listening, proactive audio and speaker discrimination all mean **an open microphone
processing ambient speech, including speech from people who never consented** — someone else in the
room, a child, a client. That engages the memory-consent invariants, Sanctuary Mode, and third-party
consent, for which MAIA's canon has no provision.

> **No always-listening capability may be designed, prototyped, or flagged-on without an explicit
> founder ruling that addresses third-party consent first.** "The member opted in" does not cover the
> other voices in the room.

Anything here requiring a persistently open mic is **blocked on that ruling**, not merely unbuilt.
Record the interaction insight; do not build the listening. This rider was added by JARVIS and is
awaiting founder ratification — treat it as binding until ruled otherwise.

Periodically review current **primary-source** developments from OpenAI · Anthropic ·
Google/DeepMind/Gemini · Apple interaction and accessibility guidance · and relevant primary research

Prefer primary documentation, research papers, system cards, developer documentation, and direct
product evidence.

- ⛔ Do not use promotional claims as proof of interaction quality.
- ⛔ Do not copy another product's visual language.

## The ten domains

### 1. Turnless conversation
Watch for systems moving beyond rigid *member speaks → stops → model processes → model speaks*.
Study: full-duplex listening and speaking · interruption handling · overlapping speech · pauses ·
hesitation · continuation after interruption · conversational backchannels · deciding to remain
silent · turn-end detection.

Ask whether MAIA could become less dependent on explicit turn boundaries. The desired direction is
**not faster conversation** — it is **more natural conversational temporality**.

### 2. Attentional presence
Study systems distinguishing: speech intended for the AI · speech to another person · ambient sound ·
thinking aloud · conversational pauses · unfinished utterances · genuine requests for response.

> **Can MAIA become better at knowing when she is being addressed and when she should simply remain
> present?**

Explore whether the Living Field can communicate attentional presence **without** labels, panels,
indicators, or audible interruptions. Prefer quiet visual acknowledgement over gratuitous
anthropomorphic backchanneling.

### 3. Modality without modes
Track movement toward continuous combinations of speech · typing · images · documents · camera ·
screen · gesture · environmental context. MAIA should understand these as ways a member participates
in **one conversation**, not separate application modes.

Preserve the standing invariant: **member-input modality and MAIA-output modality are independent.**
Changing how the member communicates must never silently change how MAIA responds. ⛔ Do not collapse
semantically independent controls merely because another product uses one microphone button.

### 4. Relational timing
Study silence tolerance · pause detection · interruption · response timing · backchannel timing ·
end-of-turn prediction · conversational pacing.

**Relational latency:** the best response is not necessarily the lowest-latency one. Ask *what timing
serves this moment?* Outcomes include immediate response · quiet acknowledgement · continued
listening · brief pause · allowing the member to continue · choosing not to speak yet.

⛔ Never introduce artificial delays merely to simulate depth. Timing must arise from conversational
evidence.

### 5. Visual backchanneling
Investigate whether the Living Field can provide what human listeners give through gaze, posture,
nodding, expression and quiet vocal acknowledgement. Possible field functions: *still with you ·
hearing you · gathering · something changed · response emerging · MAIA speaking.*

- ⛔ Do not turn the Holoflower into an audio visualizer.
- ⛔ Do not map syllables directly to luminance.
- Prefer slow, continuous, atmospheric transformation.

> The field should communicate **attention**, not audio amplitude.

### 6. Acoustic and paralinguistic understanding
Track advances in understanding pace · hesitation · interruptions · emphasis · volume changes ·
cadence · nonverbal vocalization. **Distinguish acoustic observation from psychological
interpretation.**

- **Admissible:** *"Your pace slowed and there were several longer pauses."*
- **Requires humility:** *"You are afraid."*

⛔ Never turn acoustic cues into confident hidden-state diagnosis. Ask whether acoustic information
helps MAIA *listen better* without making unwarranted claims about the member.

### 7. Semantic activity indication
Study interfaces moving away from generic *loading · spinner · processing · thinking* toward truthful
indication of actual activity — e.g. *remembering something relevant · looking through material the
member shared · gathering conversational context.*

⛔ Only surface an activity when the runtime can **substantiate** it is actually occurring. Never
invent inner experience to decorate latency.

> **Communicate meaningful process, not machine waiting.**

### 8. Conversation memory UX
Track approaches letting people understand what the system remembers · why something returned · when
continuity is active · how to correct remembered information · how to forget · and the distinctions
between conversation, episodic memory, preferences, relationships and durable memory.

MAIA's memory should create continuity **without becoming surveillance**. Evaluate against: *does
this increase relational continuity while preserving intelligibility and agency?*

### 9. Generative / adaptive interfaces
Watch for systems generating controls, cards, visualizations, tools or temporary surfaces in response
to conversation. Value: the interface need not display every capability permanently — a relevant one
can appear when needed and disappear after. Aligns with **Encounter → Conversation → Controls**.

But generative UI must not create instability. Ask: does the member know what changed? · does it
remain learnable? · can important controls be found again? · is generated UI accessible? · does the
conversation remain primary?

### 10. Ambient and proactive intelligence
Track systems remaining available without repeated explicit prompting. **Apply an unusually high
bar.** Distinguish **attentive presence** from **attention-seeking proactivity**.

⛔ Reject: engagement hooks · unnecessary notifications · manufactured urgency · unsolicited
psychological interpretation · retention mechanics disguised as care.

Consider proactivity only when it materially supports an already-established member intention or
relationship. **MAIA should become more available, not more intrusive.**

## Evaluation filter

For every emerging capability, produce:

**DEVELOPMENT** — what became technically possible?
**HUMAN PROBLEM** — what conversational limitation does it address?
**EVIDENCE** — what primary evidence demonstrates the capability?
**MAIA RELEVANCE** — does MAIA actually have this problem?
**MAIA EXPRESSION** — if the principle belongs here, what is its distinctly MAIA expression?
**COST** — complexity, latency, privacy, cognitive load, accessibility burden, compute, relational risk?
**VERDICT** — exactly one:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **WATCH** | important but premature |
| **PROTOTYPE** | relevant enough for an isolated experiment |
| **DESIGN INPUT** | principle should influence current design without adding the technology yet |
| **ADOPT** | evidence justifies bounded implementation |
| **REFUSE** | conflicts with MAIA's relational, ethical, attentional, privacy or sovereignty principles |

> ⛔ **Do not equate novelty with progress.**

---

## Standing MAIA refusal test

Reject an innovation when its primary effect is to: increase screen time · manufacture engagement ·
make MAIA seem human through deception · fill silence unnecessarily · increase stimulation without
increasing meaning · infer inner states beyond available evidence · obscure whether MAIA is listening,
remembering, reasoning, retrieving or generating · take agency away from the member · add permanent
chrome for an occasional capability · make the interface more impressive but the relationship less
clear.

## Current research horizon

1. full-duplex / turnless voice
2. silence and interruption intelligence
3. proactive listening
4. acoustic nuance without psychological overclaiming
5. continuous multimodality
6. adaptive/generative conversational UI
7. visual rather than verbal backchanneling
8. relational timing
9. memory transparency
10. graceful transitions between spoken and written conversation

**Research horizons, not roadmap commitments.**

### Where MAIA could be distinctive rather than catching up

Founder assessment: **visual backchanneling (5) + relational timing (4).** The Holoflower already
gives MAIA a vocabulary that OpenAI and Google do not really have. Everything else on this list is
mostly parity work.

### Convergence note

The current cleanup — less chrome, truthful states, one conversational viewport, field yielding to
transcript — is **not** polishing the old chat paradigm. It is clearing away the machinery that would
prevent moving beyond it. A surface with three competing authorities over vertical space and a
reserved status band cannot become a continuous field. The single-owner viewport unit is therefore a
**prerequisite** for this track, not a detour from it.

---

## Final standard

An emerging technology deserves MAIA's attention when it allows **less interface to support more
relationship.**

The test is not *does this make MAIA seem more advanced?* It is:

> **Does this let the member attend less to the machinery and more fully to the encounter?**
